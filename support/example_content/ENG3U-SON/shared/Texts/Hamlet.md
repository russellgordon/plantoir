William Shakespeare wrote *Hamlet* around 1600–1601, most likely for
performance at the newly built Globe Theatre in London. It survives in
three early printed versions that don't fully agree with each other —
scholars still argue about which passages belong in a "definitive" text,
which is worth knowing before you assume any single edition is the only
correct one.

## Why we're reading it

*Hamlet* opens Unit 3, and it's the reason this unit exists in the form it
does: a play, meant to be heard and staged, not just read silently off a
page. Everything the unit builds toward — staged readings, a
performance-based task — starts from taking that seriously from the first
scene.

## Who's who, at the start

| Character | Who they are |
|---|---|
| Hamlet | Prince of Denmark; his father, the king, has just died |
| Claudius | Hamlet's uncle; has married Hamlet's mother and taken the throne |
| Gertrude | Hamlet's mother, now Queen, married to Claudius |
| The Ghost | Appears claiming to be Hamlet's dead father |
| Polonius | Claudius's chief advisor |
| Ophelia | Polonius's daughter |
| Laertes | Polonius's son |
| Horatio | Hamlet's close friend, present from the opening scene |

## What to watch for on a first read

The language is Early Modern English, and it is meant to be spoken aloud —
read it that way, even quietly to yourself, before you decide a line is
impossible to follow. [[Iambic Pentameter and Dramatic Verse]], the
concept the class meets the class after this one, gives you the rhythm
Shakespeare is actually writing in, which makes the lines easier to hear
correctly once you know what to listen for.

Watch, from the very first scene, how much of what characters say to each
other is not what they actually mean — Claudius's public words to Hamlet
and his private motives are already pulling apart before Hamlet has said a
word. That gap is most of what this play is about.
