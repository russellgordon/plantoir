**Launched at the start of Unit 2; due in the second week of the unit.**
Individual.

One Canadian firm, taken apart: what kind of thing it is, what kind of
market it operates in, and what technology has done to both.

## The firm

Choose one with published documents — a listed company with annual
filings, a co-operative with member reports, a Crown corporation with an
annual report, or a large not-for-profit. You need at least two years of
material. Small private firms are interesting and undocumented, which
makes them the wrong choice here.

## What to produce

**A written study of about 1,200 words**, in three parts.

**Part one — the form.** What legal and organisational form is this, and
what follows from it? A sole proprietorship, a partnership, a private
limited company, a co-operative, a Crown corporation, a not-for-profit and
a multinational answer differently on who bears the loss, who receives the
surplus, and who can be held to account. Say which of those differences
actually bites for your firm.

**Part two — the market.** Which structure is your firm operating in, and
how do you know? Count the competitors, look at whether the firm appears to
set its price or take it, and check for barriers to entry. Then assess the
structure from at least three stakeholder positions — the firm, its
customers, its workers — because C1.2 asks for advantages and
disadvantages, and those differ by who you are.

**Part three — the technology.** What has changed for this firm because of
online sale, payment systems, data about customers, or automation? Be
specific and dated. "Technology has transformed business" is not an
observation.

> [!question] Is your firm a price-setter or a price-taker?
> This is the question that decides part two, and you can usually answer it
> from the documents. Does the firm announce prices, or accept them? Does a
> competitor's price change force its hand within weeks? Does it advertise
> on price at all? Three pieces of evidence make the claim; one makes an
> assertion.

## How it is marked

- **The form, and what follows from it.** Naming the legal form earns
  little. Saying which of its differences actually bites for this firm —
  who bears the loss, who takes the surplus, who can be held to account
  — earns the section.
- **A structure claim with evidence under it.** Competitor count,
  price-setting behaviour, barriers to entry. Three pieces of evidence
  make the claim; one makes an assertion.
- **Three stakeholder positions, genuinely different.** The firm, its
  customers and its workers do not agree about this structure, and the
  study says so in each of their terms.
- **Technology, dated and specific.** One named change, the year it
  arrived, and what it did to cost, to price, or to the work.
- **Sources.** Every figure carries the document it came from and its
  year.

Build from [[Market Structures]] and [[Costs and the Firm]], and use
[[What the Grocery Bill Shows]] as a worked example of the same reasoning.
