**Launched mid-Unit 3, due at the end of the unit.** Pairs, arguing
opposite positions. A written position of about 700 words each, then a
structured hearing in class.

A real proposal, on real ground, where a physical feature offers
something and taking it costs something else.

## The proposals on offer

An aggregate pit proposed on an esker or moraine. A subdivision on
prime agricultural land at the edge of a settlement. A small hydro
installation at a falls. A wind or solar installation on farmland. A
marina or shoreline hardening project on the waterfront. Housing proposed
inside a conservation authority's regulated area. Your teacher will
confirm the case; it must be one with a public record.

## Each of you writes

1. **The physical resource or constraint**, described accurately: what
   the feature is, how it formed, and what it supplies —
   [[Using the Physical Environment]].
2. **The benefits**, quantified where possible and attributed.
3. **The costs**, including the ones that land on people who are not
   party to the decision, and the ones that land later rather than now.
4. **Effects beyond the site.** Sediment, water, dust, traffic, habitat
   connectivity, downstream flow — the reason
   [[Sharing a Watershed]] is on the reading for this task. At least one
   of them must leave the watershed or the region altogether: whose air
   takes the dust, whose grid the power joins, whose water the sediment
   is still in three hundred kilometres downstream. Trace it to a named
   endpoint rather than to "downstream".
5. **Your position**, stated plainly, with the condition under which you
   would change it.

## The hearing

You will be asked to summarise your opponent's strongest point before you
answer it. You will be asked what evidence would change your mind. Both
are marked.

| Criterion | What earns the marks |
| --- | --- |
| Knowledge | The physical geography of the site is correct and load-bearing |
| Thinking | Costs and benefits weighed, with distribution across people named |
| Application | Real documents and figures, sourced and dated |
| Communication | Position argued, opposing case represented fairly |
| Effects that leave | One consequence traced past the watershed or the region, to a named endpoint |
| Your answer from the floor | The question put to you is answered with evidence rather than deflected |

## Two of you, and two separate marks

You are paired so that the room hears both sides, not so that you share
anything. Each of you writes your own seven hundred words, each of you
speaks for yourself at the hearing, and each of you is marked on your own
position, your own summary of your opponent's strongest point, and your
own answer from the floor. Your partner's mark is not yours and yours is
not theirs. Research together as much as you like — the planning file is
the same file — but the argument has to be one you would still make if
you had drawn the other side.

> [!warning] Do not argue from the general case
> "Aggregate extraction damages the environment" is a claim about the
> industry, not about this esker. The marks are in what this landform,
> this water table and this community actually make true — which is why
> [[The Farm Visit]] is useful preparation even when the case is not a
> farm.

TALK — Unit 3, Day 18, at the conferences on evidence already on that

  Ask: "This is refused. Where does the same thing come from instead,
