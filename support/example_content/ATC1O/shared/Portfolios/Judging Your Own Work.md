Every task page carries its criteria, published on the day the task
launches. Most people read them once and never again, and then show work
that misses two rows they could have fixed in one working period. This
page is that working period, used properly.

We do it together the first time, on a solo of mine, so that judging
against criteria is not a new skill on the day it is your own dancing
being judged.

## The routine

1. Open the task page. Put the criteria beside you — on your phone, on
   paper, propped against the mirror.
2. Run your work once, all the way through, without stopping to fix
   anything. You cannot judge a thing you keep interrupting.
3. Go row by row — or bullet by bullet, on the two tasks whose criteria
   are a list. For each one write **yes**, **not yet**, or **partly**,
   and one sentence of evidence: the count, the moment, the thing a
   watcher would see. "Yes" with nothing under it is a wish.
4. Find your weakest row. Not the easiest to fix — the weakest.
5. Write one sentence saying what you will change and when.
6. Then change it, in the time that is left. This is why the self-check
   is never scheduled on the day the work is assessed: a judgement you
   have no period to act on is only a worry.

Steps 3 and 4 are the whole exercise. Naming your weakest row honestly
is harder than any of the fixing, because it means saying out loud the
thing you were hoping nobody would look at.

> [!example]- One row, judged honestly
> From [[The Movement Signature]]: *"Repeatability — You can perform it
> twice the same way."*
>
> **Partly.** The first three movements are the same every time. After
> the turn I am improvising, and I have been telling myself it is
> "responding to the moment". It is not; I never decided what comes
> next. Weakest row. **Change:** fix counts five to eight this period,
> write them in the journal, run it twice before the end of class.
>
> That is worth more than "looks good to me" on all five rows, and it
> took less time to write.

## Use the camera for the rows you cannot see

Two or three rows on most tasks are about what a watcher sees — shape,
projection, whether the structure reads. You are inside the work and
cannot see any of it. Film thirty seconds on a phone, watch it once with
the sound off, and judge those rows from the footage rather than from
memory. [[Filming Yourself]] is the practical side; [[Video of Yourself]]
is what to do about flinching.

## Where this fits

Your judgement of your own work is not part of your mark, and neither is
a classmate's — see [[How Marks Work]]. That is deliberate, and it is
what makes this worth doing honestly: nothing you write here can cost
you anything.

What it changes is the work. A self-check that leads to one fix is the
cheapest improvement available in this course, and by the end of the course
it is something you do in the first five minutes of a working period without
being asked, which is the actual point.

Keep the sheets. A row you marked *not yet* in Unit 1 and *yes* in Unit 4
is exactly the evidence [[The Portfolio and Reflection]] asks you for,
and it is more convincing than any sentence you could write about having
improved.
