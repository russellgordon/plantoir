## Section __SECTION_NUMBER__ — notes to myself

- **Before week one: write the local mental-health support list.** The
  pages in this course deliberately name no local program, because a
  wrong name is worse than none and every board's services are
  different. [[Looking After Yourself While You Work]] tells students who
  to go to in general terms and stops there. Put the real names — the
  school's counsellor, the board's service, the local youth agency, the
  provincial line — in front of the class the day that page is taught,
  and leave them written somewhere students can find them again.
- Book the defence audience in the last week of the start of the course. Guidance,
  administrators, and co-op employers all fill up, and every student
  needs an adult who is not me.
- Order the spreadsheet room for Unit 3, days 2 to 5. The budget cannot
  be done on a phone.
- Contact two or three workplaces before Unit 2 for the students who
  cannot find an interview subject through their own network. Every year
  it is the same four or five students, and it is not their fault.

## Which GLC2O this is

Built from the course **effective September 2024**, which replaced the
2019 document without changing the code. Resources written for 2019 look
current and are not: **A1.2 was revised and A1.3 is new**, both about
mental health. Check any inherited unit plan against the curriculum
folder before reusing it.

## The arithmetic

Forty-two periods of 75 minutes plus a two-and-a-half-hour final
evaluation is 55.0 hours — one half credit exactly. Units run 14, 16, and
12. If your periods are a different length, change the number of class
pages rather than stretching the units, and the site's hours will stay
honest.

## Where the coverage is thin

Every specific expectation is addressed, and most are addressed twice.
`C1.3` — communicating what you can do, in the right form, to somebody
who decides — is reached twice, on [[The Application]] and again on
[[The Plan Defence]]. `B1.3` and `B2.4` are each taught twice and
carried by no page in Tasks, but they are not the same case and it is
worth knowing which is which. Both of their overall expectations are
evaluated through other tasks, which is what the policy actually
requires.

**`B2.4` — networking — is a deliberate no.** Nothing marked in this
course asks a student to *analyse the role of networking*. They do it,
in [[The Informational Interview]] and in
[[Evidence of What You Can Do]], and they are never asked to argue about
it. Putting the code on a task would say the analysis is assessed when
no page asks for it.

**`B1.3` is a judgement call, and it is half met.** Its second clause —
identifying the skills you still need to develop — is exactly what
[[The Work Investigation]]'s skills section demands, with a criteria row
("Honest self-assessment") and two working periods behind it. Its first
clause, reflecting on how the skills you already have *have aided you in
your learning and in life*, is not asked for anywhere. If you want the
code on that task, write the missing clause into the skills section
first: one line asking what each evidenced skill has already got them
through. Do not transclude it and leave the section as it is.

## Where the hand-ins actually fall

Task pages name the day they are collected rather than a week, because a
week number is wrong the moment your calendar differs from mine. On this
42-period arc the six term tasks go in at periods **10, 14, 21, 30, 35
and 40** — weeks 2, 3, 5, 6, 7 and 8 of a five-period week. Four of the
six are collected part-way through the period, after a revision block:
check the class page before you announce a deadline.

The student-facing pages have not settled on a length, and I have left
that for you rather than guessing your calendar: six of them say the
course is ten weeks and [[Help Sessions]] says nine. Forty-two periods
is 8.4 weeks of five, and about nine or ten weeks of calendar once the
PA day and Thanksgiving are in. Pick the number your timetable gives you
and make the two pages agree.
