## What is assessed

Everything I mark has its own page in [[Tasks/index|Tasks]], and every one
of those pages publishes its criteria on the day the task launches, not on
the day it is collected. That includes the lab work: the five marked
write-ups live on [[Investigation Reports]], which names the six rows
every one of them is judged against. If a task page does not say how it
will be judged, that is a bug — tell me and I will fix the page.

## The seventy and the thirty

Every Grade 12 credit in Ontario is put together the same way. Seventy per
cent of your mark comes from work done across the semester, weighted
toward your **most recent and most consistent** biology rather than
averaged flat from September to January. The other thirty per cent comes
from a final evaluation at the end of the course.

**The seventy** is four unit tasks — [[Enzyme Investigation]],
[[Metabolism Case Study]], [[Biotechnology Brief]] and
[[Homeostasis Report]] — plus the five write-ups on
[[Investigation Reports]]. The tasks carry most of it, because each one
asks for several things at once across four or five periods. The write-ups
are what keeps evidence arriving in between, so that a judgement about
consistency has something to be made from. Ask me for the exact split
whenever you like; it is not a secret, it is just not the useful thing to
memorise.

**The thirty** is two pieces on two different days: the
[[Final Examination]], written on paper under the same conditions as
everyone else, and [[Population Study]], the culminating task, handed in
on Unit 5, Day 13. One of them asks whether you can reach across the whole
course alone and cold; the other asks whether you can do a real piece of
ecology properly with time and a data set. Neither one afternoon nor one
report decides this by itself.

## What is not assessed

Practice sets. Warm-ups. Rough work. Being wrong in a discussion. The
three lab write-ups named as rehearsals on [[Investigation Reports]]. The
end-of-unit checkpoints you mark against your own answers — those exist to
build you a revision list, and nothing you write on one becomes a number.
Your [[Biology Journal]] is in this group too: I read it, we talk about
it, and nothing in it moves your percentage.

If it is not on a page in [[Tasks/index|Tasks]], it is not being marked,
and you should take more risks with it than you probably will.

## The four categories

Ontario reports biology in four categories, and they measure genuinely
different things:

| Category | The question it asks |
| --- | --- |
| Knowledge and Understanding | Do you know the biology, and can you explain it? |
| Thinking and Investigation | Can you plan an investigation and reason from data? |
| Communication | Can another person follow your reasoning? |
| Application | Can you use the biology somewhere it has not been used for you? |

A perfect calculation with no diagram, no units, and no sentence is not a
perfect answer. It is a strong Knowledge answer and a weak Communication
one, and the report card will say so. Every task asks for more than one
category, and the mix is printed at the top of each task page.

## Three kinds of evidence, and two of them are not paper

**What you make** is the obvious kind: reports, briefs, pathway diagrams,
gels, graphs, a working model of a feedback loop. **What I watch you do**
is the second, and in a laboratory course it is the richest of the three,
because bench work leaves almost no trace on paper. A report shows the
numbers that survived, not the care that produced them, and two reports
carrying the same table can have come from two completely different
afternoons. I am in the room for all of them, and what I see there counts
as evidence in exactly the sense the table does. **What you tell me** is
the third — the conferences and approvals inside the working periods are
not progress checks, they are evidence in their own right, and some of
what you understand will never appear anywhere else.

If your draft is thin and you are quiet, the two minutes at your bench is
often the strongest evidence of the day. That is not a consolation prize.
It is how the mark is meant to work.

## Feedback, and where it lands

On each of the four unit tasks I read one named section of your draft
while you work, and leave a note on it — the conclusion, the "what
changes" section, the recommendation, the treatment paragraph. The note
names one change, not five, because of how much time comes after it. On
three of the four that is a named twenty minutes at the start of the next
class. On [[Homeostasis Report]] it is the working time at the start of
Unit 4, Day 13, which is also the day the report is handed in — that one
is tighter, and you should treat the note as one repair rather than a
second draft. On [[Population Study]] I read your group's residual plot
on Unit 5, Day 10 and Day 11 opens with it.

## What is not in your mark

How you work is reported separately, on the same report card, as **E, G,
S or N** — six habits: responsibility, organization, independent work,
collaboration, initiative, and self-regulation. They matter, we will talk
about them, and they do not move your percentage. The percentage is about
the work; that column is about the worker, and folding one into the other
tells you less about both.

The rule allows one exception: where a habit is written into the
curriculum itself, it is marked like any other expectation. **Grade 12
biology has no such expectation**, so the exception does not apply in this
course and I am not going to pretend it does. The closest the
expectations come is asking you to work safely and to document your
sources honestly, and those are properties of the work in front of you,
not descriptions of the kind of student you are.

Your own judgement of your work is not part of your mark, and neither is
a classmate's. You will judge your own work against the criteria often —
[[Judging Your Own Work]] is the routine — because it is the fastest way
to improve. Determining the mark from evidence is my job, not yours and
not theirs.

> [!note] Work done with other people
> Most bench work is done in pairs or threes, and so is the research on
> [[Biotechnology Brief]] and the modelling on [[Population Study]]. In
> every case the task page names what is yours alone: the report on
> [[Enzyme Investigation]], every page of the brief, your own write-up on
> [[Investigation Reports]], your named sections of
> [[Population Study]]. That, and what I see and hear while you work, is
> what your mark rests on. Nothing in this course is awarded to a group
> as a group, so nobody is carried and nobody is dragged down.

## Late work

Talk to me before the due date and it is a conversation. Talk to me after
and it is a mark. That is the whole policy.
