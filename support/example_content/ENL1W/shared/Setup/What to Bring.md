Short list.

- [ ] A notebook you do not mind writing badly in. Paper, not a device —
      the drafting in this course is by hand, for reasons under the list.
- [ ] Two pens. One always dies.
- [ ] A book of your own choosing, for the silent reading in every second
      class. Any book. Graphic novels count; so do books you have read
      before.
- [ ] Your school device, charged, on the days the class page says so.
- [ ] Whatever the current class text is, on paper or on screen.

Tick that list here as you pack (your browser keeps the ticks on this
computer), or copy it into your own notebook.

## Why the first drafting is by hand

Because you write slower by hand, and slower is the point. Handwriting
forces a sentence to be planned before it is finished, and it makes
crossing out visible, which is how a draft shows you what it is doing.
Everything gets typed eventually; nothing starts typed.

If handwriting is genuinely difficult for you — and for some people it
is — come and tell me and we will work out something better.

## The book of your own

If you do not have one, that is not a problem you need to solve alone.
The school library, the public library, this room's shelf, and a
five-minute conversation with me will all fix it in the first week. A
book you abandon after forty pages is fine. A book you re-read for the
fourth time is fine. The only wrong answer is nothing.
