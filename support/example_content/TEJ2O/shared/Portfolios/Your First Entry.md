The first entry in your [[Tech Journal]] is the hardest: the page
is blank and you do not yet trust what belongs on it. So for week
one only, the entry is guided — the three prompts below, a few
sentences each, written in class before tools away on the day of the
first teardown. Ten minutes, done.

## The three prompts

1. **What we did** — the facts, briefly. The warm-up, the machine
   your bench opened, where you got to before tools-away.
2. **What surprised me** — one specific moment you did not expect.
   In the hardware, in the tools, in yourself.
3. **Where I stand with technology right now** — honestly. Everyone
   arrives with a history — building gaming rigs, fixing the family
   printer, or never having held a screwdriver; naming yours is the
   first piece of data in the record.

## What honest looks like

These fragments from a first teardown are the register to aim for —
plain, specific, true:

> "We got the side panel off in two minutes, then spent ten finding
> the last screw on the drive cage — under a sticker. I felt dumb
> until Priya said techs get paid to find that screw."

> "The inside was mostly empty space and fans. I expected it to look
> like a movie. The CPU is smaller than a Timbit and apparently it
> does everything. I keep thinking about that."

> "I was scared to pull anything in case I broke it. Then I remembered
> this machine was donated to be taken apart. I pulled the RAM. It
> clicked. Nothing exploded. Okay."

None of these is polished, and none performs for a reader. Each
names a real moment and stays with it — which already beats a page
of "the teardown was fun, I learned a lot."

> [!important] Nobody reads this but me
> As [[Tech Journal]] promises: entries are private. I read them;
> other students never do, and nothing here is quoted without your
> explicit okay. The honesty you risk in week one becomes your best
> evidence of growth by the end — and only you decide who hears it.
