Every task page publishes a table of criteria on the day the task
launches. Most people read it once, at launch, and then hand in an essay
that misses two rows they could have repaired in half an hour. This page
is the half hour.

The first time, we do it together, on an essay belonging to nobody in the
room — so the move is not new to you on the day it is your own paragraph
being judged.

## The routine

1. Put the task page's criteria table beside your draft. Actually beside
   it: two windows, or the page printed.
2. Take the rows one at a time. Write **yes**, **not yet**, or **partly**
   against each, and one sentence of evidence — the paragraph, the
   sentence, the thing a reader would point at. "Yes" with nothing after
   it is a hope.
3. Name your weakest row. Not the quickest to fix. The weakest.
4. Write one sentence saying what you will change and when.
5. Change it — in what is left of this period, or in the next one. This
   is why the self-check is never scheduled on a hand-in day: a judgement
   you have no time to act on is only a worry with a date on it.

Steps 2 and 3 are the exercise. Naming your weakest row honestly is
harder than any of the repairing, because it means writing down the thing
you have been hoping would go unnoticed.

> [!example]- One row, judged honestly
> From [[The Lens Essay]]: *"Thinking — the second lens genuinely
> complicates the first; the cost paragraph is honest."*
>
> **Partly.** The second lens does find something the first missed, in
> my third paragraph, and I can point at the sentence. But my cost
> paragraph says both approaches "have limitations", which is not a
> cost — I never say what either one is unable to look at. Weakest row.
> **Change:** name the passage each lens has to walk past, before this
> period ends.
>
> That is worth more than "looks fine" written against all three rows, and
> it took less time.

## Judging an argument, not a feeling

Two failures are specific to essays about literature, and both look like
honesty from the inside.

**"It flows well."** Nobody can point at flow. Ask instead whether a
reader could say, at the end of each paragraph, what the paragraph
established and why it came where it did. If you cannot answer that for
your own essay, neither can they.

**"I ran out of room."** Length is a choice about what to cut, made
earlier. If a row fails because a section is thin, the honest note says
which other section was allowed to run long.

## Where this fits

What you decide about your own work is not part of your mark, and
neither is what a classmate decides — see [[How Marks Work]]. That cuts
both ways: nothing written here can cost you anything, which is exactly
what makes it worth writing honestly.

What it changes is the essay. A self-check that ends in a repair is the
cheapest improvement available in this course, and by the last unit it
is something you do unasked, which is the actual point. Keep the checks
in your [[Reading Journal]]; read them side by side when you build the
pair for [[Showing Growth]], because the row you keep marking *not yet*
is the story of your term.
