The final reflection is the last piece of writing in this course: one
sustained look **across** your whole [[Tech Journal]], written in the
final week. Every other entry looked at a single bench day. This one
reads the entire record and answers the only question a course can
really be measured by — *what do you leave with that you did not
arrive with?*

## Before you write

Reread your journal front to back in one sitting. Expect the earliest
entries to feel as though somebody else wrote them, because in a real
sense somebody else did. As you read, flag:

- one entry that embarrasses you slightly — growth is buried in there
- one prediction that missed badly, and what you found out because it
  missed
- one "what I would try next" that you actually tried, and what
  happened when you did
- one measurement you now know you took incorrectly

## The shape

Three sections, roughly a page, built the way [[Showing Growth]]
taught — date it, quote your own words, then show the now.

1. **How my hands changed.** The biggest difference between what
   first-week-you and current-you can *do at a bench*, proven with two
   cited entries. From the first time the meter's jacks confused you
   to whatever you stood behind at [[The Engineering Showcase]]. Be
   specific about instruments and technique: what can you now do
   without stopping to think, that used to take the whole period?
2. **How my judgement changed.** This is the section that separates
   Grade 11 from Grade 10. Not what you can build — what you now
   decide *before* you build. What margin you leave under a rating,
   what you test before calling something finished, when you stop and
   ask instead of pressing on. Quote your first entry's "where I
   stand" as the baseline, and say honestly what
   [[When Good Enough Is Not Safe]] changed about the way you look at
   your own work.
3. **Where this goes next.** Habits are only real once they have an
   address outside this lab: a course, a trade where these exact
   skills are the day job, a project you now mean to finish, a broken
   thing at home you now intend to open. Be concrete about which
   pathways in [[Careers and the Environment]] actually interest you,
   and name one first step you could take within the next few months.

> [!tip] Do not write it as a thank-you card
> Resist the urge to perform gratitude and round everything up. The
> strongest reflections include the concept that never quite clicked,
> the build that shipped with a known issue, the fault that won.
> Honesty and specificity are what made your journal worth keeping;
> trust them one last time. A reflection that admits what you still
> cannot do is more convincing about what you can.
