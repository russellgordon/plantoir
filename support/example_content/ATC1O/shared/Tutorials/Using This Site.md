Everything for this course lives here, and it is built to be used in a
particular way. Five minutes now saves you an hour of hunting later in the
course.

## Start where the site starts

Your section's landing page always shows the **most recent class** — the
agenda, what we did, what to do before next time. If you were away, that is
your first stop. Class pages appear the day we reach them, not in advance, so
a page you cannot find yet is a page whose class has not happened. Older
classes sit right behind, one per day, in order.

## Three habits that make it fast

1. **Search first.** Press <kbd>⌘</kbd> + <kbd>K</kbd>, or
   <kbd>Ctrl</kbd> + <kbd>K</kbd> on Windows, and type any word — a term, a
   task name, half a phrase you remember. Faster than browsing folders, every
   time.
2. **Hover before you click.** Rest the pointer on a link — try
   [[The Elements of Dance]] — and a preview opens without moving you off the
   page. You can check a word mid-sentence and carry on reading.
3. **Read the backlinks.** At the foot of every page is a list of every page
   that links *to* it. On [[The Standard Warm-Up]], the backlinks are every
   class and task that used it — a review list nobody had to build.

Some blocks are folded. A heading with a small arrow, or a coloured box whose
title ends in one, opens when you click it. If a page seems short, look for
the arrows; there is usually more underneath.

## Where things live

Concepts explain ideas. Technique and Warm-Ups are what we do on the floor.
Choreography covers making work. Tasks are the assignments, Portfolios is how
your year gets recorded, and Discussions are the seminars. The folder-by-folder
map is [[How This Site Is Organised]], and the tour of what the pages can
actually do — previews, diagrams, embedded curriculum — is
[[What This Site Can Do]].

> [!warning] The checkboxes here do not save
> Class pages list things to do with a box beside them. Those boxes are part
> of the printed page: nothing you do to one is stored, nothing is sent to me,
> and reloading returns them to how they started. Treat them as a list to copy
> into your own notes, not as a place to keep track.

Two directions get you unstuck. Lost in a class page? Follow it to the concept.
Lost in a concept? Use the backlinks to find the class where we did it. If
neither works, [[Getting Help]] is the page that says how to reach me.
