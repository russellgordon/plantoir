Thomas King was born in California and has lived and written in Canada for
decades — *The Inconvenient Indian* and this story are both among the
most widely taught pieces of Canadian writing about Indigenous experience.
In November 2025, King publicly stated that a genealogical review he had
commissioned found no Cherokee or other Indigenous ancestry in his family,
correcting what he had believed and said about himself for most of his
life. "Borders" is fiction about Blackfoot characters, written by a writer
who no longer identifies as Indigenous himself — worth naming plainly
before you read it, not glossing over. "Borders" first appeared in an
earlier form in *Saturday Night* magazine in 1991 and was collected, in
the version we read, in *One Good Story, That One* (1993). It has since
been adapted into a graphic novel illustrated by Métis artist Natasha
Donovan.

## Why we're reading it

A mother and her son try to cross from Alberta into the United States.
Asked for their citizenship at the border, she gives an answer the
crossing isn't built to process — and the story that follows is about what
happens to two people who exist in the gap a border refuses to
acknowledge. It's the shortest text in the unit and one of the most
quietly pointed: nothing about the plot is complicated, and everything
about what it's arguing takes patience to sit with.

## What to watch for on a first read

Watch the border guards' responses, on both sides, to the mother's answer
— what they offer her instead of what she's actually said. Watch, too, how
much the story trusts you to notice its own irony without underlining it;
King rarely tells you how to feel about a moment, he just shows it to you
and moves on.

> [!quote]
> "For the Blackfoot, who understand that the border is a figment of
> someone else's imagination."
>
> — the story's dedication

This concept — how the choices a writer makes in wording carry an argument
that its plot summary can't — is exactly what [[Diction, Tone, and Style]]
gives you language for, and it's the concept the class meets directly
after this text.
