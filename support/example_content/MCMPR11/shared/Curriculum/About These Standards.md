These learning standards are published by the **British Columbia Ministry of
Education and Child Care** for **Applied Design, Skills, and Technologies
(ADST): Computer Programming 11** (`MCMPR11`).

- **Official Curriculum Portal:** [curriculum.gov.bc.ca/curriculum/adst/11/computer-programming](https://curriculum.gov.bc.ca/curriculum/adst/11/computer-programming)
- **Ministry Course Code:** `MCMPR11` (Grade 11, 4 Credits)
- **Curriculum Model:** Big Ideas, Curricular Competencies, and Content —
  what BC's curriculum framework calls, respectively, Understand, Do, and
  Know.

> [!note] About the codes on these pages
> BC does not print codes on this document the way some other jurisdictions
> number their expectations. The codes you see here (`D1.1`, `S1.2`, `K1.9`,
> and so on) are **positional codes assigned for this site**, in the order
> the Ministry's own document lists them, so that a lesson or task can point
> at a specific line rather than a whole page. `D`, `S`, and `T` follow the
> Ministry's own competency groupings — Applied Design (with its seven
> named stages: Understanding context, Defining, Ideating, Prototyping,
> Testing, Making, Sharing), Applied Skills, and Applied Technologies. The
> Ministry's Content column is one flat, ungrouped list — it is not
> Ontario-style "strands" — so every Content item sits under a single `K1`
> heading here, numbered `K1.1` through `K1.17` in the Ministry's own order,
> rather than being sorted into invented categories.
>
> The wording under every code is the Ministry's own, copied without
> paraphrase. Where a competency group (for example "Defining") has no
> single descriptive sentence of its own in the source document — BC gives
> each group a short label and a bulleted list beneath it, not a topic
> sentence — the group's own page carries that label rather than an
> invented summary.

Every learning standard cited on this site links back to the page here that
carries its verbatim wording. If you ever want to check a page against the
source, follow that link.
