**Due at the end of Unit 2.** Pairs or threes. A findings report of about
900 words, plus a one-page recommendation addressed to the body you
studied.

You investigated a decision-making body in
[[Who Is Missing From the Table|Who Is Missing From the Table?]]. This task turns that into something a
real chair could read at a real meeting.

## What you produce

**A findings report** with four sections:

1. **The body** — what it decides, who sits on it, and how each got
   there.
2. **The gap** — who is affected and not represented, with figures and
   their sources. Describe structures and rules, never individuals'
   private characteristics.
3. **Why it matters, or does not** — the honest section. Point to
   decisions where the missing perspective would plausibly have changed
   the outcome. If you cannot show that, say so; a well-evidenced "we
   could not demonstrate an effect" beats an assertion.
4. **Contributions already being made** — who is doing the work of
   representing the missing group anyway, formally or informally, and
   what it has achieved. Name individuals and organisations, and assess
   which of the two has had the more lasting effect.

**A one-page recommendation** to the chair or clerk of that body, with two
specific changes, what each would cost, and who would have to agree.

**What is marked is yours alone.** The group divides the four sections
and each of you signs the ones you wrote; the recommendation says who
drafted it. On top of that, every one of you writes half a page of your
own answering section three in your own words — whether the gap you found
matters, and how you know. There is no group mark on this task, and the
half page is often the strongest evidence in it.

## How it is marked

| Criterion | What earns the marks |
| --- | --- |
| Evidence | Membership, eligibility rules, minutes and demographic figures, each sourced |
| Contributions | The significance of specific people and organisations is argued, not asserted |
| Common good | You explain how the actions you describe add to it, and at what scale |
| Judgement | The "does it matter" section is honest in both directions |
| Usability | The recommendation is short, costed, and addressed to someone real |

> [!important] This work involves real people who did not volunteer
> The people on the body you study are usually unpaid, often
> overcommitted, and did not agree to be a school assignment. Write as
> though the chair will read it — because you are going to send it. Judge
> structures, rules and outcomes. Do not speculate about anyone's
> background, and do not ask a classmate to speak for a community.

Sending the recommendation is optional here and required in
[[The Civic Action Project]]. Several groups every year find that this
task becomes their civic action, which is a good outcome and entirely
allowed.

[[Who Gets Included]] and [[Service and Contribution]] are the ground.
