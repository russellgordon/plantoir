## What belongs in Texts

A framing page for one text the class reads — never a reproduction of the
text itself (copyright, and also not the point). Cover who wrote it, in a
sentence or two you have actually verified; why the class is reading it
now; and what to watch for on a first pass. [[Boys and Girls]],
[[The Lottery]], and [[Indian Horse]] are three examples already here.

## Reminders

- **Verify before you publish, every time.** Author, dates, and any
  biographical claim — check them against a real source before this page
  repeats them as fact. A plausible-sounding detail is not a verified one.
  This course swapped an entire planned Unit 1 text after verification
  turned up content unsuitable for a Grade 11 classroom — and that check
  happened before any page for it existed, which is the point: verify
  first, write second, never the other way around.
- **For English specifically, preference Canadian writers, and include
  Indigenous writers and Indigenous ways of knowing as substantive
  presence** in the reading and discussion this course does — not a token
  text visited once and never returned to.
- **Link it from a class page**, directly or through one page a class page
  already links to.
- **If the text carries genuinely difficult content** — historical trauma,
  violence, material some students may need warning about — consider
  whether it needs its own content-note page first, the way
  [[Before We Begin - Residential Schools and Indian Horse|Before We Begin: Residential Schools and Indian Horse]]
  does for *Indian Horse*. Not every text needs one; a text that puts real
  historical harm on the page usually does.
- Curriculum-expectation transclusions are optional here; add the block
  only if this specific page genuinely teaches or evidences a specific
  expectation — most framing pages won't.
