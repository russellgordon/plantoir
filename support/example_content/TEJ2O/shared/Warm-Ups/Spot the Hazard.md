A photo of a bench goes up — staged the night before, never a real
student's workspace — and the question is simple: what here could
hurt someone or something? Drinks beside open cases, a powered
supply with its cover off, a cable run across a walkway.
[[Safety in the Lab]] gets read once at the start of the course; this routine is
how it stays read all semester.

## How to run it

1. Show the photo. One quiet minute; everyone counts hazards.
2. Poll the counts — the spread between them is the hook.
3. Name hazards one at a time. For each: what is the harm, who or
   what gets hurt, and what is the fix?
4. Close on the sneakiest one nobody caught.

> [!example]- A staged bench to steal (click to expand)
> An open case flat on the bench; a water bottle at its elbow; a
> screwdriver resting inside the case; the power bar switched on;
> an anti-static strap coiled, unworn, in the corner of frame.
> Five hazards — most classes find four and miss the strap, which
> is the point: absence is the hardest hazard to see.

## One variation

Students stage the photo. Building a convincingly hazardous bench
— without creating a real hazard while shooting it — requires
knowing every rule you are pretending to break.

> [!tip] Fix beats find
> Spotting the hazard is step one; the fix is the prize. End every
> round by describing the safe version of the same bench, and pull
> in [[Anti-Static Habits]] whenever the strap comes up.
