**1.** Where does each stage of aerobic respiration happen, and what is
the ATP yield of each?

> [!success]- Answer 1
> Glycolysis, cytoplasm, 2 ATP. Pyruvate oxidation and Krebs cycle,
> mitochondrial matrix, 2 ATP. Electron transport and chemiosmosis, inner
> membrane, about 26–28 ATP.

**2.** Cyanide blocks the final electron acceptor in the transport chain.
Explain why ATP production collapses rather than merely slowing.

> [!success]- Answer 2
> Electrons back up, the chain stops, no protons are pumped, so no
> gradient and no chemiosmosis. Only glycolysis remains — about 2 ATP per
> glucose instead of 30.

**3.** Why does a sprinter's muscle produce lactate?

> [!success]- Answer 3
> Oxygen delivery cannot keep up with ATP demand, so anaerobic fermentation
> regenerates NAD⁺ from NADH to keep glycolysis running. Lactate is the
> reduced by-product of pyruvate acting as the temporary electron acceptor.

**4.** In photosynthesis, where does the oxygen come from, and how was
that established?

> [!success]- Answer 4
> From splitting water at Photosystem II, not from carbon dioxide. Isotope
> labelling with $\ce{^{18}O}$ showed the label appearing in released
> oxygen only when $\ce{H2^{18}O}$ was supplied, confirming water photolysis.

**5.** Name the mechanism ATP synthase uses, and where else in the cell
you have met it.

> [!success]- Answer 5
> Chemiosmosis: a proton gradient driving a molecular rotor. It operates
> across the thylakoid membrane during photosynthesis and across the inner
> mitochondrial membrane during respiration — the same machinery, twice.

**6.** In bioremediation, *Pseudomonas* bacteria are introduced to degrade
crude oil spills. Which metabolic process allows these microbes to convert
hydrocarbons into non-toxic products, and what abiotic factor is essential?

> [!success]- Answer 6
> Aerobic cellular respiration: the bacteria oxidise hydrocarbon alkanes as
> carbon and energy sources, breaking them down into $\ce{CO2}$, water, and
> biomass. Dissolved oxygen is the essential abiotic electron acceptor.

**7.** Construct a comparative summary of matter and energy transformations
in chloroplasts and mitochondria:
a. Initial energy source.
b. Ultimate electron donor.
c. Terminal electron acceptor.
d. Primary high-energy product.

> [!success]- Answer 7
> a. Photons (solar energy) vs glucose/organic molecules (chemical energy).
> b. Water ($\ce{H2O}$) vs NADH / $\ce{FADH2}$.
> c. $\ce{NADP+}$ vs oxygen ($\ce{O2}$).
> d. Glucose / G3P (stored chemical energy) vs ATP (immediate cellular
> work).

**8.** Mitochondrial diseases, such as MELAS syndrome, arise from mutations
in mitochondrial genes encoding electron transport proteins. Explain why
symptoms predominantly affect skeletal muscle and the nervous system.

> [!success]- Answer 8
> Skeletal muscle and neurons have the highest baseline ATP demands of any
> tissues. Impaired oxidative phosphorylation reduces ATP output, causing
> exercise intolerance, muscle weakness, and neurological deficits.
