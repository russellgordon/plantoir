## The question

A school hears that something posted publicly is affecting students in
its building. Should it look? Should it act? Where does its authority
stop?

## The cases

Argue each one separately. They are ordered so that your position will be
tested by the next one.

1. A public post naming a student and mocking them, made at 9 p.m. from
   home.
2. A private group chat doing the same, screenshotted and handed to the
   school by another student.
3. An account posting about the school itself: criticism of a teacher,
   sharply worded, not naming anyone.
4. A post that suggests somebody is in danger.

## Positions to consider

| Position | The argument | The objection |
| --- | --- | --- |
| A school's authority ends at the door | Otherwise it never ends; students are entitled to a life | Harm does not respect the door, and the target has to come back in on Monday |
| Public means public | If anyone can read it, so can the school | "Anyone can" is not "anyone should"; a rule that treats teenagers' posts as evidence forever is a heavy rule |
| Harm is the test, not location | Act when there is harm to a student, wherever it happened | Who defines harm, and does criticism of the school count? |
| Safety overrides everything | Case 4 answers itself | If safety overrides everything, it will be used to justify everything |

## What the curriculum has to do with it

[[A2.1|Digital citizenship and online identity]] asks you to explain your
rights *and* responsibilities online, and to make decisions that build the
reputation you want. This discussion is that expectation with the comfortable
parts removed.

## Ground rules for today

- No real cases from this school. Hypotheticals only, and no names.
- Argue the principle. "It depends" must be finished: on *what*?

## Afterwards

Write the policy. Four sentences, no more: what the school may look at,
when it may act, what it owes the student it is investigating, and what
it may never do. Bring it to the next class; we will read three of them
and try to break each.
