A species, in the definition this course uses, is a group that can
interbreed and produce fertile offspring. Speciation is how one becomes
two.

## The usual route

1. A population is divided — a river shifts, a glacier advances, a few
   individuals reach an island.
2. The two groups experience different selection, and accumulate
   different mutations.
3. Eventually they cannot interbreed successfully even if reunited.

That is **allopatric** speciation, and geographic isolation is doing the
first step. **Sympatric** speciation happens without physical separation,
often through chromosome changes — common in plants, rare in animals.

## Isolating mechanisms

Before reproduction: different mating seasons, different courtship,
incompatible anatomy. After: hybrids that do not survive, or survive and
are sterile — a mule being the standard example.

## Where the definition strains

Bacteria do not interbreed at all. Fossils cannot be tested. Ring species
interbreed with their neighbours all the way around a barrier but not
across the ends. The definition is useful and leaky, and saying so is
better biology than pretending it is airtight.
