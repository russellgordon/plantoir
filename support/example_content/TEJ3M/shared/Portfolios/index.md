Almost everything that happens at a bench evaporates as it happens.
The circuit comes down, the breadboard gets stripped, the reading you
took goes back to being air, and the component that died in your hands
goes in the bin holding the best evidence of what you learned that
day. Your journal is the part that stays. In this course the journal
*is* the portfolio — the place where a burnt resistor and a
prediction that missed by a factor of ten turn into visible growth.

It keeps its Grade 10 name on purpose. Same routine, same ten minutes
at tools-away, same honesty. What is new is what an entry has to
carry: numbers with units, a sketch of what you actually wired, and
the prediction you made before the meter told you the truth.

The hub is [[Tech Journal]]. These pages support it, roughly in the
order you will need them:

Entries take ten minutes and feel trivial while you are writing them.
The value arrives months later, when [[Showing Growth]] calls
first-week-you as a witness — and that only works if first-week-you
wrote honestly, including the part where the circuit did not work and
you did not know why.
