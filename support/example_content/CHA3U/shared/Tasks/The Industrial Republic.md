**Due: the middle of Unit 3.** Pairs. A document set and a ten-minute
presentation.

Between 1877 and 1945, industry reorganised where Americans lived, what
they did all day, and who they lived beside. That sentence gets said
quickly. This task asks the three questions it skips over: who built it,
who took the profit, and who organised.

## What you choose

**One industry, in one city.** Steel, meatpacking, textiles, garments,
mining, rail, automobiles, canning, timber, shipbuilding. Narrow enough
that you can name streets and firms.

Then build the case.

1. **What was made, and how.** The process, the plant, the hours, the
   pace of the work, the injuries it produced.
2. **Who did the work.** Which people, arriving from where, and when. Be
   specific about who was hired, who was hired last, who was barred, and
   who was paid a different rate for the same work.
3. **Who owned it, and what they took.** Firm, ownership, and profit,
   with figures. Say where the money went afterwards.
4. **How the workers organised.** Which union or association, or none;
   what was demanded; what happened; who was arrested, sacked, deported,
   or shot; and what changed in the end, if anything.
5. **What the city looked like as a result.** Housing, neighbourhoods,
   sanitation, schooling, and how the boundaries between them were kept.

## What you hand in

A **document set** of five to eight sources you located yourselves, each
with a one-line note on what it is evidence of, and a **ten-minute
presentation** built on those documents rather than on a summary of them.

The presentations run in rounds on the hand-in day: two pairs at a time,
twice over, so that everybody presents inside one period and everybody is an
audience twice. Ten minutes to four people is still ten minutes.

## What is marked as yours

You work in pairs and you are marked individually — there is no shared
mark on this task. Split the five questions between you, in writing, at the
start, and put your initials against each source in the set: those sources
and those one-line notes are yours. In the presentation you take five of the
ten minutes, on the questions you researched. And the conference in the week
before it is with each of you separately, on your own half.

Divide the questions; do not divide the thinking. You are each expected to
be able to answer for the whole case.

## How it is marked

| Criterion | What earns the marks |
| --- | --- |
| Specificity | Detail that could only be about this industry in this city |
| Labour | The workforce named precisely, including who was excluded and how |
| Figures | Real numbers, sourced, with their units and their year |
| Organising | Workers as actors, with the response to them documented |
| Presentation | Ten minutes an audience learns something from |

> [!note] The numbers for this exist, and they are findable
> A federal census has been taken every ten years since 1790, and its
> published tables give you population, occupation, and place of birth
> down to small areas. State labour bureaus published wage and hours
> reports. Factory inspectors, coroners' inquests, and commissions of
> inquiry all published. If your presentation has no figures in it, the
> problem is the search, not the archive — start at
> [[Statistics and the Census]].

[[Industry, Labour, and the Cities]] is the survey this sits inside, and
[[Migration and a Changing Population]] is where your second question
leads. [[Photographs]] and [[Newspapers and Print Culture]] are the two
source types that will carry most of your document set — read the
warnings on both pages before you rely on them.

OBSERVE — Unit 3, Day 3, the research period after the photographs lesson
  Watch for: what gets given up when a figure will not come. There are two
  moves and they cost very different amounts. Loosen the CLAIM — "wages
  were low" instead of a wage — or loosen the SCOPE, quietly letting one
  city become "American cities". A document set of eight sources looks
  equally complete after either, and by the presentation nobody can tell
  which happened.
  Going well: the claim narrows to exactly what one published table
  supports — one mill, one year — and the city survives.
  Stuck: a round number with no publisher attached; or "we'll just do steel
  in general", said quietly, twenty minutes in.
  Record: initials and one word on the day plan, claim or scope. It takes
  the length of a lap of the room.

TALK — Unit 3, Day 7, the conferences already on that agenda
  Ask each partner separately: "Your wage figure is per what — per hour,
  per day, per piece? And what did that buy in that city, in that year?"
