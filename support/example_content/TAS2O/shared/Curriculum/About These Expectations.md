The course has two strands. Strand A is the design process — planning,
making, testing and refining, and doing all of it safely — and the
document opens it with a single sentence: "Students engage in an
engineering design process throughout this strand." Strand B is the world
the work lives in: who needs it, what it changes, and the careers and
pathways it can lead to.

## A course that reaches across technology areas

TAS2O is a broad-based course. Two of its expectations say so in as many
words: A1.1 asks students to work with fundamental technological concepts
"in a variety of broad-based technology areas", and B3.1 asks them to
explore careers "including a variety of broad-based technology areas, and
the skilled trades". A teacher can lean the examples towards an area they
know well, but the projects should still reach into more than one area,
and the careers the class looks at further still.

## The codes

Each page keeps the expectation's official **code** — `A1.3`, `B2.1`, and
so on, exactly as the Ministry prints them — and every lesson, project, and
task in this course links back to the codes it addresses. The codes are
also tags, so every page tagged `#A3` can be gathered in one place.

## Why the codes matter more than they look

A code is a shared address. When a student asks "why are we doing this?",
the answer is a link. When a department writes a course outline, or a
teacher takes over a class partway through, the codes are what make one
person's planning legible to another.
