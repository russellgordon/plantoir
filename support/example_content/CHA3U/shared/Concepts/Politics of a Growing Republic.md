The Constitution says almost nothing about political parties, and the men who
wrote it mostly disliked the idea. Parties appeared anyway, within a few
years, because a republic that governs by counting votes needs machinery for
assembling them. Watching that machinery form — and then break in 1860 — is
the best single way into the politics of this period.

## The formal changes, and what each one did

| Change | What it altered |
| --- | --- |
| Twelfth Amendment, 1804 | Separate electoral ballots for president and vice-president |
| Franchise reform, 1820s–30s | States dropped property tests for white men, and several states removed free Black men who had previously voted |
| Second party system | Democrats and Whigs competing nationally, with conventions and a partisan press |
| Thirteenth Amendment, 1865 | Ended slavery and involuntary servitude except as punishment for a crime |
| Fourteenth Amendment, 1868 | Citizenship by birth; due process and equal protection binding the states |
| Fifteenth Amendment, 1870 | The vote not to be denied on race, colour, or previous condition of servitude |

Note what the second row does. "The expansion of democracy" under Jackson is
a real phenomenon and a partial one: the same decades that removed property
qualifications for white men removed the vote from free Black men in states
where they had held it. An expansion and a contraction, at once, in the same
reform. Say both or you have not described it.

## Machines, patronage, and cities

Party organizations did work that no government agency did — finding
newcomers jobs, housing and legal help, and collecting their votes in return.
Tammany Hall in New York is the famous example, and the honest description
of it includes both halves: genuine services delivered to immigrants nobody
else would help, and systematic corruption on a large scale.

## Foreign relations, territory, and domestic power

Foreign policy in this period repeatedly redrew domestic politics. The War of
1812 and the Treaty of Ghent cemented American sovereignty and destroyed the
Federalist Party after its opposition at the Hartford Convention. The Monroe
Doctrine of 1823 asserted hemispheric authority against European powers, while
the Texas annexation, the Mexican-American War (1846–1848), and the Treaty of
Guadalupe Hidalgo forced the question of whether newly acquired territories
would permit enslavement — turning foreign expansion into the primary trigger
of domestic crisis. During the Civil War, Union diplomacy worked to block
British recognition of the Confederacy, while the Confederacy pursued "cotton
diplomacy" in an unsuccessful bid for European intervention.

## When the machinery failed

Parties had held the Union together by being national — each with northern
and southern wings, each with reasons to avoid the one question that split
along the same line as the map. When that stopped working, the political
system stopped absorbing the conflict, southern states seceded, and the war
came. Lincoln was assassinated in April 1865, days after the Confederate
surrender, and the Reconstruction that followed was fought out between a
president and a Congress with fundamentally different intentions.

Read the amendments in the table as promises, then ask when each was actually
enforced. The gap between the two is most of the century that follows. Trace
it in [[Jim Crow and Resistance]], and use [[Continuity and Change]] to fix
the two points you are comparing.
