These questions follow [[The Inverted Pyramid]] and the drafting you did
for [[Your First Byline]]. A lede has one job — earn the next sentence.

## Questions

1. Three facts, one story: the volleyball team won the regional final
   3–1 on Thursday; it is the school's first regional title in nine
   years; the gym roof leaked during play. Which fact leads, and why?
2. **Fix this buried lede.** "On Tuesday the student council held its
   regular monthly meeting in Room 114. Several items were discussed.
   Near the end, council voted to cancel the winter semi-formal."
3. Compress these facts into one lede of 25 words or fewer: the food
   drive ran two weeks; the goal was 800 items; students brought in
   1,214; everything goes to the food pantry on Bridge Street.
4. **Explain why** this lede needs surgery: "In an amazing display of
   school spirit that made everyone proud, students packed the gym."
   Which words belong to the reporter, and which to the reader?
5. Which lede would you run, and why? (a) "The cafeteria will stop
   selling bottled water at the end of the course, after a student petition gathered
   400 signatures." (b) "Change is coming to the cafeteria — and
   students are the reason."
6. **Challenge.** Write the lede from these notes: robotics club
   placed second at Saturday's qualifier; that earns a spot at the
   provincial championship a year from now; a first for the school; coach
   Ms. Arbour: "the rookies carried us."

## Answers

> [!success]- Answer 1
> The first title in nine years. Teams win matches every week — the
> nine-year wait ending is the rarity, so it leads, with the score in
> support. The leaking roof might even be its own story.

> [!success]- Answer 2
> The news hid in sentence three: "Student council voted Tuesday to
> cancel the winter semi-formal." Meetings are routine; decisions are news.

> [!success]- Answer 3
> "Students collected 1,214 items for the Bridge Street food pantry in
> two weeks — blowing past the goal of 800." The numbers do the work.

> [!success]- Answer 4
> "Amazing" and "made everyone proud" are the reporter's opinions —
> conclusions the reader should reach alone. "Students packed the gym"
> is the fact. Report the packed gym; let it do the amazing.

> [!success]- Answer 5
> A judgement call — but (a) has the stronger case: what, when, and why
> in one sentence. (b) withholds the news to sound dramatic — a tease,
> not a lede — and here the tease does not earn what it costs.

> [!success]- Answer 6
> One defensible version: "The robotics club will make its first-ever
> trip to the provincial championship after placing second at Saturday's
> qualifier." The quote goes in the body — a lede states the news.
