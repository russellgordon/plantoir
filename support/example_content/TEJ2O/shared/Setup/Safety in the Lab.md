This course puts real tools, real electricity, and real (sometimes
sharp-edged) hardware in your hands. That is the best thing about it —
and it only stays the best thing in a lab where safety is everyone's
habit, not a poster on the wall. This agreement binds everyone,
including your teacher.

> [!danger] The three absolutes
> **Power off and unplugged** before a case opens. **No energized
> circuit** touched without the go-ahead. **Anything smoking, sparking,
> or smelling hot** — hands off, power off at the bench master switch,
> teacher, in that order.

## What we agree to

- **We start with the walkthrough.** Every lab page opens with its
  safety notes; we read them together before tools move.
- **ESD is invisible and real.** Components die silently from static —
  [[Anti-Static Habits]] (strap on, bag until seated) are how we show
  the hardware respect.
- **Tools go back sharp-end away**, benches stay clear enough to see
  every cable, and food and drink live outside the lab entirely.
- **Report every incident** — cut, shock, tripped cord, near miss —
  immediately, no matter how minor it feels. Reporting is information,
  never trouble; see [[Getting Help]].
- **Nobody works alone** on powered projects. A partner within arm's
  reach is part of the equipment.
- **Soldering irons are $350^\circ\text{C}$ and silent.** The iron lives in
  its stand whenever it is not in your fingers — never set a hot iron down on
  the bench. Safety glasses on before the station turns on, fume ventilation
  running, and wash hands with soap after handling solder.

## Why this is not just rules

Every working technician follows exactly these habits, every day of
their career — lockout before service, ESD discipline, incident
reporting. Learning them now IS learning the trade; the
[[Careers in Computer Technology|professionals you may become]] are
just people who practised until the safe way became the only way that
feels normal.

- [ ] Read this page with a parent or guardian.
- [ ] Bring one question about anything above — we walk the lab and
