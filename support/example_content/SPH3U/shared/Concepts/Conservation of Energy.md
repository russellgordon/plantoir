Energy is never created or destroyed. It changes form, and it moves, and
in a closed system the total stays exactly the same.

$$E_{total, before} = E_{total, after}$$

## Using it

For anything moving under gravity with friction small enough to ignore:

$$mgh_1 + \tfrac{1}{2}mv_1^2 = mgh_2 + \tfrac{1}{2}mv_2^2$$

Notice what is absent: time, path, and forces. A ball rolling down a
smooth curved track arrives with the same speed as one dropped from the
same height, regardless of the shape of the curve. That is a strong claim,
and it is testable — [[Model Roller Coaster]] tests it.

## When energy seems to disappear

It went to heat and sound. A cart that arrives with less kinetic energy
than you predicted did not break conservation; it heated its axles and the
track by exactly the missing amount. Saying WHERE it went is the mark of a
good conclusion, and the reason [[Efficiency and Power]] exists.

> [!tip] The bookkeeping question
> Ask "what forms of energy are here at the start, and what forms at the
> end?" before writing anything. Most energy problems are solved by
> answering that in words first.
