> [!warning] Check which CHC2D a resource was written for
> This course was **revised in place**. The code did not change, so two
> versions of CHC2D exist: the one in *The Ontario Curriculum, Grades 9
> and 10: Canadian and World Studies, 2018*, which applied until the end
> of 2025–26, and this one, which applies from 2026–27.
>
> That makes older resources look correct when they are not. **The codes
> are not comparable between versions** — 2026 `E3.4` is not 2018
> `E3.4`. And five expectations here are new or reworded: `C1.6` on
> extreme ideologies, `C2.6` on Nazism and the Holocaust, `C3.4` on the
> Holodomor, and `D2.4` and `E3.3` on Black history in Canada. A course
> built from the 2018 document teaches four fewer topics than this one
> requires and mislabels the rest.

Each page here holds one expectation, word for word. The codes are the
Ministry's own, and they are also tags, so every page tagged `#C2` can be
gathered in one place.

## The shape is deliberate

Strands B, C, D, and E each carry the same three overall expectations —
context, communities and conflict, identity and heritage — applied to a
different period. You will get better at those three questions precisely
because the period keeps changing underneath them.

Strand A is different again: its stem reads **"Throughout this course,
students will:"** while the others say *by the end of this course*.
Historical inquiry, source criticism, and the concepts of historical
thinking are not a unit — they are what every unit is made of.

## The concepts of historical thinking

The 2026 course **reprints all four in full** — historical significance,
cause and consequence, continuity and change, and historical perspective
— along with the historical inquiry process. So when this site explains
them, it is explaining the Ministry's own current wording rather than
importing definitions from a superseded document.

The citizenship education framework is a different case: this course
names its four elements but does not reprint their definitions, and this
site does not import them either.

## Why the codes matter more than they look

A code is a shared address. When a student asks "why are we studying
this?", the answer is a link. When a department writes a course outline,
or a teacher takes over a class partway through, the codes are what make one
person's planning legible to another.
