The journal is the written half of [[The Dance Portfolio]]. It exists because
studio work vanishes: you leave the room knowing exactly what happened, and by
Thursday three sentences of it are left. Fifteen minutes a week keeps a year.

## When to write

Right after class if you can, on the bus if you cannot, and never more than a
day later. The cool-down is when to notice what to write about, which is why
[[Cool-Down and Recovery]] tells you to pay attention as your pulse
settles.[^format]

The entries that carry a mark are the ones written **in the studio, at a
milestone** — after a showing, after a seminar, after the day we watch the
recording — where the class page asks for one. Everything else in here is
practice: it is yours, it is where the marked entries come from, and it is not
something I mark. See [[How Marks Work]].

## What an entry contains

Four short things. Not an essay — a working record.

1. **What we did.** One line. The exercise, the phrase, the task.
2. **One thing your body did.** Specific and physical: where the weight went,
   which side was slower, what your shoulders did unwatched.
3. **One thing you noticed about the work.** Somebody else's phrase, a
   correction you were given, a moment in a showing that worked.
4. **What you will try next class.** One item, small enough to actually do.

Use the vocabulary. "It felt weird" cannot be worked on; "the transition into
the floor was sustained on the left and sharp on the right" can be, because it
names an element. [[The Elements of Dance]] and [[Dance Terminology]] supply
those words, and [[Writing About Dance]] is how to use them on the page.

## Writing about yourself without bragging or apologising

Both habits are ways of avoiding the description. "I nailed it" and "I was
terrible" carry the same amount of information, which is none. The fix is the
same in both directions: replace the verdict with the moment.

- Instead of *I was awful at the turns* — *I lost the spot on the second
  turn and put my heel down.*
- Instead of *my improvisation was really good* — *I stayed low for most of
  it and only got off the floor when the music changed.*
- Instead of *I don't have the flexibility for this* — *my hip stops before
  the extension does; I can work on where the weight sits.*

Nobody marks you for how you feel about your dancing. What the milestone
entries are read for is whether you can see it. The entries where you were
honest about something not working are the ones you will quote at the end of
the course, because they are the only ones that prove anything changed.

[^format]: Paper or digital, both are fine — but pick one and stay with it for
    the semester. Half a journal in a notebook and half in an app is a portfolio
    that cannot be handed in.
