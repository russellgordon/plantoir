**Due: mid-Unit 2.** Pairs. A short presentation with a document set.

One place in Canada during the Depression, and what happened to the
people in it.

## What you do

Choose a specific place — a prairie town, a mining community, a city
neighbourhood, a relief camp, a reserve — and build a case study.

Then answer:

1. **What the economy did there**, with figures where you can find them.
2. **What people actually experienced**: work, relief, migration,
   housing, food.
3. **What government did**, at which level, and whether it reached the
   people in your place.
4. **Who was left out** of whatever relief existed, and by what rule or
   practice.

## What you hand in

A **document set** of four to six sources you have found, each with a
one-line note on what it is evidence of, and a **ten-minute
presentation** built on them.

You build both together and you are marked separately. Sign the one-line
note under every source you found yourself, and take a named half of the
ten minutes. What I mark is the notes with your name on them and the half
you spoke. There is no shared mark for the pair.

## How it is marked

Every row below is applied to your own half — the notes you signed and the
half of the presentation you gave — and not to the pair's work as a whole.

| Criterion | What earns the marks |
| --- | --- |
| Specificity | A real place, with detail that could only be about that place |
| Evidence | The sources you found yourself, located and read, not summarised from a textbook |
| Causation | You connect national policy to local experience |
| Exclusion | You identify who did not receive help, and why |
| Your half of the presentation | Five minutes the class learns something from, built on the sources under your initials |

> [!warning] Relief was not distributed evenly, and that is the finding
> Eligibility rules varied by municipality, by marital status, by
> citizenship, and by race. A case study that reports averages will miss
> the most important thing about the decade — see
> [[Governments Respond]].

[[The Crash and the Thirties]] is the national picture your local one
sits inside.
