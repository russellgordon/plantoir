The last portfolio entry, written in the second-last week, and the one
that turns everything else into something usable. It is a plan for your
first year after secondary school and for the two years of school
between here and there — written knowing it will change.

## Sort what you know into three columns

Before writing anything, empty your research and your notes into this
shape. Most students discover the middle column is larger than they
expected, which is normal in Grade 10 and is itself worth writing down.

| Settled | Still open | Have to find out |
| --- | --- | --- |
| The destination type, or two of them | Which specific program, employer, or trade | The exact entry requirements, from the source |
| The Grade 11 courses you need either way | Whether to work first and study later | What the first year would actually cost |
| That you want to stay near home, or not | Whether a co-op or OYAP placement fits | Whether your school offers the program |

## Then write the plan

Six parts, and it can be two pages:

1. **The goal** for your first year after secondary school, in one
   sentence, plus a second goal if you are keeping two open.
2. **The steps**, in order, with the term each one belongs to. Course
   selection is a step. So is a visit, a fair, an application, an
   apprenticeship enquiry, a conversation with guidance.
3. **The skills** you will need and do not yet have, and where each one
   could be built — a course, a job, a club, a volunteer role.
4. **The people** you will need: who writes your reference, who knows
   about this pathway, who you will ask when something goes wrong.
5. **What could go wrong**, honestly. Marks, money, a program filling, a
   plan changing because you changed. One line each.
6. **What you would do instead** in each of those cases. Not a
   catastrophe plan — the point is to notice that most of them have a
   next move, and to find it now rather than in a panic later.

## Then say what changed

Close with a short paragraph comparing this plan to what you thought in the
start of the course. Name what changed and what caused it — a piece of
research, an interview, a budget that did not balance, a conversation. That
is the part you will be asked about in [[The Plan Defence]], and it is the
clearest evidence that the course did something.
