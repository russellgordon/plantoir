## The question

Do the witches cause anything in *Macbeth*, or do they only describe?

## The evidence to bring

- The first prophecy, and what it does and does not instruct.
- Banquo, who hears the same words and acts on none of them.
- Lady Macbeth's letter scene: what has already been decided, and by
  whom.
- The apparitions in Act 4, and their equivocation.
- Whether Macbeth ever names them as his reason.

## Positions to consider

| Position | The argument | The objection |
| --- | --- | --- |
| They cause it | The play opens with them; everything follows | Nothing they say is an instruction, and Banquo is the control |
| They predict it | Prophecy without compulsion; the choices are his | Then why does the play give them the first and last word on the future? |
| They are psychological | Externalised ambition, made visible for a stage | Banquo sees them too, and so does the audience |
| The question is the point | The play refuses to settle it in order to keep responsibility with Macbeth | A refusal to settle can also be a text that does not know |

## Where it goes

This is the discussion that produces the best essays in this unit,
because "what would count as evidence?" is a genuinely hard question
here. Take notes; the position you defend today is very often the thesis
of [[The Critical Essay]].
