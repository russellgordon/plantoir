[[Your Reading Life]] is where you record what you notice while reading —
this workshop is about sharpening *what* you notice. A journal entry that
only tracks your reaction to a novel is a start. An entry that catches how
the writer built that reaction is what actually feeds an essay later.

## The difference in practice

A reaction entry says a scene was sad, or tense, or surprising. A
craft-noticing entry says *how* the writer made it that way — and that's
the harder, more useful habit, because "the writing made me sad" gives you
nothing to quote in an essay, while "the writing switched from long
sentences to short, clipped ones right before the sad part" gives you
something you can point to.

## What to look for

Copy this into your journal, or keep it open beside you while you read —
it's a set of questions to ask, not a form to fill out line by line:

- [ ] What changed in the *sentences themselves* right before or after the
      moment that struck you — longer or shorter, more or fewer commas,
      a shift from one character's name to "he" or "she"?
- [ ] Is there a word or image that keeps returning? Where else have you
      seen it, and has what it means shifted since the last time?
- [ ] Whose thoughts are you inside of in this passage — and whose are you
      locked out of?
- [ ] What did the writer choose *not* to tell you here, and what does
      that choice do to you as a reader?

## A worked example

> [!fail] Reaction only
> This part of *Indian Horse* was really upsetting. I felt bad for Saul.

> [!success] Notices craft
> Wagamese doesn't describe what happens to Saul directly here — the
> chapter cuts away right before it, and we only learn about it later,
> secondhand, in a single flat sentence. The gap is the point: it forces
> the reader to feel the same silence Saul lived inside.

Neither entry is wrong to feel — the second one just gives you a sentence
you could build a whole paragraph around, and the first one doesn't.

An entry like the second is also exactly what
[[Tracking a Developing Theme]] asks you to gather up and organize once
you have several of them sitting in your journal.
