Gene therapies for single-gene disorders now exist and work. One approved
treatment for sickle-cell disease is priced in the millions of dollars per
patient.

## The biology you need first

[[Biotechnology]] — what the therapy actually does, why it is
patient-specific, and why manufacturing is the expensive part rather than
the science.

## The question

If a cure exists but almost nobody can afford it, has medicine succeeded?
And who should decide: the public system, the manufacturer, or the family?

## Positions worth taking seriously

- High prices fund the next therapy, and without them there is no next
  one.
- A public system negotiating as a monopsony can lower prices — Canada
  does this — but slower access is part of the trade.
- Sickle-cell disease is most common in populations historically
  underserved by research funding. That is a fact about the case, and it
  belongs in the argument.
