Six kingdoms is a useful simplification, and knowing where it simplifies
too far is part of learning it.

| Kingdom | Cells | Nutrition | Example |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Bacteria | Prokaryotic | Varies | *E. coli* |
| Archaea | Prokaryotic | Varies, often extreme habitats | Methanogens |
| Protista | Eukaryotic, mostly single-celled | Varies | *Amoeba*, algae |
| Fungi | Eukaryotic | Absorptive | Mushrooms, yeasts |
| Plantae | Eukaryotic | Photosynthetic | Mosses to maples |
| Animalia | Eukaryotic | Ingestive | Sponges to us |

## What separates them, beyond the cell

Nutrition and cell type are the two lines usually drawn first, and they
are not enough on their own — fungi and plants are both eukaryotic and
both mostly stay put, and telling a student that is all they have in
common is how a mushroom ends up filed as a vegetable. Three more
characteristics do most of the remaining work.

| Kingdom | Typical reproduction | Where they live | General physical structure |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Bacteria | Binary fission; variation by transformation, transduction, conjugation | Everywhere — soil, water, gut, rock | Single cell, wall of peptidoglycan, one circular chromosome |
| Archaea | Binary fission | Widespread, and the only life in some extremes — brine, hot springs, deep rock | Single cell, distinctive membrane lipids, genetic machinery closer to ours |
| Protista | Both — fission when conditions are good, sexual when stressed | Almost always wet: ponds, oceans, damp soil, other organisms | Single cell or simple colony; many are motile, with cilia or flagella |
| Fungi | Spores, sexual and asexual | On or in whatever they are digesting; mostly damp and dark | A network of thread-like hyphae (a mycelium); the visible mushroom is only the fruiting body |
| Plantae | Alternation of generations; spores or seeds | Terrestrial and aquatic, wherever there is light | Multicellular, cellulose walls, non-motile, organised into root, stem and leaf |
| Animalia | Almost all sexual | Every habitat on Earth | Multicellular, no cell walls, motile at some stage, tissues and organs |

Read the two tables together and the unifying characteristics show up as
clearly as the distinguishing ones. **Everything here is cellular, uses
DNA, and runs on the same genetic code** — which is the single strongest
piece of evidence that all six share an ancestor, and is why
[[Evidence for Evolution]] can compare a bacterium's genes with yours at
all. The differences are variations on one machine, not six separate
inventions.

One habit worth building now: when you meet an unfamiliar organism in
[[Using a Dichotomous Key]] or under the microscope, ask the table's
questions in order — one cell or many, nucleus or not, how does it feed,
how does it reproduce, does it move. Those five answers place almost
anything, and they are the questions the key is asking you in its own
words.

## The one that is not a real group

Protista. It is where everything eukaryotic that is not a plant, animal,
or fungus was put, which makes it a leftovers drawer rather than a branch
of the tree. Modern classification splits it into several unrelated
groups, and this course says so rather than pretending otherwise.

## The dividing line that matters most

Prokaryote versus eukaryote — no nucleus versus nucleus — is a deeper
split than any other on the table. It is about two billion years old.
