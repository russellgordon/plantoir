## The question

Why does Hamlet not act? And is that even the right question?

## The evidence to bring

- The vocabulary of proof across the play: *seems*, *prove*, *ground*,
  *catch the conscience*.
- Laertes, given the same situation and no hesitation.
- The prayer scene: Hamlet's stated reason, and whether the play endorses
  it.
- The Mousetrap, and what it establishes.
- The sea voyage, after which he acts decisively about Rosencrantz and
  Guildenstern.

## Positions to consider

| Position | The argument | The objection |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Temperament | A mind that thinks instead of acting; the soliloquies are the evidence | He acts fast when he stabs the arras, and faster at sea |
| Epistemology | He wants proof, and the play gives him no reliable way to get it | The Mousetrap arguably gives it, and he still delays |
| Structure | The play needs three acts; delay is a formal requirement dressed as psychology | True of any plot; explains nothing specific to this one |
| The question is wrong | The play is about grief and a court under surveillance; "delay" is a critical tradition, not the play's own frame | Then why does Hamlet himself keep raising it? |

## Where it goes

Whatever position you take today usually becomes a thesis for
[[The Critical Essay]]. Take notes on the objection that landed hardest —
that is your counter-reading section.
