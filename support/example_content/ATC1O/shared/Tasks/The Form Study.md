**Due: mid-Unit 3.** Pairs. A performance and a short spoken
introduction.

One dance form, practised properly and presented honestly.

## What you do

**Learn and perform a sequence** in a form we have worked in class —
about 45 seconds, arranged by you from the vocabulary of that form,
using its own technique rather than your own movement dressed up.

**Introduce it** in two minutes: where the form comes from, how it
developed, what the body does differently in it, and one thing it shares
with another form you have practised. **You take a minute each**, and you
say at the start which half is yours.

**Use the form's own terminology** correctly, in the introduction and
when you talk about your rehearsal.

## What is marked

You arrange the sequence together and you both dance it, but the mark is
yours. What is judged as yours: **your own dancing** of the sequence, the
**minute of the introduction you write and speak**, and the terminology
you use at the check-in on Unit 3, Day 6 and in the working period on
Day 7.

| Criterion | What it looks like |
| --- | --- |
| Technique | The movement belongs to the form; the details are attended to |
| Arrangement | A sequence with a shape, not a list of steps — and you can say which decisions in it were yours |
| Terminology | Correct, and used because it is precise |
| Origins | Where the form came from and how it developed, accurate and respectfully told, in your minute |
| Comparison | The shared characteristic is real and demonstrated |

> [!warning] A form is a practice, not a costume
> Every form in this course belongs to people who dance it now. Learn
> where it comes from, name that, and never present it as exotic. If you
> cannot say where a movement came from, that is the first thing to find
> out — see [[Where a Form Comes From]].

[[Forms From Around the World]] and [[Three Forms We Practise]] are the
studio work behind this, and [[Dance Terminology]] is the vocabulary.

Triangulation — the evidence you will not have unless you go and get it.

OBSERVE — Unit 3, Day 7, the working period, while the sequence is being
arranged
  Watch for: whether the pair arranges from the form's vocabulary, or
  strings the three steps they were taught in the order they were taught
  them. A3.3 asks them to ARRANGE a sequence, and the 45 seconds you watch
  on Day 8 is a sequence either way — only the making shows you that an
  order was chosen.
  Going well: steps get reordered, something is repeated on purpose, a
  transition gets invented that the form would recognise.
  Stuck: step one, step two, step three, and the whole discussion is about
  who stands where.
  Record: a tick beside the pair on your day plan the first time you see
  an order changed on purpose.

TALK — Unit 3, Day 6, in the working period after the forms are chosen,
each partner on their own
  Ask: "Show me the step you find hardest in this form, and tell me what
  it is called and what the name is actually describing."
  Then: "Which word from this form has no equivalent in the other two —
  what does it name that the others never need to name?"
  A strong answer does the step and the naming in the same breath, and the
  second lands on something the form cares about that the others do not: a
  relationship to the floor, a way of carrying weight, a way of counting.
  That is C1.3, which asks for demonstration AND description together. The
  introduction on Day 8 is prepared, rehearsed, and can be read off a
  card; this is where you find out whether the terminology is theirs.
  Record: initials, and the word they gave you.

The product evidence is the performance and the introduction on Day 8.
