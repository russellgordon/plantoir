This page lists what a Visual Art website made with Plantoir can show. Each item names a page of the ready-made course where you can see it, and the Help page that says how it is done.

## The ready-made Visual Arts course

One code has ready-made pages: **AVI1O**, Visual Arts, Grade 9.

To get it, choose {{menu:newCourse}}, type AVI1O and leave {{control:newCourseSheetPrePopulateWithExampleContent}} turned on. [Make a new course](page:new-course) has the steps. It comes with its folders, its shared pages, and 86 class pages in four units for every section. The class pages are published and dated across the semester, so the first deploy may ask about classes dated later. See [Classes dated after the next class, at Deploy](page:later-classes).

To read it without teaching from it, put it on the Reference Courses shelf. See [Bring in last year's courses](page:import-for-reference).

Any other Visual Arts code offers a starting point for the subject instead. Its folders are Concepts, Techniques, Studio Time, Critiques, Portfolios, Tasks, Setup, Style, Tutorials and Curriculum.

## What the pages show

- **Pictures, each with a description.** The page Looking Twice, in Critiques, has a part named Three works to look at twice. It holds pictures of three works in the Art Institute of Chicago. They are Hokusai's *Under the Wave off Kanagawa*, Caillebotte's *Paris Street; Rainy Day* and Seurat's *A Sunday on La Grande Jatte*. Each picture is written with a description of what it shows, for students who cannot see it. A credit line and one question follow each. The museum marks all three as public domain.

{{picture:subject-visual-art-1}}
alt: The page Looking Twice on the class website, showing one of its three pictures with its credit line and its question.

In this ready-made course, the three picture files are in the Critiques folder, beside the pages that show them. Put pictures of your own in the course's Media folder. See [Pictures, PDFs and the Media folder](page:media).

- **A sheet that prints.** The page A Looking Sheet, in Critiques, is the same routine on paper, for one work. It holds the Caillebotte picture and five questions. Questions 3 and 4 have answers folded under them, and a student clicks to open one. The other questions ask for the student's own reaction. The page has a {{control:printButton}} button on its date line. See [A handout that prints](page:printing), which also says what goes on the paper.

{{picture:subject-visual-art-2}}
alt: The page A Looking Sheet on the class website, with its picture, its questions and the Print button on its date line.

- **Both pages, linked from a class.** The class page for Unit 3, Day 1 links to Looking Twice and to A Looking Sheet in its agenda.
- **Pages written once and linked from class pages.** The pages in Techniques, Studio Time and Critiques are each written once. Class pages link to them. See [One lesson, written once](page:one-lesson).
- **A checklist students tick.** Every class page has one, under the heading Things to do before our next class. See [Checklists students tick](page:task-boxes).
- **Diagrams drawn from text.** The page The Creative Process, in Concepts, has a flowchart. Working in a Series, in Studio Time, has a diagram too. See [Diagrams drawn from text](page:diagrams-from-text).
- **Tables, boxes and footnotes.** The page What This Site Can Do, in Style, shows each with examples from the course. See [Tables, boxes and footnotes](page:tables-boxes-footnotes).

## Folders made for the subject

- **Techniques** has seven pages: Drawing, Painting, Printmaking, Sculpture, Surface and Texture, Digital and Emerging Media, and Materials and Safety.
- **Studio Time** has four: How a Studio Period Works, Working From Reference, Working in a Series and Clearing Up.
- **Critiques** has six: A Looking Sheet, Assessing Your Own Work, Giving Feedback, Judging Your Own Work, Looking Twice and The Class Critique.
- **Style** holds How This Site Is Organised, What This Site Can Do and Writing About Art.
- **Tasks** is the folder whose work counts for marks.

[The folders Plantoir treats specially](page:special-folders) says what the folder for marks does.

## What AVI1O does not use

AVI1O has no question that students check themselves, no video, and no success criteria for students to tick. A page can hold a question or success criteria all the same. See [Questions students check themselves](page:quizzes) and [Let students track their progress](page:my-progress).
