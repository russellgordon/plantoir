Two roles, private briefs, twenty minutes to reach an agreement — or to
walk away, which is sometimes the right outcome.

## The situation

A community centre and a youth sports league share one gymnasium. The
league wants four evenings a week for the winter season; the centre has
committed two of those evenings to a seniors' program that has run for
eleven years.

You will be given one of the two briefs. Each contains information the
other side does not have — including, in both cases, one flexibility your
opening position hides.

## The rules

- Twenty minutes. No agreement is a legitimate result; a bad agreement is
  worse than none.
- You may share anything in your brief, or none of it. What you say must
  be true.
- Write your agreement in one sentence. If you cannot, you do not have
  one.

## The debrief

1. Which style from [[Conflict Styles]] did you *plan* to use? Which one
   did you actually use when the other side pushed?
2. Find the moment where positions turned into interests, if it happened.
   What was said just before it?
3. Which pairs found the solution that needed information from both
   briefs? What did those pairs do differently in the first five minutes?
4. Whose agreement will still be working later in the course? Read the sentences out
   and decide as a class.

> [!tip] The two questions again
> "What would you need for this to work?" and "What am I missing?" —
> watch who used them, and what happened next.
