The first entry in your [[Tech Journal]] is the hardest, and it is
hardest for a reason that has nothing to do with writing: the page is
blank and you do not yet trust what belongs on it. So for week one
only, the entry is guided. Five prompts, a few sentences each, written
the same day as class. Ten minutes, done.

## The five prompts

1. **What we did** — the facts, briefly. The warm-up, what your bench
   worked on, where you got to before tools-away.
2. **A number I wrote down** — one measurement from the day, with its
   unit and its condition. If no instrument came out, name the
   quantity you would have wanted to know and say why it would have
   settled something.
3. **What surprised me** — one specific moment you did not expect. In
   the circuit, in an instrument, or in yourself.
4. **Something I would now design differently** — think back to
   anything you built in an earlier year, or last week, or at home.
   One choice you made then that you would not make now, and one
   sentence on what changed your mind. If nothing comes, say that
   honestly; it is a perfectly good baseline.
5. **Where I stand as a designer right now** — not as a builder. Most
   of you can follow a procedure and get a working circuit. This year
   asks you to choose the procedure. Say honestly how that feels, and
   what you think you are missing.

Prompts four and five are the new ones, and they are new because this
is the year the course stops handing you the decisions. Naming your
starting point is the first piece of data in the record, and it is the
one you will want most a year from now.

## What honest looks like

These fragments are the register to aim for — plain, specific, true,
and unembarrassed about not knowing things:

> "We got 4.63 V at the board with the fan running and 5.01 V with it
> off. I would have called that 'about 5 V' last year and moved on.
> Today somebody asked which one I would design against and I did not
> have an answer."

> "I put the scope probe's ground clip on what I thought was ground
> and it was the low side of a divider. Nothing bad happened because
> the supply was current-limited. I did not know that was why nothing
> bad happened, which bothers me more than the mistake did."

> "Last year I built a thing with a 220 Ω resistor because the lab
> sheet said 220 Ω. I could not tell you today why it was 220 and not
> 180, and I am realising that is the whole difference between the
> two courses."

> "Where I stand: I am fast at building and slow at deciding. Give me
> a schematic and I will have it working. Give me a requirement and I
> freeze."

None of these is polished and none performs for a reader. Each names a
real moment and stays with it, which already beats a page of "the lab
was interesting and I learned a lot". Notice that three of them record
a limit rather than an achievement. Those are the parts that will still
be useful to you in four months, because they are the ones
[[Showing Growth]] can measure against.
