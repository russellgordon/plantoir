Take one finished piece through the complete [[Filing a Story]]
workflow to actual publication on the class site — headline, image
with caption and credit, category, final read-through, publish. When
your story loads on a screen that is not yours, the newsroom stops
being a metaphor; every session before this one was for today.

> [!warning] Care and consent notes
> **Published means public** — confirm every person named or pictured
> has [[Releases and Consent|consent on file]] before anything goes
> live; "I'll check after" is how a takedown request starts. **Every
> asset carries its credit**, and [[Copyright and Fair Dealing]] is
> the test borrowed material must pass before it ships. **Corrections
> travel slower than mistakes** — screenshots outlive fixes, so the
> final read-through is the last cheap moment to catch an error.
> **Final files back up before you celebrate** — the published page
> is a copy, not an archive.

## What you need

- [ ] One piece your editor has signed off on — words final, facts checked
- [ ] Its image, with caption drafted and credit confirmed
- [ ] The [[Filing a Story]] checklist, open beside you
- [ ] Fifteen unhurried minutes — publishing rushed is how errors ship

## The work

1. **Write the headline last, and honestly.** The story is finished,
   so the headline can promise exactly what it delivers — every
   [[Headline Practice]] rep was for this one.
2. **Attach the image with its caption and credit.** The caption
   answers who, doing what, when; the credit names who made the
   frame. A published image missing either is a defect.
3. **Choose the category deliberately.** Categories are how readers
   find stories next month — a piece filed under the wrong desk is
   published and lost at the same time.
4. **Preview the page as a stranger.** Read every word aloud under
   your breath — the eye skims what the ear catches — click every link.
5. **Publish, then verify.** Load the live page on a different device
   than the one you published from. What you see now is what every
   reader sees — headline, image, caption, credit, all present.
6. **File the record.** Note the date in your [[Newsroom Journal]]
   and add the live piece to your portfolio folder —
   [[Your First Byline]] explains why this moment is worth keeping.

## What can go wrong

- **The preview looked perfect; the live page does not.** An image
  too large, a page still held back — publishing ends at verification.
- **A working headline shipped.** "Sports story v2 FINAL" is live
  with your name near it — slow down at step one.
- **The credit conversation happens after publication.** Someone sees
  their photo uncredited, or their face unexpected — the callout's
  checks come first because order is the protection.

## Level up

Trade final read-throughs with another desk before either of you
publishes — a stranger's eyes catch what yours have gone blind to.
Then watch for both pieces on [[Publication Day]].
