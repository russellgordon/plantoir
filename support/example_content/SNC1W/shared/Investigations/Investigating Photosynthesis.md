## Purpose

Determine how light intensity affects the rate of photosynthesis, using floating
leaf discs.

## The trick that makes this work

Leaf discs sink when the air is drawn out of them with a syringe. As
photosynthesis produces oxygen, the discs become buoyant and rise. **Time to
float** is a measure of photosynthetic rate.

## Materials

- Spinach leaves, hole punch, 10 mL syringe (no needle)
- Sodium bicarbonate solution (the $\ce{CO2}$ source), drop of dish soap
- Clear cups, lamp, ruler, timer

## Procedure

1. Punch 10 discs per trial. Avoid large veins.
2. Draw bicarbonate solution and discs into the syringe, seal the tip, pull the
   plunger to remove gas from the leaves, and repeat until all discs sink.
3. Place discs in a cup of bicarbonate solution at a measured distance from the
   lamp.
4. Record the time for **each** disc to reach the surface.
5. Repeat at three distances: 10 cm, 20 cm, 40 cm.

> [!warning] Control your variables
> Only distance from the lamp changes. Same solution, same leaf, same disc
> count, same water temperature — and watch the last one, because lamps heat
> water.

## Analysis

1. Graph median float time against distance. Describe the shape.
2. Light intensity falls off with the **square** of distance:
   $I \propto \frac{1}{d^2}$. Does your data support that? Explain what would
   convince you either way.
3. What would happen with no bicarbonate in the water? Predict, then explain
   using the equation on [[Photosynthesis]].
4. Leave a floating disc in the dark and it sinks again. Using
   [[Cellular Respiration]], say what happened to the oxygen — and then
   compare the two processes: what does each one take in, what does each give
   out, and why does a pond need both of them running at once?
