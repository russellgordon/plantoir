## What is assessed

Everything I mark has a page in [[Tasks/index|Tasks]], and every one of
those pages publishes its success criteria on the day the task launches.
That includes the lab work: the five marked write-ups live on
[[Investigation Reports]], which says what each one has to contain and
links to the investigation behind it. If a task page does not say how it
will be judged, that is a bug — tell me and I will fix the page.

## The seventy and the thirty

Every Grade 11 credit in Ontario is built the same way. Seventy per cent
of your mark comes from work spread across the whole semester, and it
leans towards your **most recent and most consistent** work rather than
averaging September against January. The remaining thirty per cent comes
from a final evaluation at the end of the course.

**The seventy** is one task per unit — [[Biodiversity Field Study]],
[[An Evolution Case Study]], [[Genetic Counselling Brief]] and
[[Body Systems Report]] — together with the five write-ups on
[[Investigation Reports]], each written at the bench in a period set
aside for it. They are not equally weighted; the four unit tasks carry
most of it, because each asks for more at once, and the write-ups are
what keeps evidence arriving steadily in between. Ask me for the exact
split whenever you want it — it is not a secret, it is just not the
useful thing to memorise.

**The thirty** is two things, on two different days: the
[[Final Examination]], written under the same conditions as everybody
else, and the [[Plant Growth Investigation]], the culminating task, whose
three weeks of growing end in the last week of classes. Two very
different rooms, deliberately, so that neither one afternoon nor one
experiment decides this alone.

Practice sets, rough work, warm-ups, diagnostics, drafts, and being wrong
in a discussion are not marked at all. If it is not on a page in
[[Tasks/index|Tasks]], it is not being marked, and you should take more
risks with it than you probably will. Your [[Biology Journal]] is in that
group: it is yours, I read it, and nothing in it moves your percentage.

## The four categories

Ontario reports biology in four categories, and they measure genuinely
different things:

| Category | The question it asks |
| --- | --- |
| Knowledge and Understanding | Do you know the biology, and can you explain it? |
| Thinking and Investigation | Can you plan an investigation and reason from data? |
| Communication | Can another person follow your reasoning? |
| Application | Can you use the biology somewhere it has not been used for you? |

A perfect calculation with no diagram, no units, and no sentence is not a
perfect answer. It is a strong Knowledge answer and a weak Communication
one, and the report card will say so. Every task asks for more than one
of these, and the balance shifts with the task — the mix is printed at
the top of each task page.

## Three kinds of evidence, and two of them are not paper

**What you make** is the obvious one: reports, briefs, drawings, data
tables, write-ups. **What I watch you do** is the second, and in a
science course it is the richest of the three: how you place a quadrat
when nobody is looking, whether you change one variable at a time,
whether you ask for consent before the cuff goes on, whether you retake a
measurement that looked wrong or write down the tidier number. **What you
tell me** is the third — the conferences at the working periods are not a
progress check, they are evidence in their own right, and some things you
understand will only ever show up there.

If your write-up is unfinished and you are quiet, the two minutes at your
bench is often where the strongest evidence of the day comes from. That
is not a consolation. It is how the mark is meant to work.

## What is not in your mark

How you work is reported separately, on the same report card, as **E, G,
S or N** — six habits: responsibility, organization, independent work,
collaboration, initiative, and self-regulation. They matter, we will talk
about them, and they do not move your percentage. Your mark is about the
work; that column is about the worker, and mixing the two tells you less
about both.

There is one exception in principle: where a habit is itself named in the
curriculum, it gets marked like anything else in the curriculum. It does
not arise here. Nothing in the Grade 11 biology expectations is a
learning skill wearing a curriculum number — the closest they come is
asking you to document your sources and to judge them for bias, and those
are things you do to the work, not ways of being a student. So in this
course the line is clean: the percentage is the biology, and the letters
beside it are the habits.

Your own judgement of your work, and your classmates', are not part of
your mark either. You will assess your own work against the criteria
often — see [[Judging Your Own Work]] — because it is the fastest way to
get better. But the mark is mine to determine, from evidence.

> [!note] Work done in a group
> Most of the bench work and three of the tasks are done with other
> people, and in every case the task page names the part that is yours
> alone. That part, and what I see and hear at your bench, is what your
> mark rests on. Nobody in this course is carried, and nobody is dragged
> down.

## Late work

Talk to me before the due date and it is a conversation. Talk to me after
and it is a mark. That is the whole policy.
