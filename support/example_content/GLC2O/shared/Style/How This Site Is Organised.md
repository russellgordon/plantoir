This site is the course. Ten weeks of classes, the ideas behind them,
everything you are marked on, and the Ministry expectations all of it
comes from.

## The folders, and what each is for

| Folder | Holds | You will use it |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **All Classes** | One page per period — what we did and what to do next | Every day |
| **Concepts** | The ideas, stated plainly | After the class that met the idea |
| **Activities** | The things we do in class, with the method written down | On the day, and again when you repeat one alone |
| **Portfolios** | The record you keep, the entries that build it, and how to judge your own work against the criteria | Several times a unit, and every time something is close to being handed in |
| **Tasks** | Assessed work, with what earns the marks | Whenever something is due |
| **Tutorials** | How to do the things this course asks for | The first time you have to do one |
| **Setup** | How the class runs | Week one, and when you forget |
| **Style** | How the site works | Now |

## How the pieces connect

Class pages are the spine. Each one links to the concept, activity, or
task that period consists of, so the quickest way to find where
something was used is the backlinks list at the foot of its page.

The three units run in order — you, then work, then the plan and what it
costs — and the tasks accumulate. [[The Pathway Plan]] is assembled
almost entirely from work handed in earlier, which is why nothing in
this course is safe to skip and catch up on later.

## The curriculum connection at the foot of pages

Most pages end with a **Curriculum connection** block: the Ministry
expectations that page addresses, in the Ministry's own words. Those are
also gathered in the curriculum folder, and the built site draws a
coverage map from them — which is how a student, a parent, or a teacher
taking this course over can see what has actually been taught against
what the policy requires.

Setup and Style pages have no curriculum block, because they are about
running the course rather than teaching it.

## Two conventions

- **Concept pages are not textbook chapters.** They assume you have
  already met the idea in class, and they are short for that reason.
- **The newest class is always on the section landing page.** If you
  were away, start there.

[[What This Site Can Do]] is the tour of the site's features, and is
worth five minutes if you are the one writing pages rather than reading
them.
