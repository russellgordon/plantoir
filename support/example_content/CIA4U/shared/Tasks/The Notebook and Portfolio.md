**Runs from the first week of the course to the last.** Individual.

The notebook you keep all term, one portfolio piece built across Units 1
and 2, and one written at the end of the course. This page exists so
that you know from the first week which parts of that work carry a mark
and which are practice, and what each of the marked parts is judged on.

## What is practice, and what is marked

You add to [[Your Economics Notebook]] every week: a definition in your
own words, one dated figure with its table, and the citation that lets
somebody else find it again. **That weekly work is practice, and I do
not mark it.** What you do between classes is yours. It is also the
thing that makes the marked parts quick, which is the only argument for
it that matters.

Three things carry a mark, and all three are written in class time.

**Three milestone entries**, one at the end of each of the first three
units. I put a table in front of you that day — one you have not worked
with — and you write a complete entry from it in the twenty-five minutes
set aside for it, in class, never at home: the
value, the reference period, the table number, the release date, the
date you retrieved it, one sentence on what the figure means in the
vocabulary of this course, and one sentence on what it does not tell
you. Your own notebook is open beside you. That is the point of having
kept one.

**[[A Model of Your Own]]**, launched in the fourth week of Unit 1,
drafted in the last twenty minutes of Unit 2 Day 15, conferenced on Unit
2 Day 21 and revised in the period after it, and handed in on the first
day of Unit 3. One decision you actually face, modelled, with every
assumption written down and the first one to fail named.

**[[Where Economics Leads]]**, written in class in the period after the
culminating report is delivered. Where the reasoning in this course
lands outside it — argued from the term you have actually had, not
copied off a list of job titles.

> [!note] Why the marked parts are written in the room
> Work that carries a mark is done where I can watch it happen. That is
> partly fairness: the room is the one place where everybody has the
> same hour, the same desk and the same access to me. It is also the
> only way the second and third kinds of evidence in [[How Marks Work]]
> exist at all — I cannot see you decide which of two tables to trust
> if the deciding happened on a bus.

## What I am looking for

| Criterion | What it looks like on the page |
| --- | --- |
| The entry is complete | Value, reference period, table number, release date and retrieval date — all five, every time |
| The citation could be followed | Somebody else opens the same table and finds the same number without having to ask you |
| The vocabulary is this course's | Real or nominal, level or rate, seasonally adjusted or not — used precisely, not sprinkled |
| The limit is real | The sentence on what the figure does not tell you names something the figure genuinely cannot show |
| The assumptions are all there | Every one the model needs, including the ones that felt too obvious to write down |
| The break is named | The first assumption to fail, and what happens to the conclusion when it does |
| Both sides are in the same units | The financial choice is costed on both sides, so that the two can be compared at all |
| The transfer is argued | A skill named against the piece of your own term's work it came out of |
| The careers are defended | Roles chosen against what you can now actually do, with the reason given |

Rows one to four are judged on each milestone entry, rows five to seven
on [[A Model of Your Own]], and the last two on
[[Where Economics Leads]].

## How to make this easy

Keep the notebook honestly and every marked piece here is a transcript
of work you have already done twice. Keep it badly and the milestone
period is the first time this term you have written a citation, which
is a poor afternoon to find that out.

[[Where Canadian Economic Data Lives]] is where the tables are;
[[Judging an Economic Claim]] is the test that turns a figure into
evidence; [[Judging Your Own Work]] is how to read the table above
against your own draft before I do.
