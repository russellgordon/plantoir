Everything this newsroom publishes — stories, captions, posts, scripts
— reads like it came from one team. That is what a house style is for,
and this page is ours. It has one rule that does most of the work:

> [!important] Write for the reader who was not there
> Every story is addressed to someone who missed the game, the meeting,
> the moment. If a sentence only makes sense to people who were in the
> room, it is not finished.

## The house rules

| Rule | Because |
| --- | --- |
| Names: full name first use, checked twice | A misspelled name breaks trust instantly |
| Quotes: exact words, verified on the recording | Quotation marks are a promise |
| Numbers: scores and dates checked at the source | "About 30" and "31" are different claims |
| Captions: who, what, when — every image | A photo without a caption is a rumour |
| Bylines and credits: on everything | Work is owned, help is named |

## Voice

Short sentences carry news best. Active voice — "the Wolverines won",
not "a victory was achieved". Say what happened, then why it matters,
in that order: that is the
[[The Inverted Pyramid|inverted pyramid]] working at the sentence
level. Opinion lives in clearly labelled opinion pieces and nowhere
else.

## Sentence stems that unlock a stuck draft

- What would I tell a friend happened, in one breath?
- The single most surprising thing here is…
- The person this affects most is… because…
- A reader's first question after my lede would be… — answer it next.

The same standards run through your [[Newsroom Journal]], with one
addition: name what *you* did and decided, not just what the team did.
"We covered the game" hides the learning; "I reshot the celebration
from the baseline because the first angle buried it" shows it — and it
is the second version you will be able to point at in
[[Showing Growth]] in January.
