Every task in this course publishes what it is judged on before you start
it. Almost nobody looks at those rows a second time, which is how work
gets handed in missing two things that twenty minutes would have fixed.
This page is the twenty minutes, and it is a physics skill rather than a
study skill: deciding whether your own result is good enough is most of
what an experimentalist does.

The first time, we do it out loud, on somebody else's write-up, so that
the move is familiar before it is uncomfortable.

## The routine

1. Put the rows beside the work. The task page's table, or the ones on
   [[Investigation Write-Ups]] if it is a lab write-up.
2. Take them one at a time and decide: **yes**, **partly**, or **not
   yet**. Then give the reason in one line, and point at something: this
   paragraph, that axis, the number in the third column. A "yes" standing
   on its own is a hope, not a judgement.
3. Pick the row you are worst on. Not the quickest to repair. The worst.
4. Say in one sentence what you are going to do about it, and when.
5. Do it, in what is left of this period or in the next one. Nothing
   here ever falls on a day something is due, and that is on purpose —
   a verdict you cannot act on is just something to think about on the
   bus home.

Steps 2 and 3 are the exercise. The fixing is the easy part; writing down
the row you had been quietly hoping nobody would read is not.

> [!example]- One row, judged honestly
> From [[Investigation Write-Ups]], the uncertainty row: *"The dominant
> source named, with a number, what that number does to the conclusion,
> and one change to the method that would actually shrink it."*
>
> **Partly.** I said the timing was the biggest uncertainty, which is
> true, and then I stopped. Ten revolutions by hand is about ±0.2 s in
> 8.4 s, call it 2%. My two speeds differ by 9%. So timing does not
> explain the gap and I have been implying it might. Worst row.
> **What I will do:** put the 2% in, say plainly that the other 7% is
> unaccounted for, and add the one change that would help — a photogate
> on the string instead of a thumb on a stopwatch. Next period.
>
> That paragraph took four minutes and is worth more than seven ticks.

## After a checkpoint

The end-of-unit checkpoints run the same way, except the physics stands
in for the criteria table. You mark your own, none of it touches your
mark (see [[How Marks Work]]), and what comes out of it is a **revision
list**. A list you can act on names questions, not topics:

- No use: "go over circular motion".
- Use: "I keep drawing a force pointing outwards. Redo questions 3 and 7
  of [[Circular Motion Practice]] starting from the free-body diagram,
  and say out loud what is supplying the centripetal force each time."

Bring the list to the next class. There is time set aside for working
through it, and it is the least expensive way to move a mark in this
course.

## Why none of it counts

Because a judgement that could cost you something is a judgement you will
shade. Nothing written here reaches the percentage, which is the only
reason it is worth writing truthfully. What it changes is the work.

> [!tip] Say it to the person beside you
> Read your verdict out loud. The rows where you were being kind to
> yourself tend to announce themselves on the way past your own ears.
