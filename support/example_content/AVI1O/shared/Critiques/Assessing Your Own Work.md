Judging your own work is a different job from judging anybody else's, because
you can see what you meant. The picture in your head sits on top of the one
on the table, and you have to move it aside before you can assess anything.
The method is to compare the work against your own stated intention rather
than against a vague sense of whether it is good.

## Start from what you set out to do

Which means you have to have said it. One sentence in your sketchbook before
you start — *this should feel cramped and airless* — turns a useless question
into an answerable one. Not "is this any good?", which nobody can answer,
but "does this feel cramped to someone who did not read my sentence?"

Three outcomes are all normal. It does what you intended. It does something
else that is better, so you keep it and change your intention. Or it does
not work, and you can now say which part failed — a diagnosis, not a defeat.

## Tired is not the same as unsuccessful

By the third period on a piece you will hate it. That feeling is fatigue,
not judgement, and it arrives on almost every work of any length. Test it:
leave it overnight and look cold, look from across the room, or photograph
it and look at the picture. Distance and a change of format are how you get
a stranger's eye on your own work. If it still bothers you afterwards, the
problem is real — and now you can usually name it.

## Knowing when it is finished

A work is finished when further changes stop improving it. That is not the
same as being bored of it and not the same as the bell going.

The two failure modes look opposite and cost the same. Stopping too early
leaves a work that reads as an unfinished thought. Overworking destroys
the thing that was good in it — colour mixed to mud, paper worn through,
a block cut past the point of recovery. Overworking is the more common of
the two in Grade 9, and it happens quietly, one small improvement at a
time.

> [!success]- Four questions before you call it done (click to expand)
> Does it do what I said it should do? If I stopped right now, what would
> a viewer notice that I have not dealt with? Is the next change I am
> planning fixing something, or just something to do? And what would I do
> differently if I made this again — because that answer belongs in
> [[The Portfolio]] whether or not you make it again.
