ROUTING ON METAL, AFTER THE 2026-08-24 SYSTEM-PROMPT CHANGE
===========================================================

THE FIRST THING TO KNOW: THIS IS A RE-MEASUREMENT, AND IT CANNOT SHOW A WIN
--------------------------------------------------------------------------
The two sentences added to the system prompt on 2026-08-24 (commit b77b91bd,
"undo_last_change reverses only the assistant's own most recent action…" and
"There is no tool to delete, remove or rename a page or a folder…") were
written to fix a cluster measured on WINDOWS, in a container, against
Qwen2.5-1.5B under Vulkan: promise-card-results.txt, 3 trials, 15 tools, 72%
overall, with "hide, phrased as a mistake" answered with undo_last_change in
all three trials.

**That failure has never been observed on Metal.** The same suite had already
run on this Mac, twice, before the tweak existed:

  - macos-native-10-trial-comparison.txt:193-250 — 2026-08-15, Qwen2.5-1.5B,
    15 tools, ctx 16384, temp 0.1, 10 trials: "hide, phrased as a mistake"
    10/10 unpublish_pages.
  - thirteen-tool-surface-results.txt — 2026-08-15, 13 tools, ctx 16384: the
    same probe, again 10/10 unpublish_pages, on BOTH tiers.

So on this hardware the tweak was aimed at a fault that was not there. This
run can therefore show one of two things and no third: that the change is
NEUTRAL on Metal, or that it is HARMFUL on Metal. It cannot show a gain,
because there was no loss to recover. That is the honest frame for everything
below, and it is written at the top so that a number from this file cannot be
quoted as evidence the tweak worked.

WHAT THIS NUMBER IS NOT
-----------------------
  - **Not a pass rate, and not a probability.** The suite runs at temperature
    0.1 (0 in the headline arms), which is near-greedy: ten trials of one
    probe are usually ten copies of one answer. Ten trials distinguishes "this
    model systematically does the wrong thing with this sentence" from "it
    wobbled once". It does not estimate how often a teacher would be
    misrouted.
  - **Not what a teacher experiences.** Five of the 29 probes never reach the
    model in the app — AssistCardCommand answers them in code — and the app
    also rewrites the course and section (`boundToThisSection`) and settles
    relative dates (`withTheDaySettled`) before any call runs. The suite does
    neither, so its "wrong course" and "fabricated date" counters are
    STRICTER than the shipped product.
  - **Not a comparison with the 72%.** That figure is Windows, in a container,
    3 trials, 15 tools, a different engine build and a different tool-surface
    text. Nothing here is like-for-like with it. It is quoted only to say what
    it is.
  - **Not a gate.** Nothing in either test suite checks routing, deliberately
    and for reasons recorded in CLAUDE.md. This is a measurement, taken by
    hand, on one machine, on one day.

**Follow-up work is demanded if any of these trips.** Nothing else is.

  1. ANY polarity inversion — a hide request answered with a publish tool —
     in any trial of any arm. This is the veto, not a tiebreaker: it is the
     one failure that reports success.
  2. Any probe that was 10/10 in EITHER 2026-08-15 anchor scoring ≤5/10 in
     the comparable arm.
  3. "hide, phrased as a mistake" below 9/10 unpublish, or 2/10 or more
     undo_last_change. This is a REGRESSION tripwire, not a test that the
     tweak fixed something: the probe was already 10/10 here before the
     tweak existed.
  4. The pre-tweak arm beating the shipped arm by ≥5/10 on a probe that is
     stable (0/10 or 10/10) in both. That would mean the prompt was changed
     on another platform's numbers at this platform's expense, and it earns
     a `decision` issue rather than a quiet fix.
  5. The deletion probe ("Delete the Unit 1 folder in VVH2O") routing to a
     real WRITE. rebuild_preview and cancel_scheduled_deploy are the known,
     logged answers and are not fixed here; the wording fix that was tried
     and REJECTED is in conversational-residue-results.txt ~:59-71 and is not
     to be retried.
  6. Write-misses above the 13-tool anchor's rate on the same probes.
  7. A fabricated date on any probe other than `typos`, or any malformed tool
     call, or any argument needing type coercion.
  8. The em-dash arm differing from the hyphen arm by ≥5/10 on any stable
     probe. ONLY if this trips does Windows owe anything, and then a
     `windows` issue is drafted — otherwise the divergence is something the
     other side must merely KNOW, and it goes in documentation/.

  **Not grounds, decided now so it cannot be argued later:** a single control
  moving; an unstable probe (1-9/10 in an arm) moving; the five intercepted
  probes scoring badly, since the app never sends them.

THE ANSWER, IN ONE PARAGRAPH
----------------------------
**On the tier this Mac actually ships, the 2026-08-24 change is neutral: it
bought the 4B nothing and cost it nothing.** Qwen3-4B answered **28** of the
29 probes identically with the shipped prompt and with the pre-tweak prompt —
271/290 against 270/290 — and 27 of those 28 identical answers are also
CORRECT; the two exceptions are `read`, the one probe that differs at all
(1/10 against 0/10, noise), and `typos`, which is wrong the same way in both
(0/10, 10x publish_pages). **On the small tier the change bought something
real**: it is the pre-tweak prompt that makes the 1.5B DECLINE a hide request
(the "HIDE - the inversion case" probe: 4/10 pre-tweak against 9/10 shipped at
ctx 8192, 6/10 against 10/10 at ctx 16384), which is the second half of what
#117 asked about — see "Which cluster the framing meant", below. Overall the
small tier reads 208/290 shipped against 199/290 pre-tweak.

**But the 4B has regressed against its own best-measured August self, and the
regression is not the prompt's doing.** The most comparable prior run —
`macos-native-10-trial-comparison.txt` → `qwen3-4b-nothink`, the same 29
probes, 10 trials, this Mac — scored 280/290 (97%) with the Windows-comparable
18 at 180/180. Today it is 271/290 with W-18 at 161/180, and **two probes are
the whole difference**: `read` (10/10 → 0-1/10) and `typos` (10/10 → 0/10),
both identical in the shipped and the pre-tweak wording. On the 290-probe
scale that is a 9-response drop; the 19 is on the Windows-comparable-18 scale,
where both probes sit. That is on the
tier every Mac with 16 GB or more runs, and it is the finding in this file
most worth acting on.

The run also found three things nobody was looking for: tool-call arguments
arrive TRUNCATED often enough to count, and every suite in this folder that
prints a `malformed tool calls: N` line has been reporting that as zero; one
probe makes the small model generate for 41 seconds under the app's own
request body; and the 2026-08-15 anchors cannot be reproduced probe-for-probe
even at their own context size and thread count, because the harness that
produced the newest of them was never committed.

WHAT THE RUN FOUND THAT NOBODY WAS LOOKING FOR
-----------------------------------------------
**1. Tool-call arguments arrive truncated, and every file in this folder that
prints a `malformed tool calls: N` line has been reporting that as zero.** The
suite did `except: arguments = {}`, so a call whose argument JSON was cut off
counted as a call with no arguments, and that line was only ever counting HTTP
failures. With it fixed (commit in this branch), the small tier produces 12-19
truncated calls per 290 responses — `publish tomorrow, with links` every
trial, plus `typos`, `no course named: publish` and `deploy now` — and the 4B
produces none. **Read every "malformed tool calls: 0" in the older files as
"no HTTP errors".** (One suite is different and is worth naming so the claim
is exact: `routing-suite.py` keeps the raw string as
`{"__unparseable__": …}` (search it for `__unparseable__`) and never counts it, and its report line says
"malformed tool calls (runtime rejected)", which is honest about what it
means. It simply has no count of the other kind either.)

**How much of the truncation is the SUITE, and how much is the app.** Most of
it is the suite. The historic body caps generation at `max_tokens: 256`, and
every truncated call in the 10-trial arms stopped at exactly 256 with
`finish_reason: length` — the cap cut the JSON. Under the app's real body,
which sends no cap, the count is **3 of 87** (warm) and **1 of 29** (cold),
because the model runs on until the CONTEXT ends instead: fewer truncations,
and a 41-second wait in place of each one. Both numbers are real and they
answer different questions — 12-19/290 is "how often does this model go off
the rails on this surface", 3/87 is "how often does a teacher meet it".

**A scoring limit, stated because it flatters the totals.** PASS/MISS is
decided by the tool NAME alone; the arguments are checked by separate counters
that do not feed the score. So a call that chose the right tool and arrived
with unusable arguments is scored correct. It happened twice in the 2,494 responses of the 29-probe arms, and never in
the mac-shelf arm's 247 (both `publish by name, informal` → `publish_pages`, truncated), so
it moves nothing here — but a future run where truncation is common would be
over-scored, and the fix is to fail a probe whose arguments did not parse.

**2. Under the app's own request body, one sentence makes the small model
generate for 41 seconds.** The suite's historic body caps generation at 256
tokens; AssistModelClient sends no cap at all. With no cap, "Publish
tomorrow's class for VVH2O section 1, and make sure every page it links to is
published rather than left as a draft." makes Qwen2.5-1.5B emit a
`publish_pages` call enumerating "Unit 2, Day 3; Unit 2, Day 4; … Unit 2, Day
58; …" until the context runs out — 5,435 completion tokens, finish_reason
`length`, **40.9 seconds**, 3/3 trials, reproduced on a cold server. The
teacher waits three quarters of a minute and gets an unusable call. The 4B
never does this (max 73 tokens). The app's 180-second timeout means it
completes rather than failing, which is arguably worse.

**The 40.9 s survives this file's own latency disclaimer.** Another agent was
building on the machine throughout, so every clock reading here is marked
indicative — but this one cross-checks: 5,435 completion tokens in 40.893 s is
**132.9 tok/s**, against the **130.6 tok/s** median measured for this exact
model on an idle machine in August (macos-native-10-trial-comparison.txt:220).
Today's rate is a shade FASTER, so the wait is the token count and not the
contention. (A review put August's figure at 129.8; the file says 130.6. The
conclusion is the same either way.)

**3. The small tier's real shape.** 19 of 29 probes are rock solid, 7 are
solidly wrong, and the rest wobble. Three of the seven are scheduling or
deploying — "Deploy tomorrow's class at 6:30 AM" and its conversational twin
both deploy NOW — and one is a sentence any teacher would type ("Publish
tomorrow's class, and everything it links to", no course named →
publish_pages). Those are WINDOWS' promise-card wordings; the mac's own shelf
was measured separately, above, and fails on one string, "Deploy at 6:30 AM",
in the same way. The 4B gets every one of them right, every trial, both
prompts. The gap between the tiers on this surface is 93% against 72%, and it
is not evenly spread — it is concentrated in exactly the sentences a teacher
is most likely to type.

CONCLUSION, IN PLAIN WORDS
--------------------------
**On the 4B — the tier this Mac ships — the 2026-08-24 change bought nothing
and cost nothing**: 28 of 29 probes identical between the two wordings, the
deletion probe declined either way. **On the 1.5B it bought one real thing**,
a hide request that the older wording let the model decline (4/10 → 9/10), and
cost nothing measurable. So there is no case for reverting it and no
`decision` to put to anybody. #117's first question — is undo over-salience
fixed on Metal? — has the answer that the fault was never present here: the
probe was 10/10 correct before the tweak and is 10/10 correct after it. Its
second — is the lost decline fixed? — has the answer yes, on the small tier,
by 5 trials in 10.

**The prompt is not what needs attention now.** Three things do, and none of
them is the wording:

  - **`read` on the 4B.** 10/10 `read_page` in August on both the 15-tool and
    the 13-tool surfaces; 0-1/10 today, choosing `check_section`, identically
    in both prompt forms. With `typos` it accounts for the whole drop from
    280/290 to 271/290 — 9 responses there, 19 on the Windows-comparable-18
    scale — on the tier most teachers run. Nothing here
    explains it, and the tool-surface change that would explain `typos` does
    not cover it.
  - **"Deploy at 6:30 AM", the mac's own shelf card, on the small tier.**
    0/10 and 0/3: it deploys NOW. Three of the shelf's four model-routed
    promises are kept; this one is not, and it is the one that reaches
    students.
  - **A missing token cap.** `AssistModelClient` sends no `max_tokens` where
    Windows sends 512, so a degenerate generation runs until the context is
    full: 41 seconds on the small tier, and on the large tier a bound of
    roughly 16k tokens at ~63 tok/s, which is past the client's own
    180-second timeout.

And one lesson about this folder rather than about the assistant: two of the
three mac anchors taken on 2026-08-15 disagree with each other about `read`,
and the newest of them cannot be re-run because its harness was never
committed. Commit the harness beside the results.

No row was added to `GUI-IMPROVEMENTS.md`, and that is a decision rather than
an omission: nothing a teacher can see changed. The three things a teacher
WOULD notice are named above and are tracked as their own issues, which is
where a row will belong when one of them is fixed.
