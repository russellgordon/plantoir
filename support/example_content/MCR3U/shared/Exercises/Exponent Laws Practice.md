These questions follow [[Exponent Laws]] and [[Rational Exponents]].
Everything here yields to the laws plus the two meanings you built —
negative means reciprocal, fractional means root. Exact values only;
the calculator sits this one out until the final question.

## Numbers first

1. Evaluate $2^{-3}$ and $5^0 \cdot 3^{-2}$.
2. Evaluate $4^{3/2}$ and $27^{-1/3}$.
3. Evaluate $\left(\dfrac{16}{25}\right)^{1/2}$ and $8^{2/3}$.

> [!success]- Answer 1
> $2^{-3} = \frac{1}{2^3} = \frac{1}{8}$ — reciprocal, not negative.
> $5^0 \cdot 3^{-2} = 1 \cdot \frac{1}{9} = \frac{1}{9}$. Anything
> nonzero to the power 0 is 1; the descending pattern demands it.

> [!success]- Answer 2
> Root first, power second: $4^{3/2} = \left(\sqrt{4}\right)^3 =
> 2^3 = 8$. And $27^{-1/3} = \frac{1}{27^{1/3}} = \frac{1}{3}$ —
> unpack in order: reciprocal, then root.

> [!success]- Answer 3
> $\left(\frac{16}{25}\right)^{1/2} = \frac{\sqrt{16}}{\sqrt{25}} =
> \frac{4}{5}$. $8^{2/3} = \left(\sqrt[3]{8}\right)^2 = 2^2 = 4$.
> Taking the root first kept every number single-digit.

## Now with variables

4. Simplify $x^5 \cdot x^{-2}$ and $\dfrac{\left(y^3\right)^4}{y^7}$.
5. Simplify $\left(x^6 y^3\right)^{1/3}$.
6. Simplify $x^3 \div x^{1/2}$.

> [!success]- Answer 4
> $x^5 \cdot x^{-2} = x^{5 + (-2)} = x^3$.
> $\frac{(y^3)^4}{y^7} = \frac{y^{12}}{y^7} = y^5$. Add across
> products, multiply across powers of powers, subtract across
> quotients — each rebuildable by counting factors.

> [!success]- Answer 5
> The exponent $\frac{1}{3}$ distributes across the product:
> $x^{6/3} y^{3/3} = x^2 y$. A quick check with $x = y = 1$ won't
> catch much — try $x = 2, y = 1$: $\left(64\right)^{1/3} = 4 = 2^2$. ✓

> [!success]- Answer 6
> Subtract exponents: $x^{3 - 1/2} = x^{5/2}$. Fractional exponents
> obey the same quotient law as integers — that was the whole point
> of defining them the way we did.

## Rewriting and reality

7. Rewrite $9^x$ as a power of 3, and $8^x$ as a power of 2.
8. Now the calculator: evaluate $1.01^{120}$, and write one sentence
   about why a number so close to 1 produced a result so far from 1.

> [!success]- Answer 7
> $9^x = \left(3^2\right)^x = 3^{2x}$ and
> $8^x = \left(2^3\right)^x = 2^{3x}$ — power of a power. Same
> functions, new bases: the costume change that lets exponential
> models be compared on equal footing.

> [!success]- Answer 8
> $1.01^{120} \approx 3.30$. Multiplying by 1.01 gains only 1% — but
> doing it 120 times compounds each gain on top of the last, and the
> total more than triples. Hold that thought: in
> [[Money Over Time]], "1% per period for 120 periods" is called a
> loan.
