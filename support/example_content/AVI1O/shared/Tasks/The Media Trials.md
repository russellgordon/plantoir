**Due: the end of Unit 2.** Individual. A set of trials, annotated.

Before you can choose a medium, you have to know how it behaves. This
task is a deliberate tour of the four the course is built on, plus the
digital one, with the evidence kept.

## What you produce

For **each** of drawing, painting, printmaking, and sculpture: a short
trial that tests what the medium does — not a finished picture, a test.
Your drawing trial is the mark-making and texture work from Unit 1,
annotated to the same standard as the rest — there is a period for that
on Unit 2, Day 10. A fifth trial in digital or photographic media comes
out of the same day.

Each trial is annotated with:

- The technique you were testing, named correctly.
- The tools and materials, named correctly.
- What the medium did that you did not expect.
- One effect you would use again, and where.
- The safety and clean-up practice that applied.

And every one of the four shows a **second go**, kept beside the first,
because knowing how a medium behaves means having been wrong about it
once:

- **Drawing** — the texture study as it was, and as it was after the
  revision period in Unit 1.
- **Painting** — the subject painted once, then painted again
  differently on Unit 2, Day 3.
- **Printmaking** — the block as first cut on Unit 2, Day 5 and printed
  on Day 6, and the second state cut on Day 7.
- **Sculpture** — the maquette, and what changed when you built it.

Beside each pair, one sentence saying what the first one told you.

## What is marked

| Criterion | What it looks like |
| --- | --- |
| Exploration | You genuinely tested the medium rather than making something safe |
| Vocabulary | Techniques, materials, and tools named accurately |
| Effects | You can connect a technique to the effect it produces |
| Second attempt | In each of the four, a first go and a second kept together, with the change between them named |
| Safe practice | Hazards named, precautions taken, and tools, waste and the sink dealt with as [[Materials and Safety]] requires |
| Reflection | You can say which medium suits which of your ideas, and why |

> [!warning] Safety is assessed here, not assumed
> Blades, solvents, spray fixative, dust, and the press all have rules,
> and following them is part of the mark — see [[Materials and Safety]].
> So is what goes down the sink and what does not.

[[Drawing]], [[Painting]], [[Printmaking]], and [[Sculpture]] are the
four; [[Digital and Emerging Media]] is the fifth, and
[[Surface and Texture]] is where most of the effects come from.

Triangulation — the evidence you will not have unless you go and get it.

OBSERVE — Unit 2, Day 5, the block-cutting period
  Watch for two things on the same circuit. First, how the blade is
  used: the free hand behind the cut, the block turned rather than the
  tool, a bench hook in place. A pulled print carries no trace of how
  the block was cut, and the annotation asks the student to write down
  the safe practice that applied — which is a self-report. This is the
  period that turns it into evidence, and C3.2 is one of only three
  expectations here that are genuinely about conduct, so it is worth the
  walk.
  Second, what happens when a cut goes wrong: whether the student cuts
  the mistake into something or abandons the block and starts again.
  Going well: the block moves and the gouge stays still; a bad cut gets
  incorporated and the student can point at it later.
  Stuck: the block held in the palm with the blade travelling towards
  the thumb. Stop the room rather than the student.
  Record: two lines in your day plan — who you had to stop, and who you
  did not. The second list is the evidence; the first is a lesson.

TALK — Unit 2, Day 7, the conferences already on that agenda
  Ask: "Somebody has to cut a block tomorrow and has never held a gouge.
  Past the safety rule — which we both know you will say first — what do
  you tell them, and what do you let them find out for themselves?"
  Then: "You watched other people's prints come off the press. What did
  somebody else do that you did not?"
  A strong answer to the first names a technique and the effect it
  produces, in the right words, and separates the part that is dangerous
  from the part that is merely hard — C2.1 and C3.2 heard together, and
  the only place you will find out whether safe practice is understood
  or copied. The second question reaches C1.2 sideways: describing
  somebody else's tool use accurately is harder than describing your
  own, and neither answer can be read off an annotation the student
  wrote about their own trial.
  Record: a word per student beside the class list — the property of the
  medium they named.

The product evidence is the annotated trials, handed in on Unit 2,
Day 11.
