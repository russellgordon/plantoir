What I check for at the end of the semester, so you can check for it
first.

## Contents

- [ ] [[Your First Entry]] present, from the first week
- [ ] An entry for each investigation you ran — there are ten of them
- [ ] Every one of those entries contains a prediction recorded
      **before** the measurement, with a reason attached
- [ ] Raw readings written down, not only the calculated answers
- [ ] At least one entry where a number came out wrong and you chased it
- [ ] Notes from at least two discussions, saying what moved
- [ ] Both [[Showing Growth]] comparisons, written in class
- [ ] [[Final Reflection]] completed
- [ ] Dates on everything, in order, readable by a stranger

## How it is assessed

The entries that carry a mark are the ones written **in class, at a
milestone** — the end-of-unit entries, the two [[Showing Growth]]
comparisons, and [[Final Reflection]]. Everything else in here is yours,
and it is what makes those entries possible; see [[How Marks Work]]. The
criteria below are what I read the milestone entries against, and what
you should read them against first — [[Judging Your Own Work]] is the
routine for doing that.

| Criterion | What I am looking for |
| --- | --- |
| Selection | A range, not only the entries where you looked good |
| Evidence | Numbers with units and the right significant figures — not adjectives |
| Quantitative honesty | Differences compared against your uncertainty, not eyeballed |
| Reasoning | Mechanism attempted, not just the pattern described |
| Limitations | Named by you, sized, and with the direction they pushed |
| Growth | Change shown with two pieces side by side, not asserted |
| Care | Organised well enough that a reader can follow it cold |

## The two rows that separate this course from last year

**Quantitative honesty.** Grade 10 accepted "the two results were about
the same". Grade 11 does not, because "about the same" is a claim about
precision made without consulting the instrument. The replacement is
short and mechanical: *unchanged to within the balance's resolution of
0.01 g*. It takes one clause and it turns an impression into a finding.

**Limitations with a direction.** "Some product was lost" tells me you
know a limitation exists. "Product left on the filter paper can only
lower the mass we recovered, so 88% is a floor and the true yield is
higher" tells me you know what it *did*, and it makes your conclusion
more useful rather than less. Every limitation pushes the answer one
way. Work out which way and say so — [[Writing a Lab Report]] has the
full version.

> [!tip] The five-minute audit
> Open three entries at random. If none of them contains a number with a
> unit, that is your gap. If none contains a sentence beginning "I was
> wrong about", that is your other one. And if every number in all three
> is quoted to the same number of decimal places regardless of what
> measured it, go and read [[Significant Figures in Practice]] before
> you hand it in.
