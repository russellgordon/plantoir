## The idea

**WHMIS** — the Workplace Hazardous Materials Information System — is the
Canada-wide system for telling you what a chemical can do to you, using symbols
you can read at a glance.

| Pictogram | Meaning | Example |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Flame | Flammable | Ethanol |
| Exclamation mark | Irritant, harmful | Dilute acids |
| Corrosion | Burns skin, eats metal | Concentrated $\ce{HCl}$ |
| Skull and crossbones | Acutely toxic | Methanol |
| Health hazard | Long-term harm | Some solvents |
| Gas cylinder | Gas under pressure | Compressed $\ce{CO2}$ |

> [!danger] Non-negotiable rules in this room
> - Goggles on **before** anything is opened, off after everything is put away.
> - Never taste. Never smell directly — waft.
> - Long hair tied back, sleeves clear, bags off the floor.
> - Tell me about every spill, cut, and burn. Every one, immediately.
> - Know where the eyewash, shower, extinguisher, and exits are **now**, not
>   when you need them.

## Reading a label

Every container names the product, shows its pictograms, and gives a signal word
— **Danger** (more severe) or **Warning**. The Safety Data Sheet has the full
detail, including first aid and disposal.

> [!question] Before every investigation
> What could go wrong here, and what would I do about it? Write the answer in
> the safety section of [[Your Lab Notebook]] before you touch anything. That
> note is what [[A1.5|the safety expectation]] means by applying safe practice
> *while planning*, and it is the part of [[Lab Reports]] that is marked
> without ever appearing in the report.
