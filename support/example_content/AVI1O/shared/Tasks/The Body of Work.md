**Launched at the start of Unit 4; due before the exhibition is
installed.** Individual.

Three related works, made deliberately as a set, in at least two of
drawing, painting, printmaking and sculpture. A fourth medium on top of
those is welcome; instead of them it is not, because this is where the
course checks that you can work in the four it is built on.

## What "related" means

Not three pictures of the same thing. Related by **idea** — a question
you are asking, a place, a material property, a feeling — so that seeing
all three tells a viewer something no single one does.

## The process, which is marked

1. **Generate.** Ideas and plans in the sketchbook, several options, in
   more than one strategy — alone and with others.
2. **Choose**, and be able to say why this one.
3. **Make**, revising as you go. Exploration, reflection, and revision
   are the expectation here, so the sketchbook must show them.
4. **Work in a series** — each piece informed by the last, which is what
   [[Working in a Series]] is for.

## What is marked

| Criterion | What it looks like |
| --- | --- |
| Idea | A coherent thread a viewer can find without being told |
| Process | Sketchbook evidence of generating, choosing, and revising |
| Range | At least two of drawing, painting, printmaking and sculpture, each chosen for a reason |
| Craft | Finished to the standard the medium allows at this level |
| Development | The third piece is visibly informed by the first two |

> [!tip] Decide the idea before the images
> Sets that start from "I'll do three drawings of hands" run out at the
> second. Sets that start from a question — what does a hand do when
> nobody is watching? — keep producing.

This is the work that goes into [[The Exhibition]], so make it with a
wall in mind.

Triangulation — the evidence you will not have unless you go and get it.

OBSERVE — Unit 4, Day 2, the period that plans the set
  Watch for: how a student closes the choice of medium and scale. Some
  put two or three versions of the plan on the page in different media
  and compare them out loud; some name a medium in the first minute and
  spend the rest of the period making the idea fit it. A1.1 asks for a
  VARIETY of strategies used to generate ideas, and the finalised plan
  in the sketchbook cannot distinguish alternatives that were really
  weighed from alternatives drawn afterwards so the page shows some.
  The Process row will be satisfied either way; only this period tells
  you which happened.
  Going well: more than one plan open at once, and the student can say
  what each one costs — this size will not survive the wall, that medium
  will not dry in time.
  Stuck: one plan, drawn large, being tidied.
  Record: write a single digit beside each name — how many distinct
  plans are on the page when you pass.

TALK — Unit 4, Day 9, the conferences already on that agenda
  Ask: "Was there a piece in the plan that is not in the set now? What
  happened to it?"
  Then: "If there were a fourth, what would it have to do that these
  three do not?"
  The first question reaches the abandoned option, which by definition
  is not on the wall. A1.1 is about generating several ideas and
  reflecting on their suitability before choosing, and a student who can
  say what the dropped piece was and what killed it — the scale, the
  time, the fact that it turned out to be the same piece as the second
  one — is doing the half of the creative process that leaves no trace.
  The second question is the check on that: a strong answer describes a
  gap rather than a subject ("all three of them are loud, so a fourth
  would have to be quiet, and I do not know how to do that yet"), which
  is the difference between a set that was planned and three pieces that
  were made in a row.
  Record: a phrase per student on the class list — what they dropped, and
  the gap they named.

The product evidence is the three works, handed in on Unit 4, Day 10,
and the sketchbook that ran beside them.
