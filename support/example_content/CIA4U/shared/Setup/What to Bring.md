The list of physical objects is short. The habit at the end of it is the
part that actually decides how this course goes for you.

## The objects

- **A notebook that will survive the term.** Bound, not loose sheets. You
  will be drawing curves by hand and annotating them, and a curve you drew
  yourself is worth more than one you were shown.
- **A calculator.** Percentage change, index numbers and real-versus-nominal
  conversions come up constantly. Your phone counts, but see below.
- **A device that can reach a table.** Statistics Canada, the Bank of Canada
  and the Department of Finance publish everything this course needs, free,
  with no account required. A phone is enough to look a figure up; a laptop
  or tablet is much better for reading a table properly.
- **Something to write with that is not the same colour as the figure.**
  Marking up a printed table is half the work in Unit 3.

## The habit, which is the actual requirement

Every figure you write down gets three things beside it: **what it is, what
table or series it came from, and when that figure was released.** Not
"unemployment 6.4%" but "unemployment rate, 6.4%, July 2026, Statistics
Canada Table 14-10-0287-01, released 7 August 2026".

This looks fussy at the start of the course. By the end it is the reason
your work can be checked, updated and defended, and everyone else's cannot.
[[Your Economics Notebook]] shows what a page of that looks like when it is
being kept properly.

## A list to copy into your notebook

Copy this onto the inside cover and read down it before you hand anything
in. It is printed here, not interactive — nothing on this site records what
you have done.

- [ ] Notebook, bound, with the inside cover kept for this list
- [ ] Calculator, and a device that can reach a table
- [ ] Every figure carries its table or series number
- [ ] Every figure carries its release date, not the date you copied it
- [ ] Any figure older than the most recent release is labelled as such
- [ ] Any number I could not source is marked as unsourced, not quietly used
- [ ] Every borrowed chart names who made it

The last three are where marks are usually lost, and they are the three
that cost nothing to fix.
