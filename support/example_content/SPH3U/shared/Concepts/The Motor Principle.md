Put a current-carrying wire in a magnetic field and the wire feels a
force. That single fact runs every electric motor on Earth.

The force is perpendicular to BOTH the current and the field — which feels
wrong the first time and is why the right-hand rules exist. Fingers along
the field, thumb along the current, and the force pushes out of your palm.

## From force to rotation

A loop of wire in a field feels forces in opposite directions on its two
sides. That pair of forces twists it. Left alone the loop would settle
halfway and stop — so a motor reverses the current every half turn, with a
split-ring commutator on a DC motor, keeping the twist always in the same
direction.

Build one and it becomes obvious in a way no diagram manages — see
[[Building a Simple Motor]].

## The same physics backwards

Move a wire through a magnetic field instead, and a current appears. That
is [[Electromagnetic Induction]], and it is the reason a motor spun by
hand becomes a generator. One device, one principle, run in either
direction.
