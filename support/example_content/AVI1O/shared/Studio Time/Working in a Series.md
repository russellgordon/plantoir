A series is several related works made deliberately as a group — the same
subject in three media, the same composition at three times of day, the
same idea pushed until it either works or is exhausted. In the last unit
you will make one, and the reason is worth understanding before you
start.

## Why one work teaches you less

The first version of anything is where you find out what the problem actually
is. You discover halfway through that the real subject is the shadow, not
the object, or that the composition needed more space at the top. In a
single-work assignment, that discovery arrives too late to use. In a series
it arrives exactly on time, because there is another one to make.

## Hold something still

A series where everything changes is just a pile of unrelated works. The
method is to fix most of it and vary one thing deliberately: same subject
in a different medium, same medium in a different palette, same palette
at a different scale. Holding the rest still is what lets you see what
your one change did — a controlled experiment that happens to be made of
art.

Write the variable down before you begin the second piece. One sentence in
your sketchbook: *the same still life in three colours only.* Later, when
you reflect on the whole group, that sentence is your evidence.

## What a series does to your nerve

Working in a series quietly removes the fear of ruining something. When
there are three, the second can be the reckless one, and the reckless one
is usually where the discovery happens. Artists have always worked this
way — the works that reach a gallery are survivors of a much larger set.

It also builds a group with a visible relationship between pieces, which is
what a viewer at [[The Exhibition]] reads and what [[The Body of Work]]
asks you to produce.

Keep the failures — in the sketchbook, not on the wall. A set of three
whose record shows the attempts that did not survive is more honest and
more useful than three tidy pieces with nothing behind them, and
[[Assessing Your Own Work]] is how you tell which is which.
