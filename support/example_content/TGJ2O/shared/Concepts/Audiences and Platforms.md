On [[Publication Day]] the championship story goes out four times: on
the website, as a social card, in the paper, and on the news program.
Same facts, same reporting — four different shapes. Learning to reshape
a story without losing it is a core newsroom skill, because no single
version reaches everyone.

## What each platform rewards — and costs

| Platform | It rewards | It costs |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Website | Depth, links, permanence | Readers must be sent there |
| Social | A [[Design a Social Card\|stopping image]] and speed | Context, stripped by the scroll |
| Print | Browsing, keeping, passing around | A deadline that cannot slip |
| Broadcast | A [[Record a Standup\|face and a voice]] | Every second must earn its keep |

The web version can hold the full interview and link to last year's
coverage. The social card gets one image and a dozen words to stop a
thumb — and travels without any of its surroundings. Print is the
version families cut out and keep, but there is no fixing it after the
presses run. Broadcast carries feeling no text can match, yet viewers
cannot skim: lose them for ten seconds and they are gone.

## Meeting readers where they are

You do not choose a platform for your convenience; the audience chose
theirs already, and the story travels to them. In the terms of the
communications model, the message and sender hold steady while the mode
of transmission changes to fit the receiver — and each mode reshapes
what arrives. What survives every reshaping is the journalism: the lede
from [[The Inverted Pyramid]] becomes the headline of the card and the
first sentence of the broadcast script, and the judgement in
[[News Values]] decides what leads in every format. Platforms change
fast — each new one redraws how news finds people, as anyone's feed
proves — but a newsroom that can reshape a true story for wherever
readers gather will survive every platform shift.
