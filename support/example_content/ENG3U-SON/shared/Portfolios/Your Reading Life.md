A running record of what you notice as a reader, across the whole course —
not a summary of what happened in the text, but a record of your own
thinking while you were in it.

## What goes in an entry

A few sentences, written in class at a milestone in a reading — never
assigned as take-home homework, because the point is to catch what you
noticed while it was still fresh. A strong entry does one of these:

- Names a moment that surprised you, and says why
- Asks a genuine question the text raised and hasn't answered yet
- Notices something about HOW the writer built an effect, not just what
  happened
- Connects what you're reading now to something from earlier in the course

A weak entry retells plot. If a stranger who hadn't read the text could
guess your entry just from knowing what happens, it's summary, not a
reading-life entry.

## When you write one

Your reading journal comes out at specific points the class page names —
the first reaction to a new text, partway through a longer one, and at the
end. It travels with you (see [[What to Bring]]) because entries are always
written in the room, not at home.

## What this is not

Reading-life entries are formative — practice for noticing, not evaluated
work. They are never marked, and they are never read by anyone but you and,
if you choose to share one aloud in a discussion, whoever's in the room that
day.

By the end of the course, flip back through what you wrote in September.
The gap between your first entry and your last one is usually the most
honest evidence you have of how much you've actually grown as a reader.
