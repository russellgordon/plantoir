> [!abstract] At a glance
> **William Shakespeare**, written around 1595 · a tragedy in five acts ·
> read across seven weeks, aloud, in this room

> [!warning] What this text deals with
> Suicide, violence between young men, and adults arranging a
> thirteen-year-old's marriage. We read the last of those as the play
> presents it: as a horror the text is aware of.

## Before we start

Two things worth knowing:

**The Prologue gives away the ending.** In fourteen lines you are told
that both of them die and that the feud dies with them. That is
deliberate — see [[Plot and Structure]] — and it changes the question
from *what happens* to *how does this happen to people who could have
stopped*.

**Juliet is thirteen.** The play says so. Productions often cast adults,
which quietly softens what the adults in the play are doing. Watch for
it.

## How we will read it

- **Aloud, always**, with parts swapped every scene.
- **Paraphrase the hard speeches** in the margin, line by line, per
  [[Reading Shakespeare]].
- **Watch two productions of key scenes**, after reading them — never
  before.
- **Stage one scene yourselves**, which is [[The Scene, Staged]].

## What to track from the first scene

| Thread | Watch for |
| --- | --- |
| Light and dark | Who uses the images, and how the meaning reverses by Act 3 |
| Haste | Every character telling somebody to slow down or hurry up |
| Names | "Deny thy father and refuse thy name" — the play is about property and inheritance as much as about love |
| Who speaks alone | Which characters get soliloquies, and which never do |
| Hands and touch | From the shared sonnet at the party to the tomb |

## One line to check before we get there

The most quoted line in the play is also the most often misread. Check
your reading of it now; nothing you answer here is sent to anyone.

> [!quiz] In Act 2, Scene 2, Juliet says "O Romeo, Romeo, wherefore art thou Romeo?" What is she asking?
> - [ ] Where Romeo is hiding in the orchard
> - [x] Why he has to be Romeo, a Montague
> - [ ] Whether Romeo will come back to her
> - [ ] What Romeo's real name is
>
> > [!explain]- Why
> > "Wherefore" means "why", not "where". Juliet believes she is alone,
> > so she cannot be looking for him. Her next line settles it: "Deny thy
> > father and refuse thy name." The trouble is not where he is. It is
> > that the boy she met at the party is a Montague.

## Questions we will argue about

1. Whose tragedy is this? The Prologue blames the parents; the title
   names the children.
2. Is the Friar a good man with a bad plan, or a man who repeatedly
   chooses concealment over conflict?
3. Does the play believe in love at first sight, or is it examining
   people who do?
4. Mercutio dies in Act 3 and the play changes. What exactly changes?
