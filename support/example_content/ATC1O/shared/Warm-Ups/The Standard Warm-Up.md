Most days start the same way, with the same twelve or fifteen minutes. Within a few weeks you will be able to run it without being led, which is the point —
a dancer who can warm themselves up properly can train anywhere, in any
studio, for the rest of their life.

## The order

```mermaid
graph LR
    A["Pulse raiser<br/>easy travel"] --> B["Joint mobility<br/>ankles up"]
    B --> C["Spine<br/>roll down, spiral"]
    C --> D["Dynamic range<br/>swings, reaches"]
    D --> E["Under load<br/>plié, weight shift"]
    E --> F["Small jumps<br/>last"]
```

## Why that order and not another

Warming up is literal. Muscle that is a couple of degrees warmer is more
elastic and receives nerve signals faster, and joints only produce the fluid
that lubricates them once they have been moved. So the pulse raiser comes
first, and everything after it is spending warmth you have already banked.

Joints are mobilised from the ankles upward because the ankles and feet will
carry every landing for the rest of the class. The spine comes before the
big swings because a spine that has not articulated yet does not like being
whipped around. Dynamic movement through your full range comes before load,
and long held stretches are not part of a warm-up at all — held at length,
a cold muscle is exactly where a strain happens, and even a warm one
temporarily produces less power afterwards. Save those for
[[Cool-Down and Recovery]].

Jumping is last because it asks the most of the ankle and the calf tendon of
anything in a studio day. If you arrive late and miss the warm-up, you join
the class — but you do not join the jumps.

## The other thing a warm-up is for

It is a daily check-in. You find out today whether your right hip is stiff,
whether you slept, whether yesterday's class is still in your legs. That
information is not a verdict on you and not a comparison with anyone else in
the room; it is just what your body is like this morning, and it tells you
how hard to work and what to modify.

Warming up well is also a matter of the boring things — sleep, water, and
having eaten — which [[Body Image and Healthy Practice]] takes seriously
rather than as a lecture. The warm-up is the first section of
[[A Technique Class]], and it is the one part of the day that is never
optional.
