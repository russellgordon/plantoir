This course spends a lot of time in argument, in teams, and in other
people's organizations. That only works if the room is safe to be wrong
in. This is the agreement we work under.

## In discussion

- **Argue with the idea, not the person.** "That assumes the workload is
  fixed" is an argument. "That's a stupid thing to say" is not.
- **Bring evidence.** You are entitled to your own view; you are not
  entitled to your own facts.
- **Let people finish.** Especially when you are certain they are wrong.
- **Change your mind out loud.** It is the most respected thing anyone
  does in a seminar, and saying what moved you is exactly the reasoning
  I am listening for — see [[How Marks Work]] on evidence you speak
  rather than write.

## About real people

Some of what we discuss will touch workplaces you know, managers you have
had, and people in your family.

- No names, no identifying details, unless the person has said you may.
- What is said in the room stays there.
- You never have to share an experience. "Pass" is a complete answer.

## In teams

- Do what you said you would do, by when you said it.
- Say early when you cannot. A missed deadline you flagged on Tuesday is
  a problem the team can solve.
- Disagreement inside the team gets handled inside the team first, using
  what your charter says.

## Equity, in a course about workplaces

This course deals with hiring, promotion, discipline, harassment, and
accommodation. Those are not abstract: they are the parts of management
where people get treated unfairly, and Ontario law says clearly what an
employer must do. We will discuss them frankly, and this room is not a
place where anyone's identity is up for debate.

> [!warning] If something in this course is hard for a real reason
> A discussion about workplace stress, discipline, or harassment can land
> close to home. Tell me before or after — not necessarily why — and we
> will arrange it. That is a professional conversation, not a favour.
