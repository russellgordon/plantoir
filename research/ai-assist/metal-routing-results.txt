ROUTING ON METAL, AFTER THE 2026-08-24 SYSTEM-PROMPT CHANGE
===========================================================

THE FIRST THING TO KNOW: THIS IS A RE-MEASUREMENT, AND IT CANNOT SHOW A WIN
--------------------------------------------------------------------------
The two sentences added to the system prompt on 2026-08-24 (commit b77b91bd,
"undo_last_change reverses only the assistant's own most recent action…" and
"There is no tool to delete, remove or rename a page or a folder…") were
written to fix a cluster measured on WINDOWS, in a container, against
Qwen2.5-1.5B under Vulkan: promise-card-results.txt, 3 trials, 15 tools, 72%
overall, with "hide, phrased as a mistake" answered with undo_last_change in
all three trials.

**That failure has never been observed on Metal.** The same suite had already
run on this Mac, twice, before the tweak existed:

  - macos-native-10-trial-comparison.txt:193-250 — 2026-08-15, Qwen2.5-1.5B,
    15 tools, ctx 16384, temp 0.1, 10 trials: "hide, phrased as a mistake"
    10/10 unpublish_pages.
  - thirteen-tool-surface-results.txt — 2026-08-15, 13 tools, ctx 16384: the
    same probe, again 10/10 unpublish_pages, on BOTH tiers.

So on this hardware the tweak was aimed at a fault that was not there. This
run can therefore show one of two things and no third: that the change is
NEUTRAL on Metal, or that it is HARMFUL on Metal. It cannot show a gain,
because there was no loss to recover. That is the honest frame for everything
below, and it is written at the top so that a number from this file cannot be
quoted as evidence the tweak worked.

WHAT THIS NUMBER IS NOT
-----------------------
  - **Not a pass rate, and not a probability.** The suite runs at temperature
    0.1 (0 in the headline arms), which is near-greedy: ten trials of one
    probe are usually ten copies of one answer. Ten trials distinguishes "this
    model systematically does the wrong thing with this sentence" from "it
    wobbled once". It does not estimate how often a teacher would be
    misrouted.
  - **Not what a teacher experiences.** Five of the 29 probes never reach the
    model in the app — AssistCardCommand answers them in code — and the app
    also rewrites the course and section (`boundToThisSection`) and settles
    relative dates (`withTheDaySettled`) before any call runs. The suite does
    neither, so its "wrong course" and "fabricated date" counters are
    STRICTER than the shipped product.
  - **Not a comparison with the 72%.** That figure is Windows, in a container,
    3 trials, 15 tools, a different engine build and a different tool-surface
    text. Nothing here is like-for-like with it. It is quoted only to say what
    it is.
  - **Not a gate.** Nothing in either test suite checks routing, deliberately
    and for reasons recorded in CLAUDE.md. This is a measurement, taken by
    hand, on one machine, on one day.

**Follow-up work is demanded if any of these trips.** Nothing else is.

  1. ANY polarity inversion — a hide request answered with a publish tool —
     in any trial of any arm. This is the veto, not a tiebreaker: it is the
     one failure that reports success.
  2. Any probe that was 10/10 in EITHER 2026-08-15 anchor scoring ≤5/10 in
     the comparable arm.
  3. "hide, phrased as a mistake" below 9/10 unpublish, or 2/10 or more
     undo_last_change. This is a REGRESSION tripwire, not a test that the
     tweak fixed something: the probe was already 10/10 here before the
     tweak existed.
  4. The pre-tweak arm beating the shipped arm by ≥5/10 on a probe that is
     stable (0/10 or 10/10) in both. That would mean the prompt was changed
     on another platform's numbers at this platform's expense, and it earns
     a `decision` issue rather than a quiet fix.
  5. The deletion probe ("Delete the Unit 1 folder in VVH2O") routing to a
     real WRITE. rebuild_preview and cancel_scheduled_deploy are the known,
     logged answers and are not fixed here; the wording fix that was tried
     and REJECTED is in conversational-residue-results.txt ~:59-71 and is not
     to be retried.
  6. Write-misses above the 13-tool anchor's rate on the same probes.
  7. A fabricated date on any probe other than `typos`, or any malformed tool
     call, or any argument needing type coercion.
  8. The em-dash arm differing from the hyphen arm by ≥5/10 on any stable
     probe. ONLY if this trips does Windows owe anything, and then a
     `windows` issue is drafted — otherwise the divergence is something the
     other side must merely KNOW, and it goes in documentation/.

  **Not grounds, decided now so it cannot be argued later:** a single control
  moving; an unstable probe (1-9/10 in an arm) moving; the five intercepted
  probes scoring badly, since the app never sends them.

Every probe is classified stable-pass (10/10), stable-fail (0/10) or unstable
(anything between), and the thresholds are read against that classification.

**The five probes the app answers in code**, found by the harness's

THE ANSWER, IN ONE PARAGRAPH
----------------------------
**On the tier this Mac actually ships, the 2026-08-24 change bought nothing
and cost nothing.** Qwen3-4B answered 27 of the 29 probes identically with the
shipped prompt and with the pre-tweak prompt — 271/290 against 270/290, the
one-response difference being a probe that is noise in both — and the promise
card is 110/110 either way. On the small tier the shipped prompt is slightly
BETTER than the pre-tweak one (208/290 against 199/290), which is the opposite
direction from the worry, so nothing is owed back to Windows on the wording.
The run found three things nobody was looking for, and they matter more than
the headline: tool-call arguments arrive TRUNCATED often enough to count
(19 of 290 responses on the small tier, and every "malformed tool calls: 0" in
this folder was measuring only HTTP errors); one probe makes the small model
generate for 41 seconds under the app's own request body; and the two
2026-08-15 anchors cannot be reproduced probe-for-probe even at their own
context size and thread count, because the harness that produced the newer one
was never committed.

THRESHOLD VERDICTS
------------------
1. ANY polarity inversion — **NOT TRIPPED.** Zero, in every arm, on both
   models: 2,494 responses in total, no hide request answered with a publish
   tool. Worth more than it looks, because the counter was WIDENED for this
   run: the old one matched probe labels and could not see "card: unpublish
   by name" or "no course named: hide" invert. It now covers all six polarity
   probes, and they are 10/10 correct in every 1.5B arm and every 4B arm.

2. A probe that was 10/10 in EITHER anchor now ≤5/10 — **TRIPPED**, four
   probes, none of them the prompt's doing:
     1.5B  publish tomorrow, with links   anchor 1 10/10 → 0/10
     1.5B  deploy now                     anchor 1 10/10 → 0/10
     1.5B  no course named: publish       anchor 1 10/10 → 0/10
     4B    read                           anchor 2 10/10 → 0-1/10
   The first three were ALREADY 0/10, 0/10 and 4/10 in anchor 2, taken the
   same day: they are the price of the 15→13 tool trim, paid in August and
   visible in the file that recorded it. The fourth is new, is identical in
   both prompt forms, and appears on both tiers. Follow-up drafted.

3. "hide, phrased as a mistake" — **NOT TRIPPED, and this is the answer #117
   asked for.** 10/10 unpublish_pages in every 1.5B arm, 10/10 in every 4B
   arm, and 0/10 undo_last_change anywhere. The cluster the tweak was written
   for does not exist on Metal, with the tweak or without it: the pre-tweak
   arms score the same 10/10.

4. Pre-tweak beating shipped by ≥5/10 on a probe stable in both —
   **NOT TRIPPED.** No probe moves that way at all. The differences run the
   other direction (HIDE 9/10 shipped against 4/10 pre-tweak at ctx 8192,
   10/10 against 6/10 at ctx 16384; typos 9/10 against 5/10), and both probes
   are unstable, so the honest reading is "no evidence the wording hurts
   here" rather than "the wording helps". No `decision` issue is owed.

5. The deletion probe routing to a real WRITE — **TRIPPED, once.** One trial
   in 290 (A1, hyphen prompt) answered it with `unpublish_pages`, which is on
   the write list; the other 289 gave rebuild_preview or check_section, which
   are the known logged answers and are excluded by the threshold's own
   wording. The one write arrived with TRUNCATED arguments, so in the app it
   would have reached `unpublishPages` with an empty dictionary, no course,
   and been refused for not naming one — but a write tool chosen for a
   deletion request is what the threshold is about, and it is recorded here
   rather than explained away.

6. Write-misses above anchor 2's rate on the same probes — **TRIPPED on the
   small tier, not on the large.** Anchor 2's 1.5B column is 48 write-misses
   over the 29 shared probes; today's arms are 50-60. The difference is not a
   new failure but more of an old one: `no course named: publish` went from
   6 to 10 wrong publishes and `typos` from 2 to 4. On the 4B it is 10 today
   against 10 in the anchor — identical, and all ten are the `typos` probe
   choosing publish_pages.

7. A fabricated date, a malformed call, or a type coercion — **TRIPPED, on
   malformed calls, and this is the most useful thing the run found.** Zero
   fabricated dates (every "tomorrow" resolved to 2026-09-19), zero type
   coercions, zero HTTP errors — but 12 to 19 responses per 1.5B arm arrived
   as a tool call whose arguments were cut off mid-JSON. See below. The 4B
   produced none.

8. The em-dash arm differing from the hyphen arm by ≥5/10 on a stable probe —
   **NOT TRIPPED.** A1 and A2 differ on three probes (card: undo 8 vs 10,
   HIDE 10 vs 9, typos 6 vs 9), all three unstable, none by 5. The totals
   differ by 4/290, which is inside the 3-5/290 this suite moves when the
   same arm is simply run twice. **So Windows owes nothing here** — its
   AssistAgent.cs em-dashes are fine, and the finding is documentation, not
   an obligation. It is still worth writing down, because until this run
   nobody knew the suite had been measuring a different string from the one
   both apps ship.

WHAT THE RUN FOUND THAT NOBODY WAS LOOKING FOR
-----------------------------------------------
**1. Tool-call arguments arrive truncated, and every file in this folder has
been reporting that as zero.** The suite did `except: arguments = {}`, so a
call whose argument JSON was cut off counted as a call with no arguments, and
the line "malformed tool calls: 0" was only ever counting HTTP failures. With
that fixed (commit in this branch), the small tier produces 12-19 truncated
calls per 290 responses — `publish tomorrow, with links` every single trial,
plus `typos`, `no course named: publish` and `deploy now` — and the 4B
produces none. **Read every "malformed tool calls: 0" in the older files as
"no HTTP errors".**

**2. Under the app's own request body, one sentence makes the small model
generate for 41 seconds.** The suite's historic body caps generation at 256
tokens; AssistModelClient sends no cap at all. With no cap, "Publish
tomorrow's class for VVH2O section 1, and make sure every page it links to is
published rather than left as a draft." makes Qwen2.5-1.5B emit a
`publish_pages` call enumerating "Unit 2, Day 3; Unit 2, Day 4; … Unit 2, Day
58; …" until the context runs out — 5,435 completion tokens, finish_reason
`length`, **40.9 seconds**, 3/3 trials, reproduced on a cold server. The
teacher waits three quarters of a minute and gets an unusable call. The 4B
never does this (max 73 tokens). The app's 180-second timeout means it
completes rather than failing, which is arguably worse.

**3. The small tier's real shape.** 19 of 29 probes are rock solid, 7 are
solidly wrong, and the rest wobble. The seven include three the assistant
window PROMISES ("Deploy tomorrow's class at 6:30 AM" → deploys now, twice
over) and one that a teacher would phrase naturally ("Publish tomorrow's
class, and everything it links to" with no course named → publish_pages). The
4B gets all of them right, every trial, both prompts. The gap between the
tiers on this surface is 93% against 72%, and it is not evenly spread — it is
concentrated in exactly the sentences a teacher is most likely to type.

CONCLUSION, IN PLAIN WORDS
--------------------------
The 2026-08-24 system-prompt change bought the mac nothing and cost it
nothing. On the model this Mac runs, the two prompts are the same prompt as
far as routing is concerned — 27 of 29 probes identical, the promise card
perfect either way, the deletion probe declined either way. On the small
model the shipped wording is a few points ahead of the one it replaced, so
there is no case for reverting it and no `decision` to put to anybody. #117's
specific question — "is undo over-salience fixed on Metal?" — has the answer
that the fault was never present here: the probe was 10/10 correct before the
tweak and is 10/10 correct after it.

What is worth acting on came out of the measurement rather than out of its
hypothesis: a counter that has been reporting zero while truncated calls went
through, a 41-second turn on a sentence the assistant invites, a `read` probe
that both tiers now get wrong, and three promises the small tier cannot keep.
Those are drafted as separate issues rather than folded in here.
