Style is not ornament. At this level it is the difference between an
essay a reader has to decode and one they can follow at speed.

## Five habits worth building

1. **Subject and verb early and close together.** Everything else is
   easier once the reader knows who is doing what.
2. **Vary sentence length deliberately.** A short sentence after two long
   ones lands. That is the effect.
3. **Prefer strong verbs to nominalisations.** *Shakespeare compresses
   the timeline* beats *there is a compression of the timeline*.
4. **Put old information first, new information last.** It is why some
   paragraphs feel effortless and others feel like stairs.
5. **Cut throat-clearing.** *It is important to note that* — delete, and
   start with the note.

## Diction

Choose the plain word unless the precise word is a technical one. *Uses*
is usually better than *utilises*; *equivocation* is better than
*trickery*, because it is the play's own term and it means something
specific.

## Reading your own draft for style

Mark every sentence you had to breathe twice in. Mark every sentence
beginning the same way as the one before. Those two marks will improve a
draft more than an hour of adding adjectives.

## Register

Academic writing is a register, not a personality transplant. It permits
"I" in many contexts, permits humour rarely, and requires precision
always. If a sentence sounds like nobody, it is not more academic — it is
just harder to read.
