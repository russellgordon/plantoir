> [!abstract] At a glance
> Solo · launched mid-unit · three working periods · a gallery walk on
> the due date · eight graphs, eight captions, one artist's statement

## What you are making

Choose one parent function — $f(x) = x^2$, $\sqrt{x}$, or
$\frac{1}{x}$ — and curate its family. Your gallery holds eight
portraits: the parent, then seven children chosen so that every move
in $y = af(k(x - d)) + c$ appears somewhere, including at least one
portrait that combines three moves. Each portrait carries a caption:
the equation, the moves in the order you applied them, the domain and
range, and one point tracked from parent to image.

You finish with a short **artist's statement**: which portrait was
hardest to hang, what it taught you about the recipe, and one portrait
tied to a real situation it could model — naming what that situation
forbids from the domain or the range, the way a thrown ball forbids
negative time.

## Milestones

- [ ] Parent chosen; its key points tabled and its domain and range
      stated
- [ ] Four single-move portraits sketched by hand, then confirmed with
      sliders in [[Using Desmos]]
- [ ] Three combined-move portraits, including one using $a$, $k$, and
      $d$ together
- [ ] Captions written — order of moves stated and defended, one point
      tracked through each portrait
- [ ] One portrait tied to a real situation, with the restriction that
      situation puts on its domain or range
- [ ] Artist's statement drafted; gallery assembled for the walk

Notice that the milestones above are a checkbox list — tick them off
on paper as you go. The order matters: hands before technology, per
[[Transformations of Functions]].

## How it is assessed

This is evidence of growth, not a race — [[How Marks Work]] explains
the difference. Three things carry the mark, and the table below is the
whole of it: the gallery, the answer you give me at the walk about a
portrait of my choosing, and the ten minutes of [[Math Journal]] you
write in class on the day it is due. On the last working period,
[[Judging Your Own Work]] opens the class, so that the rest of it can be
spent on whatever you find.

## Success criteria

| Quality | What it looks like in your gallery |
| --- | --- |
| Complete family | Every letter of the recipe appears at least once |
| Honest sketches | Hand-drawn first; technology confirms, not creates |
| Tracked points | One parent point followed through every portrait |
| Precise captions | Domain, range, and order of moves stated exactly |
| A real statement | The artist's statement names a genuine difficulty |
| A restriction from the world | One portrait tied to a real situation, with what that situation forbids from its domain or range |
| An answer you own | At the walk you explain one portrait's moves without reaching for its caption |
| The entry that goes with it | Your in-class journal entry names the portrait that was hardest to hang, and what it changed |

> [!success]- If a portrait will not behave
> The two inside letters act backwards — $f(2x)$ squeezes and
> $f(x - 2)$ slides right. If a sketch and Desmos disagree, track one
> point by hand and see which of the two is lying. It is never Desmos.

Triangulation — the evidence you will not have unless you go and get it.

TALK — Unit 1, Day 9, at the conference already on that agenda

  Ask, of the portrait they are part-way through: "If I made your k negative
  instead of positive, what happens to the point you are tracking — and what
  stays exactly where it is?"

  Then: "Take the portrait you have not drawn yet. Before you draw it, tell me
  where that tracked point lands. The coordinates, not the moves."

  On the first, listen for a METHOD rather than a verdict: the tracked point's
  x-coordinate is divided by k, sign and all, and its y-coordinate is never
  touched — the same horizontal compression as before, with a flip over the
  y-axis on top of it. That is the language the recipe uses and it is what
  A1.8 asks for, describing the ROLE of a parameter as a transformation. If
  somebody volunteers that the flip is really about the vertical line the
  slide put the image on, they have gone past A1.8: write it down, but do not
  set it as the bar. On the second, what you want is a pair of numbers reached
  by applying the map in order — A1.9 done out loud, before the pencil gets
  there. A hung portrait shows you the result of both and neither of the
  routes.

  Record: one line each down your class list, right then.

OBSERVE — Unit 1, Day 10, the gallery work time at the end of the period

  The day after those conferences, watch the ten seconds that follow a hand
  sketch and Desmos disagreeing. The folded callout tells them to track one
  point by hand and find out which of the two is lying; nothing on the wall on
  Day 14 records which way that went. The "Honest sketches" row has them CLAIM
  hands-first, so this corroborates a self-report rather than replacing one,
  and it still earns the two minutes: it is the one row of that table a
  finished gallery cannot prove.

  Going well: a finger held on one parent point, a value worked out on paper,
  and a sentence about which of the two was wrong.

  Stuck: the hand sketch erased without comment, or never drawn at all.

  Record: three columns down the margin of your day plan — tracked, redrew,
  no disagreement yet.

The product evidence is the gallery on Day 14, the answer given at the walk,
and the journal entry written in class the same day.
