Reviewer: independent pass, read-only. Target: `support/example_content/CIA4U/`
after commit `dee13da0` ("CIA4U: Growing Success conformance"), which was
**not** independently reviewed — the adversarial sub-agent it launched never
returned, so its fifteen findings came from the author's own second reading.

Mechanical gates re-run here: `lint_payload.py CIA4U` → `256 pages checked; 86
class pages / coverage: 64/64 expectations addressed; 2 addressed exactly once
/ clean`. As the brief says, that proves almost nothing about what follows.

Findings are numbered most serious first. CONFIRMED means I verified it against
the primary source named in the finding — the expectation's verbatim text, the
day's agenda line, or a count I ran. SUSPECTED means the evidence is real but
the judgement is arguable.

**Three of the four most serious findings are things this pass created.** That
is not a criticism of the direction of the work, which is right; it is the
reason an independent pass exists.

## CONFIRMED

### 1. The fix for the common group mark leaves every student unevaluated on half the expectations their task page declares assessed

This is failure mode 17 in its exact shape — the fix becoming the defect it was
meant to cure — and it is new in `dee13da0`.

**`shared/Tasks/The Market Model.md`.** The pass added:

> ## Working in pairs, marked individually
>
> You work as a pair and you are marked as two people, so there is no shared
> mark on this task and nothing your partner says about your work goes into
> yours.
>
> Split the four requirements above between you and say on the front page who
> wrote which. **The model and the shifters are one half; the elasticity
> estimate and the consumer decisions are the other.**

and, in the criteria:

> - **Your own half, named.** The front page says which two of the four
>   requirements each of you wrote. **I mark you on your two**, and on how you
>   answer for the whole argument when you are questioned on Day 21.

The four requirements map onto the page's three transcluded codes almost
one-to-one:

| Requirement | Half | Code it evidences |
|---|---|---|
| 1. Build the model | A | B2.1 |
| 2. Identify the shifters — "Which of **B2.2's** factors moved your market" | A | B2.2 |
| 3. Elasticity | B | B2.1 ("including price elasticity") |
| 4. Consumer decisions — "**B2.3's** question" | B | B2.3 |

So a student who takes half A is never evaluated on B2.3, and a student who
takes half B is never evaluated on B2.2. And `The Market Model` is the **only**
page in `shared/Tasks/` that carries any B2 code — I checked all ten:

```
Tasks/The Market Model.md :: ![[B2.1]] ![[B2.2]] ![[B2.3]]
```

There is nowhere else the missing half can be picked up, so overall expectation
B2 ("demonstrate an understanding of supply and demand models, including how to
apply these models, **and of factors that affect supply and demand**") is
evaluated for each student on half its content.

**`shared/Tasks/The Policy Brief.md` has the same structure and no repair at
all.** The pass added:

> Two authors, two marks. Divide the brief so that each of you writes and signs
> named sections — **the fiscal side and the politics, say, against the monetary
> side and the risks** — and put on the page who wrote what. ... What I mark on
> each of your papers is whether **YOUR sections** actually carry that
> recommendation, and your own answer when a question comes from the floor at
> the delivery.

and in the criteria:

> - **Your own answer at the delivery.** One question from the floor, taken
>   without help, **on your own sections** — judged against the five above,
>   exactly as the writing is.

The fiscal side is D2.1 and the politics is D2.2; the monetary side is D3.1 and
D3.2. So the partner who writes the fiscal half produces no marked evidence for
D3.1 or D3.2 at all — and the oral component is explicitly restricted to "your
own sections", so it does not reach across. `The Policy Brief` is the only
`Tasks/` page carrying any D2 or D3 code:

```
Tasks/The Policy Brief.md :: ![[D2.1]] ![[D2.2]] ![[D3.1]] ![[D3.2]]
```

Overall expectations **D2 (Fiscal Policy)** and **D3 (Monetary Policy)** are
therefore each evaluated for only half the class.

`The Market Model` is half a step better because it says "Whichever half you
take, you answer questions on the whole thing at the presentation" — but the
class page limits that to one question per student:

> `per_section/All Classes/Unit 1, Day 21.md`: 2. Each partner takes one
> question of their own

One question is not evaluation of two requirements, and see finding 5 for
whether the period can even deliver it.

Before this pass both tasks were marked as a pair, which is a genuine policy
breach — but every student's report covered all four requirements. The trade
made here swaps a Chapter 5 breach for a Chapter 3 one (evaluation must rest on
evidence of achievement of the **overall** expectations, for each student).

*Smallest correct fix:* keep the individual attribution but require **each**
student's own sections to touch every declared code — e.g. on `The Market Model`
split by market rather than by requirement ("each of you models a different
year/segment, all four requirements each"), or make the questioned component a
published, criteria-backed oral covering the half the student did not write, and
give it a row.


> **APPLIED — 2026-08-20 fix round.** Not by the smallest fix named above; by
> the shape the task brief pointed at. Both pair tasks now require BOTH
> partners to work across the WHOLE task, with authorship attributed at
> paragraph level: "initial every paragraph you wrote", and every one of the
> required parts must carry initialled paragraphs from both. Splitting by
> requirement is gone from `The Market Model` and splitting by section is gone
> from `The Policy Brief`, so every declared code is now in every student's own
> marked evidence. The oral component was un-restricted on both: the Policy
> Brief's question "can come from anywhere in the brief", the Market Model's
> is "judged against the four criteria above exactly as the writing is", and
> the delivery agendas (Unit 3 Days 20 and 21) say the same.
>
> Three siblings of the same sentence were fixed with it, and each would have
> been left behind by a fix confined to the two "marked individually" sections:

### 2. Three curriculum codes sit on task pages that never ask for the thing

Failure mode 22 exactly: the pass audited the six codes it *added* to
`The Notebook and Portfolio` and left the twenty-nine already on the other task
pages unexamined. Three have **no** section that asks for the thing, **no**
criteria row and **no** class period. I flattened whitespace and searched each
page for every keyword in the expectation; the searches return nothing.

**(a) `B3.4` on `shared/Tasks/The Intervention Argument.md`.** The expectation:

> explain the concepts of negative and positive externalities, and apply these
> concepts when analysing the third-party costs and benefits of various
> policies, including those intended to enhance environmental sustainability

The task's six required sections are `The need`, `The economics`,
`The trade-off`, `The distribution`, `The evidence`, `The verdict`. The words
*externality*, *third-party*, *environment* and *sustainab* appear **nowhere on
the page**; a 60-character-window search of the flattened file returns exactly
one hit, and it is a link label:

> Build from [[Why Governments Intervene]], [[Efficiency and Equity]] and
> [[Public Goods]].

The interventions the page offers are all social policy — "Rent supplements in
one province, a dental or pharmacare benefit, a child benefit, a training
subsidy, social assistance rates, a housing accelerator, a targeted tax credit"
— so a student following the instructions will not meet an environmental policy
at all. The page's own triangulation block cites C3.2 twice and never mentions
B3.4.

*Smallest correct fix:* add a section row — "The third parties | Who outside the
transaction gains or bears a cost, named" — with a matching criteria bullet, or
drop `![[B3.4]]`. It is separately addressed on `Concepts/Externalities.md` and
`Discussions/Who Should Bear the Cost of Emissions.md`, so dropping it costs the
map only the assessed shading.

**(b) `B3.3` on `shared/Tasks/The Indicators Report.md`.** The expectation:

> assess the benefits and costs, including the environmental costs, of economic
> growth (e.g., ... job creation, loss of jobs as a result of relocation
> offshore ... overfishing, deforestation, air and water pollution, climate
> change)

The report's three sections are "the state of things" (output, employment,
prices), "the model" (AS–AD), "distribution" (Lorenz, Gini, MBM). Its criteria
are currency of data, charts built by the student, the model used, distribution
taken seriously. A flattened search of the whole page for `environment`, `growth`
and `sustainab` returns **zero hits** — the only near-match is the letters
"efficien" inside *coefficient*. Nothing here asks a student to assess a cost of
growth, environmental or otherwise.

*Smallest correct fix:* drop `![[B3.3]]`; it is already addressed on
`Concepts/Growth and Productivity.md`, `Concepts/Sustainability and the
Economy.md` and `Discussions/Is Growth the Right Goal.md`.

**(c) `B3.2` on `shared/Tasks/The Scarcity Analysis.md`.** The expectation:

> analyse issues associated with the efficient use of the factors of production
> and the relationship between efficiency, growth, and sustainability

A flattened search of the entire page for
`efficien|growth|sustainab|environment|external` returns **zero hits**. The six
required sections are `The scarcity`, `The three questions`, `The system`,
`Positive and normative`, `The curve`, `What was given up`; the four criteria
are precision about the resource, the two kinds of claim kept apart, a curve
that means something, and sources. Efficiency never appears.

This is note 17's lie in the direction that hides a gap: the map says "assessed"
about work nobody does, and the linter reports `64/64 clean` because it counts
transclusions, not truth.


> **APPLIED — three different ways, one per code, because the three are not the
> same case.**
>
> **(a) `B3.4` on `The Intervention Argument`: leg written, code kept.** The
> finding is confirmed — the words were nowhere on the page — but dropping it

Unit 2 Days 19–20 and Unit 3 Days 20–21 already split their defences and
deliveries, and move the `A Model of Your Own` launch to Day 22.
