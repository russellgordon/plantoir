From footage of a school athletics practice — provided, or shot by
your desk — cut a forty-five second highlight reel: the best moments,
in a rhythm that carries, telling the truth about how the practice
actually went. This is the editing engine behind
[[The Athletics Package]] — the real job is choosing, not trimming.

> [!warning] Care and consent notes
> **The people in this footage are real** — recognisable athletes
> need [[Releases and Consent|consent on file]] before anything
> publishes, and a clip that makes someone look foolish gets cut, not
> captioned. **Honesty is an equipment rule here** — you may choose
> moments, but never reorder them to invent an outcome; a save cut
> before its shot is a lie with a timeline. **Borrowed cards and
> drives** go back the same period, and source footage stays
> untouched — edit copies, never originals. **Save often** and back
> the project up before leaving; [[Backing Up Your Media]] covers where.

## What you need

- [ ] The practice footage, copied into your own working folder
- [ ] Your editing software, with a new project named and saved first
- [ ] Headphones, at moderate volume
- [ ] Your notebook, for the shot log

## The work

1. **Watch everything once, hands off the keyboard.** Log each usable
   moment with its time and one word — "save", "drill", "laugh".
   Skip the log and you cut the first good thing instead of the best.
2. **Pick your opening before anything else.** The first two seconds
   decide whether anyone watches the other forty-three — a reel is
   [[The Inverted Pyramid|an inverted pyramid]] made of motion.
3. **Choose about eight moments, then defend each one.** Say what
   each clip shows that the others do not — two clips doing the same
   job means one of them goes.
4. **Cut on action.** End each clip mid-motion — the ball leaving the
   hand, the jump at its peak — so the next clip inherits the energy.
   Cutting on stillness makes eight clips feel like eight videos.
5. **Build a rhythm, then break it once.** Steady clip lengths make a
   pulse; one longer moment lands harder because the pulse pauses.
6. **Watch the final cut as a stranger who was there.** A reel that
   says flawless when the footage says otherwise is effective and
   false — [[Spot the Edit]] taught you how visible that choice is.

## What can go wrong

- **Forty-five seconds becomes ninety.** Every clip felt essential —
  cut to thirty, then let the best three breathe; tight beats loose.
- **The reel is all one drill.** The edit mined one stretch of
  footage. Go back to the full log — variety is a choosing problem,
  not a shooting problem.
- **The project plays but the footage is offline.** Files got moved
  after importing. Keep the working folder frozen until export.

## Level up

Cut a second reel from the same log that tells the practice's other
true story — the struggle instead of the wins — and ask your desk
which one they would publish.
