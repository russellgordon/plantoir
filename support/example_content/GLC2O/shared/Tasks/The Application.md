**Unit 2. Individual. Handed in on Day 16, the last class of the unit.**
A complete application package, plus one page of explanation.

A real posting, a real application, and an honest account of why that
posting and not another one. You do not have to send it. You do have to
write it as though you would.

## Choose the opportunity

Any of these counts, and none is a lesser choice: a job, an
apprenticeship or pre-apprenticeship opening, a co-op or volunteer
placement, a summer program, a scholarship or award, or admission to a
program you would apply to in Grade 12. Pick one you found in
[[Reading a Job Ad]], or one you have come across since.

## What to hand in

1. **The posting or the program's own requirements**, copied and dated.
2. **A one-page résumé**, targeted at that posting.
3. **A covering message** — letter, email, or application statement,
   whichever the opportunity actually asks for.
4. **A one-page explanation.** Why this opportunity, what in your
   research made it credible, and — the part that matters — which
   factors other than your skills and interests shaped the choice. Money,
   location, transit, family expectations, who else works there, what
   you can afford to train for. Name them. Name the one you seriously
   considered instead, too, and what decided between the two: a choice
   with nothing rejected behind it is not yet a choice.
5. **Your online presence**, checked against the entry in
   [[Evidence of What You Can Do]]: what an employer would find, and
   what you changed before submitting.

## What earns the marks

- **Targeting.** Every line of the résumé earns its place against this
  posting.
- **Evidence over adjectives.** Specific situations, checkable, in your
  own voice.
- **Research showing.** Something in the covering message could only
  have been written by someone who looked this organisation up, and the
  explanation compares this opportunity with the one you did not take.
- **Honesty about factors.** The explanation names real influences
  rather than the flattering ones.
- **Care with information.** Nothing shared that should not be, and a
  presence you would be content for them to find.

> [!example] What "research showing" looks like in one line
> Weak: "I have always been interested in your company." Strong: "You
> have added evening hours since September, and evenings are when I am
> available and when I have worked before." One of those could be sent
> to any employer; the other could only be sent to this one.

Triangulation — the evidence you will not have unless you go and get it.

OBSERVE — Unit 2, Day 14, the first forty minutes, before the
conferences and the peer read
  Watch for: whether the posting is open and being used, or merely
  present. A line that happens to fit this posting and a line chosen
  against its words read identically on a finished résumé, which is why
  Targeting is the criterion you cannot mark honestly from the page
  alone. C1.3 asks for effective and appropriate forms, media and styles
  for a particular reader; choosing WHICH of your evidence that reader
  needs is the part of it that only shows here.
  Going well: the annotated posting is beside them and the page gets
  SHORTER — entries come off because nothing in the posting asks for
  them.
  Stuck: the mock application from Days 11 and 12 open and the posting
  not. Reusing that draft is the point of having written it; working
  from it without rereading what the employer asked for is not.
  Record: circle the names working with the posting shut. It is the
  shorter list, and it is who the first ten minutes of Day 15 belong to.

TALK — Unit 2, Day 15, during the revision period
  Ask: "Who would be disappointed if you got this, and who if you did
  not?"
  Then, for the online half: "What did you find and decide NOT to
  change?"
  Every factor worth naming is printed somewhere in this unit, so do not
  ask which ones apply — they have a list. Ask instead where the pressure
  is coming from: B2.2 wants them to explain which factors are
  influencing their OWN decision, and a named person is the version of
  that a page rarely carries. The second question is B2.3's other half —
  it asks for managing a presence, not deleting one, and the written
  entry records what changed while a decision to leave something alone
  leaves no trace at all.
  Record: the name they said, and a Y or an N for the account question.
  Twelve seconds each — and the name is the part you will not remember.

The product evidence is the package, handed in on Day 16.
