**1.** A $0.145\ \text{kg}$ baseball arrives at $38\ \text{m/s}$ and
leaves at $45\ \text{m/s}$ in the opposite direction. Find the impulse
and the average force if contact lasts $0.70\ \text{ms}$.

> [!success]- Answer 1
> $\Delta p = 0.145(45 - (-38)) = 12.0\ \text{kg}\cdot\text{m/s}$;
> $F = \Delta p/\Delta t = 1.7 \times 10^4\ \text{N}$.

**2.** A $2.0\ \text{kg}$ cart at $3.0\ \text{m/s}$ strikes a
stationary $4.0\ \text{kg}$ cart and they stick. Find the final velocity
and the kinetic energy lost.

> [!success]- Answer 2
> $v = (2.0)(3.0)/6.0 = 1.0\ \text{m/s}$. Before:
> $9.0\ \text{J}$; after: $3.0\ \text{J}$. Two thirds lost, to
> deformation, heat, and sound.

**3.** Two skaters, $55\ \text{kg}$ and $70\ \text{kg}$, push apart from
rest. The lighter one moves at $2.8\ \text{m/s}$. Find the other's speed.

> [!success]- Answer 3
> $v = (55 \times 2.8)/70 = 2.2\ \text{m/s}$, opposite direction. Total
> momentum stays zero.

**4.** A $1200\ \text{kg}$ car at $15\ \text{m/s}$ east collides with a
$1800\ \text{kg}$ van at $10\ \text{m/s}$ north and they lock together.
Find the velocity just after.

> [!success]- Answer 4
> $p_x = 18000$, $p_y = 18000$, total mass $3000\ \text{kg}$, so
> $v = \sqrt{2} \times 6.0 = 8.5\ \text{m/s}$ at $45^\circ$ north of
> east.

**5.** A forensic crash investigator examines a collision where an eastbound
compact car ($m_1 = 1100\ \text{kg}$) and a northbound pickup truck
($m_2 = 2200\ \text{kg}$) collided and skidded locked together at $32^\circ$
north of east for $16.0\ \text{m}$ across wet asphalt ($\mu_k = 0.45$).
(a) Determine the pre-collision speeds of both vehicles. (b) Propose two
practical engineering improvements to highway barrier design or vehicle
crumple zones that maximize impact duration $\Delta t$ to reduce peak impulse
forces on occupants.

> [!success]- Answer 5
> (a) From post-collision skid work:
> $v_f = \sqrt{2\mu_k g d} = \sqrt{2(0.45)(9.8)(16.0)} = 11.9\ \text{m/s}$.
> Total post-collision momentum:
> $p_{\text{tot}} = (m_1 + m_2) v_f = (3300)(11.9) = 3.93 \times 10^4\ \text{kg}\cdot\text{m/s}$.
> Components: $p_x = p_{\text{tot}} \cos 32^\circ = 3.33 \times 10^4\ \text{kg}\cdot\text{m/s} = m_1 v_{1x} \implies v_{1x} = 30.3\ \text{m/s}$ ($109\ \text{km/h}$).
> $p_y = p_{\text{tot}} \sin 32^\circ = 2.08 \times 10^4\ \text{kg}\cdot\text{m/s} = m_2 v_{2y} \implies v_{2y} = 9.47\ \text{m/s}$ ($34\ \text{km/h}$).
> (b) Practical improvements include: staged crushable aluminum honeycomb or
> hydroformed steel crash-box structures that collapse at progressive force
> thresholds, and sand-barrel or water-filled inertial barrier attenuators on
> highway medians that deform smoothly over 2 to 3 metres, extending $\Delta t$
> from $15\ \text{ms}$ to over $120\ \text{ms}$ and reducing peak deceleration
> by an order of magnitude.

**6.** A single-stage sounding rocket of initial mass $m_0 = 850\ \text{kg}$
carries $600\ \text{kg}$ of propellant and burns it at a steady rate with an
effective exhaust velocity $v_e = 2400\ \text{m/s}$. (a) Using momentum and
impulse principles, calculate the burnout velocity $\Delta v = v_e \ln(m_0/m_f)$
in vacuum. (b) Propose two practical procedural or technological
modifications that increase the rocket's payload capacity or final speed.

> [!success]- Answer 6
> (a) Empty mass $m_f = 850 - 600 = 250\ \text{kg}$.
> $\Delta v = 2400 \ln(850 / 250) = 2400 \ln(3.40) = 2400(1.224) = 2.94 \times 10^3\ \text{m/s}$.
> (b) Practical improvements include: multi-stage design (discarding dead tank
> and engine structural mass as soon as stages burn out, reducing $m_f$ in the
> rocket equation), and bell/aerospike nozzle optimization with cryogenic
> liquid propellants (e.g. liquid hydrogen/liquid oxygen) to raise the
> effective exhaust velocity $v_e$ from $2400\ \text{m/s}$ to over
> $4400\ \text{m/s}$.

**7.** During nuclear beta decay ($n \to p + e^-$), experimentalists in the
1920s observed that emitted electrons had a continuous kinetic energy
spectrum rather than a single discrete energy line, and the recoiling proton
and electron did not travel along a straight line in opposite directions.
Explain how Wolfgang Pauli applied the conservation of energy, linear
momentum, and angular momentum to predict the existence and characteristics
of the neutrino ($\nu_e$).

> [!success]- Answer 7
> In a two-body decay at rest, conservation of momentum requires the two
> fragments to be collinear and conservation of energy fixes the electron's
> kinetic energy to a single value. The continuous energy spectrum and
> non-collinear recoil paths meant that energy and momentum appeared missing.
> Pauli showed that a three-body decay ($n \to p + e^- + \bar{\nu}_e$) where a
> third uncharged, nearly massless, spin-$1/2$, weakly interacting particle
> shares the reaction's total energy and momentum completely restores exact
> conservation of energy, momentum, and angular momentum for every individual
> decay event.
