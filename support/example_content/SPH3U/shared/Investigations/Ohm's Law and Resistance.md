Ohm's law is a claim about a graph being straight. So build the graph.

## Safety

Low-voltage supplies only, maximum 6 V. Power off while you rewire —
every time, not just when it seems to matter. A resistor that has been
overdriven is hot enough to burn.

## Method

1. Build a series circuit: supply, resistor, ammeter. Put the voltmeter
   ACROSS the resistor.
2. Raise the supply voltage in six steps. At each step record $V$ and $I$.
3. Repeat for: a second, different resistor; then a filament lamp.

## What to record

| Component | $V$ (V) | $I$ (A) | $V/I$ (Ω) |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |

Plot $V$ against $I$ for all three on the same axes.

## Analysis

1. Which components gave straight lines through the origin? Those are the
   ohmic ones.
2. Find the resistance of each resistor from the SLOPE, not from a single
   pair of readings. Why is the slope the better estimate?
3. The lamp's line curves. Its resistance rises as it heats. Sketch what
   the graph would look like if you took readings quickly, before it
   warmed up, and say how you might test that.
