Ontario's grid is unusual: roughly half nuclear, a quarter hydro, with
gas, wind, and solar making up most of the rest. Almost all of it arrives
by the same physics — a magnet spun near a coil, as on
[[Electromagnetic Induction]].

## Before the discussion

Bring one number you looked up yourself, with its source: a capacity, a
cost per kilowatt-hour, an emissions figure, or a land-area requirement.

## The question

If Ontario had to add the equivalent of one more large generating station
by 2035, what should it be — and what does your answer cost, in money, in
land, in risk, and in whose backyard?

## Rules for this one

- A number without a source does not count.
- "It is clean" is not an argument until you say clean of WHAT.
- Everyone has to state one genuine disadvantage of the option they
  favour. If you cannot find one, you have not looked.
