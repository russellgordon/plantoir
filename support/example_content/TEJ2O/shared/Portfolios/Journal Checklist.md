Your [[Tech Journal]] is collected at the end of each unit. Before
you hand it in, run this check — it is the same lens I read with,
so anything you catch here is something we never need to discuss.
Ten minutes with this list is usually worth one extra entry. It is
[[Judging Your Own Work]] applied to the journal, and like every
self-check in this course, nothing you write here reaches your mark.

## The check

**Coverage**

- [ ] There is an entry for every bench day in the unit — the days
      whose agenda ended by sending you to your journal
- [ ] Entries were written in class on the day, not reconstructed the
      night before collection — reconstructed entries always show
- [ ] All four prompts appear across the unit: what I built, what
      fought back, what I learned, what I would try next
- [ ] At least one entry includes a real artifact — a photo of my
      own bench work, a beep code, or a reading from the tester

**Quality**

- [ ] Every fault is named and located, not just felt — "slot B
      fails with either stick", not "the computer was being weird"
      (see [[What a Strong Entry Looks Like]])
- [ ] At least one entry records something that genuinely fought
      back or confused me, not just victories
- [ ] My own part is named, not hidden inside "we" — what *I*
      seated, crimped, traced, spotted, or misread
- [ ] Help is credited where it happened — a partner, a manual, a
      forum, an AI assistant — the way [[Our Classroom Norms]] asks

**Growth**

- [ ] Each entry ends with one concrete "what I would try next"
- [ ] At least one earlier "try next" item shows up later as a thing
      I actually tried — with a sentence about how it went

> [!tip] The last two boxes are the whole game
> Anyone can write "I will check my cabling more carefully." The
> journal becomes evidence of growth the day a later entry says "I
> labelled both ends before closing the case, and found the swap in
> thirty seconds" — a loop from intention to attempt to result. That
> loop is exactly what [[Showing Growth]] will ask you to point at,
> so leave yourself things to point at.
