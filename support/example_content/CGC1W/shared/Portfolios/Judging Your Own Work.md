Every task page carries its criteria before you start. Most people read
them once, at launch, and then hand in a map with no scale bar and a
report whose strongest section is the one nobody asked for. This page is
the twenty minutes that would have caught both.

We do this together the first time, on a study somebody else wrote, so
that the move is familiar before it is your own work under the
microscope.

## The routine

1. Put the task page beside your draft. Not from memory — open it.
2. Go down the criteria one at a time. For each, write **yes**, **not
   yet**, or **partly**, and one line of evidence: the figure, the
   sentence, the feature on the map that a reader would point at. "Yes"
   with nothing beside it is a hope.
3. Name your weakest row. Not the quickest to fix — the weakest. This is
   the whole exercise, and it is harder than any of the fixing, because
   it means saying out loud the thing you were hoping nobody would open.
4. Write one sentence: what you will change, and in which period.
5. Change it — in the rest of this period, or in the next one. That is
   why this is never scheduled on a due date. A judgement you have no
   time to act on is just a worry.

> [!example]- One row, judged honestly
> From [[The Risk Report]]: *"Reasoning — hazard, exposure, and risk
> kept distinct."*
>
> **Partly.** The hazard section is clean: what the water does, how
> fast, how often. But the exposure section says "this puts the whole
> town at risk", which is a risk sentence sitting in the exposure
> section, and I never say who is *most* exposed. Weakest row.
> **Change:** split it — the map shows who is in the way, then a
> separate paragraph on how likely and how severe. Next period.
>
> That is worth more than "looks good" written against all four rows,
> and it took less time.

## Three rows this course keeps failing

Read these before you start, because they are the ones a self-check
usually skims past.

- **The map row.** Most tasks here ask for a map, and one without a
  scale, a north arrow, a legend and a source is not a map you can be
  marked on — it is a picture of one. Check those four first, every
  time. It takes ten seconds and it is the cheapest set of marks in
  the course.
- **The "explain, don't describe" row.** Read your own strongest
  paragraph and underline the word that carries the explanation —
  *because*, *which is why*, *this follows from*. If there isn't one,
  you have described something beautifully and answered nothing.
- **The honesty row.** Find the sentence you are least sure of. If it
  has no hedge and no source, either go and get one or say plainly
  that you could not.

## Where this fits

Your judgement of your own work is not part of your mark, and neither is
a classmate's — [[How Marks Work]] says why. That cuts both ways:
nothing you write here can cost you anything, which is exactly what
makes it worth writing honestly.

What it changes is the work. A self-check that leads to one real fix is the
cheapest improvement available in this course, and by the end of the course
it is something you do without being told — which is the actual point of it.

One thing worth trying: read your judgement of the weakest row out loud
to the person beside you. You will usually hear the generous bits
yourself, before they say a word.
