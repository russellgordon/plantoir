A big vocabulary is not memorised, mostly. It is assembled: English words
are built out of parts, and knowing about forty of those parts lets you
work out thousands of words you have never met.

## The three parts

- **Base** — the core meaning: *port* (carry), *dict* (say), *struct*
  (build).
- **Prefix** — goes in front and changes the meaning: *trans-* (across),
  *un-* (not), *pre-* (before).
- **Suffix** — goes on the end and usually changes the part of speech:
  *-tion* makes a noun, *-ible* makes an adjective, *-ly* makes an adverb.

*Transportation* = across + carry + the act of. You never needed the
definition.

## Twelve worth knowing cold

| Part | Means | Words |
| --- | --- | --- |
| pre- | before | predict, prevent, preview |
| re- | again, back | revise, reject, reconcile |
| trans- | across | translate, transform, transmit |
| in-, im-, un- | not | invisible, impossible, unwilling |
| sub- | under | submerge, subtle, subordinate |
| dict | say | dictate, contradict, verdict |
| spect | look | inspect, spectator, perspective |
| port | carry | import, portable, deport |
| struct | build | structure, instruct, destruction |
| -tion | act of | narration, migration, revision |
| -ology | study of | biology, anthology, technology |
| -ist | one who | novelist, activist, journalist |

## Working out a word in place

1. **Read the whole sentence** — context does half the job.
2. **Break the word** into parts you recognise.
3. **Guess, then check.** A wrong guess you check is how vocabulary
   grows; a wrong guess you keep is how misreadings start.
4. **Write it down** with the sentence you found it in, not just the
   definition. Words are learned in company.

## Register, again

English keeps two or three words for most things, and they usually differ
in origin: *ask* (Old English), *question* (French), *interrogate*
(Latin). None is better. They are for different rooms, which is the
[[Voice|register]] point again, and it is why "utilise" in a personal
narrative sounds like someone borrowed a suit.

> [!tip] The habit worth building
> Keep a page at the back of your notebook for words you meet in your own
> reading. Ten a week, with the sentence you found them in, is about 180
> by January — and they will be words you actually encountered, which is why
> they will stay.
