This course reads work that gets into real difficulty — a residential school
survivor's story, a village that stones one of its own, a border that will
not let a family be what it says it is. We are not going to soften any of
that, and we are not going to talk about it carelessly either. This page is
the agreement the whole class builds together, out loud, on the second day
of the year — what is written here is a starting draft, and your class's
real version may add to it.

## What we start from

- **Texts get read generously before they get argued with.** Understand what
  a writer is actually saying before you decide what you think of it.
- **Disagree with the idea, not the person who raised it.** "I read that
  differently" is a sentence that keeps a room working; "that's a stupid
  thing to say" ends it.
- **You can pass.** Nobody is required to share a personal connection to
  difficult material out loud. You can always answer about the text instead
  of about yourself.
- **What gets shared by a classmate in a discussion stays in the room**,
  unless they choose to say it somewhere else themselves.
- **Silence is allowed to mean thinking.** Not every pause needs to be
  filled by whoever talks fastest.

## If something in a text or a discussion is genuinely too much

Tell me. Privately, in writing, at the end of class, whenever works — you do
not need to explain why. There is always another way to meet the same
learning goal.

> [!success]- What this agreement is NOT
> It is not a rule that nothing here can be uncomfortable. Some of what we
> read is supposed to be uncomfortable — that is often the point. It is a
> rule about *how* we handle the discomfort together, not a promise to avoid
> it.

This page, once your class has built its real version, becomes something you
can hold each other to — including holding me to it.
