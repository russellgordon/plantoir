> real package, one optimum, one honest accounting

## What you are making

The package you brought from home was designed by somebody — and your
pair has just been hired to beat them. The brief: a redesign that
holds exactly the same volume, fits the client's shelf (no taller
than the shelf gap you measure in class), and uses **less material**.
You finish with a **mathematical model** — variables named, the
volume constraint folded in so material becomes a function of one
variable — the **optimum located** with a derivative and confirmed
with a sign argument, a **comparison** against the real package in
square centimetres and in percent, and the honest paragraph: what
your "best" ignored, and why the manufacturer might still be right.

## Milestones

- [ ] The real package measured; its volume and surface area computed,
      then doubted and measured again
- [ ] Variables named and the volume constraint used to write material
      as a function of one variable, with its sensible domain stated
- [ ] Candidate optima located with the derivative and confirmed with
      a sign argument or [[Curve Sketching]] thinking
- [ ] Your optimum compared with the real design — material saved
      stated in $\text{cm}^2$ and as a percentage
- [ ] The honest paragraph: stacking, gripping, branding, the label's
      face — what the model never saw

## How it is assessed

Per [[How Marks Work]], the reasoning is the product: a modest saving
with an airtight argument and an honest accounting outranks a
spectacular claim that skipped the domain. On the due date your pair
defends the design out loud — including what "best" cost — and each of
you takes one of my questions alone.

The second piece that is yours alone is your [[Math Journal]] entry on
where your model and the real world part company, written in class on
the due date. Between them, the pair's brief and your own two
contributions are the whole of the evidence.

## Success criteria

| Quality | What it looks like in your work |
| --- | --- |
| A constraint obeyed | Volume held exactly; the shelf respected |
| One honest variable | The constraint folded in before deriving |
| A proven optimum | The critical point confirmed, not assumed |
| A fair comparison | Savings stated in units and in percent |
| Costs admitted | What the optimum ignores, said plainly |
| Yours alone | Your own note on where model and world part company, and your own answer when I put a question to you rather than to your partner |

> [!success]- If the algebra grows thorns
> Fold the constraint in *before* differentiating, not after — one
> variable is always tamer than two. And keep the domain in view:
> a cut, a radius, or a height that goes negative is the model
> telling you where its world ends.

OBSERVE — Unit 3, Day 12, the working period on the container and the

  Watch for: how the pair decides WHICH variable the constraint gets used
  to eliminate. The brief shows you only the survivor, always looking
  like the obvious choice; whether it was reasoned or whether it was
  simply the letter written first in their notes is visible for about
  four minutes and then gone forever. That decision sits inside the word
  "solve" in B2.4, and it is the half of solving a write-up never shows.
  Going well: a pair solves the constraint one way, sees the algebra turn
  ugly, says so, and switches deliberately.
  Stuck: an expression in two variables being differentiated, or numbers
  substituted into the constraint before anything is differentiated at
  all.
  Record: two columns down the class list — chose and said why, took the
  first letter to hand. One circuit of the room.

TALK — Unit 3, Day 12, at the conference already on that agenda
  Take them one at a time. Both of them will sign the same brief, and
  the brief cannot tell you which of them decided anything.
  Ask: "The pair at the next table kept a different variable from yours.
  Whose expression is going to be kinder to differentiate — and how can
  you tell before either of you does it?"
  Then, and make them commit out loud before they compute: "Is your best
  package going to come out taller than it is wide, or wider than it is
  tall? Say which, and say what makes you think so."

  Record: a tick beside each name, and the word the answer turned on.
