**The culminating task. The last eight periods of Unit 4, ending with a
public opening.** The show is the whole class's; the mark is yours
alone.

You select, prepare, install, and label your own work, and stand beside
it while people look.

## What is marked is yours, not the show's

We curate the room together and everybody's hands are on the walls. In
period 5 you take **a wall or a bay of your own**, and that is what your
mark comes from: the works you chose for it, how you prepared them, your
labels, your statement, your permissions note, how you hung it, and what
you can say about those decisions. What the class settles collectively —
the order of the rooms, the lighting, whose wall is where — is not marked
at all. That is why period 1 asks you to defend your selection out loud
rather than waiting for the show to speak for you.

## The eight periods

| Period | What it is for |
| --- | --- |
| 1 | Selection: which of your works, and why those |
| 2 | Preparation: mounting, edges, backing, and the permissions and credit note written |
| 3 | Photographing everything for the portfolio and the record |
| 4 | Artist statements drafted and workshopped |
| 5 | Curating: how the show is grouped, sequenced, and lit |
| 6 | Installation: hanging, spacing, labels, sight lines |
| 7 | Final checks, and the environmental audit of what we used |
| 8 | The opening: you stand with your work and answer questions |

## What you deliver

- **Work prepared properly for presentation** — finished, clean, mounted
  or plinthed, ready to be handled.
- **A label** for each piece: title, medium, dimensions, year.
- **An artist statement** of about 150 words that a visitor can read in
  a minute — see [[Artist Statements]].
- **A permissions and credit note**, written in period 2: anything
  referenced, and where it came from, plus one line on anything you
  decided not to show, or had to change, because of who owns it.
- **Three lines on your audience**, handed in with the labels: who this
  show is for, where you want them standing when they look at your
  work, and what would have to change if the same pieces went somewhere
  else — a library foyer, a corridor, a screen.
- **Your share of the environmental audit**, written in period 7: what
  mounting and installing your wall consumed, what you reused or found
  rather than bought, and the one thing you would do differently next
  time.

## What is marked

| Criterion | What it looks like |
| --- | --- |
| Preparation | The work is genuinely ready for an audience |
| Presentation | Your own section reads as a considered wall — height, spacing and sight lines serving the work |
| Audience | You can say who this show is for, and what would have to change to reach a different one |
| Ethics | Sources credited; nothing shown that you do not have the right to show, and your note says what you changed or left out because of it |
| Environment | Materials and waste handled responsibly, and you can say what you changed |

> [!warning] Hanging is a skill, and it is assessed
> Centre lines, consistent spacing, and labels at a readable height are
> not fussiness — they are the difference between work on a wall and an
> exhibition. We practise it in period 6 and it counts.

[[Copyright, Ownership, and Credit]] governs the ethics, and
[[Art and the Environment]] the last row of that table.

Triangulation — the evidence you will not have unless you go and get it.

OBSERVE — Unit 4, Day 17, period 6, the installation
  Watch for: where a student stands. The ones curating walk to the far
  end of the room and look back; the ones hanging stay at arm's length
  from the wall the whole period. By the opening every piece will be
  level and every label at a readable height — the day asks for exactly
  that and most of the class will manage it. The three lines they hand in
  will name a viewing distance, so this is corroboration of a
  self-report rather than a discovery; what it tells you is whether the
  distance was ever tested or only written down.
  Going well: something comes down after a look from across the room and
  goes back up somewhere else, or lower.
  Stuck: the first position is the final position, for every piece.
  Record: a tick beside anyone who moved a piece after walking away from
  it. One circuit while the hanging is happening — not during the
  rehearsal that closes the period.

TALK — Unit 4, Day 12, the studio time on that agenda, NOT the
selections item — that one is chaired and the room is listening
  Ask: "Whose work should not hang next to yours, and what would go
  wrong if it did?"
  Then: "Somebody is going to walk straight past your wall without
  stopping. Why?"
  A strong answer to the first is about the two works together and not
  about the two people: value ranges that cancel each other, two pieces
  that both want the same slow kind of looking, a scale collision that
  makes one of them look like a study for the other. Once the room is
  hung that question cannot be asked, because the answer is on the wall.
  The second question is the A3.3 one — the expectation is about the
  ways work is presented TO REACH AN AUDIENCE, and a student who can
  describe the visitor who does not stop, and say what about the hanging
  loses them, has understood something a defended selection does not
  show.
  Record: one line per student on the class list, in the period.

Do NOT try to gather either of these at the opening on Day 19. You will
be hosting, and a student standing beside their work with a visitor in
front of them is not available to you. The product evidence is the
prepared work, the labels, the statement, the permissions note, the
three lines on audience, and the environmental audit on Day 18.
