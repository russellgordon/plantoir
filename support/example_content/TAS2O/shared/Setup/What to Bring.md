The newsroom provides the cameras, recorders, and machines; you
provide the curiosity. The list is short — and on shop days it gains
four lines.

## Every class

- [ ] Your reporter's notebook (see [[Newsroom Journal]] for what
  goes in it) — yes, paper; every working reporter carries one,
  because batteries die mid-quote
- [ ] Something to write with
- [ ] Your school login details, memorised — class time spent on
  password resets is class time gone

## Sometimes, and I'll say when

- Headphones or earbuds, for editing days — one ear only when a
  partner is talking.
- Weather-appropriate layers on shoot days: the athletics beat does
  not move indoors for drizzle.
- Your own phone CAN be a capable camera and recorder on shoots —
  welcome but never required: the newsroom kit covers everyone.

## Shop days, from Unit 3

The laser cutter and the 3D printer come with a dress code, and it is
not about looks: every item below keeps something from being caught,
burned, or thrown into an eye.

- [ ] **Closed-toe shoes** — a dropped sheet of plywood or a hot offcut
  lands on feet
- [ ] **Long hair tied back**, and nothing dangling — lanyards, scarves,
  and hoodie strings come off or get tucked in
- [ ] **Sleeves that stay put** — no loose, floppy cuffs near a machine
- [ ] **Safety glasses** on whenever you are trimming, sanding, or
  pulling supports off a print — the room provides them, and they go
  on before the tool comes out

Nothing here is optional on a shop day, and nobody is sent away over
it either — spare hair ties and glasses live at the front desk.

> [!warning] The phone rule
> During desk time, phones are tools or they are away — shooting for a
> story is work; the feed is not. And nothing recorded anywhere, on
> any device, is published or posted outside our channels without
> [[Our Newsroom Standards|the standards]] being met.

Forgot something? Borrow from the front desk, return it at the bell, and
nobody will make it a thing. If gear or connectivity at home is ever a
genuine barrier, tell me quietly — see [[Getting Help]] — and we will
sort it out.
