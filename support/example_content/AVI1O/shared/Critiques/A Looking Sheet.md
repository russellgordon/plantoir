This is [[Looking Twice]] as a sheet you can print: one work and five
questions. Questions 3 and 4 have answers folded under them. Look only
after you have written your own.

![A wide Paris street in the rain, in greys and tans. A couple under one umbrella walks toward us at the right. A green lamp post rises up the middle of the picture, and wet paving stones stretch back to a wedge-shaped building.](Critiques/caillebotte-paris-street-rainy-day.jpg)

*Gustave Caillebotte, Paris Street; Rainy Day, 1877. Oil on canvas, about
2.1 by 2.8 m. The Art Institute of Chicago.*

### Question 1

Write your first reaction in one or two honest sentences, before you
analyse anything. What mood does the work carry? What puzzled you?

### Question 2

Where did your eye go first, and what put it there? Name the element doing
the work: line, shape, value, colour, texture, or space.

### Question 3

Describe what is there. Another person should be able to check each answer
against the picture.

1. What is the subject?
   > [!answer]-
   >
   > A wide city street in the rain. People walk under umbrellas; a man and
   > a woman sharing one umbrella walk toward us at the right.
2. Where are the darkest values?
   > [!answer]-
   >
   > In the clothes of the nearest figures: the top hats and the dark
   > coats.
3. What are the colours, apart from grey?
   > [!answer]-
   >
   > Tan and cream stone, a green lamp post, and one rust-red wall at the
   > right.
4. What divides the picture into a left half and a right half?
   > [!answer]-
   >
   > The green lamp post, which runs from the top edge down to the
   > pavement.

### Question 4

Choose one principle of design that is at work here and say what it does
to the way you read the picture.

> [!answer]-
>
> One possible answer. Emphasis: the couple are the nearest whole
> figures, large and dark against the pale street, so the eye starts
> there. Another.
> Repetition: the same umbrella shape comes back smaller and smaller down
> the street, and that shrinking is how you measure the distance.

### Question 5

Look again for a full minute by the clock. What did you find that you had
not seen the first time? Has your reaction from Question 1 changed, and if
so, what changed it?
