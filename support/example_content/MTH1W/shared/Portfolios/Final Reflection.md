The final reflection is the last piece of writing in this course: one
sustained look **across** your whole [[Math Journal]], written in the
final week. Every other entry looked at a single day. This one reads
the entire record and answers the only question a course can really be
measured by: *what do you leave with that you did not arrive with?*

## Before you write

Reread your journal front to back in one sitting — expect the
your earliest entries to feel written by someone else. As you read, flag:

- one entry that embarrasses you a little — that is usually where the
  growth is buried
- one "what I would try" that you actually tried, and what happened
- one conjecture you made, and what became of it

## The shape

Three sections, roughly a page, built the way [[Showing Growth]]
taught — date it, quote your own words, then show the now:

1. **How my mathematics changed.** The biggest difference between what
   the you who started this course and now-you can *do*, proven with two cited entries —
   a method gained, a kind of problem that stopped being frightening,
   a check that became automatic.
2. **How my relationship with mathematics changed.** The other axis,
   just as real: what being stuck feels like now, what you do when an
   answer smells wrong, whether "I am not a math person" survived the
   year. Your first entry is the baseline; quote it.
3. **What leaves the room with me.** Where the thinking shows up
   beyond this course — reading a phone plan the way
   [[Which Phone Plan]] taught, hearing a statistic and asking who
   collected it, estimating before committing. Name real places in
   your life, other courses, and the work you imagine doing; the
   habits are only real once they have an address outside this room.

> [!tip] Do not write it as a thank-you card
> The temptation is to perform gratitude and round everything up.
> Resist it. The reflections worth reading include the unit that never
> clicked, the conjecture that died, the fog still standing. You have
> a year of evidence that honesty is what makes this writing good.
> Trust it one last time.
