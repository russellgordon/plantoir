A newsroom publishes — which means our work leaves this room and lands
in front of real people, with real names in it. That privilege runs on
standards, and they bind everyone, including your teacher-editor.

> [!danger] The core agreement
> We never publish anything about a person that we would not say to
> their face — and nothing with a recognisable face or name in it moves
> without [[Releases and Consent|consent]]. Ever.

## What we agree to

- **Accuracy before speed.** Names spelled right, quotes checked
  against the recording, facts [[Verification Practice|verified]]
  before filing. A correction is honourable; a shrug is not.
- **Fairness.** People we cover get the chance to respond, especially
  in an [[The Investigation|investigation]]. Athletics coverage cheers
  effort without mocking anyone's worst moment.
- **Consent and dignity.** Interviews begin with who we are and where
  this will appear. Anyone can decline; some moments are not ours to
  publish even when the photo is spectacular.
- **Credit everything.** Someone else's photo, words, music, or idea —
  including AI-assisted drafts — gets named, per
  [[Copyright and Fair Dealing]]. A byline is a promise about who did
  the work.
- **Equipment comes back.** Cameras and recorders are signed out,
  carried with both straps, and returned charged with cards cleared —
  after your media is [[Backing Up Your Media|backed up]].
- **Deadlines are promises to the team.** A late story is not one
  person's problem; it is a hole on someone else's page. Trouble
  coming? Say so early — see [[Getting Help]].

## Why this is not just being nice

Every working newsroom lives by exactly these rules, because publishing
is power and standards are what make power trustworthy. Learning them
now IS learning the field — and they are why our community will keep
talking to us after the first issue.
