Before anything you write gets a mark, you look at it yourself and decide
whether it does what it's supposed to do. This is a skill, not an instinct —
which is why we practise it together the first time, on someone else's
writing, before you ever turn it on your own.

## The four questions

Whatever the task, the same four questions apply. What changes is what a
"yes" looks like for that specific piece of work — each task page names its
own success criteria; this page is how you check your draft against them.

| Question | What a "yes" sounds like |
|---|---|
| Does it do what the task actually asked? | You could point to the task's success criteria and show where each one is met |
| Would a reader who hasn't seen the task understand it? | Nothing depends on you being in the room to explain it |
| Is the weakest part something you can name? | You know which paragraph, line, or moment you'd fix first if you had one more day |
| Have you checked it against feedback you already got? | A checkpoint comment from earlier in the process actually got addressed, not just noted |

## How the first walkthrough works

The first time your class uses this page — a workshop day early in Unit 1 —
we do it together, out loud, on an anonymized sample paragraph, before
anyone touches their own draft. That order matters: judging someone else's
work is easier than judging your own, and the habit needs to be built on
easier ground first.

> [!success]- What a strong self-check sounds like, out loud
> "The task asked for two pieces of evidence and I only clearly have one —
> my second paragraph mentions a detail but doesn't explain what it shows.
> That's the part I'd fix first."
>
> Notice what this is NOT: it isn't "I think this is good," and it isn't
> "I worked really hard on this." Both of those are true things to feel and
> neither one answers any of the four questions above.

## What this page is not

This is not a peer-marking form, and nothing you or a classmate concludes
here becomes part of a mark — see [[How Marks Work]]. What it builds is your
own judgement, which is worth having whether or not anyone is grading the
result.

After the first modelled use, every task in this course asks you to use
this page again, on your own, before you hand work in — Unit 1's essay,
Unit 2's essay, Unit 3's written companion piece, and Unit 4's Independent
Culminating Study each schedule a day for exactly that.
