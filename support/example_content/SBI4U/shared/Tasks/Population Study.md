> [!abstract] At a glance
> **Format:** a data analysis plus a five-page report. **Groups of three,
> with one named section written by each of you alone.**
> **This is the culminating task**, and part of the final thirty per cent.
> Handed in on Unit 5, Day 13. **Assessed on:** all four categories.

Take a real population data set and do what an ecologist does: model it,
find where the model fails, and say what should happen next.

## Choose one

Great Lakes sea lamprey and lake trout · Ontario moose and wolf ·
zebra mussels since 1988 · Atlantic cod before and after 1992 · a local
species from NatureCounts or eBird data.

## What to produce

1. **The data**, sourced and cited, plotted properly, with the
   population's size and — where the source gives them — its density and
   its range. *Together.*
2. **A model fitted** — exponential, logistic, or predator–prey — with
   your estimates of $r$ and $K$ and how you got them. *Together.*
3. **Where the model fails**, identified from the residuals rather than
   from impressions. *The first of you.*
4. **The biology of the failure**: what the model leaves out that mattered
   here. *The second of you.*
5. **Our part in it.** What human demand, harvest, technology or
   transport put this population where it is, with a figure — from your
   own series or from a source you cite — and where that sits inside the
   footprint idea from [[Human Population and Sustainability]].
   *The third of you.*
6. **A management recommendation**: what should be done, what it costs,
   how uncertain it is, and whose decision it actually is. Written by
   whoever wrote section 5, because a recommendation that does not follow
   from our part in it is a wish. *The third of you.*

## Which part is yours

Sections 3, 4 and 5–6 are divided between the three of you and signed. Two
things carry your mark, and both are individual:

- **Your own section**, judged against its own row below and against the
  last row.
- **What I see and hear from you** in the working periods on Days 9 to 12
  and at the conference on Day 9 — which is how the first two rows are
  judged. Sections 1 and 2 are produced by the three of you together, so
  the file itself cannot tell me who fitted what; standing beside you
  while it happens can, and that is the whole reason those periods are in
  the schedule.

Nobody's mark is an average of the other two, and no row is awarded to the
group as a group. The presentation on Unit 5, Day 13 is not marked at all.
It is there so that everybody leaves having met every group's population
and not only their own.

## The standard

An honest analysis of a messy data set beats a tidy fit to a chosen one.
Real ecological data is noisy; a report that pretends otherwise is
reporting on the wrong thing.

## How this is assessed

Published at launch, on Unit 5, Day 8. Run them against your own section
with [[Judging Your Own Work]] on Unit 5, Day 12.

| Quality | What it looks like in your report |
| --- | --- |
| The data is real and traceable | Sourced, cited to something a reader could open, plotted with the gaps and the changes of method left visible rather than smoothed, and the population described as well as counted — density and range where the source has them |
| The fit is calculated, not eyeballed | $r$ and $K$ estimated by a method you describe, and the method's assumptions stated |
| The residuals are read (section 3, yours alone) | Where the model departs is identified from the residuals themselves, with the size and the sign of the departure, not from how the curve looks |
| The biology explains the departure (section 4, yours alone) | What the model leaves out is named as a mechanism operating in those years, and tied to the residuals it would produce |
| Our part is quantified (sections 5 and 6, yours alone) | The human demand, harvest or technology named, with a figure — from your own series or from a cited source — set against the population's own numbers rather than mentioned beside them |
| The recommendation is decidable (sections 5 and 6, yours alone) | Addressed to whoever can act, costed, with its uncertainty stated and the evidence that would change it named |
| Written so an ecologist would recognise it | Carrying capacity, fecundity, mortality, density and the rest used as the quantities they are, and every figure labelled well enough to stand on its own |

OBSERVE — Unit 5, Day 10, the fitting period
  Watch for: how the group treats the years the model does not like.
  Trimmed out of the series, kept and argued about, or never noticed
  because the fit was judged by looking at the curve. A finished report
  with a tidy series and a tidy fit reads exactly like an honest one that
  had nothing to trim.
  Going well: somebody saying which points they are keeping and why, and
  the residuals plotted before anybody claims the model works.
  Stuck: the axes rescaled until the departure stops showing.
  Record: three columns on the day plan — plotted residuals, judged by
  eye, trimmed the series. One pass while you are leaving your notes on
  the plots anyway.
  Note: the first criteria row does require the gaps and the changes of
  method to be left visible, so a group that trims is breaking a published
  rule. What it cannot do is show you the trimming, and that is the whole
  reason to be standing there.

TALK — Unit 5, Day 9, at the conference already on that agenda
  Ask: "Your residuals will be worst in one stretch of years. Before you
  look — which stretch do you expect, and what do you think was happening
  then that the model does not know about?"
  Then: "How would you find out whether you are right, without the model?"
  By this conference their series is already plotted — that was Day 8's
  homework — and nothing is fitted yet, so this is data being read before a
  model has told them what to see.
  A strong answer names a period and a mechanism together — a fishery
  opening, a winter, an introduction, a change in how the counting was
  done — and can say what independent record would confirm it. That is
  A1.8 heard: evidence weighed against a prediction, out loud. The report
  will contain an account of the departure written after the residuals
  were in front of them, and by then it is impossible to tell an
  explanation from a rationalisation.
  Record: two columns per group — named a period and a mechanism, or
  named neither.
