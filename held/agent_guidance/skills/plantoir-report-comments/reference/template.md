# The report comment shape of one teacher's template

The `plantoir-handouts` skill's section 13 holds one teacher's template, "Lessons students can catch up
on". This is the shape of the report card comments that teacher writes. It is offered, never applied:
the template only to a teacher who has shown the interest that section describes, and this shape to any
other teacher only as one teacher's example, when they have no convention of their own to give and ask
for a suggestion. It is never the default. A school's or board's own convention always comes first.

## The shape

- **Formal, in the past tense, about the student** ("{first name} balanced ..."), since the comment
  describes work that was done.
- **Three parts, in this order**: what the student did well, with evidence from the assessments; where
  they are still working; one next step they can take.
- **Written for a parent as well as the student**: plain words, no marking shorthand (no point IDs, no
  "K/T/C/A"), the course's own terms explained by what they mean.
- **Three options per student**, each drawing on the assessments in a different mix (the test alone, an
  assignment alone, both), so the teacher picks the one that best describes the student or blends them.
- **450 characters**, the limit in that teacher's report system, counted with the first name in.

## Three example options

Drafted from the invented marks record in Plantoir's tests (a Grade 10 science test on chemical
reactions: every formula right, a single displacement complete, oxygen miscounted in one equation, the
same evidence given twice). They show the shape; they are not wording to reuse.

```text wrap
{first name} wrote every chemical formula correctly on the chemical reactions test and predicted, wrote and balanced a single displacement reaction in full. Balancing equations is still developing: oxygen was counted as 12 atoms rather than 14 when it appeared in two products. As a next step, {first name} would benefit from adding up the atoms of each element in every product before choosing a coefficient.
```

```text wrap
{first name} showed secure knowledge of chemical formulas and reaction types, naming a single displacement reaction and predicting its products correctly. Giving evidence of a chemical change is an area for growth: a gas given off was offered twice, in different words, as two pieces of evidence. A next step is to name two different observations, such as a change of colour and a new solid forming.
```

```text wrap
On the chemical reactions test, {first name} explained in clear, original words why the mass of an open dish changed, and wrote each formula with confidence. Counting atoms across several products was the main source of lost marks. {first name} is encouraged to check each balanced equation by listing every element and its total on both sides, a habit that will support the next unit.
```
