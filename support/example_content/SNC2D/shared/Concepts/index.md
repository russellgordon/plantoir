A concept page is where an idea gets said cleanly, once, in a form you can
reread later in the course and still follow.

That is deliberately the *second* time you meet it. In this course the idea
almost always arrives first as something you did — a slide under a microscope,
a bag that refused to change mass, a lamp and two jars, a pin and a mirror.
The doing comes first because an explanation you have already half-worked-out
sticks, and an explanation handed to you cold does not. So these pages open by
naming the investigation you came from, and then they say the thing properly.

They also do the part a lab period cannot. In the room there is never quite
enough time to explain the **mechanism** — not just that the mass stayed the
same, but why it had to; not just that the image flipped, but what the rays
did to flip it. That is the Grade 10 move, and this is where it lives.

Class pages link here. These pages do not go stale, so start here when you are
studying, when you missed a class, or when a lab makes no sense afterwards.
