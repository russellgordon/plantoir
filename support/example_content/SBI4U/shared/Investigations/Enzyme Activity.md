> [!abstract] At a glance
> **Time:** one class. **Groups of three.** Three graphs, one limiting
> factor argument.

Catalase, from liver or potato, breaks hydrogen peroxide into water and
oxygen. The oxygen is measurable, so the rate is measurable.

## Safety

Hydrogen peroxide bleaches skin and clothing and is an eye hazard: goggles
and gloves throughout. Liver is biological material — gloves, and hands
washed after.

## Method

Measure the height of foam, or the volume of gas, in a fixed time.

1. **Temperature:** 5, 20, 37, 60, 80 °C.
2. **pH:** buffers at pH 3, 5, 7, 9, 11.
3. **Substrate concentration:** peroxide diluted to 5 levels.

Three trials per level. One variable at a time; the others held and
recorded.

## Analysis

1. Three graphs, each with the axes labelled and the trend drawn.
2. Where is the optimum for temperature and for pH? Explain both with
   protein structure from [[Proteins and Enzymes]].
3. Your three trials will not agree exactly — report the spread, and see
   [[Uncertainty and Error]] for what to say about it.
4. The temperature curve falls after its peak while the substrate curve
   levels off. Explain the difference — one is denaturation, the other is
   saturation, and saying which is which is the point.
5. Predict the effect of boiling the enzyme first, then test it.
