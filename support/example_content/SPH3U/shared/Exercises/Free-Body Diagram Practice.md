Draw each one before reading the answer. Label every force with what
exerts it — the rule from [[Forces and Free-Body Diagrams]].

**1.** A book resting on a table.

> [!success]- Answer 1
> Two forces: gravity down (Earth on book), normal force up (table on
> book). Equal in size because the book is not accelerating — but NOT a
> third-law pair, since both act on the book.

**2.** A box being pushed at constant speed across a rough floor.

> [!success]- Answer 2
> Four: gravity down, normal up, applied force forward, friction backward.
> Applied equals friction, and normal equals gravity, because the
> acceleration is zero in both directions.

**3.** A ball at the highest point of its flight, thrown straight up.

> [!success]- Answer 3
> One force: gravity, down. Nothing else touches it. Its velocity is zero
> and its acceleration is $9.8\ \text{m/s}^2$ downward — which is why it
> does not stay there.

**4.** A crate sliding DOWN a ramp, slowing as it goes.

> [!success]- Answer 4
> Gravity down, normal perpendicular to the ramp surface, friction UP the
> slope (opposing the sliding). Friction must exceed the component of
> gravity along the slope for it to be slowing.

**5.** A sprinter wearing spiked athletic shoes accelerates out of the starting blocks with $a = 4.5\ \text{m/s}^2$. Draw the free-body diagram for the sprinter and explain the role of Newton's third law and static friction in enabling maximum forward acceleration.

> [!success]- Answer 5
> FBD: Gravity $\vec{F}_g$ downward (Earth on sprinter); Normal force $\vec{F}_N$ upward (ground on sprinter); Forward static friction force $\vec{F}_{fs}$ (ground on shoes).  
> The sprinter applies a large backward/downward action force on the ground/blocks ($\vec{F}_{\text{foot on ground}}$). By Newton's third law, the ground exerts an equal and opposite forward reaction force ($\vec{F}_{\text{ground on foot}} = \vec{F}_{fs}$). High-friction spikes prevent slipping, converting leg power directly into net horizontal driving force $\vec{F}_{net} = \vec{F}_{fs} = m\vec{a}$.

**6.** A skydiver with total mass $80\ \text{kg}$ falls at terminal velocity, then opens a parachute. Draw two separate free-body diagrams:
- (a) At terminal velocity prior to parachute deployment ($v = 55\ \text{m/s}$).
- (b) Immediately after the parachute opens (drag force increases to $1800\ \text{N}$).
- (c) Calculate the net force and instantaneous acceleration immediately after the parachute opens.

> [!success]- Answer 6
> (a) Terminal velocity: Two balanced forces — Gravity $\vec{F}_g = mg = 784\ \text{N}$ downward, Air drag $\vec{F}_{air} = 784\ \text{N}$ upward. $\vec{F}_{net} = 0$, $\vec{a} = 0$.  
> (b) Parachute opening: Gravity $\vec{F}_g = 784\ \text{N}$ downward, Air drag $\vec{F}_{air} = 1800\ \text{N}$ upward.  
> (c) $\vec{F}_{net} = 1800 - 784 = +1016\ \text{N}$ upward. $\vec{a} = \vec{F}_{net} / m = 1016 / 80 = +12.7\ \text{m/s}^2$ upward (rapid upward deceleration slowing the downward velocity).
