Proteins do nearly everything: catalysis, structure, transport,
signalling, defence. Their monomers are twenty amino acids, and their
function follows entirely from their shape.

## Four levels of structure

1. **Primary:** the sequence, held by peptide bonds.
2. **Secondary:** helices and sheets, held by hydrogen bonds along the
   backbone.
3. **Tertiary:** the whole three-dimensional fold, held by interactions
   between side chains — including the hydrophobic clustering from
   [[Water and Life]].
4. **Quaternary:** several folded chains together, as in haemoglobin.

Change one amino acid in the primary sequence and everything above it can
change. Sickle-cell disease is a single substitution.

## Enzymes

Catalysts that lower activation energy. The substrate binds at the active
site, which is shaped for it — and shaped by the fold, which is why
temperature and pH matter so much: they change the shape.

Denaturation is that shape being lost. It is usually irreversible, which
is why a cooked egg does not uncook.

## What affects rate

Temperature, pH, substrate concentration, enzyme concentration, and
inhibitors — competitive (competing for the active site) or non-competitive
(binding an allosteric site to induce a conformational shift).
[[Enzyme Activity]] measures three of them.

## Maud Menten and enzyme kinetics

Canadian biochemist and physician **Maud Menten** (often cited as Maude Menten
in Ontario curriculum documents) co-developed the Michaelis–Menten model in
1913. Her work established that an enzyme first binds its substrate to form a
transient enzyme–substrate complex before catalysing the reaction, providing
the mathematical foundation ($V_{\max}$ and $K_m$) for modern pharmacokinetics
and enzyme biochemistry.

## Industrial and pharmaceutical applications

Enzyme specificity makes them invaluable tools across manufacturing and
medicine:
- **Food and dairy:** immobilised lactase hydrolyses lactose into glucose
  and galactose for lactose-free products; pectinases clarify fruit juices;
  chymosin coagulates milk proteins in cheesemaking.
- **Pharmaceuticals:** many therapeutics are designed enzyme inhibitors.
  Penicillin irreversibly inhibits bacterial transpeptidase to prevent cell
  wall formation; ACE inhibitors block angiotensin-converting enzyme to
  manage hypertension; protease inhibitors block viral replication in HIV.
