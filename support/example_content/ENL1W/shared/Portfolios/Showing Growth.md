Growth is invisible from the inside. It becomes visible when you put two
pieces of your own work side by side and say what changed — which is
exactly what [[The Portfolio Conversation]] asks you to do.

## The method

1. **Choose a pair.** An early piece and a late one, ideally of
   the same kind: two paragraphs about a text, two openings, two
   journal entries.
2. **Read them out loud, in order.** Out loud. You will hear things you
   cannot see.
3. **Name three differences**, concretely. Not "it is better" — *what*
   is better?
4. **Point at the cause.** What did you learn, practise, or decide that
   made that change?
5. **Name what has not changed yet.** This is the part that convinces
   anybody you are being honest, and it is the most useful part for you.

## Useful sentences

> At the start of the course I explained the quotation by repeating it. Here I say what
> the word choice does.

> My an early paragraph had one sentence length. This one has three, and
> the short one is where I want the reader to stop.

> I still start every paragraph with the same phrase. That is the next
> thing.

## What does not count as growth

"I tried harder." "I got a better mark." "I understand more." All may be
true and none is evidence. Evidence is a difference somebody else could
see in the two pieces without you explaining it.

> [!tip] Photograph your drafts
> A page of crossed-out first attempt beside the final version is the
> single most convincing artefact in a portfolio, and it takes three
> seconds to keep.
