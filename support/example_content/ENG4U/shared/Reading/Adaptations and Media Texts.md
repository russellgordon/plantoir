What we watch, and what it is for.

| Text | Form | What it teaches |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Two film versions of *Hamlet* 3.1 | Film | How a soliloquy's address changes its meaning |
| Two film versions of the closet scene | Film | Casting, blocking, and what the camera decides about Gertrude |
| A television adaptation of *The Handmaid's Tale*, one episode | Television | What a serial adds, and what it must invent |
| A documentary or essay film on surveillance or reproductive politics | Documentary | An argument built from footage, and what it leaves out |
| A trailer for any of the above | Persuasion | Thirty seconds that make a claim about the whole |

## How we watch

Never before reading the text. Always twice: once for what happens, once
with the technique table from [[Adaptation and Media]] open.

## The comparison that does the most work

One scene, two productions, side by side. The table of differences takes
fifteen minutes and produces better analysis than an hour of watching a
whole film — because the differences are where the interpretation lives.

## On the television adaptation

A serial has to invent: it needs more plot than the novel supplies, and
it can show what Offred cannot see. Ask what that does to a novel whose
whole method is a restricted, compromised testimony — see
[[Narration and Testimony]].

## Audience, perspective, and form

When evaluating these media texts, examine:

- **Audience reception**: How different audiences (e.g., theatrical vs
  television viewers, fans of the original text vs newcomers) respond to
  changes in tone and characterisation.
- **Perspectives and power**: How casting decisions, camera angles, and
  score choices construct beliefs about authority, gender, and agency.
- **Production models**: How industry constraints — from commercial broadcast
  standards to festival distribution — dictate narrative pacing.

## Models for what you make

The trailers, director's concepts, and video essays on this list serve as
mentor texts for your own creative production in [[The Adaptation Study]].
Deconstruct their topic, purpose, and audience, and note how their chosen
form and technical conventions communicate an interpretive claim.

## What it feeds

[[The Adaptation Study]], which asks you to analyse and then to make.
