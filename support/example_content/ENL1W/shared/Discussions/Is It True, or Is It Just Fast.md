## The question

If a claim is designed to travel, and you are designed to share it, whose
job is the truth?

## Start here

Everyone brings one thing they saw this week that they are not sure
about. Not something you know is fake — something you *nearly* believed,
or did believe, or still might.

We check three of them together, live, using the lateral method from
[[Finding and Judging Sources]]. Expect at least one of them to survive
the check. That matters: the point is not that everything is false.

## Positions to consider

| Position | The argument | The objection |
| --- | --- | --- |
| It is the platform's job | They built the feed, they profit from the engagement, and only they can act at scale | They have every incentive not to, and "someone else's job" is how nothing gets checked |
| It is the government's job | Some harms are large enough to need law: election lies, health lies | Who decides what is false, and what happens when they are wrong or hostile? |
| It is the sharer's job | Nothing spreads without people choosing to spread it | Individuals cannot out-check an industry built to bypass checking |
| It is the school's job | This is a skill, and skills are taught | Skills taught at fifteen have to survive thirty years of new formats |

## The uncomfortable finding

Research on this consistently finds that people who are confident they
can spot fakes are fooled *more* often — because confidence skips the
check. See [[How Misinformation Travels]]. Any position you take today
has to survive that finding, including the position you find flattering.

## Ground rules for today

- Bring the claim, not the person who shared it. No names.
- "I fell for it" is the most useful sentence available and will be
  treated with respect.
- If we cannot settle whether something is true, say so and say what
  would settle it.

## Afterwards

In your journal: the rule you will actually follow before sharing
something. One sentence, realistic. "I will check everything" is not
realistic and both of us know it.
