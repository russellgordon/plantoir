Career Studies is a **half credit — 55 hours**, which here is 42 periods
plus a two-and-a-half-hour final evaluation, running from September to
early November in the period Civics takes over afterwards. Half a credit
is still a credit on your transcript and it is compulsory for your
diploma, so the ten weeks count for exactly as much per hour as any
other course.

## The seventy and the thirty

Every Ontario credit, half or whole, is built the same way. **Seventy per
cent** of your mark comes from the work you do across the term, and it
leans towards your **most recent and most consistent** work rather than
averaging September against November. Something you could not do in the
first week and can do in the eighth is something you can do. **Thirty per
cent** comes from a final evaluation at the end: [[The Plan Defence]],
where you present [[The Pathway Plan]] and walk an adult through
[[Your Career Portfolio]].

**The seventy** is the six pieces you hand in during the term — in the
order you meet them, [[The Skills Inventory Task]], [[The Decision]],
[[The Work Investigation]], [[The Application]], [[The Budget]], and
[[The Pathway Plan]]. They are not equally weighted: the Pathway Plan is
worth several of the smaller ones on its own, because the whole course
feeds it and it asks for everything at once, and the two Unit 1 pieces
are the rehearsals that make the rest of it possible. Ask me for the
exact split whenever you want it. It is not a secret; it is a
professional judgement rather than arithmetic, and it is not the useful
thing to memorise.

**The thirty** is the defence, and it is deliberately not a written
paper. It is the one place where all of it — what you know about
yourself, what you found out, what it would cost, and what you would do
if it went wrong — has to travel at once, to somebody who has never
heard any of it before. I am in the room and I am the one who marks it;
the adult you present to is your audience and your questioner, not your
examiner.

[[Your Career Portfolio]] is not a seventh mark. It is where the
evidence lives all term and what the defence is argued from, so it is
judged once, at the end, as part of the thirty.

## Four kinds of thinking, not one

Every task asks for more than one of these, and the balance shifts with
the task. A correct list of what a program requires is never the whole
of it.

| What is being judged | What it means here |
| --- | --- |
| What you know | What is changing in a field, what a pathway requires, gross against net, where support exists |
| How you think | Weighing options against criteria you named first, testing a claim against its source |
| How you communicate | A résumé read in thirty seconds, a plan somebody could follow, an answer to a question you did not expect |
| Where you apply it | All of it, on your own circumstances, which nobody worked through in front of you |

The fourth is why Unit 3 exists. Everyone can follow a worked example of
a budget; the course is asking whether you can build the one that fits a
life nobody has costed for you.

## Three kinds of evidence, and two of them are not paper

**What you make** is the obvious one: the inventory, the investigation,
the application package, the budget, the plan. **What I watch you do** is
the second: whether you go to the source or to a summary of it, whether
you write down what a person actually said or what you expected them to
say, whether the criteria come before the options or after them. **What
you tell me** is the third — the conferences in the working periods are
not progress checks, they are evidence in their own right, and some of
what you understand will only ever show up there.

If your writing is careful and quiet, the conference is often where the
strongest evidence of the week comes from. That is not a consolation
prize. It is how the mark is meant to work.

## What earns a high mark here

- **Real information.** A sourced number beats an estimate, and a real
  posting beats an imagined one.
- **Evidence for claims about yourself.** A situation someone else could
  confirm, every time.
- **Thinking that changed.** Showing what you believed in September and
  what moved it is worth more than consistency.
- **Named unknowns.** "I could not find this out, and here is who I
  would ask" is a strength, not a gap.

## What is not in your mark

How you work is reported separately, on the same report card, as **E, G,
S or N** — six habits: responsibility, organization, independent work,
collaboration, initiative, and self-regulation. They matter, we will
talk about them, and they do not move your percentage. Your mark is
about the work; that column is about the worker, and mixing the two
tells you less about both. Nothing here is marked for turning up, for
volunteering an answer, for effort, or for how much you say.

There is one exception, it is narrow, and it comes from the curriculum
rather than from my opinion of you. Two expectations in this course are
about how a person goes about something: **A2.1** asks you to apply
decision-making strategies as you set goals and then to revise those
goals on the strength of what you learn about yourself during the
course, and **A2.2** asks you to reflect on and document that process
and say how well the strategies actually served you.

Those two are assessed in halves, deliberately. [[The Decision]] is
where you apply a strategy and set a goal, and it is handed in a third
of the way through — nothing you learn in Units 2 and 3 could revise a
goal already submitted, so it would be dishonest to say the revising is
marked there. The revising is marked at the end, in
[[The Plan Defence]], which is the only point at which enough of the
course has happened for there to be anything to revise on. A2.2 is
assessed in both. The plural in A2.1 is deliberate as well: you meet
several ways of deciding in Unit 1, you use one of them on
[[The Decision]], and in the defence you are asked which one you
actually used on your plan and where it stopped helping. What is assessed in every case is the account you
produce and that I can read, not how organised you looked while
producing it.

**A1.1** is worth saying plainly for the same reason. It asks you to
show that you understand why resilience and perseverance matter, and to
analyse how developing them would help you. It does not ask me to grade
how resilient you are, and I do not.

Your own judgement of your work, and your classmates', are not part of
your mark either. You will judge your own work against the criteria
several times — see [[Judging Your Own Work]] — and other people will
read your application and tell you what landed. Both change the work;
neither moves a number.

Practice you do between classes is practice. The reading, the searching,
the sending of a message, the three situations you think of on the bus:
they are what make the periods possible, and none of them is marked on
its own. The thinking that carries marks is done in this room, where I
can see it being done — every task in this course is launched, built and
revised in periods, and what you carry home is the finding-out that
makes those periods possible.

## Checkpoints, and the time to act on them

Every task you hand in during the term has a checkpoint before it is
marked, and a named block of time afterwards whose whole job is acting
on what the checkpoint found. ([[The Plan Defence]] is the exception,
and it is the only one: a final evaluation cannot have a revision period
after it.) How much time depends on the task, it is written on the class
page, and in a course this short it is exactly this much:

- [[The Skills Inventory Task]] gets a **whole period** — the self-check
  at the start of it, the revision for the rest, and the task handed in
  at the end.
- [[The Application]] gets a **whole period** as well, the class before
  it is handed in.
- [[The Pathway Plan]] gets **all of its fourth working period bar the
  first ten minutes**, which are the self-check.
- [[The Decision]] gets the **first half of the period it is handed in**.
- [[The Budget]] gets the **first twenty-five minutes** of the class
  after the conference.
- [[The Work Investigation]]'s checkpoint comes early, on the research
  period, so the **two working periods after it** are where you act.

That is what there is. It is on the schedule, and it is not optional.

## Late work, and second attempts

Talk to me before the deadline and we will set a different date. After
it, the conversation is harder, and eventually the mark reflects the
work I have rather than the work you meant to hand in. In a ten-week
course that happens faster than in a full-year one, which is the only
reason I press about it.

Drafts are different. Bring me a rough version of anything at any point
and I will tell you what would improve it most. That is not a favour;
it is how the course is meant to run.

> [!tip] The criteria live on the task page
> Every task page ends with what earns the marks for that particular
> piece of work, and it is there from the day the task is launched. Read
> it before you start rather than after you finish — and if a line is
> unclear, ask, because that question helps everybody.
