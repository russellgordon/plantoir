Your sketchbook is a working record, not a gallery. That single sentence
saves most people a year of misery. It is where thinking happens on paper —
rough, dated, unfinished, argued with — and a book full of neat, finished,
beautiful pages is usually one that was not used for anything.

## What goes in it

- [ ] Thumbnails — small, fast compositions, six or eight to a page
- [ ] Plans, with notes about materials and size
- [ ] Observation drawings of ordinary things, done from life
- [ ] Colour and texture tests before you commit to a surface
- [ ] Sources for anything you did not invent, written down
- [ ] Notes from a critique, and what you decided to do about them
- [ ] The attempts that failed, left in place

Those boxes are printed, not interactive — nothing here is recorded and the
site does not know who you are. Copy the list inside your own front cover.

## Thumbnails are not small drawings

A thumbnail is a compositional decision made at a size too small for detail,
which is exactly the point. At three centimetres across you can only draw
the big shapes and where they sit, so that is what you compare. Six bad
thumbnails in ten minutes settle a composition faster than an hour spent
starting the real thing three times.

## Date everything, keep the failures

Put the date on every page. It costs two seconds and it turns the book
into a record of change — by March you will be able to open it to October
and see, precisely, what you could not do yet.

Tearing out a page you dislike removes the evidence that you improved. This
course expects your portfolio to show how your thinking changed, and a
drawing that failed with a note beside it saying *the perspective is wrong
at the back and I could not fix it* is worth more than a tidy page. Write
next to your drawings: a sentence about what you were trying to do turns a
picture into a document.

## The habit

Little and often beats a panicked evening. Ten minutes a day drawing the
uninteresting things in front of you will do more for your hand than a
weekend at it. None of that is marked — [[The Sketchbook Habit]] is
assessed on the pages made in studio periods, and it says which ones —
but it is what makes those pages worth reading.

Everything in this book eventually feeds [[The Portfolio]].
[[Making Marks]] is where the loosest pages come from, and
[[Working From Reference]] is why the sources get written down.
