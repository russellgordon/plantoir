Everything for this course lives on this site, and it rewards being
used in a particular way. Five minutes here saves you an hour of
wandering later in the course.

## Start where the site starts

The landing page for your section always shows the **most recent
class** — the warm-up, the thinking task, what is due. If you were
away, that page is your first stop; if you were here, it is still the
fastest answer to "what was that condition on the discriminant again?"
Older class pages sit right behind it, one per day, so a week away is
five short pages read in order.

## Three habits that make the site fast

1. **Search first.** Press <kbd>⌘</kbd> + <kbd>K</kbd> (or
   <kbd>Ctrl</kbd> + <kbd>K</kbd>) and type any word — "vertex",
   "midpoint", a task name, a phrase you half remember. Search is
   almost always faster than browsing folders.
2. **Hover before you click.** Rest your pointer on any link — try
   [[The Vertex Form]] — and the page appears in a small preview
   window. You can check a formula mid-solution without losing your
   place.
3. **Read the backlinks.** At the bottom of every page is a list of
   every page that links *to* it. On [[Solving Systems Algebraically]],
   the backlinks show each class and task where elimination earned its
   keep — a ready-made review list nobody had to build.

## Try before you peek

Exercise pages such as [[Quadratic Graphing Practice]] fold their
answers away behind a click. That fold is load-bearing: an honest
sketch before peeking teaches more than ten confirmations after.
Predict the vertex, commit to the sketch, *then* expand — the order is
the whole trick.
