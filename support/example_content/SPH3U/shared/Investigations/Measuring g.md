Everyone is told that objects fall at $9.8\ \text{m/s}^2$ regardless of
mass. This is where you find out whether that is true in this room, with
this equipment, to the precision you can actually manage.

## Safety

Nothing heavier than 200 g, dropped from no higher than shoulder height,
over a clear floor. Feet out from under.

## Method

Two independent methods, run by the same group, then compared.

**A — Video.** Film the fall against a metre stick taped to the wall, as
described in [[Analysing Video of Motion]]. Extract position and time,
build a velocity–time graph, take the slope.

**B — Timing a pendulum.** Time 20 swings of a pendulum of measured
length $L$, then use $T = 2\pi\sqrt{L/g}$ rearranged for $g$. The factor
of 20 is what makes stopwatch timing good enough — see
[[Uncertainty and Error]].

## What to record

- Video: your table of position and time, and the graph with its slope.
- Pendulum: length, time for 20 swings, at least three trials.
- Both: your value of $g$ with an uncertainty, and the per cent difference
  from $9.8\ \text{m/s}^2$.

## Analysis

1. Which method gave the closer value, and which gave the more
   consistent one? Those are different questions.
2. Name the single largest source of uncertainty in each, and say which
   direction it would push your result.
3. A heavier object was dropped by the group beside you. Compare. Does
   mass appear anywhere in your reasoning?
