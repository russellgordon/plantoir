Before you record a single frame or a single second of audio, plan it on
paper. A storyboard is cheap to change and painful to skip — revising a
box on a page costs you a pencil; revising a finished edit costs you an
afternoon.

## Why storyboard before you shoot or record

This is the planning tool for the media component of
[[Your Canada, Your Story]], your Unit 4 Independent Culminating Study.
Whether you are making a short video essay or an audio piece, the
storyboard forces you to decide, before you touch any equipment, what the
audience sees or hears in what order, and why. A storyboard does not have
to be good art. Stick figures and boxes are enough — the plan is what
matters, not the drawing.

## The storyboard template

Copy this table for each segment of your piece. A short video or audio
essay is usually six to ten rows; more than that and you are probably
planning a longer piece than the task calls for.

| # | Visual (or what's happening on screen) | Audio / narration | Approx. duration | Notes |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | | | | |
| 2 | | | | |
| 3 | | | | |

If you are making an audio-only piece, the "Visual" column becomes sound
design instead — music, ambient noise, a pause — and the "Audio /
narration" column carries the words.

## Filling it in

Work through the table in order, but expect to go back and change earlier
rows once later ones force a decision:

1. **Sketch the opening three rows first.** How you begin decides whether
   an audience keeps watching or listening — spend real time here before
   moving on.
2. **Write the narration or dialogue for each row before deciding on the
   visual.** The words usually reveal what needs to be shown or heard
   underneath them, not the other way around.
3. **Time each row honestly.** Read the narration aloud with a stopwatch
   running, the same way you tested a paragraph in
   [[Podcast Basics - Writing for the Ear|Podcast Basics: Writing for the Ear]].
   A script that reads fine silently is often twice as long once it is
   actually spoken.
4. **Check the whole board against your topic and purpose.** Does every
   row earn its place, or is a row there because it seemed easy to film
   rather than because it serves the piece?

## From storyboard to production

Bring the finished storyboard to your production days. A teacher checking
in on your progress will ask to see it before asking to see any footage or
recording — the storyboard is what makes a two-minute production
conference possible, because it shows the plan rather than making someone
guess at it from an unfinished file.
