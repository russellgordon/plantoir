Every task page on this site publishes its criteria before the work starts,
and [[Lab Reports]] publishes the same eight rows you will meet four times.
Most people read the table once on launch day and never open it again — then
hand in something that misses two rows they could have fixed in a quarter of
an hour. This page is that quarter of an hour, and all four unit tasks give
it to you at the start of their last working period, on the class page.

We do it together the first time, on somebody else's case study, so that the
move is not new to you on the day it is your own work being judged.

## The routine

1. Put the criteria table beside your draft. Not in another tab — beside it.
2. Go down the rows. Against each one write **yes**, **partly**, or **not
   yet**, and one sentence of evidence: the paragraph, the table, the line a
   reader would point at. "Yes" with nothing after it is a hope.
3. Find your weakest row. Not the quickest to fix — the weakest.
4. Write one sentence saying what you will change, and when.
5. Change it, in the time that is left. That is why this never happens on a
   hand-in day: a judgement you have no period to act on is only a worry.

Steps 2 and 3 are the exercise. Naming your weakest row honestly is harder
than any of the fixing, because it means writing down the thing you were
hoping nobody would look at.

> [!example]- One row, judged honestly
> From [[The Systems Case Study]], the **Limits** row: level 3 *"mentions
> that more research exists"*; level 4 *"states precisely what this case
> study does not establish"*.
>
> **Partly.** My last paragraph says "more research is needed into this
> condition", which is the level 3 sentence and I could have written it
> before I started. What my study actually cannot establish is narrower: I
> found screening rates for the province but nothing for this school board,
> so I cannot say the programme reaches students here. Weakest row.
> **Change:** replace the closing sentence with that one, and move the
> provincial figure up so a reader can see what it does and does not cover.
> Fifteen minutes, and it is the paragraph I would have lost marks on.
>
> That is worth more than "looks fine" written beside all six rows, and it
> took less time.

## Why this is not part of your mark

Your judgement of your own work does not move your percentage, and neither
does a classmate's — see [[How Marks Work]]. That is a rule, and it cuts in
your favour: nothing you write on this page can cost you anything, which is
exactly what makes it worth writing honestly. A self-check performed for an
audience is useless to the only person it was built for.

What it changes is the work. A self-check that leads to one fix is the
cheapest improvement available in this course, and by the end of the course
most of you run it without being asked, which is the actual point.

> [!tip] The version that takes ninety seconds
> No time for the whole table? Read the criteria row you liked least when
> you first saw it. That is almost always the weakest one, and you already
> knew.
