## Three things worth knowing

1. **Links go both ways.** At the bottom of every page is a
   **Backlinks** list showing which pages link *to* it. Land on
   [[The Mole]] and you can see every class, investigation, and exercise
   set that used it.
2. **Hover to peek.** Hovering over a link previews the page in a small
   window without leaving the one you are on. Useful for checking one
   value or one definition mid-calculation.
3. **Search is fast.** Press <kbd>Ctrl</kbd> + <kbd>K</kbd> — or
   <kbd>⌘</kbd> + <kbd>K</kbd> — and start typing. It searches the text
   of every page, not just the titles, so searching a formula or an ion
   name finds the page that discusses it.

## The Reference folder is the one to bookmark

This course leans on a handful of tables far more than Grade 10 did. You
will open [[Polyatomic Ions]], [[Solubility Rules]],
[[The Activity Series]], and [[Significant Figures and Units]] dozens of
times between now and the end of the course — during class, during homework,
and in the middle of a lab with wet hands.

They are all short, they are all in the same place, and they are all
searchable. Learn where they are in the first week and you will save
yourself a great deal of hunting later.

## The order pages get written in

Concept pages assume you have already met the idea. That is on purpose:
in this course you do the investigation first, we compare what different
groups actually measured, and only then does the idea get its name and
its clean statement. So a concept page will often open by referring to
the lab you have just finished. If you are reading ahead and it feels
like it starts in the middle, that is why.

> [!tip] The habit that gets you unstuck fastest
> Lost on a class page? Follow its link to the concept. Lost on a
> concept page? Scroll to its backlinks and find the class where we did
> it, then read what we actually did that day. Between them, those two
> directions answer most "when did we cover this" questions in under a
> minute.
