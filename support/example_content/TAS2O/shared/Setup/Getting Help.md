Stuck is a normal working condition in technology — half this course is
learning to be stuck WELL. The order to try things in:

1. **Read your own work as a stranger.** Half of stuck is really "I
   know what I meant" — read the draft aloud, look at the photo at
   thumbnail size, play the cut without watching the screen.
2. **Check the site.** The class page says what we did; the
   [[Concepts/index|concept pages]] explain every idea; every practice
   set in [[Exercises/index|Exercises]] has worked answers folded below.
3. **Ask your desk.** Explaining the problem out loud fixes a
   startling fraction of it — see [[Getting Unstuck]] — and every
   newsroom runs on colleagues reading each other's drafts.
4. **Search like a professional.** [[Finding Answers Online]] is a
   taught skill in this course: what to type, which results to trust,
   and how to credit what you use.
5. **Ask your editor — me — in class.** Desk time is what it is for,
   and a story in trouble flagged early is a story saved.
6. **Come to a help session.** Times on [[Help Sessions]]. Bring the
   specific broken thing; "my program doesn't work" is hard to help,
   one error message is easy.

> [!tip] Name where it broke
> "My lede buries the score until paragraph four" is an edit note a
> professional would be proud of — and it is halfway to the fix.

One more thing: if the trouble is bigger than the course — time,
workload, something at home, or anything a story stirs up — an interview that went somewhere
uncomfortable, pressure from someone who does not want a story told,
anything online that worries you — tell me straight away, in whatever
way is easiest. Reporters always have editors behind them; that is
what the room is for.
