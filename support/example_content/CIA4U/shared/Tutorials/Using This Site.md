This site holds everything for the course except your own notebook. Spend
five minutes at the start of the term learning how it is laid out, because
you will be sent here several times a week and the quickest route through
it is not always the obvious one.

## What is where

Start from the schedule. It is the spine of the course: every class has an
entry, and that entry links out to whatever the class needs — a concept, a
model, a data page, a task. If you miss a day, the schedule tells you what
you missed and in what order to catch it up.

- **Concepts** — the ideas, defined once and linked from everywhere else.
- **Models** — ways of reasoning; a model is never the economy itself.
- **Data** — where Canadian figures live, and how to read one honestly.
- **Cases** — one real Canadian issue at a time, worked through.
- **Discussions** — questions economists genuinely disagree about.
- **Tasks** and **Portfolios** — what you hand in, and what you build up.
- **Tutorials**, **Setup**, **Style** — how the course and the site work.

## Getting around

Words in double brackets are links, and clicking one opens that page. The
search reads the text of every page rather than only the titles, so a
half-remembered phrase will find what a forgotten title will not. Pages
sit inside the folders above, and the folder is usually a good clue to
what kind of thing you are about to read.

> [!tip] Two habits worth forming in the first week
> Search for a phrase, not a title — "reserve ratio" will find the page
> even when you have forgotten what it is called. And follow the links
> listed at the foot of a page backwards: they show which other pages
> depend on the one you are reading, which is often the better route in.

One thing to be clear about: the site does not know who you are. Nothing
you open, search for or click is recorded against your name, and nothing
is marked here. The one thing that carries over is a box you tick on a
checklist, and only in this browser, on this computer, where nobody else
sees it. Your work lives in your notebook, not in this site. For why it is laid out this way, read
[[How This Site Is Organised]]; for what the pages themselves can do, read
[[What This Site Can Do]].
