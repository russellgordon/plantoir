> [!abstract] At a glance
> **Time:** one class, written up in the next period and handed in at the
> bell — the last of the marked [[Investigation Write-Ups]]. **Pairs at
> the bench, write-up yours alone.** You will measure a length of about
> 600 nanometres with a metre stick.

## Safety

> [!danger] Laser rules, without exception
> Below shoulder height. Never at a person, a mirror, or a window. Off
> between measurements. Anyone who points it at someone is finished with
> lasers for the semester, and that is not a threat, it is a procedure.

## Method

1. Mount the laser, the double slit, and a screen in a line. Measure the
   slit-to-screen distance $L$ as precisely as the room allows.
2. Measure across **ten** fringe spacings and divide by ten.
3. Compute $\lambda \approx d\,\Delta y / L$, with $d$ from the slide.
4. Repeat with a second slit separation.
5. If a second colour of laser is available, repeat and compare.

## Analysis

1. Your wavelength, with uncertainty, against the manufacturer's value.
2. Which measurement dominates the uncertainty? Justify with numbers, not
   impressions.
3. Halving $d$ should double $\Delta y$. Did it?
4. Explain to someone who has not taken physics why this experiment shows
   that light is a wave — in four sentences, with no equations.
