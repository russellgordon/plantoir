British Columbia's curriculum has two halves. One is the subject: dictionaries, loops, 
testing, the things this course teaches and [[How Marks Work|marks]]. The other runs through 
every course you take, from Computer Programming to Woodwork to English — the **Core 
Competencies**. They are not marked. They are reported, and you are the one who writes them.

> [!important] You write these, nobody scores them
> A self-assessment against the Core Competencies is your own account of what you can do and 
> what you are working on next. It goes into your reporting in your words. Your teacher does 
> not convert it into a number, and nothing you admit here can lower a mark.

## The three areas, in the words you would use

| The Ministry's name | What it actually asks |
| --- | --- |
| **Communication** — Communicating | Can you make somebody else understand your thinking? A commit message, a docstring, a plain-language advisory for a fire coordinator, a walkthrough of your own code at a milestone check. |
| **Communication** — Collaborating | Can you work with somebody so that the pair gets further than either of you alone? Swapping driver and navigator honestly, saying "I don't follow" out loud, giving a peer review that is usable rather than polite. |
| **Thinking** — Creative Thinking | Can you generate an approach nobody handed you? A different data structure, an interface for a user who cannot see the screen, a test nobody thought to write. |
| **Thinking** — Critical and Reflective Thinking | Can you judge your own work and change it? Tracing a loop instead of guessing, deciding a design is wrong and rewriting it, noticing what your program assumes. |
| **Personal and Social** — Personal Awareness and Responsibility | Can you manage yourself through a long piece of work? Fourteen days of a culminating project, asking for help at the right moment, keeping the journal honest on a bad week. |
| **Personal and Social** — Positive Personal and Cultural Identity | Do you know what you bring — your languages, your community, what you care about — and does it show up in what you choose to build? |
| **Personal and Social** — Social Awareness and Responsibility | Can you see who your software affects? OCAP® and community data, an accessible interface, an advisory that a frightened person can actually read. |

The Ministry's full descriptions, including the "I can" statements written for students, are 
at [curriculum.gov.bc.ca/competencies](https://curriculum.gov.bc.ca/competencies).

## How we do this here

At the end of every unit there is a self-assessment episode on the class page. The first one 
is done together: your teacher takes one competency, thinks out loud about their own work in 
front of you, and shows what a specific, honest self-assessment sounds like — because 
"I collaborated well" is not one.

After that, each unit asks you for the same three things, written into your 
[[Learning Journey Log]]:

1. **Name one competency** and the moment in this unit that is your evidence for it. A 
   specific hour, a specific bug, a specific conversation — not the whole unit.
2. **Say what you can do now** that you could not do at the start of the unit.
3. **Set the next goal**, small enough that the next unit can actually show whether you met 
   it.

- [ ] Skim all seven descriptions once, early in the course, so you know what is on offer.
- [ ] Keep your unit self-assessments together in one place in your journal — the last one 
      of the course is written by reading the earlier ones.
