## What you are making

A running record of your thinking: one short entry after every class,
written the same day while the details are warm. Ten honest minutes
beats thirty performed ones — and entries are **private**. I read
them; other students never do. The journal only works if you can admit
in it what you would not say at the boards — that the factoring left
you behind at the third example, that the sine law still feels like a
spell you recite rather than a thing you understand.

## How entries work

Each entry answers four prompts, in whatever order helps:

- **What we did** — the facts, briefly: the warm-up, the thinking
  task, where your group got to.
- **What clicked** — the moment something connected, and what did the
  connecting: a sketch, a classmate's phrasing, a smaller case.
- **What is foggy** — named precisely. "Quadratics are confusing" is
  weather; "I lose the sign when $h$ is negative in vertex form" is a
  location.
- **What I would try** — one concrete move for next class. Not "focus
  more": something you could actually do.

Conjectures are welcome any time. "I think the diagonals of every
rectangle bisect each other — [[Always, Sometimes, Never]] made me
want to prove it" is a perfect journal sentence — dated, testable,
and yours.

## Success criteria and collection

The **milestone entry** is the one that is evaluated: the last ten
minutes of each unit's collection day, written here in class, looking
back over the unit with the whole journal open in front of you. That is
deliberate — an entry that counts towards your mark is written where I
can see the work is yours and where you have the evidence to hand. The
daily entries you write between classes are the raw material for it, and
they are yours; per [[How Marks Work]], practice done at home is
practice, and I do not mark it.

The whole journal comes in alongside it, so that the milestone entry can
point at things in it. Here is what the milestone entry is read against —
[[Journal Checklist]] turns these into a self-check before handing in.

| Quality | What it looks like |
| --- | --- |
| Grounded | Claims point at dated entries, not at a general impression |
| Honesty | Real fog named, not just victories |
| Precision | Foggy things located, not just felt |
| Growth | A "what I would try" item is traced to what happened when you tried it |
