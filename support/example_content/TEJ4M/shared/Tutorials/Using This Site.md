This site is the course's memory — every class, concept, lab, practice
set, and bench habit lives here. Five habits make it fast to use, and
fast matters when you are looking something up with one hand while a
supply waits at the bench.

## The five habits

1. **Land on today.** The home page always surfaces the most recent
   class, so "what did we do?" and "what is due?" are one click from
   anywhere. If you were away, start there before asking anyone.
2. **Search first.** Press <kbd>⌘</kbd> + <kbd>K</kbd> on a Mac or
   <kbd>Ctrl</kbd> + <kbd>K</kbd> on Windows and type any word —
   "derating", "hysteresis", "aliasing", "CPOL", "watchdog". Search
   reads every page at once and is nearly always faster than
   navigating.
3. **Hover before you click.** Rest your pointer on a link like
   [[Reliability and Derating|this one]] and a preview of the target
   page appears. You can confirm a rule or a limit without leaving the
   procedure you are halfway through.
4. **Read the backlinks.** At the bottom of every page, *Backlinks*
   lists every page that links **to** this one. On a concept page that
   is an instant map of every lab, task, and practice set where the
   idea does real work — the site's web, walked backwards. It is also
   the fastest way to answer "where else does this decision show up?",
   which is a question you will be asked at a design review.
5. **Predict before you unfold.** Practice pages fold their worked
   answers away behind a callout. The fold is a tool, not a lock:
   commit to your own number first, then open it and compare. Opening
   first feels efficient and teaches nothing — the same mistake as
   switching on a supply before writing down a prediction.

## Where things live

The map of folders — Concepts, Labs, Exercises, Code, and the rest — is
on [[How This Site Is Organised]]. The full tour of what a page can
*do*, including several features this page skips, is on
[[What This Site Can Do]]. Ten minutes with those two in the first week
repays itself several times over a year from now, particularly if you end up
writing your capstone documentation in the same format.

> [!tip] The site is not a substitute for the bench
> Everything here was written after somebody measured something. Read
> the page when you need the precise statement, the limit, or the
> procedure — but the understanding arrives at the bench, and no
> amount of hovering over links will produce it.
