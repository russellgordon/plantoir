DNA and RNA are polymers of nucleotides — a sugar, a phosphate, and a
base. The differences between them are small and consequential.

| | DNA | RNA |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Sugar | Deoxyribose | Ribose |
| Strands | Double | Usually single |
| Bases | A, T, G, C | A, U, G, C |
| Job | Long-term storage | Working copies and machinery |

## Why DNA stores and RNA works

Deoxyribose lacks one hydroxyl group, which makes DNA chemically more
stable — an advantage for an archive. RNA's extra hydroxyl makes it
reactive enough to fold into shapes with catalytic activity, which is why
the ribosome's catalytic core is RNA rather than protein.

That detail is the strongest evidence for the RNA world hypothesis: the
oldest machinery in the cell is made of the less stable molecule.

## ATP

Also a nucleotide. Its energy is not "stored in the bond" in the way
textbooks once said — it is released because the products are more stable
and better hydrated than the reactants. Worth stating carefully, because
"high-energy bond" is a phrase that has misled generations.
