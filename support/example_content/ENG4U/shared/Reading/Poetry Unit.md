Ten poems across the semester, each read beside something it argues with.

| One of your own | You | The last week: bring one and defend it |

## How we read a poem here

Aloud, twice, before anything is said. Then annotated for the turn, the
line breaks, and the syntax. Then one claim each, with a line as
evidence. Then read aloud again by somebody with a different reading —
because a reading aloud is an interpretation with a body.

## What to look for at this level

- **Syntax across line breaks.** A sentence spread over eight lines is
  doing something a short one cannot.
- **The volta**, and what it costs the poem to turn.
- **Diction registers**: where a poem changes vocabulary, somebody has
  changed footing.
- **Form as argument**: a sonnet is a machine for changing its mind, and
  a poem that uses one is claiming that shape for its subject.
- **What the poem refuses to resolve** — see
  [[Ambiguity and Indeterminacy]].
