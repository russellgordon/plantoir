A CHANGING magnetic field through a coil drives a current in it. Not a
strong field — a changing one. A magnet sitting inside a coil does
nothing; the same magnet moving does.

## What controls the size

- How fast the field changes (move the magnet faster).
- How many turns the coil has.
- How strong the magnet is.

Reverse the motion and the current reverses too.

## Why almost all our electricity comes from this

Every generating station in Ontario — nuclear, hydro, gas, wind — is a
machine for spinning magnets near coils. The differences are only in what
does the spinning: steam from a reactor, falling water, expanding gas,
wind. See [[Where Our Electricity Comes From]].

## Transformers

Feed an alternating current into one coil and its changing field induces a
current in a second coil wound on the same iron core. Change the number of
turns and you change the voltage — which is how power travels the
province at hundreds of thousands of volts and arrives at your house at
120 V. Transformers only work on AC, because DC does not change.
