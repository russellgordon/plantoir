One minute, one current technology story, three questions: what is
being *claimed*, what is actually *known*, and who *benefits* if you
believe it? Technology news runs hot — every product is
revolutionary, every risk is an apocalypse — and this routine is the
thermostat. It also stocks the shelves for every discussion we hold,
from [[Who Owns Your Data]] to [[Will AI Take the Jobs]].

## How to run it

1. One student brings the story — headline and two sentences of
   summary, no more. Rotate the job daily.
2. The room answers the three questions in order. Keep claimed and
   known strictly apart; that boundary is the whole skill.
3. "Who benefits?" goes last: who is selling something, and who
   wrote the headline?
4. Park anything juicy on the board for a future discussion.

## One variation

Same story, two sources: read a company's announcement beside a news
report about it, and list what each one left out.

> [!tip] "We don't know yet" is a finding
> The strongest thing said in this warm-up is often that the honest
> answer is not known. Practise saying it plainly — it is rarer in
> technology writing than it should be.
