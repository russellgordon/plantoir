**The question:** is sustainable development a genuine way to run an
economy inside environmental limits, or a phrase that lets the same
extraction continue with better wording?

You have met the models — the three pillars, the doughnut, the UN goals,
and Indigenous frameworks that begin from obligation to the generations
after us. [[What Sustainable Development Means]] sets them out; this
seminar asks whether adopting one changes what an organization does.

## Prepare

Find **one** Canadian organization that publishes a sustainability
commitment — a mining or forestry company, a utility, a municipality, a
grocery chain. Bring the commitment, its target year, and evidence of
what happened since. A press release is a claim; a measured number is
evidence, and [[Judging a Source]] tells you which you are holding.

## Steelman both sides

**It is real.** Firms that measured emissions, water, and waste found
costs they did not know they had. Regulation, procurement rules, and
lenders now price environmental risk, so the reconciliation is not
sentimental — it is on the balance sheet. Projects once imposed now get
negotiated, and communities hold a seat they did not have.

**It is cover.** The phrase cannot be proven wrong: a target thirty years
out binds nobody now, an offset moves an accounting entry rather than a
tonne, and a company can grow production while its emissions *per unit*
fall. When every side of a dispute claims the word, it has stopped doing
work.

> [!question]- The test that separates the two (click to expand)
> Ask what the organization gave up. Sustainable development that costs
> nothing — no forgone site, no lower output, no smaller margin — is a
> description of business as usual. Find the sacrifice, or find that
> there wasn't one. Either answer is a finding.

## In the seminar

Present your organization in ninety seconds, then argue the *opposite*
side to the one your evidence supports — you will find out fast whether
you understood it. Close by naming what would have to be true, and
measurable, for you to change your verdict by the end of the course.
[[Arguing About Sustainability]] and [[Strategies and Whether They Work]]
take this further; your organization goes into [[The Resource Investigation]].
