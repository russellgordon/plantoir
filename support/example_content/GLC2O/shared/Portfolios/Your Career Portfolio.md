The portfolio is the one thing in this course that outlasts it. It is
where the inventory, the interview notes, the applications, the research,
and the plan all end up, and it is what your final evaluation is argued
from. Start it in the first week, because a portfolio assembled in the
last week is obviously a portfolio assembled in the last week.

## What goes in it

Three kinds of thing:

- **Products.** The pieces you made — inventory, profile, résumé,
  research write-ups, budget, plan. The finished versions.
- **Traces.** The rough versions, the feedback you were given, the
  posting you decided not to apply for. These are what make the products
  believable.
- **Reflections.** Short, dated notes on what changed and why. Three or
  four sentences each. Not a diary.

## Where to keep it

Somewhere you will still have access in Grade 12 — a school drive
folder, a document, a physical folder, or your Individual Pathways Plan.
Whichever you choose, choose one, and put everything in it the same day
you make it. Assembling a portfolio from six locations at the end is a
task nobody completes.

## The entries that matter most

Counter-intuitively, they are the ones about things going wrong. A
portfolio of successes shows what you were already good at. A portfolio
that includes the application that failed, what you asked afterwards,
and what you did differently next time shows something an employer or a
program actually wants to know: that setbacks do not stop you for long.

Record those the same way each time — what happened, what you did next,
what you would do differently. Over ten weeks that becomes a record of
perseverance with dates on it, which is much harder to say convincingly
in a sentence than to demonstrate in a folder.

## How it is looked at

At the end of the course you will walk someone through it in
[[The Plan Defence]]. Not read it aloud — walk through it, choosing what
to show, explaining what each piece demonstrates. Build it in the order
you would present it and that conversation gets much easier.

> [!info] Two entries per unit is the working pace
> Six to eight entries total, spread across the ten weeks. That is small
> enough to keep up with and large enough to show change over time,
> which is the only thing a portfolio can show that a single assignment
> cannot.
