The Ministry's course page carries no expectations of its own. Ontario
has rebuilt only part of its curriculum onto that platform, and none of
the three economics courses is among them — so CIA4U's page exists to
hand you the 2015 PDF, which remains in force.

Each page here holds one expectation, word for word. The codes are the
Ministry's own, and they are also tags, so every page tagged `#D2` can be
gathered in one place.

## Sixty-four expectations, eighty-six periods

Fifteen overall expectations and sixty-four specific ones, in a course
that meets eighty-six times. That arithmetic is worth doing before you
plan: several pages here address more than one code, because there is no
honest way to give each one a period of its own.

Strand B is also the only strand with four overall expectations rather
than three. Scarcity and choice, supply and demand, growth and
sustainability, and economic thought all sit in the opening strand
because everything after them uses them.

## Strand A is not a unit

Strand A's stem reads **"Throughout this course, students will:"** while
B through E say *by the end of this course*. The economic inquiry
process, the handling of data, and the concepts of economic thinking are
meant to run through every unit rather than be taught as a block in
the first week, and this course is built that way.

## What "FOCUS ON" means

Every overall expectation in strands B to E ends with a bracketed label
— `(FOCUS ON: Economic Significance; Stability and Variability)` and so
on. Those name the concepts of economic thinking the Ministry expects
that expectation to lean on: **economic significance**, **cause and
effect**, **stability and variability**, and **economic perspective**.
The labels appear here because they are part of the printed expectation.
Their definitions are not — those live in the document's Economics
introduction, printed pages 70–71, with the economic inquiry process on
72–73.

## What this folder leaves out

The document opens the course with an at-a-glance **Overview** spread —
big ideas and framing questions, strand by strand — and prints **Sample
questions** under many specific expectations. Neither is reproduced here;
this folder is the expectations themselves. Both repay an hour in the PDF
when you are planning.

## Why the codes matter more than they look

A code is a shared address. When a student asks "why are we studying
this?", the answer is a link. When a department writes a course outline,
or a teacher takes over a class partway through, the codes are what make one
person's planning legible to another.
