Everything for this course lives here, and it is built to be used in a
particular way. Five minutes now saves you an hour of hunting later in the
course.

## Start where the site starts

Your section's landing page always shows the **most recent class** — the
agenda, what we made, what to do before next time. If you were away, that
is your first stop. Class pages appear the day we reach them, not in
advance, so a page you cannot find yet is a page whose class has not
happened. Older classes sit right behind it, one per period, in order.

## Three habits that make it fast

1. **Search first.** Press <kbd>⌘</kbd> + <kbd>K</kbd>, or
   <kbd>Ctrl</kbd> + <kbd>K</kbd> on Windows, and type any word — a
   technique, a task name, half a phrase you remember. Faster than
   browsing folders, every time.
2. **Hover before you click.** Rest the pointer on a link — try
   [[The Elements of Design]] — and a preview opens without moving you off
   the page. You can check a word mid-sentence and carry on reading.
3. **Read the backlinks.** At the foot of every page is a list of every
   page that links *to* it. On [[Materials and Safety]] the backlinks are
   every class and task where it applied — a review list nobody had to
   build.

Some blocks are folded. A coloured box whose title ends in a small arrow
opens when you click it. If a page seems short, look for the arrows; there
is usually more underneath.

## Where things live

Concepts explain ideas. Techniques and Studio Time are what happens at the
table. Critiques cover looking at work, Portfolios is how your year gets
recorded, and Tasks are the assessed pieces with their criteria. The
folder-by-folder map is [[How This Site Is Organised]], and the tour of
what the pages can actually do — previews, diagrams, embedded curriculum —
is [[What This Site Can Do]].

> [!warning] The checkboxes here do not save
> Class pages list things to do with a box beside them. Those boxes are
> part of the printed page: nothing you do to one is stored, nothing is
> sent to me, and reloading returns them to how they started. Treat them
> as a list to copy into [[The Sketchbook]], not as a place to keep track.

Two directions get you unstuck. Lost in a class page? Follow it to the
concept. Lost in a concept? Use the backlinks to find the class where we
made something with it. If neither works, [[Getting Help]] is the page
that says how to reach me.
