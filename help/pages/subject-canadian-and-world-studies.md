This page lists what a Canadian and world studies website made with Plantoir can show. Each item names a page of a ready-made course where you can see it, and the Help page that says how it is done.

## The ready-made Canadian and world studies courses

Six codes have ready-made pages:

- **CGC1W**, Exploring Canadian Geography, Grade 9
- **CHC2D**, Canadian History Since World War I, Grade 10
- **CHV2O**, Civics and Citizenship, Grade 10
- **CGF3M**, Forces of Nature: Physical Processes and Disasters, Grade 11
- **CHA3U**, American History, Grade 11
- **CIA4U**, Analysing Current Economic Issues, Grade 12

To get one, choose {{menu:newCourse}}, type its code and leave {{control:newCourseSheetPrePopulateWithExampleContent}} turned on. [Make a new course](page:new-course) has the steps. Each comes with its folders, its shared pages, and class pages for every section. Five have 86 class pages in four units. CHV2O is a half credit, and has 42 class pages in three units.

The class pages are dated across the semester. All but the last are published, and the last is held back as an example of a page students do not see yet. So the first deploy may ask about classes dated later. See [Classes dated after the next class, at Deploy](page:later-classes). CHV2O is made as the second half credit of a pair with Career Studies. Its first class is dated on the 43rd school day, the day after the last class of Career Studies.

To read one without teaching from it, put it on the Reference Courses shelf. See [Bring in last year's courses](page:import-for-reference).

Any other code in geography, history, civics and politics, economics or law offers a starting point for its subject instead. In geography, for example, the folders are Concepts, Sources, Fieldwork, Discussions, Portfolios, Tasks, Setup, Style, Tutorials and Curriculum.

## What the pages show

Two of the examples below are in CHC2D only: the film, and the question students check themselves.

- **Pages written once and linked from class pages.** CHC2D's Sources pages, CGF3M's Case Studies and CIA4U's Models are each written once. Class pages link to them. See [One lesson, written once](page:one-lesson).
- **A video on a page.** CHC2D's page Photographs, in Sources, has a part named A film to practise on. It holds a film from the City of Vancouver Archives of Queen Elizabeth's visit to Vancouver in 1959, with four questions under it. Students see a YouTube player in the page. The class page for Unit 2, Day 4 links to the page.

{{picture:subject-canadian-and-world-studies-1}}
alt: The part named A film to practise on, on the page Photographs on the class website, with the film's player and the questions under it.

The film is not copied into the course. The player loads from YouTube when a student opens the page, and the film plays when the student presses play. So it needs an internet connection. The page tells students so, under the film.

On the page, the film is its address on a line of its own, in the form used for a picture:

```text wrap
![](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=Bx5wri9LHpc)
```

- **A question students check themselves.** CHC2D's page Newspapers and Propaganda, in Sources, has one under the heading Check the idea. It asks what a recruiting poster from the First World War is evidence of, and gives four choices. A student picks one and presses {{control:quizCheck}}, or presses {{control:quizShowAnswer}}. An explanation named Why appears after the student answers. Nothing a student answers reaches you. See [Questions students check themselves](page:quizzes).

{{picture:subject-canadian-and-world-studies-2}}
alt: The question on the page Newspapers and Propaganda, on the class website, with its four choices and the Check button.

- **A checklist students tick.** Every class page in all six has one, under the heading Things to do before our next class. See [Checklists students tick](page:task-boxes).
- **Diagrams drawn from text.** CGC1W's page Reading a Topographic Map, in Mapping, has one. So do CIA4U's Circular Flow, in Models, and CHC2D's Who Gets to Tell the Story?, in Discussions. See [Diagrams drawn from text](page:diagrams-from-text).
- **What a page can hold.** Each of the six has a page named What This Site Can Do, in its Style folder. It shows what a page can hold, with examples of its own.

- **Curriculum expectations and a map of them.** Each of the six has a Curriculum folder with a page for every expectation. CHC2D has the most, with 90. Lesson pages show the expectations they address, under the heading Curriculum connection. See [Curriculum expectations and the coverage map](page:coverage).

## Folders made for the subject

- **Sources** is in CGC1W, CGF3M, CHA3U, CHC2D and CHV2O. CHC2D's Sources pages are Government Records, Newspapers and Propaganda, Oral History and Testimony, Photographs, Statistics and the Census, and Where the Records Live.
- **Investigations** is in CHA3U, CHC2D and CHV2O. **Writing** is in CHA3U and CHC2D.
- **Mapping** and **Fieldwork** are in CGC1W and CGF3M. CGC1W also has **Issues**, and CGF3M has **Case Studies**.
- **Models**, **Cases** and **Data** are in CIA4U.
- **Style** holds How This Site Is Organised and What This Site Can Do. CGC1W adds Writing About Geography.
- **Tasks** is the folder whose work counts for marks.

[The folders Plantoir treats specially](page:special-folders) says what the folder for marks does.

## What these six do not use

None of the six shows a picture, has a page that prints as a handout, or has success criteria for students to tick. A page can hold each all the same. See [Pictures, PDFs and the Media folder](page:media), [A handout that prints](page:printing) and [Let students track their progress](page:my-progress).
