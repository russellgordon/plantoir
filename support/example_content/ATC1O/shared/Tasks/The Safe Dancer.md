**Due: the last week of Unit 1.** Individual. A short written piece and
a demonstration.

Dancing safely is not a warning sheet you sign — it is a set of
decisions you make about your own body, repeatedly, for years.

## What you produce

**A demonstration** (in class, three minutes): lead a partner through
three parts of a warm-up, saying what each is preparing and why the
order matters.

**A written piece** (about 500 words) covering:

1. Two movements that are **compatible with your body** and one that is
   not, with a reason based on how your body actually works.
2. What you do about the one that is not — the modification, not the
   avoidance.
3. Your own recovery practice: rest, hydration, sleep, and what happens
   when they slip.
4. The class code of conduct you would write for a studio, in five
   points.
5. **One training goal you can actually reach** by the end of Unit 2, and
   how you will know you met it. Nothing about how your body looks, and
   not "get more flexible" — something you could show me on a Tuesday.

## What is being marked

- **Accuracy** — the anatomy is right, in plain language.
- **Specificity** — it is about your body, with detail only you could
  give, not a generic one.
- **Judgement** — the modification is real and you would actually use
  it.
- **Responsibility** — your five points protect other people as well as
  you.
- **A reachable goal** — specific enough that you and I would agree, on
  the day, whether you had met it.

> [!warning] Pain and effort are not the same thing
> Effort is a burning muscle that recovers. Pain is sharp, joint-based,
> or one-sided, and it is information. Nothing in this course is worth
> dancing through pain for, and stopping is never a mark against you.

[[Body Image and Healthy Practice]] and [[The Standard Warm-Up]] are
where this comes from.

Triangulation — the evidence you will not have unless you go and get it.

OBSERVE — Unit 1, Day 12, the partner warm-up work and the working period
that follows it, where students are on their own in pairs
  Watch for: whether a dancer actually takes the modification they drafted
  the night before, when the partner work reaches something they said they
  would change, or does the full version because somebody is watching.
  C3.1 is about FOLLOWING responsible practice, not describing it, and a
  written piece can describe a modification its author has never once
  used. What you see corroborates the Judgement bullet above, which the
  writing can only claim.
  Going well: a modification taken quietly, mid-exercise, without waiting
  to be reminded of it.
  Stuck: the drafted modification never appears and the dancer pushes
  through instead.
  Not a mark, ever: a dancer who stops altogether. Go and talk to them —
  this page promises that stopping is never held against anybody, and that
  promise is worth more than the observation.
  Record: a tick beside the name on the day plan the first time you see a
  modification used. One tick is enough.

TALK — Unit 1, Day 15, at the check-in the writing period, when they
bring you their first two points
  Ask: "Show me the movement you said is not compatible. What happens at
  the point where it stops?"
  Then: "Which thing about your own body actually helps you in this room —
  not what you wish it were, what it does?"
  The first has to be danced, not described, and a strong answer takes the
  movement to the place it stops and locates what stops it — a bone
  arriving, the other side pulling, the weight sitting somewhere it cannot
  leave. That is C3.1's own example, compatibility identified rather than
  asserted, and it is the one thing the written piece cannot do: it can
  claim any limit at all. The second is C1.1 — a quality they possess that
  contributes to what they can do here — and what a student says out loud
  is less guarded than what they hand in.
  Record: one line per student on the class list.

The product evidence is the demonstration on Day 16 and the written piece
handed in on Day 17.
