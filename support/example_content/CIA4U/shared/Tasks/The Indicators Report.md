**Launched at the start of Unit 3; due in the third week of the unit.**
Individual.

A dated, sourced account of where the Canadian economy stands — written so
that a reader can tell exactly when it was true.

## What to produce

**A report of about 1,200 words with tables and at least two charts you
made yourself from retrieved data.**

Every figure carries three things: the value, the reference period, and
the table or series it came from. A report without those is not marked.

**Section one — the state of things.** Output, employment, prices. Use
Statistics Canada tables 36-10-0104-01, 14-10-0287-01 and 18-10-0004-01,
and take the most recent release available on the day you write, not the
one on any page of this site. Say where in the business cycle you think
Canada is, and defend it.

**Section two — the model.** Use aggregate demand and aggregate supply to
explain the pattern in section one, and to say what would have to change
for inflation and employment to move as policy intends. D1.2 asks
specifically for the model applied to macroeconomic aims.

**Section three — distribution.** The averages in section one hide people.
Use the Lorenz curve and the Gini coefficient (table 11-10-0134-01), and
the Market Basket Measure (table 11-10-0135-01), to say who the aggregate
picture does not describe.

## How it is marked

- **Currency of data.** The single largest category. A retrieved figure
  from the current month beats a memorised one every time, and a stale
  figure presented as current is the one error that can cost more than the
  section is worth.
- **Charts you built.** From the data, with labelled axes and a stated
  source. Not screenshots.
- **The model used, not decorated.** AS–AD has to do work in section two.
- **Distribution taken seriously.** Section three is a third of the report,
  not a paragraph of conscience at the end.

> [!tip] Write section one last
> It dates fastest. Build the charts and the argument first, then retrieve
> the numbers in the final week and drop them in. Students who write
> section one first spend the last week updating it.

Build from [[Measuring an Economy]], [[Aggregate Supply and Demand]],
[[Inequality]] and [[Reading a Time Series]].
