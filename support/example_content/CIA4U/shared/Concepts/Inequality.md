There is no single number for how unequal a country is. There are several
measures, each answering a narrower question than people assume.

| Measure | What it actually answers | Where to find it |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Lorenz curve | How far the income distribution bows away from an equal share, shown as a picture | Plotted from income distribution data; the curve, not a number |
| Gini coefficient | One number, 0 to 1, for the whole distribution's spread — market, total, or after-tax | Statistics Canada Table 11-10-0134-01 |
| Low-income cut-off | Where a family spends a much larger share of income on necessities than the average family | Statistics Canada low-income lines |
| Market Basket Measure | Whether a household can afford a specific basket of goods in its own community | Statistics Canada Table 11-10-0135-01 |

The Gini coefficient is the one to be careful with. Look it up rather
than recall it, and notice which of the three versions in Table
11-10-0134-01 you have — market, total, or after-tax income. The gap
between market and after-tax Gini is the redistribution the system does.

## What the Canadian series shows

Canada's official poverty line is the Market Basket Measure: 7.0% in
2020, 11.1% in 2023, and 11.0% in 2024 — released 29 April 2026, Table
11-10-0135-01. Composition matters more than the total. Children under 18
were at 11.5% in 2024; children in one-parent families headed by women at
29.7%, against 8.4% in two-parent families. A national rate hides that.

Trends like these drive policy in every country that measures them —
child benefits, pension design, employment insurance, tax credits — while
ageing populations, immigration and public debt compete for the same
room. Between countries the questions sharpen: marginalisation comes from
colonial legacies, control of resources, illiteracy, and market access.

Measures outside the System of National Accounts — the Human Development
Index, the Genuine Progress Indicator, happiness surveys — exist because
income leaves out health, education and time. Ask what each one drops.

The distinction driving all of it is in [[Efficiency and Equity]]; the
international version is argued in [[What Do We Owe Other Countries|What Do We Owe Other Countries?]].
