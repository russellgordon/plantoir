Your [[Tech Journal]] is collected at the end of each unit. Before you
hand it in, run this check — it is the same lens I read with, so
anything you catch here is something neither of us needs to discuss
later. Ten minutes with this list is usually worth more than one extra
entry.

## The check

**Coverage**

- [ ] There is an entry for every bench day in the unit
- [ ] Entries were written close to the day, not reconstructed the
      night before collection — reconstructed entries always show,
      because they have no numbers in them
- [ ] All six prompts appear across the unit: what I built, what I
      predicted, what the meter said, what fought back, what I
      learned, what I would try next
- [ ] Most circuit entries carry a sketch of what I actually wired,
      and at least one entry carries a photo of my own bench work

**Precision**

- [ ] Every measurement has a value, a unit, and the condition it was
      taken under — supply setting, probe points, what else was
      running
- [ ] Prefixes are right and consistent: milliamps written as mA,
      kilohms as kΩ, and nothing recorded as a bare number
- [ ] Every fault is named and located, not just felt — "the 330 Ω
      in the left branch ran too hot to touch", not "something got
      hot" (see [[What a Strong Entry Looks Like]])
- [ ] Where a prediction and a measurement disagree, the entry says by
      how much and offers at least one cause

**Honesty**

- [ ] At least one entry records something that genuinely fought back
      or confused me, not just the parts that worked
- [ ] My own contribution is named rather than hidden inside "we" —
      what *I* wired, measured, soldered, traced, or misread
- [ ] Help is credited where it happened: a partner, a datasheet, a
      forum post, an AI assistant — the way [[Our Classroom Norms]]
      asks, and for the same reason a professional cites a source

**Growth**

- [ ] Each entry ends with one concrete "what I would try next"
- [ ] At least one earlier "try next" shows up later as a thing I
      actually tried, with a sentence about how it went

> [!tip] The last two boxes are the whole game
> Anyone can write "I will be more careful with my wiring." The
> journal becomes evidence of growth on the day a later entry says
> "I checked every resistor value against the schematic before
> powering up, and found the 1 kΩ in the 10 kΩ position in about
> forty seconds" — a complete loop from intention to attempt to
> result. That loop is exactly what [[Showing Growth]] will ask you
> to point at, so spend the semester leaving yourself things to point
> at.
