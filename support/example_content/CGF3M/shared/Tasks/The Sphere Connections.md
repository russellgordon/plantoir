**Launched late in Unit 1, presented in the final week of the unit.**
Pairs. A visual explanation plus a five-minute presentation to the class.

Take one global physical feature or phenomenon and show what built it —
tracing the causal chain back through the spheres until you reach a
process rather than a place.

## Choose one, and it must be global in scale

The Pacific Ring of Fire. The Himalaya. The Sahara. The Great Lakes. The
Atlantic hurricane belt. The Gulf Stream and the climate of northwest
Europe. The tropical rainforest belt. The Canadian Shield. Take one your
partner does not already understand.

## What you produce

**A diagram** that is genuinely explanatory: a flow of causes with the
spheres identified, not a labelled picture. Hand-drawn is fine; it must
be readable from the back of the room.

**A five-minute presentation** in which each partner speaks. Assume the
audience knows the feature exists and nothing about why.

**A one-page source note** listing what you used, with dates —
[[Judging a Source]] applies here as everywhere.

## What the explanation must do

- Identify which spheres are involved and where they interact —
  [[The Four Spheres]].
- Name the process, and say what supplies its energy.
- Explain the **spatial pattern**: why here and not somewhere else, and
  what the distribution of similar features looks like globally.
- Connect back to the internal Earth where relevant, and forward to
  what living things did or now do about it.
- Where the chain reaches the inside of the planet, say where that heat
  and that structure came from in the first place. The core and the
  mantle are the early Earth's own doing, and the outer spheres arrived
  in an order — [[The Four Spheres]] has the sequence.

## How it is marked

| Criterion | What earns the marks |
| --- | --- |
| Knowledge | The mechanism is correct, and reaches the internal Earth where the chain leads there |
| Thinking | The pattern is explained, not just asserted, from the mechanism |
| Communication | Diagram carries the argument; both partners can answer questions |
| Application | Connections between spheres are specific, not decorative |
| Your own half | The links you present hold up on their own, and you answer the question afterwards without your partner |

## Two people, two marks

You build the diagram together and you present together, and you are
marked separately. Your mark comes from the section of the mechanism you
present and from your own answer to the question that follows it.
Nothing here is a shared mark, which means a partner who does less does
not cost you anything, and a partner who does everything does not carry
you.

The signed note on the back of the sheet is not marked and cannot move
anybody's grade. It exists so that I know which part of the chain to
read as yours before I start.

> [!question] The question you will be asked afterwards
> "Where else on Earth would you expect to find this, and why?" Prepare
> an answer. If your explanation is a real mechanism it will generate a
> prediction; if it is a description it will not, and the question will
> find that out. See [[Plate Tectonics and What It Builds]] and
> [[Climate and Its Controls]] for how a mechanism generates a pattern.

Triangulation — the evidence you will not have unless you go and get it.

OBSERVE — Unit 1, Day 21, the diagram workshop
  Watch for: a pair that has cut the feature in half, one of them owning
  the early end of the chain and the other the late end, with nobody
  crossing the join.
  The sheet comes out whole either way, because both halves are drawn on
  it, and on presentation day each partner speaks about their own half,
  so the split never has to surface.
  Going well: they argue about the middle of the chain, and one of them
  redraws an arrow the other put there.
  Stuck: two conversations at one desk and a blank strip down the centre
  of the paper.
  Record: the pairs list, one letter each — J for joined, S for split.
  Ten seconds a pair, and it tells you whom to question on Day 22.

TALK — Unit 1, Day 21, at the ninety-second checkpoint already on that
  agenda
  Ask: "Take your partner's half of the chain and explain it to me
  instead of your own." A strong answer is slower than their own half
  and still gets the mechanism out. A pair who cannot do this has two
  explanations rather than one, whatever the diagram shows.
  Then, for a feature the interior built: "If the mantle stopped
  convecting tomorrow, which parts of this would still be here in a
  million years?" A strong answer separates what the inside is still
  building from what the surface is taking apart — that is B3.2, the
  outer spheres set against the internal ones, and a sheet of arrows
  will not tell you whether they can do it.
  Record: on the same pairs list, a tick for the swap and one word for
  the counterfactual.

The product evidence is the diagram and the presentation on Unit 1,
Day 22. Those arrive on their own.
