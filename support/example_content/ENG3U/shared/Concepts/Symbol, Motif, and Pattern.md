A **symbol** carries meaning beyond itself. A **motif** recurs and
accumulates. A **pattern** is any repetition a reader can track: images,
sentence shapes, structures, silences.

## Track it, then interpret it

Do not decide what blood means in *Macbeth* and then hunt for
quotations. Build the table first:

| Where | Form | Who | What it means there |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 2.2 | "a little water clears us of this deed" | Lady Macbeth | Guilt as something washable |
| 5.1 | "will these hands ne'er be clean?" | Lady Macbeth | The same image, inverted, and she is asleep |

Two rows, one reversal, one paragraph. A motif that changes meaning is
the text arguing with itself, and that is always where the essay is.

## In prose

In [[Monkey Beach]] track water, the b'gwus, and what Lisamarie is told
not to talk about. In "The Boat", track the room, the door, and the
mother's hands.

## Patterns that are not symbols

- **Structural**: a chapter that always ends on a question.
- **Syntactic**: a character who never finishes a sentence.
- **Social**: who is interrupted, and by whom, every time.

These are often stronger essay material than symbols, because fewer
students look for them.
