> [!abstract] At a glance
> Solo reporting with a peer edit desk · seven desk periods across two
> weeks, early in the term · one short story, at least two sources,
> published with your name on it

## What you are making

A short, real news story from school life — an event that happened, a
change that matters, a person worth knowing. Not an essay about news:
actual news, reported with at least two sources, written in the
inverted pyramid, edited by classmates at a peer desk, and published
with your byline where our readers will see it.

The floor is low on purpose — a clean two-hundred-word story about the
new bell schedule is a complete success. The ceiling is high on
purpose too — a story nobody else noticed, told so well it travels.

## How to work

1. Find a story and test it against [[News Values]] — would someone at
   this school stop scrolling for this? Pitch it to me in one breath.
2. Report it. At least two sources: two people, or a person plus a
   document. Prepare and record conversations the way [[Interviewing]]
   shows, and get consent on the record.
3. Write it in the inverted pyramid — the most newsworthy thing first,
   as [[The Inverted Pyramid]] explains, in the plain public voice of
   [[Writing for Publication]].
4. File it properly — [[Filing a Story]] covers naming, folders, and
   what "done" means at our desk.
5. Sit the edit desk. Your story gets a peer edit, and you edit
   someone else's — both halves are the task. An editor marks up the
   draft, never the mark: what I evaluate is what you did with what
   they found.
6. Correct, polish, and publish. Byline on.

The desk periods are class time, and that is deliberate: the work
I evaluate is made in this room, where I can watch it being made and
you can ask. What you do between classes is practice, and practice is
never the thing being marked — [[How Marks Work]] explains why.

## Success criteria

| Quality | What it looks like in your story |
| --- | --- |
| A real story | It passes the reader test, and you can say why |
| Two sources | Both are named in the story and in your notes |
| Inverted pyramid | The lead carries the most newsworthy thing first |
| Accuracy | Names spelled right, quotes checked against the recording |
| Consent | Every named or pictured person agreed on the record |
| Credit | Anything not yours — words, images, help — is named |
| Visible editing | The published version shows the desk made it better |

## Reflect

After publication, write a [[Newsroom Journal]] entry: what changed
between your first draft and the published story, and who changed it?
The distance between those two versions is the learning.

> [!success]- If you cannot find a story (click to expand)
> Stories hide in changes. What is different at school this month — a
> schedule, a menu, a team, a room, a rule? Find the change, then find
> the person it affects most, and ask them what it is like.

  Watch what a writer does with the buried-lede note in the first two

  Record: three columns down the margin of your day plan — moved /
