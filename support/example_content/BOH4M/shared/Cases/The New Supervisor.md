Priya has been a dispatcher at a regional courier company for four years
and was the fastest one on the floor. In September she was promoted to
supervise the eight dispatchers she used to sit with.

By November her manager notices three things. Dispatch errors are down —
Priya catches them herself, often by redoing the work at the end of a
shift. Two experienced dispatchers have stopped bringing her problems.
And Priya has missed both of the last two planning meetings with the
depot managers, because the floor was busy.

Priya's own account: "I know the work better than anyone here. If I let
it go wrong I'm the one who answers for it."

> [!note] How we work a case
> Read it once for the story, then again with a pen. Facts on one side,
> inferences on the other. The discussion starts from the facts — see
> [[Reading a Business Case]] if this is your first one.

## Questions

Under each question a note is folded away, saying what a strong answer
includes. Work the question before you open one.

### Question 1

Which level of management is Priya working at, and which level is she
being paid for? Use [[Levels of Management]] and be specific about the
time horizon of the work she is choosing.

> [!answer]-
>
> She is paid for first-line management: getting this week's work done
> through these eight people. She is working below that, at the work
> itself, redoing individual dispatches at the end of a shift. The
> horizon she chooses is this shift. The one she skips, the planning
> meetings, is the weeks ahead.

### Question 2

Priya's technical skill is the strongest on the team. Explain, using
the levels, why that is now part of the problem rather than the
solution.

> [!answer]-
>
> Technical skill matters less at each level up, and working through
> other people matters more. Because she can fix any error herself, she
> does, so the errors stay hidden from the people who made them, nobody
> else gets better, and her time goes to dispatching instead of to the
> eight dispatchers.

### Question 3

What has her behaviour taught the two dispatchers who stopped raising
problems?

> [!answer]-
>
> This one is an inference, so say so, and tie it to a fact. One reading:
> that she will redo the work whatever they say, so raising a problem
> changes nothing. Another: that a problem brought to her is a job taken
> away from them. Either way, the fact underneath is that she corrects
> the work herself.

### Question 4

Her manager plans to send her on a two-day time-management course.
Using the schools in [[Schools of Management Thought]], say what
assumption that solution makes and whether you agree with it.

> [!answer]-
>
> The course assumes the problem is how efficiently one person works,
> which is a classical answer: study the work and train to the standard.
> A behavioural reading says the problem is between Priya and her team:
> she does not yet hand work over. A systems reading adds the missed
> planning meetings, where her choice reaches the depots. A strong
> answer names the assumption and then tests it against the facts of the
> case; whether you agree is yours to argue.

### Question 5

Write the two sentences you would say to Priya first, if you were her
manager.

> [!answer]-
>
> No fixed answer. Strong ones start from something in the case that
> both people would accept as true, such as the fall in errors or the
> missed meetings, and not from a judgement of her character.
