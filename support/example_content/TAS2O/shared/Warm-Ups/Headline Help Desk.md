A limp headline goes up on the screen — invented, but true to life —
and the room becomes its help desk. The patient is not sick, just
boring: no verb doing work, no specifics, no reason to read on. The
medicine never changes — stronger verbs, sharper facts, honest but alive.

## How to run it

1. Post the limp headline and one line about what the story says.
2. Two quiet minutes — everyone writes a rewrite that stays true.
3. Hear five. For each: what got stronger, and did it stay honest?
4. Vote for the one that would actually run, and say exactly why.

> [!example]- One patient, three treatments (click to expand)
> The story: gym flooding forced the semi-formal to move to May.
> Patient: *Student Council Discusses Dance Issues*
> 1. *Council votes to move semi-formal to May* — a real verb and
>    a real decision have arrived.
> 2. *Flooded gym pushes semi-formal to May* — now the cause leads,
>    and "pushes" carries the whole story.
> 3. *You Won't BELIEVE What Happened to the Dance* — livelier, and
>    disqualified: it teases instead of telling.

## One variation

Reverse the desk: post a strong headline and have the room wreck it —
drain the verb, blur the specifics. Subtraction teaches faster.

> [!tip] The verb is the headline
> Read only the verbs in your last three headlines. "Discusses",
> "holds", and "is" mean nothing happened. In a good headline
> something HAPPENS, and it says so plainly — the drill behind
> every round of [[Headline Practice]].
