## Three things flagged when these pages were written

**`Cases/The Tariff Year` rests on a fast-moving file.** Canada's trading
relationship with the United States is being renegotiated in public and
in stages. Read the page yourself the week before you teach it and check
what is actually in force; the page is written to be worked that way, but
it will still say things that have been overtaken. That is the lesson,
not a defect — but it is a lesson that only works if you have checked
first.

**`Tutorials/The Economic Inquiry Process` spells it "Gather and
Organize" on purpose.** That is the Ministry's own printed component
name, from the Economics introduction to *The Ontario Curriculum, Grades
11 and 12: Canadian and World Studies, 2015*. It is quoted, not written.
Do **not** "correct" it to Canadian spelling — the rest of the payload is
Canadian throughout, and this one word is deliberate.

**The pages name live data series instead of quoting current values.**
`V39079` for the target overnight rate, `FXUSDCAD` for the exchange rate,
CPI and Labour Force Survey tables by number. Students are meant to go
and get the number themselves and write the retrieval date beside it. If
you edit these pages, keep the series names and resist the urge to paste
in a value to be helpful — a stale figure on a course site teaches
exactly the habit this course is trying to break.

## Which CIA4U this is

Built from *The Ontario Curriculum, Grades 11 and 12: Canadian and World
Studies, 2015*, unrevised. Five strands, fifteen overall and sixty-four
specific expectations.

Note for the citation-minded: **"110 hours" is not printed in the
curriculum document.** What is printed is that each course has a credit
value of 1. The 110-hour equivalence comes from Ontario's secondary
schools policy, and the pages attribute it that way.

## How the schedule is laid out

Twenty-two periods each for Units 1 to 3, twenty for Unit 4. Inside Unit
4: eight content periods, then the eight named culminating periods, one
reflection period, and three review periods before the examination.

Strand A is not a unit. Its stem is *throughout this course*, so the
inquiry process, the data-handling pages, and the concepts of economic
thinking recur in every unit rather than sitting in a block in September.
The models work the same way: supply and demand is built in Unit 1, comes
back as a labour market and a housing market in Unit 2, and is set
against aggregate supply and demand in Unit 3. If you reorder anything,
keep those returns.

## Why The Trade Question lands where it does

It is handed in on Unit 4, Day 8, in the second week of the unit rather
than the third. The eight culminating periods begin on Day 9 and should
not be interrupted by another deadline. If you lengthen Unit 4, add the
days before Day 9, not after — the culminating ends the course.
