# The marking rulings of one teacher's template

The `plantoir-handouts` skill's section 13 holds one teacher's template, "Lessons students can catch up
on". These are the marking rulings that go with it. They are offered, never applied: only to a teacher
who has shown the interest that section describes, and each ruling that changes a mark only after the
teacher says yes. For any other teacher, ask the open question when a paper raises one of these, and
mention a ruling here only if the teacher asks for a suggestion. Each has the reason the teacher who
made it gave.

## Marks

- **Each mark point is 0 or 1.** One reason per mark, so every lost mark can be explained.
- **Benefit of the doubt, with the reason stated** on the sheet. A student given a mark should know why.
- **Follow-through after a slip.** Later work that is right for the student's own earlier answer earns
  its marks: the slip is counted once.
- **A syntax slip costs half a mark once per test**, taken at its first occurrence and named without a
  deduction after that. It is one habit, not many faults.
- **A small slip (a missing label, a wrong letter in a name) costs half a mark once**, never carried
  onto a point already lost.
- **The type rule is separate**: a wrong type is a gap in understanding, not a slip.
- **Handwriting forms are handwriting**: capitals, spaced names and `≥` written by hand are read for
  what they mean.
- **Meaning over punctuation.**
- **Spelling is never a deduction.** It may be noted, in words that say it did not change the mark.

## The sheet

- **Levels with steps** (`4++`, `4+`, `4`, `4-`, `4--`, down to `1`) on the teacher's percentage scale,
  where the course reports levels.
- **Colours on a feedback sheet**: green for what was done well, orange for what is still missing or
  not quite right, black for what is noted and did not change the mark ("Noted, but it did not change
  your level."). An orange item names the gap in a bold one-sentence headline, gives its evidence, and
  ends with one short prompt of about fifteen words, never the fix and never next steps.
- **Never cite an earlier sheet's item numbers**; say it in words.

The colours and the orange item's shape are this teacher's way of giving feedback. Many teachers give
descriptive feedback with next steps instead; that is their call, and the default sheet does not
assume either.
