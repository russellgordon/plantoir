The energy released when a nucleus changes — and the reason Ontario's
grid looks the way it does.

## Fission vs. Fusion

- **Nuclear Fission**: A heavy nucleus (such as $\ce{^{235}_{92}U}$) absorbs a thermal neutron, splits into two lighter daughter nuclei (e.g., $\ce{^{141}_{56}Ba}$ and $\ce{^{92}_{36}Kr}$), and releases $2\text{--}3$ fast neutrons along with roughly $200\ \text{MeV}$ of energy. Ontario's CANDU reactors use unenriched natural uranium moderated by heavy water ($\ce{^2_1H2O}$ or $\ce{D2O}$, containing deuterium isotopes).
- **Nuclear Fusion**: Two light nuclei (such as hydrogen isotopes deuterium $\ce{^2_1H}$ and tritium $\ce{^3_1H}$) fuse under extreme temperature and pressure to form helium ($\ce{^4_2He}$) and a neutron, releasing immense energy per nucleon without long-lived high-level radioactive waste.

## Where the energy comes from: $E = mc^2$

The total mass of the reaction products is slightly less than the initial mass of the reactants. This mass defect ($\Delta m = m_{initial} - m_{final}$) is converted directly into thermal and kinetic energy according to Albert Einstein's mass-energy equivalence:

$$E = (\Delta m)c^2$$

Because $c^2 \approx 9.0 \times 10^{16}\ \text{m}^2/\text{s}^2$, destroying even one gram of matter yields $9.0 \times 10^{13}\ \text{J}$ of energy (equivalent to burning over 2 million litres of gasoline).

## Radiation, half-life, and Canadian contributions

- **Alpha ($\alpha$)**: Helium-4 nuclei ($\ce{^4_2He^{2+}}$), high mass and ionizing power, stopped by a sheet of paper or skin.
- **Beta ($\beta$)**: High-speed electrons ($\beta^-$) or positrons ($\beta^+$), moderate penetration, stopped by a few millimetres of aluminium.
- **Gamma ($\gamma$)**: High-energy electromagnetic photons, high penetration, requiring thick lead or high-density concrete shielding.
- **Half-life ($t_{1/2}$)**: The time required for half the radioactive nuclei in a sample to decay: $N(t) = N_0\left(\frac{1}{2}\right)^{t/t_{1/2}}$. Applications include medical diagnostic imaging (e.g., Technetium-$99\text{m}$, cobalt-60 therapy) and archaeological radiocarbon dating ($\ce{^{14}C}$).

**Canadian Contributions**:
- **Harriet Brooks** (McGill University) was Canada's first female nuclear physicist. Working with Ernest Rutherford, she identified the recoil of radioactive nuclei and discovered radon gas, proving that radioactive decay transmutes one chemical element into another.
- **Louis Slotin** (Winnipeg-born physicist) conducted critical assembly experiments with fissile cores during the Manhattan Project.

Ontario gets roughly half its electricity from nuclear generation, which
makes this a local question rather than an abstract one. See
[[Where Our Electricity Comes From]].
