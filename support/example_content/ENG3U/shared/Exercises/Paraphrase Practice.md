Method on [[Reading Shakespeare]]. Paraphrase first, then unfold.

**1.** *"If it were done when 'tis done, then 'twere well / It were done
quickly."*

> [!success]- Answer 1
> If the deed were finished the moment it was committed, then it would be
> best to commit it fast.
> Note what a paraphrase loses: the repetition of *done* four times in
> two lines, which is the whole psychological point.

**2.** *"Look like the innocent flower, / But be the serpent under't."*

> [!success]- Answer 2
> Appear as harmless as a flower, but be the snake hidden underneath it.
> Ask who is giving instructions here, and what that tells you about the
> marriage.

**3.** *"I have no spur / To prick the sides of my intent, but only /
Vaulting ambition, which o'erleaps itself / And falls on th' other."*

> [!success]- Answer 3
> I have nothing driving me on except ambition itself, which overreaches
> and falls on the far side.
> Macbeth is his own horse and rider here. The metaphor collapses
> mid-sentence, which is the point: he cannot finish it, and he does not
> finish the thought either.

**4.** *"Out, damned spot! Out, I say! One, two: why then 'tis time to
do't."*

> [!success]- Answer 4
> Get out, cursed mark! Out! One o'clock, two o'clock — well then, it is
> time to do it.
> She is counting the bell from the night of the murder. The scene is in
> prose; ask what that changes, per [[Shakespeare's Language]].

**5.** *"Life's but a walking shadow, a poor player / That struts and
frets his hour upon the stage / And then is heard no more."*

> [!success]- Answer 5
> Life is only a moving shadow, a bad actor who postures anxiously for
> his brief time on stage and then is never heard again.
> Two metaphors in one breath — shadow, then actor. Ask why the second
> arrives, and whether it is a recognition or the failure of one.

**6.** Take fifteen lines from the act your group is seminaring and
paraphrase them in the right-hand column. Mark the two you could not do.

> [!success]- Answer 6
> The marks are the point. Bring them; fifteen lines with two marks is a
> better period than forty lines skimmed.
