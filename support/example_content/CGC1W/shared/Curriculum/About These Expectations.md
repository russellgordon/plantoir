**This course replaced CGC1D and CGC1P.** If you find a Grade 9
geography resource that talks about *Issues in Canadian Geography*, it
was written for the 2018 courses, and the expectations it cites are not
these ones. The strands are similar in shape and different in wording,
which is exactly the kind of difference that is easy to miss.

Each page here holds one expectation, word for word, and nothing else.
Every lesson, source, fieldwork page, and task in this course links back
to the codes it addresses, and the codes are tags, so every page tagged
`#C2` can be gathered in one place.

## The codes are the Ministry's own

Unlike some older curricula, this one prints its codes: `A1`, `B1.3`,
`E2.5`. The overall expectations also carry printed titles — `A1` is
"Geographic Inquiry", `C2` is "Sustainability and Economic Development" —
and those titles are used as the page names here.

## Strand A works differently

Strand A's stem is **"Throughout this course, students will:"** while
B through E say *by the end of this course*. That is not a typographical
accident: geographic inquiry, geospatial technology, the concepts of
geographic thinking, and the transferable-skills work are meant to run
through every unit rather than be taught as a block. This course is
built that way — you will meet Strand A expectations in the first week
and in the last.

## The concepts of geographic thinking

The Ministry names four — **spatial significance**, **patterns and
trends**, **interrelationships**, and **geographic perspective** — and
tags each strand with the ones it leans on. CGC1W names them but does
not reprint their definitions; its links point back into the 2018
document that this course replaced. So the explanation you will find on
[[The Concepts of Geographic Thinking]] is written in plain words for
this course rather than quoted, and only the four names are the
Ministry's.

## Why the codes matter more than they look

A code is a shared address. When a student asks "why are we doing this?",
the answer is a link. When a department writes a course outline, or a
teacher takes over a class partway through, the codes are what make one
person's planning legible to another.
