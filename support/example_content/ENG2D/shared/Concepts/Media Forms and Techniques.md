A media text is anything made to communicate to an audience: an
advertisement, a trailer, a front page, a music video, a documentary, a
feed. They can be read as closely as a play, using the same questions
plus some of their own.

## The five questions

1. Who made this, and what did they want to happen?
2. Who is it for? Audiences are chosen before anything is made.
3. What is the message — stated and implied?
4. What techniques carry it?
5. What is left out?

## Techniques

| Technique | What it does |
| --- | --- |
| **Framing and angle** | Low angle empowers; high angle diminishes; close-up demands feeling |
| **Colour grade** | Warm for nostalgia and safety; cool for control and distance |
| **Editing pace** | Fast cuts excite and prevent thought; long takes ask for patience |
| **Sound and score** | Tells you how to feel before the image does; silence is a technique |
| **Typography** | A serif claims tradition; a heavy sans claims urgency |
| **Composition** | What is centred is the subject; what is at the edge is an argument |
| **Casting** | Who is allowed to represent what |
| **Absence** | Who is not there, and what is never mentioned |

## Form matters as much as content

The same claim behaves differently as a headline, a thirty-second
advertisement, a documentary segment, and a post. The form sets the
audience's expectations, the time available, and what counts as
evidence — which is why [[The Media Deconstruction]] asks you to make media
texts as well as take one apart.

## Documentary is not neutral

A documentary chooses what to film, what to cut, whom to interview, and
what music to put underneath. It can be honest and it cannot be neutral;
those are different words. Ask what a documentary would look like if made
by somebody with the opposite conclusion.
