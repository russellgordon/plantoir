A work record is what a tradesperson writes so that somebody else — an
inspector, a client, next year's crew, or their own future self — can
tell what was done. In this course it is how most of your practical work
is assessed.

## What a record contains

| Field | Why |
| --- | --- |
| Date and site | Conditions on the day change what happened |
| What the job was | One line, in plain words |
| What you actually did | Steps, in order, with quantities |
| Materials used | Species, sizes, amounts — as used, not as planned |
| Measurements achieved | What the tape said at the end, not what the drawing said |
| Problems and changes | What went wrong and what you did about it |
| Time | How long it took, honestly |

## Write it the same day

A record written on Friday about Tuesday is a guess. Ten minutes at the
end of a working period is worth an hour of remembering later, which is
why every period here ends the same way — clean, store, record. See
[[How This Course Works]].

That is also the entry I mark. Records are assessed from what you write
at the bench or on the site, on the day the work happened. When the
"before our next class" list asks you to check your log, that is a
reminder to make sure it happened, not an instruction to write it up at
home — see [[How Marks Work]].

## Say what happened, not what should have happened

The difference between a record and a report card:

- *"Planted 24 shrubs"* — a record.
- *"Planted 24 shrubs; 3 had damaged root balls and were held back for
  the next order; ground was wetter than expected on the north end so
  spacing was widened"* — a record someone can use.

The second one is worth more marks and takes twenty extra seconds.

> [!tip] Photographs belong in the record
> Before, during, and after, with the date. On a build or a planting they
> are the only evidence left once the job is finished — see
> [[Photographing Your Work]].

Your records feed straight into [[The Evidence File]], which becomes the
final evaluation.
