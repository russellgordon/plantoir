**The question:** a country cannot commemorate everything. Choosing what
to remember is therefore also choosing what to let go. On what grounds
should that choice be made, and who should make it?

Commemoration takes many forms, and they do not all do the same work.

| Form | What it does | What it costs |
| --- | --- | --- |
| A monument | Fixes one version in stone, in a place people pass | Land, money, and permanence — it is hard to revise |
| A day | Recurs, so each generation meets it again | Competes with every other day for attention |
| A name on a building | Puts a person in daily use | Ties an institution to a reputation that may change |
| A register of names | Restores individuals to a number | Requires records that were often never kept |
| An apology | Puts an admission in the official record | Can close a subject rather than open one |

The National Centre for Truth and Reconciliation's National Student
Memorial Register is the fourth kind: a public list of children who died
as a result of the residential school system. The NCTR reported in 2025
that it holds more than 4,000 named and unnamed children, and that the
work is ongoing. It commemorates by naming, which is only possible where
a name was recorded.

## Prepare

Find one commemoration within reach of this school — a cenotaph, a
plaque, a street, a school name, a day marked here — and answer three
questions: who put it there, when, and what argument were they making?
[[Commemoration and Public Memory]] gives you the vocabulary and
[[Historical Significance]] gives you the test.

## Positions

**A debt.** The dead are owed remembrance whether or not it teaches
anyone anything. Judging a memorial by its usefulness misunderstands it.

**A teaching tool for the living.** Commemoration shapes what a country
believes about itself, so it should be judged by what it teaches now.
That reasoning licenses renaming and removal, which is why it is
contested.

**A finite resource.** Attention, money, and public space are limited.
Every commemoration displaces another, and the displaced one is usually
the history of people with less standing.

**A substitute for action.** Remembering a wrong can feel like
addressing it. Ask, of any commemoration, what changed afterwards.

into [[The Commemoration Inquiry]].
