Characters are not people, and treating them as people is the fastest way
to a weak essay. They are constructions doing work in a text — and asking
what work is the analytical move.

## Four questions

1. **What does this character make visible** that could not be shown
   otherwise?
2. **What do they want, and what does the text think of that wanting?**
3. **Who are they placed beside?** A foil is an argument by comparison.
4. **What are they denied?** A character with no soliloquy, no name, or
   no last scene has been denied it on purpose.

## Worked example

Banquo hears the same prophecy as Macbeth and does nothing. That is not a
personality note; it is the play controlling a variable. Everything
Macbeth does after Act 1 has to be explained by something other than the
prophecy, because Banquo proves the prophecy alone is insufficient.

Write that sentence and you have a thesis.

## In prose

In [[Monkey Beach]], ask what Ma-ma-oo makes possible that no other
character can — knowledge held and transmitted on terms the narrator has
to earn. Then ask what the novel is arguing by putting that knowledge in
an elder rather than in a book.

## Avoiding the two traps

- **Psychologising.** "Macbeth has low self-esteem" is unfalsifiable and
  not in the text.
- **Moralising.** "Lady Macbeth is a bad person" ends the analysis at the
  point it should start.
