**Temperature** is how fast the particles are jiggling, on average.
**Thermal energy** is the total of that jiggling. **Heat** is thermal
energy on the move, from hotter to colder.

A cup of tea and a swimming pool can be at the same temperature; the pool
holds far more thermal energy.

## Warming something up

$$Q = mc\Delta T$$

where $c$ is the specific heat capacity — the joules needed to raise one
kilogram by one degree. Water's is unusually large
($4180\ \text{J/kg}^\circ\text{C}$), which is why coastlines have mild
climates and why a hot water bottle beats a hot rock.

## Changing state

$$Q = mL_\text{f} \qquad Q = mL_\text{v}$$

Melting and boiling take energy at CONSTANT temperature. The thermometer
sits still while the energy goes into breaking bonds rather than speeding
particles up — which is why ice water stays at $0^\circ\text{C}$ until
the last ice is gone.

## Why this belongs in an energy unit

Because heat is where energy goes to become unrecoverable. Every
efficiency figure in [[Efficiency and Power]] is really a statement about
how much energy left as heat.
