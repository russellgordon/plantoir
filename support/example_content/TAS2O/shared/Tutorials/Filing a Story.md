"Done writing" and "published" are different countries, and filing
is the border between them. A story is **filed** when it sits in
the right place, under the right name, with everything an editor
needs to move it; **published** when it survives the editor pass
and goes in front of readers. You control the first; the newsroom
controls the second — the daily walk through
[[The Production Pipeline]].

## Where drafts live, and what to call them

Drafts live in your desk's folder on the team drive — never only
on a device in your bag ([[Backing Up Your Media]]). Name files
date, slug, stage — `2026-02-12-keeper-save-draft`, then `-filed`,
then `-final`. The slug is two or three words a stranger could
match to the story; `final-FINAL-v2` is how work gets lost.

## The filing checklist

- [ ] **Headline** — a working one, honest about what the story is
- [ ] **Lead** that passes [[The Inverted Pyramid]]
- [ ] **Image** chosen, with a **caption** naming who, what, and
      when, and a **credit** naming who made it
- [ ] **Consent** confirmed for every recognisable face or name, per
      [[Releases and Consent]]
- [ ] **Quotes** checked against the recording, names spelled right
- [ ] **Credit** on anything not yours, per
      [[Copyright and Fair Dealing]]

## The editor pass

Filed work goes to an editor — sometimes me, sometimes a classmate
on the edit desk — who reads it as a stranger would: where they
stopped, what they doubted, what a reader would still ask. Bouncing
back once or twice is not a verdict; it is the system working, the
muscle [[How the Newsroom Runs|edit and compare]] builds daily.

> [!tip] Filed on time beats perfect and late
> Per [[Our Newsroom Standards]], deadlines are promises to the
> team. A filed story with a flagged weakness is workable; a
> perfect story that misses the page leaves a hole on it.
