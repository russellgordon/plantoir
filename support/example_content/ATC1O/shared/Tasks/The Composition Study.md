**Due: the end of Unit 2.** Groups of three or four. 90 seconds to two
minutes.

A short dance composition made from a shared stimulus, revised at least
once after feedback.

## The process, which is marked as much as the product

1. **The stimulus** is given to the whole class — the same one for
   everyone. What you do with it is yours.
2. **Generate more than you need.** Groups that keep the first idea
   almost always show the weakest work in the room.
3. **Structure it** using something from [[Structures for Making]].
4. **Try it two ways** — with sound and without, or fast and slow — and
   keep the better one, with a reason. That experiment is required.
5. **Show it, take feedback, revise it.** The version you are marked on
   is the second one.

## What is marked

The piece is made by the group; the mark is yours. Four things are yours
alone: the **material you brought**, logged in your own journal and dated
on Unit 2, Day 9; the **reason for the version you kept**, written in the
studio in the last ten minutes of Day 11; what you say in the **minute
with me on Day 14**; and **your own dancing** in the assessed run. A
group that carries you cannot lift your mark, and a group that falls
apart cannot lower it.

| Criterion | What it looks like |
| --- | --- |
| Composition | A clear structure a viewer can follow |
| Communication | The stimulus is legible in the movement, without explanation |
| Experimentation | You can name what the two versions did differently, and what that told you |
| Revision | You changed something specific after the mid-point showing, and can say what and why |
| Your material | The movement you brought is in your journal, dated, and you can point at what became of it |
| Performance | Your part of the assessed run is danced full-out and accurately |

> [!tip] Keep both versions in your journal
> The version you abandoned is evidence of your thinking, and it is worth
> marks later in [[The Portfolio and Reflection]]. Film both — see
> [[Video of Yourself]].

[[With Music and Without]] is where the required experiment comes from,
and [[Devising in a Group]] is how the group work is supposed to run.

OBSERVE — Unit 2, Day 11, the required experiment, when each group runs

  Watch for: whether the second version is danced or walked. A group that
  has already decided marks the version it does not want through at half
  energy, and then reports an experiment it never ran. A2.3 asks for
  experimentation that enhances the piece's communicative power; the run
  you assess on Day 15 is one version, so this is the only day it is

  Going well: the second run is full-out and something in the room changes

  differently.
  Stuck: two of the four watching the clock while the other two mark it
  through.
  Record: one word per group on your day plan — "both" or "one".

TALK — Unit 2, Day 14, in the revision period, catching each dancer on
their own
  Ask: "Which note from yesterday's showing did your group decide not to
  take — and what would it have cost you?"
  Then: "What have you changed since Day 13 that nobody asked you to
  change?"

  lost by taking it, and then points at the ones they did take. That is
  A4.1 heard — applying feedback is a set of decisions rather than a
  transcription — and the assessed run shows you only the revisions that
  survived, never the ones weighed and refused.
  Record: a line per dancer on the class list — per dancer, because the
  mark is theirs.
