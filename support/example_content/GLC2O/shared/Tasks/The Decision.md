**Unit 1. Individual. Handed in half-way through Day 14, at the end of
the revision block.** About two pages, plus whatever working you did.

You make one real decision using a named strategy, document the process
as you go, and then say honestly how well the strategy served you.

## Choosing the decision

It has to be genuinely undecided and genuinely yours. Good candidates:
which Grade 11 courses to pick, whether to take a part-time job, whether
to try out for something, whether to keep doing an activity you have
outgrown, how to spend the money you have saved.

Not suitable: a decision you already made and are writing up backwards.
Everyone can tell, including you.

## What to hand in

1. **The decision**, stated in one sentence, and why it matters.
2. **The strategy you chose** from [[How You Actually Decide]], and why
   that one suited this decision.
3. **The working** — the criteria, the list, the map, the notes from the
   conversation. Rough is fine; this is evidence, not a display.
4. **Who you consulted**, and what each of them changed about your
   thinking. "Nothing" is an acceptable answer if it is true.
5. **The decision you reached**, and the reasoning behind it, written
   the day you reached it.
6. **A goal** that follows from the decision, written in the four-part
   form from [[Setting a Goal You Will Keep]].
7. **The review** — how well the strategy worked, what you would use
   next time, and what you now know about how you decide.

## What earns the marks

- **Strategy used, not named.** The working shows the method actually
  being applied, not a method mentioned in the introduction.
- **Honest criteria.** What matters to you appears, including the
  reasons that are not impressive.
- **Consultation with consequence.** You can point to something someone
  said and what it changed.
- **A goal that can be checked.** Specific, dated, with a first step.
- **A real review.** The strategy's weaknesses are named, not just its
  successes.

> [!question]- What if the decision is still not made by the due date?
> Then hand in the process to that point and say where it has stopped,
> and why — the information you are missing, the conversation you have
> not had, the date you have set to decide by. A documented unfinished
> decision with a named obstacle is worth full marks here. An invented
> conclusion is worth much less, and is usually obvious.

OBSERVE — Unit 1, Day 13, on the walk round you take before the

  Watch for: whether the working is being made or being illustrated. The

  way; what the page cannot tell you is when it was produced. A2.1 asks
  them to APPLY a strategy, and applying it looks like a mess that

  Going well: something on the desk gets struck out — a criterion
  dropped, an option added that they plainly dislike, a rank reordered.
  Stuck: a clean page with nothing crossed out, and a student who can

  Record: a tick, a dot, or a blank beside each name on your day plan.

TALK — Unit 1, Day 14, during the revision block at the start of the period
  Ask: "Read me your criteria in the order they matter. Which one moved
  after you first wrote it down, and what moved it?"
  Then: "Who did you not ask, and why not?"
  The first asks them to explain a decision they have already taken, and
  it is the fastest test of A2.1 there is: a ranked list that never
  changed is nearly always a list written backwards from an answer. The
  second is A2.2's "identifying areas where more work may be needed", and
  it is franker out loud than on the page, where the person who was
  avoided tends not to appear at all.
  Record: two things beside each name — the criterion that moved, and the
  person they did not ask. Both fit in five words.

The product evidence is the two pages and the working, handed in
half-way through Day 14.
