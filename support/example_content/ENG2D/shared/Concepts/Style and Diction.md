**Diction** is word choice. **Syntax** is sentence construction.
**Style** is the pattern the two make, in a writer's work and in yours.

## Reading for style

Ask four questions of any passage:

- **What kind of words?** Concrete or abstract, plain or Latinate, formal
  or colloquial.
- **How long are the sentences, and how varied?**
- **What is the ratio of verbs to adjectives?** Verb-driven prose moves;
  adjective-driven prose describes.
- **What is repeated?** Sound, structure, and word.

## Writing with style rather than about it

| Instead of | Write |
| --- | --- |
| *It is evident that Shakespeare utilises* | *Shakespeare gives* |
| *In today's modern society* | (delete, start with the point) |
| *very important* | the specific claim: *decisive*, *load-bearing*, *the hinge of the scene* |
| A string of eight-word sentences | Vary: one long, one short. The short one is the point |

## Register

Formality is a choice you make about a situation, not a level you are
stuck on. The register of a seminar, an essay, a news report, and a text
message should all be different, and moving between them deliberately is
a skill this course practises — see [[The Literacy Test]] for two of them
side by side.

## Reading your own draft for style

Read it aloud. Mark every sentence you had to take a second breath in.
Mark every sentence that begins the same way as the one before. Those two
marks will improve a draft more than an hour of adding adjectives.
