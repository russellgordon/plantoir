## Verse, prose, and who gets which

Blank verse is the default for nobles; prose belongs to servants, to
madness, and to comedy. A character who switches has changed state — and
in *Macbeth*, Lady Macbeth's sleepwalking scene is in prose, after four
acts of verse. That change is the scene's first argument.

The witches speak in rhymed trochaic tetrameter, a metre nobody else
uses. When Macbeth's own lines begin to echo their rhythm, the play is
saying something it never states.

## Imagery clusters

Shakespeare works in fields of imagery rather than isolated metaphors. In
this play: blood, sleep, clothing, light and dark, and the unnatural.
Track one across five acts — the method is on
[[Symbol, Motif, and Pattern]] — and the essay writes itself.

## Rhetoric worth naming

| Device | What it does |
| --- | --- |
| **Antithesis** | Balanced opposites: "fair is foul, and foul is fair" |
| **Equivocation** | Saying something true that misleads; the play's central obsession |
| **Apostrophe** | Addressing the absent: "Come, you spirits" |
| **Anaphora** | Repetition at line openings; builds and traps |
| **Stichomythia** | Rapid alternating single lines — pressure made audible |

## The soliloquy

The audience is trusted with the inside. Ask, every time: what is being
worked out here that could not be said to another character, and what has
changed by the last line? A soliloquy that ends where it began is a
character stuck; most of Macbeth's do not.
