**Launched mid-Unit 2, due at the end of the unit.** Small groups of
three. A written audit of about 1200 words, with a one-page summary a
non-specialist could act on.

Audit how one community actually prepares for one hazard — not how it
should, how it does — and assess whether that preparation would work.

## Why this is possible to do properly

Under Ontario's *Emergency Management and Civil Protection Act*, every
municipality must maintain an emergency management programme: an
emergency plan adopted by by-law, annual training and exercises, public
education, a designated Community Emergency Management Coordinator, and a
Hazard Identification and Risk Assessment. Much of this is public. Start
with your municipality's own emergency plan and its HIRA, then the
conservation authority for flood, and the provincial or federal agency
for the hazard itself.

## What the audit must cover

1. **The hazard**, physically characterised, with a frequency estimate
   and a source for it.
2. **Monitoring and prediction.** What technology and which agency
   watches for this — gauges, seismographs, Doppler radar, satellites,
   drought indices, fire danger rating — and how much warning it buys.
3. **The warning chain.** Who decides, how the message reaches people,
   and what proportion of the population it plausibly reaches. Alert
   Ready, sirens, phone trees, door-knocking, or nothing.
4. **Adaptation and protective measures** already in place: structural
   and non-structural, per [[Hazard, Exposure, Vulnerability]].
5. **Response and recovery**: evacuation routes and capacity, reception
   centres, mutual aid, and what happens if the primary route is the
   thing that fails.
6. **A comparison** with one other community facing the same hazard, in
   Canada or elsewhere, and one measure you would import from it.

## How it is marked

| Criterion | What earns the marks |
| --- | --- |
| Knowledge | Technologies and measures described accurately, with what each cannot do |
| Thinking | Effectiveness assessed against stated criteria, not asserted |
| Application | Real documents used; gaps named as gaps |
| Communication | The summary page is genuinely usable by someone in a hurry |
| Your own sections | The two components you signed for stand up to the four rows above on their own, and the comparison answer is in your words |

## Three people, three marks

You work as a group of three and you are marked one at a time. Split the
six components so that each of you signs for two of them, and put the
names on the contents page. The comparison in component 6 is written
separately by each of you — same community, same hazard, your own choice
of the measure you would import and your own reason for it. The criteria
you write down before researching are the group's; what you did with them
is yours. Nothing on this task carries a common mark.

> [!tip] Assess against criteria you write down first
> Decide before you research what "effective" means here — lead time
> achieved, proportion of people reached, cost per household, whether
> the most vulnerable are covered — and then measure against it. An
> audit that arrives at "quite good" with no yardstick has not audited
> anything.
