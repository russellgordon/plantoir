[[Our Newsroom Standards]] makes the promise: nothing with a
recognisable face or name in it moves without consent. This page is
how that promise gets kept in practice — when to ask, how to ask,
and what to do with the answer.

## When consent is needed

- **A recognisable person is the subject.** If a reader could name
  them, they get asked — in a photo, a video, a quote, a story.
- **Minors.** Almost everyone we cover is one. Anything beyond an
  incidental appearance also needs a parent or guardian's yes on the
  release form.
- **Private and semi-private spaces.** Classrooms, hallways outside
  a change room, someone's home. A public game is public; a quiet
  moment in a corridor is not, even if you were standing in it.

The athletics line: a wide shot of a cheering crowd treats the crowd
as the subject — no single person is singled out. A close-up of one
fan's face makes that person the subject, and they get asked. Same
stands, different photograph, per [[Composition and Framing]].

## How to ask

Before recording, or right after — never at publication time. Say
who we are, what we are making, where it will appear, and that no is
a fine answer: "I'm reporting for the school news site — could I use
your name and this photo in a story that will be published online?"
A yes gets captured on the release form — name, date, what was
agreed, where it may appear — and the signed form is filed with the
story's media, per [[Filing a Story]].

## When someone declines

Thank them and mean it. The story survives: shoot wider, quote
someone else, describe without naming. Declining is not an obstacle
to route around later — some moments are not ours to publish even
when the photograph is spectacular, which is the whole argument of
[[Whose Story Is It]]. A newsroom that takes no gracefully is a
newsroom people keep saying yes to.
