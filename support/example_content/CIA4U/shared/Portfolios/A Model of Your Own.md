Once in this course you build a model instead of using one. It is small
on purpose, and it is about a decision you actually face — a job against
more study hours, renting against staying home, one phone plan against
another. The point is not the answer. The point is that you must state
what you assumed to get it.

## What you produce

Three short sections, no more than a page in total.

**The decision, stated as a trade-off.** Not "should I get a job" but
"what do I give up per hour worked, and what do I gain". Put both sides
in the same units — dollars a month, or hours a week — so they can be
compared at all.

**The assumptions, listed.** Every one of them, including the ones that
feel too obvious to write. Constant wage. Constant hours. Marks respond
to study time in a predictable way. Nothing changes at home. The moment
you write these down you will see which ones you do not believe.

**Where it breaks.** Name the assumption that fails first and say what
happens to your conclusion when it does. A model whose author can say
where it stops working is worth more than one presented as the truth.

> [!example] A worked one, kept deliberately small
> **Decision.** Take eight hours a week at the Ontario general minimum
> wage of \$17.60 an hour — rising to \$17.95 on 1 October 2026, per the
> province's announcement of 1 April 2026 — or keep those eight hours
> for study. **In the same units:** about \$140 a week before deductions,
> against eight study hours. **Assumptions:** the shifts really are eight
> hours and do not move; those hours would otherwise have been spent
> studying rather than scrolling; an hour of study raises marks by the
> same amount whether it is the first or the eighth. **Where it breaks:**
> the third assumption, immediately — returns to study hours fall off, so
> the first hours given up cost more than the last. And the wage rises in
> October, which changes one side of the comparison and not the other.

The reasoning underneath is in [[Opportunity Cost]]; the model you will
have met first is [[Supply and Demand]]. What this piece is judged on,
and when it is due, is on [[The Notebook and Portfolio]].
