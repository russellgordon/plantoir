This page lists what a Mathematics website made with Plantoir can show. Each item names a page of a ready-made Mathematics course where you can see it, and the Help page that says how it is done.

## The ready-made Mathematics courses

Six codes have ready-made pages:

- **MTH1W**, Mathematics, Grade 9
- **MPM2D**, Principles of Mathematics, Grade 10
- **MCR3U**, Functions, Grade 11
- **MHF4U**, Advanced Functions, Grade 12
- **MCV4U**, Calculus and Vectors, Grade 12
- **MDM4U**, Mathematics of Data Management, Grade 12

To get one, choose {{menu:newCourse}}, type its code and leave {{control:newCourseSheetPrePopulateWithExampleContent}} turned on. [Make a new course](page:new-course) has the steps. Each comes with its folders, its shared pages, and between 84 and 86 class pages in four units for every section. The class pages are published and dated across the semester, so the first deploy may ask about classes dated later. See [Classes dated after the next class, at Deploy](page:later-classes).

To read one without teaching from it, put it on the Reference Courses shelf. See [Bring in last year's courses](page:import-for-reference).

Any other Mathematics code offers a starting point for the subject instead. Its folders are Concepts, Thinking Tasks, Exercises, Discussions, Portfolios, Tasks, Setup, Style, Tutorials and Curriculum.

## What the pages show

Several of the examples below are in MPM2D. It is the only one of the six with a GeoGebra sketch, a drawn diagram, a graph students can move, or a handout that prints.

- **Mathematics, typeset.** All six write their mathematics between dollar signs. MPM2D's class page for Unit 2, Day 9 writes `$x^2 + y^2 = 25$`, and its page What This Site Can Do has a part named Mathematics, typeset properly. See [Mathematics and chemistry notation](page:notation).
- **Chemistry notation.** MHF4U's page Logarithmic Scales, in Concepts, writes pH with `$\ce{H+}$`. The same Help page covers it.
- **A GeoGebra sketch.** MPM2D's page The Slope-Angle Conjecture, in Explorations, holds a sketch of three right triangles that share an angle, which a slider sets. See [GeoGebra sketches](page:geogebra).

{{picture:subject-mathematics-1}}
alt: The GeoGebra sketch on the page The Slope-Angle Conjecture, open on the class website.

- **A drawn diagram.** MPM2D's page Similar Triangles, in Concepts, shows two similar triangles with the lengths of their sides. Similar Triangles Practice and The Slope-Angle Conjecture have diagrams too. See [Diagrams and graphs](page:figures).
- **A graph students can move.** MPM2D's page Transformations of Parabolas, in Concepts, graphs `y = x^2` and `y = -2(x - 1)^2 + 3` together. The same Help page covers it.

{{picture:subject-mathematics-2}}
alt: The graph of two parabolas on the page Transformations of Parabolas, on the class website.

- **A handout that prints, with its answers on a page of their own.** MPM2D's page Similar Triangles Practice, in Exercises, has seven questions, each with its answer folded under it. See [A handout that prints](page:printing).

{{picture:subject-mathematics-3}}
alt: The page Similar Triangles Practice printed as a handout, with its questions on one sheet and its answers on another.

- **A checklist students tick.** Every class page in all six has one, under the heading Things to do before our next class. See [Checklists students tick](page:task-boxes).
- **A program shown on a page.** MTH1W's Coding folder has five pages. One of them, Simulating Dice, shows a Python program for students to read. See [Show code on a page](page:code-blocks).
- **Diagrams drawn from text, tables and coloured boxes.** Each of the six has a page named What This Site Can Do, in its Style folder, which shows them with examples from Mathematics. See [Diagrams drawn from text](page:diagrams-from-text) and [Tables, boxes and footnotes](page:tables-boxes-footnotes).

{{assistant}}
- **Curriculum expectations and a map of them.** Each of the six has a Curriculum folder with a page for every expectation. Its lesson pages show the expectations they address, under the heading Curriculum connection. See [Curriculum expectations and the coverage map](page:coverage).

## Folders made for the subject

- **Number Talks** is in all six.
- **Coding** is in MTH1W.
- **Data Sets** is in MDM4U, with pages such as Where to Find Real Data and Cleaning Messy Data.
- **Style** holds How This Site Is Organised, What This Site Can Do and Writing About Math. In MDM4U the last is Writing About Data.
- **Tasks** is the folder whose work counts for marks.

[The folders Plantoir treats specially](page:special-folders) says what the folder for marks does.

## What these six do not use

None of the six has a question that students check themselves. A page can hold one all the same. See [Questions students check themselves](page:quizzes).
