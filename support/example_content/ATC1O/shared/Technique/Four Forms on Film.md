Some of these forms you have now had in your body for a period or two, and
the rest are coming. Here is each one in the body of someone who has had it
for years. Each film is a lesson or a demonstration, so you see the
vocabulary built up slowly, the way you met it — not a finished performance
where the work is hidden.

Do not try to watch all four in one sitting. Pick one, watch the first five or ten minutes,
and answer the questions under it in your journal before you start the next.

## How to watch

These are the questions [[The Critical Analysis Process]] uses to name a
form, and they work on any of the four:

- Where is the **weight** — down into the floor, or lifted away from it?
- What does the **spine** do — held long, curved, tilted, bouncing?
- What are the **feet** doing, and is the foot pointed, flexed, or flat?
- Where is the **rhythm** — in the music, in the feet, or in the breath?

Describe first. Whether you like it comes later, and counts for less.

## Ballet

A beginner class for adults from Canada's National Ballet School, in
Toronto. It is a whole class, close to an hour, with barre work and then
centre work. Watch the first ten minutes at the barre.

![](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=TjQB0qN4jaE)

- Watch the standing leg, not the moving one. What is it doing the whole time?
- What does the teacher keep reminding you about? Name the part of the
  body.

## Jazz, at its roots

The Charleston is one of the African American social dances of the 1920s
that jazz dance grew out of. This is a short lesson from the Kennedy Center,
in Washington.

![](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=yXDpR-MthLo)

- Which counts carry the accent? Clap them, then check against the feet.
- What stays loose while the legs work?

## Hip hop

Breaking is one of the founding dances of hip hop, from the Bronx in the
1970s. A teaching artist from Seattle works through top rock, the standing
steps a breaker opens with, and then footwork on the floor.

![](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=hb4tT3Ie_B8)

- Find the groove: the bounce or rock that carries on under every step.
- Which moves stay standing, and which go to the floor? What gets the
  dancer from one to the other?

## Bharatanatyam

A film on *chaari*, the ways of moving the legs and feet, published by
India's National Council of Educational Research and Training.

![](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=UGOYHsRFSTo)

- Draw the shape the legs make when the dancer is still.
- Where does the sound of the rhythm come from?

## After you have watched two

Put two forms side by side and write three lines: one thing they share, one
thing each insists on that the other does not, and which one your own body
found harder — you already know that from the studio.
[[Forms From Around the World]] has the list of what forms tend to share;
check yours against it.
