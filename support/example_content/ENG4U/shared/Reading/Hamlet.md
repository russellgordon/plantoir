> [!abstract] At a glance
> **William Shakespeare**, around 1601 · the longest of the plays · read
> across nine weeks, aloud, and seminared act by act

> [!warning] What this text deals with
> Grief, a young man's contemplation of suicide, a drowning that may be
> one, surveillance, and the treatment of Ophelia by every man in the
> play including the one who claims to love her.

## Before we start

**The play is about proof.** The vocabulary of evidence, testing, and
seeming runs through it from the first scene. Read the delay as an
epistemological problem before you read it as a psychological one — see
[[Structure and Delay]] — and then argue about which reading the text
supports.

**Laertes is the control.** Same situation, immediate action. Any account
of Hamlet's delay has to survive him.

**It is long, and we will not read every line aloud.** The class page
each day says which scenes are read in full and which in summary.

## What to track from Act 1

| Thread | Watch for |
| --- | --- |
| Proof and seeming | "Seems, madam? Nay, it is" — count the play's tests |
| Ears and poison | The murder method, and how corruption is described |
| Acting | Players, performance, and the question of whether feeling can be shown |
| Women's speech | How often Gertrude and Ophelia speak, and who interrupts them |
| Delay | Every postponement, and the reason given for it |
| Death and the body | From the ghost to the graveyard |

## Questions we will argue about

1. Is the delay temperamental or epistemological — and what would count
   as evidence?
2. Is the ghost reliable? The play gives evidence three ways.
3. Is Hamlet's madness feigned, real, or feigned and then real?
4. What does the play do with Ophelia, and what does it let us see about
   the men around her?
5. Why does the play end with a foreign prince arriving to inherit
   everything?

## The work it feeds

[[The Hamlet Seminar]], [[The Lens Essay]], and half of
[[The Comparative Essay]]. Annotate for all three from the first scene.
