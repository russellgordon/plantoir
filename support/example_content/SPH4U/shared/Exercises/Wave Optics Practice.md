**1.** Two slits $0.25\ \text{mm}$ apart give fringes
$4.2\ \text{mm}$ apart on a screen $1.60\ \text{m}$ away. Find the
wavelength.

> [!success]- Answer 1
> $\lambda = d\Delta y/L = (2.5\times10^{-4})(4.2\times10^{-3})/1.60 = 6.6 \times 10^{-7}\ \text{m}$,
> or 660 nm — red.

**2.** What happens to the fringe spacing if the slits are moved closer
together?

> [!success]- Answer 2
> It increases: $\Delta y = \lambda L/d$, so smaller $d$ means wider
> spacing. Counter-intuitive until you see it in the equation.

**3.** A grating with $600\ \text{lines/mm}$ is lit with
$532\ \text{nm}$ light. Find the angle of the first-order maximum.

> [!success]- Answer 3
> $d = 1/600\ \text{mm} = 1.67\times10^{-6}\ \text{m}$;
> $\sin\theta = \lambda/d = 0.319$, so $\theta = 18.6^\circ$.

**4.** Why does a soap film show colours while a window pane does not?

> [!success]- Answer 4
> The film's thickness is comparable to the wavelength of light, so the
> reflections from its two surfaces interfere. A pane is thousands of
> wavelengths thick, so the effect washes out.

**5.** A ray of unpolarized white light passes through a linear polarizing
filter, is transmitted through a glass prism producing angular dispersion,
and then illuminates a narrow single slit to project an interference
pattern onto a distant screen. Use precise wave terminology to define the
physical processes of polarization, dispersion, diffraction, and nodal
lines in this apparatus, and explain how polarization proves light
oscillates as a transverse wave rather than a longitudinal wave.

> [!success]- Answer 5
> Polarization restricts the oscillation of the transverse electric field
> vector $\vec{E}$ to a single plane; longitudinal waves oscillate parallel
> to propagation and cannot be polarized. Dispersion separates polychromatic
> light into its component frequencies because the refractive index $n(f)$ of
> the prism varies with wavelength. Diffraction is the bending and spreading
> of wave fronts around the edges of the slit aperture into the geometric
> shadow. Nodal lines are regions of complete destructive wave interference
> where waves arriving from different portions of the slit oscillate
> $180^\circ$ out of phase ($\Delta \phi = \pi$), resulting in zero net
> electric field amplitude and dark fringes.

**6.** A soap bubble film ($n = 1.33$) in air is illuminated normally by
white light. (a) Explain using reflection phase changes and optical path
differences why different colours are reflected constructively at different
film thicknesses. (b) Calculate the minimum non-zero thickness $t$ of the
soap film that will reflect green light ($\lambda = 532\ \text{nm}$) with
maximum constructive interference.

> [!success]- Answer 6
> (a) Light reflecting from the front air-to-film surface ($n_1 = 1.00 < n_2 = 1.33$)
> undergoes a phase reversal of $\pi$ radians ($\frac{1}{2}\lambda$). Light
> reflecting from the back film-to-air surface ($n_2 = 1.33 > n_3 = 1.00$)
> undergoes no phase shift ($0$). The extra path travelled inside the film is
> $2t$. Due to the single phase flip, constructive interference occurs when
> $2t = \left(m + \frac{1}{2}\right)\frac{\lambda}{n_{\text{film}}}$. Because
> each visible colour has a distinct wavelength $\lambda$, varying film
> thicknesses constructively reflect distinct colours across the surface.
> (b) For minimum non-zero thickness ($m = 0$):
> $2t = \frac{1}{2}\frac{\lambda}{n} \implies t = \frac{\lambda}{4n} = \frac{532\ \text{nm}}{4(1.33)} = 100\ \text{nm}$.

**7.** A vertical broadcast antenna operates as an oscillating electric dipole
driven by a high-frequency AC generator. (a) Describe in qualitative terms
how the continuous acceleration of electrons back and forth along the
antenna produces self-propagating electromagnetic radiation in space.
(b) Compare this radio dipole radiation mechanism with the generation of
microwave radiation, medical X-rays via bremsstrahlung, and the emission of
visible light by atomic electron transitions.

> [!success]- Answer 7
> (a) As electrons oscillate along the dipole axis, the accelerating charges
> produce a time-varying electric field $\vec{E}$. By Maxwell's equations, a
> changing electric field induces a time-varying magnetic field $\vec{B}$
> perpendicular to it. The oscillating $\vec{B}$ field in turn regenerates an
> oscillating $\vec{E}$ field. The self-sustaining wave fronts decouple from
> the antenna and propagate outward at the speed of light $c$, with $\vec{E}$
> and $\vec{B}$ oscillating in phase, mutually perpendicular, and transverse
> to the radial direction of wave propagation.
> (b) All four are electromagnetic radiation produced by accelerating charge:
> radio waves are generated by macroscopic electronic dipole oscillations in
> wire conductors; microwaves are generated by resonant electron cavity
> oscillations in magnetrons; X-rays are generated by rapid deceleration of
> high-energy electrons colliding with heavy metal anode targets (bremsstrahlung)
> or inner-shell atomic transitions; and visible light is generated when atomic
> electrons transition between quantised orbital dipole energy levels.
