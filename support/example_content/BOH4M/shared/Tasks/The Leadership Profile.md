**Due: the last day of Unit 2.** Individual. A written profile of about
1,200 words.

Choose a leader, argue what makes them effective or ineffective, and
defend the theory that best explains them.

## Choosing well

Choose someone whose decisions you can find: a Canadian business leader,
a not-for-profit director, a union leader, a municipal official, an
Indigenous business or governance leader, a sports coach with a
documented record. Living or dead, celebrated or not.

Avoid the leader everyone has already been told is great. A profile that
argues against the received view, with evidence, is the most interesting
thing you can hand in.

## What it must contain

1. **Three decisions**, described factually, with dates and sources.
2. **The characteristics** those decisions demonstrate — using
   [[Leadership Characteristics]], not adjectives.
3. **The style**, named and evidenced from
   [[Leadership Styles]], including what happened under pressure.
4. **The theory that explains them best**, argued against at least one
   alternative from [[Leadership Theories]]. This is the analytical
   centre of the task.
5. **Your assessment**: effective, and at what cost, and for whom.

## How it is marked

- **Argument** — a defended claim, not a biography.
- **Use of theory** — theories compared, not listed.
- **Evidence** — three real decisions, sourced.
- **Fairness** — you deal with the strongest evidence against your view.

There is a checkpoint with me in the fourth week of the unit — bring
your three decisions and the theory you are defending — and the class
after it opens with time to make the one change we name.

> [!warning] A profile is not a tribute
> The word "inspiring" earns nothing here. What did they decide, who was
> affected, and would the same approach have worked in a different
> situation? That last question is contingency theory doing its job.

Triangulation — the evidence you will not have unless you go and get it.

OBSERVE — Unit 2, Day 12, the working period after the launch, while
  they are finding and sourcing the three decisions
  Watch for: what they type into the search box. A student searching the
  leader's name plus "leadership style" lands on profiles that have
  already decided; a student searching for the decision — the closure,
  the strike, the merger, the year — lands on the record and has to work
  out the characteristic themselves. The finished profile cites real
  sources either way, and the second kind is not visibly better on the
  page; it is only visibly different here. That is B3.1 (analyse the
  characteristics of effective leaders) being analysed rather than
  borrowed.
  Going well: two tabs open on the same event from outlets that disagree
  about it.
  Stuck: one long admiring feature article, and notes being taken from it
  in order.
  Record: a tick or a dash beside each name as you walk the room. One
  pass, two minutes. Item 2 of that agenda has just taught them where to
  look, so half of what you see here is a check on whether the teaching
  took — note the names that need it said again before Day 19, and treat
  only the Day 19 answers as evidence for the mark.

TALK — Unit 2, Day 19, the checkpoint in the last twenty minutes of that
  period
  Ask: "Which of the characteristics you have credited them with would
  the people who worked FOR them have named — and which would only a
  biographer name?" A strong answer can separate the two and knows which
  of its evidence is testimony and which is publicity. That is B3.1, and
  it is the distinction a written profile can hide by citing both kinds
  of source in the same paragraph.
  Then: "Name two theories that would have given your leader the SAME
  advice about one of your three decisions — and the moment where those
  two would part company." A strong answer holds two theories side by
  side and finds the seam. That is B3.2 (compare a variety of
  contemporary leadership theories), and it is a step past what the page
  asks for: the page asks them to argue their theory against ONE
  alternative, which they will arrive with written out. Agreement between
  two rejected theories is not on the page anywhere.
  Do not ask whether the same approach would have worked elsewhere,
  either: the task page prints that question in its closing warning.
  Record: a letter beside each name on the class list, right then.

The product evidence is the written profile, handed in on the last day
