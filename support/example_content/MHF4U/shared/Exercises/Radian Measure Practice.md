## From degrees to radians and back

1. Convert to radians, exactly: $60°$, $225°$, $330°$.
2. Convert to degrees: $\dfrac{\pi}{6}$, $\dfrac{3\pi}{4}$,
   $\dfrac{5\pi}{3}$, and $2$ radians (one decimal place).
3. An arc subtends an angle of $\dfrac{2\pi}{3}$ at the centre of a
   circle of radius 5 cm. How long is the arc?

> [!success]- Answer 1
> Multiply by $\frac{\pi}{180°}$: $60° = \frac{\pi}{3}$,
> $225° = \frac{225\pi}{180} = \frac{5\pi}{4}$,
> $330° = \frac{330\pi}{180} = \frac{11\pi}{6}$. Sanity check the
> middle one: $225°$ is a half turn plus a $45°$, and
> $\pi + \frac{\pi}{4} = \frac{5\pi}{4}$. ✓

> [!success]- Answer 2
> Multiply by $\frac{180°}{\pi}$: $\frac{\pi}{6} = 30°$,
> $\frac{3\pi}{4} = 135°$, $\frac{5\pi}{3} = 300°$. For 2 radians:
> $2 \times \frac{180°}{\pi} \approx 114.6°$ — a plain number with
> no $\pi$ in it is still a perfectly good radian measure.

> [!success]- Answer 3
> $s = r\theta = 5 \times \frac{2\pi}{3} = \frac{10\pi}{3} \approx
> 10.5$ cm. The formula is the definition of the radian rearranged —
> and it only works because the angle was in radians.

## Exact values on the unit circle

4. State exact values: $\sin\dfrac{\pi}{3}$, $\cos\dfrac{3\pi}{4}$,
   $\tan\dfrac{\pi}{6}$.
5. State exact values: $\sin\dfrac{7\pi}{6}$, $\cos\dfrac{5\pi}{3}$,
   $\tan\dfrac{3\pi}{4}$.
6. State exact values: $\csc\dfrac{\pi}{4}$, $\sec\dfrac{\pi}{6}$,
   $\cot\dfrac{\pi}{2}$.
7. Find all angles $\theta$ in $0 \le \theta \le 2\pi$ with
   $\cos\theta = -\dfrac{\sqrt{3}}{2}$.

> [!success]- Answer 4
> $\sin\frac{\pi}{3} = \frac{\sqrt{3}}{2}$ (first quadrant, straight
> from the special triangle). $\cos\frac{3\pi}{4} =
> -\frac{\sqrt{2}}{2}$ — reference angle $\frac{\pi}{4}$, second
> quadrant, where x-coordinates are negative.
> $\tan\frac{\pi}{6} = \frac{1}{\sqrt{3}} = \frac{\sqrt{3}}{3}$.

> [!success]- Answer 5
> $\frac{7\pi}{6}$: reference angle $\frac{\pi}{6}$, third quadrant,
> sine negative: $-\frac{1}{2}$. $\frac{5\pi}{3}$: reference angle
> $\frac{\pi}{3}$, fourth quadrant, cosine positive: $\frac{1}{2}$.
> $\frac{3\pi}{4}$: reference angle $\frac{\pi}{4}$, second
> quadrant, where sine and cosine disagree in sign:
> $\tan\frac{3\pi}{4} = -1$.

> [!success]- Answer 6
> Flip the primary values.
> $\csc\frac{\pi}{4} = \frac{1}{\sqrt{2}/2} = \sqrt{2}$.
> $\sec\frac{\pi}{6} = \frac{1}{\sqrt{3}/2} = \frac{2}{\sqrt{3}} =
> \frac{2\sqrt{3}}{3}$. $\cot\frac{\pi}{2} =
> \frac{\cos(\pi/2)}{\sin(\pi/2)} = \frac{0}{1} = 0$ — defined and
> zero, even though $\tan\frac{\pi}{2}$ does not exist.

> [!success]- Answer 7
> Reference angle $\frac{\pi}{6}$ (since
> $\cos\frac{\pi}{6} = \frac{\sqrt{3}}{2}$), and cosine is negative
> in the second and third quadrants:
> $\theta = \pi - \frac{\pi}{6} = \frac{5\pi}{6}$ or
> $\theta = \pi + \frac{\pi}{6} = \frac{7\pi}{6}$. Expect a pair —
> a horizontal line cuts the circle twice.

## Angles in motion

8. A record turntable spins at 45 revolutions per minute. What is
   its angular speed in radians per second?

> [!success]- Answer 8
> Each revolution is $2\pi$ radians, so
> $45 \times 2\pi = 90\pi$ radians per minute; dividing by 60 gives
> $\frac{3\pi}{2} \approx 4.7$ radians per second. Radians make the
> arithmetic honest: multiply by the radius and you would have the
> rim speed, straight from $s = r\theta$.
