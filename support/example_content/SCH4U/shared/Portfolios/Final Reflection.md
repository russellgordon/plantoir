The closing entry. Roughly 500 words, written after
[[The Chemistry Showcase]] is finished and before the course ends.

## What to cover

1. **The sentence you wrote in [[Your First Entry]]** about what a
   scientific model is. Quote it. Would you still write it? If not, what
   replaced it, and what changed it — a specific investigation, a
   discussion, an argument you lost.
2. **The model you now trust least, and why.** Not the one you
   understand least. The one you can use confidently and have learned to
   distrust at a particular edge — collision theory where the solvent
   gets in the way, Le Châtelier where it gives a direction and no
   magnitude, standard potentials in a cell that is nowhere near
   standard, VSEPR where the shape it predicts is not quite the shape
   that is measured.
3. **The investigation you would run again.** Not the one that went
   best. The one where you now know what you should have measured, or
   how many trials you actually needed, or which variable you let move
   with another.
4. **A mechanism you can explain now that you could not at the start.**
   Explain it here, briefly, to a reader who has not taken the course.
   Why a reaction stops before it finishes. Why a catalyst changes the
   speed and not the destination. Why one liquid boils long before
   another of the same mass. Why a pH change of one unit is a factor of
   ten. One of those, properly, beats five of them in outline.
5. **What you still do not understand.** Named precisely. "Equilibrium"
   is not precise; "why the equilibrium constant depends on temperature
   but not on the amounts you started with" is.
6. **What you are taking with you.** For most of you this is the last
   chemistry course you will take before university or instead of it. Be
   honest — for some people the answer is a habit rather than a concept,
   and a habit is a perfectly good answer.

> [!question]- Prompts, if you are staring at a blank page
> When did you first argue with a number instead of accepting it?
> When did you first argue with a *model* instead of applying it?
> Which page on this site did you actually return to more than once?
> What did you believe in Unit 1 that Unit 4 contradicted?
> When did the double arrow stop being decoration?
> Whose reasoning in this room changed yours, and on what?
> What is the first thing you now do when an answer looks wrong?
> What would you tell somebody starting this course in the first week —
> not the encouraging version, the useful one?

## Who to write it for

Not for me. I was in the room; I already know what happened. Write it
for a student taking this course next year who is reading your journal
to find out what it is like and what to do differently.

That reader changes what belongs in it. They do not need "equilibrium
was hard". They need "I spent two classes treating an ICE table as a
form to fill in, and what finally worked was writing down what each row
physically meant before putting a number in it."
