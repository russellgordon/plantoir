**Assessed in Unit 2, and required before you use anything powered.**
Individual.

Not a quiz. A demonstration that you can work safely, checked by me, and
recorded — the way a real ticket works on a real site.

## The four parts

1. **Personal protective equipment.** Select the right protection for
   three different tasks, put it on correctly, and say what each piece
   is protecting you from and how you would know it had stopped working.
2. **Pre-start checks.** Walk through a task before starting it: the
   site, the tool, the weather, the people around you — see
   [[Before You Start Work]].
3. **Safe operation and maintenance.** For two hand tools and one
   powered tool you are authorised to use, demonstrate correct handling,
   the guard or safeguard, shutdown, and the maintenance the tool needs.
4. **Legislation, in your own words.** Explain your three rights under
   Ontario's health and safety law, what WHMIS symbols on our shop
   products mean, where the safety data sheets are, and what you would
   do about a hazard you could not fix yourself.

## What is marked

| Criterion | The standard |
| --- | --- |
| Selection | The right protection, correctly worn, without prompting |
| Judgement | You spot the hazard before it is pointed out |
| Operation | Correct, controlled, unhurried |
| Knowledge | Your rights and the WHMIS basics, accurately |
| Consistency | The same practice on an ordinary working day as under assessment |

> [!warning] This one is pass or repeat
> The safety ticket is not marked on a curve. If any part is not to
> standard, you repeat that part until it is, and you do not use the
> equipment in question until then. That is how it works on a job site
> and it is how it works here.

Everything in [[Safety/index|the safety pages]] feeds this, and
[[The Law on the Job]] is the fourth part.

Triangulation — the evidence you will not have unless you go and get it.

Days 7 and 8 are the ticket itself. You are assessing there, one student
at a time, and you cannot also be gathering evidence on the other
twenty-eight. Both prompts below sit before it, which is also when they
can still change how somebody prepares.

OBSERVE — Unit 2, Day 5, the supervised practice after the shop-practice
lesson
  Watch for the two seconds after a cut finishes: hand straight to the
  offcut while the machine runs down, or a wait. Then watch the
  periphery — the route a student takes past somebody else's work, and
  whether they say anything about it. That is D1.3, hazards and danger
  zones identified, and D1.4, safe shop practice applied — neither of
  which a one-at-a-time ticket assessment can show you, because on the
  ticket there is nobody else in the danger zone. (Not D1.1: that one is
  about protective clothing and equipment, and none of this is PPE.)
  Going well: waits for the blade; walks the long way round another
  student's swing; taps a shoulder before passing behind.
  Stuck: reaches across a running tool, or steps into a danger zone
  without registering that it is one.
  Record: three columns on your day plan — waited, reached, warned
  somebody. The third column is the one worth having.

TALK — Unit 2, Day 6, while they are finding and reading a real safety
data sheet
  The ticket already asks them to recite their three rights, so do not
  spend the two minutes on the list.
  Ask: "You have the sheet in front of you. Who else in this building
  is exposed to that product without ever reading this, and whose job is
  it to tell them?"
  Then: "Name a job in this shop where the safe way is the slower way —
  and say what makes people skip it."
  A strong answer names a real person or a real posting for the first,
  and for the second names a pressure — time, the end of the period,
  not wanting to look slow — rather than saying that people are
  careless. The first question is D1.5 heard as judgement instead of
  recall. The second is not an expectation question at all: it is how
  you find out which of D1.4's practices will be the ones skipped when
  nobody is standing there.
  Record: two letters beside each name — P for a person named, S for a
  system named. Both, one, or neither.

The product evidence is the ticket assessment itself, on Days 7 and 8.
