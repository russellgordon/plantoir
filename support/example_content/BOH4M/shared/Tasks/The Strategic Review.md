**Launched mid-unit; presented in the second-last week of Unit 3.**
Worked in pairs, marked individually.

Take one organization's strategy apart, and say whether it will work.

## The organization

A publicly documented one, so that you have material: a listed Canadian
company, a large not-for-profit with published reports, a hospital
network, a municipality, or a co-operative. You need at least two years
of documents.

## What to produce

**A written analysis (about 1,200 words) and a ten-minute briefing.**

| Section | What it must do |
| --- | --- |
| The strategy | State it in three sentences, at the right level — see [[Strategy and Its Levels]] |
| The tools | SWOT, plus PESTLE or Porter's five forces, each with specific evidenced entries |
| The planning tools | Which tools from [[Planning Tools]] the organization is actually using, and which it appears not to be |
| The verdict | Will it work? What would have to be true? |
| The check | What you would measure in a year to know |

## What is marked as yours

There is no shared mark for the pair. Divide the written analysis by
section and put your name on the ones you wrote — one of you on the
strategy and the tools, the other on the planning tools and the verdict,
is the split that works. In the ten-minute briefing you present your own
sections and take the board's questions on them. What you say at the
conference in the working period is yours as well.

## How it is marked

- **Specificity.** "Strong brand" is worth nothing; a named advantage
  with evidence is worth everything.
- **Analysis over description.** The grids are raw material; the marks are
  in what you conclude from them.
- **The gap.** What the organization says its strategy is, against what
  it funds — see [[The Planning Process]].
- **The briefing.** Ten minutes, recommendation first, questions taken.

> [!tip] Pick the two entries that matter
> A SWOT with twenty entries is a list. A review that says "these two
> things decide it, and here is why" is an analysis. Your briefing has
> ten minutes; choose.

OBSERVE — Unit 3, Day 12, the working period, and especially the peer
  challenge at the end of it where each pair attacks one of another
  pair's entries
  Watch for: what happens to an entry that has just been attacked. Three
  things can follow, and they are not equal — the pair deletes it, the
  pair goes and finds evidence for it, or the pair keeps it and hopes the
  challenge is forgotten. Your Specificity criterion will catch a vague
  entry when you mark the review, and it should; what the review cannot
  tell you is which of its surviving entries were ever tested at all. An
  entry that was attacked and defended and an entry nobody happened to
  pick read exactly alike. That is D3.3 (analyse organizational strategic
  plans, using a variety of management tools) happening or not happening
  in front of you.
  Going well: an entry deleted without an argument, or a document opened
  to defend it within a minute.
  Stuck: the challenge is answered with an opinion, and the pair moves on
  to the next section without opening anything.
  Record: one row per pair on the day plan, and a D, an E, or an S.

TALK — Unit 3, Day 12, the conferences already on that agenda
  Ask: "Find me two parts of this plan that clearly did not talk to each
  other — a date that cannot work with another date, money committed
  twice, a target one department cannot hit without another one moving
  first." A strong answer produces the pair and says what it costs them.
  That is D1.1 (describe the planning process and its benefits to the
  organization: flexibility, coordination, time management) approached
  from the failure end, which matters here: The Planning Process has a
  section headed "What it buys", taught on Day 1 of this unit and read
  for homework that night, so asking what planning buys would get the
  page read back at you. Asking where it failed to buy it cannot be.
  Then: "Point me at a number in this plan that the organization is

  Do not ask which planning tools the organization is using, either: the
  task page asks for exactly that in its table, and they will read you
  the row.
  Record: one line per student, right then — you have both partners in
  front of you, so make it two lines.

The product evidence is the written analysis and the ten-minute briefing
on Day 14. Do not try to gather anything else during the briefings; you
are chairing the board.
