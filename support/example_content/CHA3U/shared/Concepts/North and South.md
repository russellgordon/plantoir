For forty years American politics ran on a series of arrangements designed to
stop one argument from becoming a war. Each worked for a while. Each was
undone by the next piece of territory. Studying the sequence is the best
available lesson in how a political system absorbs conflict — and in what
happens when it can no longer do it.

## The compromises, and what each postponed

| Arrangement | What it settled, and for how long |
| --- | --- |
| Missouri Compromise, 1820 | Drew a line across the Louisiana territory; held for a generation |
| Compromise of 1850 | Admitted California free; imposed a tougher fugitive slave law on the North |
| Kansas-Nebraska Act, 1854 | Let settlers decide, and turned Kansas into a shooting war |
| *Dred Scott*, 1857 | Held Congress could not bar slavery from the territories at all |
| Secession, from December 1860 | Ended arrangement as a method |

Read the middle rows carefully. The 1850 settlement bought peace in Congress
by making northern citizens personally complicit in recapture, which
manufactured abolitionists in places that had been indifferent. "Bleeding
Kansas" was not a metaphor; rival governments, fraudulent elections and armed
raiders produced years of killing before any state had seceded.

## Was the war about slavery?

Yes, and the participants said so at the time. The secession declarations
issued by several states name slavery explicitly and at length as the cause.
The claim that the war was fundamentally about tariffs or abstract states'
rights was constructed afterward, largely from the 1870s onward, as part of a
reinterpretation now called the Lost Cause. That is an argument about how
history is written rather than about what happened, and it is a good place to
practise [[Interpretation and Historiography]].

Lincoln's own position is worth stating precisely rather than heroically. He
was consistently opposed to slavery's extension, was slower on immediate
abolition, and said publicly that his paramount object was to save the Union.
Both his caution and his eventual action are in the record, and a strong
answer explains the change rather than choosing one quotation.

## The cost, and the settlement that followed

The number of dead is not a settled figure. The long-standing estimate of
roughly 620,000 came from nineteenth-century compilations of army records;
later demographic work using census data has argued the true total was
considerably higher, with published estimates running to around three
quarters of a million within a wide range. Give the estimate, name the method,
and do not present any of them as final.

Reconstruction rebuilt southern governments with Black men voting and holding
office for the first time, protected by federal troops. It ended in a
bargain: the disputed presidential election of 1876 was resolved in 1877 on
terms that withdrew federal enforcement from the South. What replaced it took
the region another ninety years to dislodge.

Weigh the causes with [[Cause and Consequence]], then take the whole question
into [[What Held the Union Together|What Held the Union Together?]].
