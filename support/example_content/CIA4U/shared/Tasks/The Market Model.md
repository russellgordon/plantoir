**Launched mid-Unit 1; presented in the last week of the unit.** Pairs.

Take one Canadian market, model it, and then test the model against what
the market actually did.

## The market

One good or service with published price and quantity data over at least
three years. Workable choices: gasoline, new and used vehicles, rental
housing in one city, air travel on one route, a farm commodity under a
marketing board, electricity. Avoid anything where you cannot find a price
series — the model is only interesting if it can be checked.

## What to produce

**A short report (about 1,000 words) and a five-minute presentation.**

Your report has to do four things.

1. **Build the model.** Demand and supply for your market, with the
   equilibrium argument stated in words as well as drawn.
2. **Identify the shifters.** Which of B2.2's factors moved your market —
   technology, advertising, weather, input or labour shortages, a sales
   tax, a land transfer tax? Evidence for each, not a list.
3. **Elasticity.** Estimate whether demand in your market is elastic or
   inelastic, say how you can tell, and draw the consequence for who bears
   a tax on it.
4. **Consumer decisions.** B2.3's question: how did real buyers behave, and
   where did they behave in ways the model does not predict?

## How it is marked

- **The shift-versus-movement distinction, used correctly.** This single
  error accounts for most lost marks on this task.
- **Evidence for every claimed shifter.** A price series and a dated
  event, not a plausible story.
- **An elasticity claim you can defend.** Rough is fine; unsupported is
  not.
- **Honesty about failure.** A pair that says "the model predicted the
  price would fall and it rose, and here is why" scores higher than a pair
  that quietly does not mention it.
- **Your own half, named.** The front page says which two of the four
  requirements each of you wrote. I mark you on your two, and on how you
  answer for the whole argument when you are questioned on Day 21.

## Working in pairs, marked individually

You work as a pair and you are marked as two people, so there is no
shared mark on this task and nothing your partner says about your work
goes into yours.

Split the four requirements above between you and say on the front page
who wrote which. The model and the shifters are one half; the elasticity
estimate and the consumer decisions are the other. Whichever half you
take, you answer questions on the whole thing at the presentation —
because you are both responsible for the argument even though only one
of you wrote each part, and because that is where I hear each of you on
your own.

> [!warning] Do not choose a market where the price is set by a regulator
> If a board or a commission sets the price, supply and demand are not
> determining it and your model will describe something that is not
> happening. That is a legitimate topic — but then the analysis is about
> the regulation, which belongs in [[The Firm Study]], not here.

Build from [[Supply and Demand]], [[Elasticity]] and [[Incentives]].
