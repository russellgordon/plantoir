**1.** Identify the receptor, control centre, and effector for
thermoregulation when you are too warm.

> [!success]- Answer 1
> Receptors: peripheral thermoreceptors in skin and central thermoreceptors
> in the hypothalamus. Control centre: preoptic area of the hypothalamus.
> Effectors: cutaneous arterioles (vasodilation), eccrine sweat glands
> (evaporative cooling), and behavioural adaptations.

**2.** Explain why an action potential is all-or-none, and how intensity
is encoded instead.

> [!success]- Answer 2
> Once depolarisation reaches threshold ($\approx -55\text{ mV}$), voltage-gated
> $\ce{Na+}$ channels open cooperatively, producing an identical spike amplitude
> regardless of stimulus strength. Stimulus intensity is encoded as frequency
> (action potentials per second) and by recruitment of multiple neurons.

**3.** Type 1 and type 2 diabetes both raise blood glucose. Where does the
loop fail in each?

> [!success]- Answer 3
> Type 1: autoimmune destruction of pancreatic beta cells in the islets of
> Langerhans eliminates insulin synthesis (effector failure). Type 2: target
> cells (liver, muscle, adipose) downregulate or desensitise insulin receptors,
> creating insulin resistance despite normal or elevated hormone production.

**4.** Why is filter-then-reabsorb a sensible kidney design?

> [!success]- Answer 4
> A size-based filter at the glomerulus is rapid and non-specific, allowing
> metabolic wastes of unknown molecular structure to be cleared. High-affinity
> tubular transporters then selectively reclaim vital nutrients (glucose, amino
> acids, water, ions), avoiding the evolutionary need for dedicated export
> pumps for every novel toxin.

**5.** Give one example of positive feedback in the body, and say what
ends it.

> [!success]- Answer 5
> Parturition (childbirth): cervical stretch triggers oxytocin release from the
> posterior pituitary, which stimulates uterine contractions, causing further
> cervical stretch. Delivery of the fetus and placenta terminates the loop.

**6.** Synthetic estrogens (e.g., $17\alpha$-ethinylestradiol from pharmaceuticals)
and bisphenol A (BPA) leached from plastics enter municipal watersheds. Explain
how these compounds act as endocrine disruptors in humans and aquatic organisms.

> [!success]- Answer 6
> Endocrine disruptors mimic natural steroid hormones by binding to nuclear
> estrogen receptors or blocking endogenous hormone binding. In aquatic organisms,
> this causes feminisation of male fish and reproductive collapse; in humans,
> exposure is linked to altered onset of puberty, reduced sperm counts, and
> increased hormone-dependent oncology risks.

**7.** In a choice chamber experiment with terrestrial isopods (woodlice),
animals move rapidly in dry air but slow down and turn less frequently in
moist conditions. Distinguish kinesis from taxis and explain how this
invertebrate response mechanism maintains homeostasis.

> [!success]- Answer 7
> Kinesis is a non-directional change in activity rate or turning frequency in
> response to a stimulus intensity (orthokinesis/klinokinesis), whereas taxis is
> directed orientation toward or away from a stimulus. By reducing movement in
> humid zones, isopods aggregate in damp microhabitats, preventing lethal
> desiccation across their respiratory gill pleopods.

**8.** Diagram the negative feedback loop that restores blood osmolarity
when you become dehydrated.

> [!success]- Answer 8
> Stimulus: increased blood osmolarity (dehydration) $\rightarrow$ Sensor:
> hypothalamic osmoreceptors $\rightarrow$ Integrator: hypothalamus signals
> posterior pituitary $\rightarrow$ Effector output: release of antidiuretic
> hormone (ADH) $\rightarrow$ Target: renal collecting duct cells insert
> aquaporin channels $\rightarrow$ Response: increased water reabsorption into
> blood, producing concentrated urine and restoring baseline osmolarity.
