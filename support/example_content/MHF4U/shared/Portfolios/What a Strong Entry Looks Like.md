Once the journal habit exists, the next question is quality — and
quality in a journal entry comes down to one word: **specific**. A
strong entry could only have been written by you, about this class, on
this day. A weak one could have been written by anyone, about any math
class, from the hallway.

## Side by side

| Weak | Strong |
| --- | --- |
| "Today was confusing." | "I can sketch from factored form cold, but I froze when the factor was $(x - 2)^2$ and I had to say what the square does to the graph at 2." |
| "Group work went fine." | "Priya caught that our 'asymptote' at $x = 3$ was actually a hole, because the factor cancelled — and suddenly the graph stopped fighting us." |
| "I need to study more." | "I split $\log(a + b)$ into $\log a + \log b$ — twice today, same spot." |
| "Math was fun today." | "My estimate for $\log_2 100$ was 50; the real answer is under 7, and I actually wanted to know why my gut was off by that much." |

Read the weak column: nothing happened in it. The strong column is the
same day, remembered properly — a method, a moment, a cause. And
notice the strong versions are barely longer. Specific is not the same
as long; it is the difference between fog and a location, which is
exactly what [[Math Journal]] asks the "what is foggy" prompt to do.

## Feelings are welcome — anchored to a moment and a method

The journal is not a lab report; frustrated, proud, embarrassed, and
delighted all belong in it. The rule is that a feeling arrives *attached to
the moment that caused it and the method involved*. "I felt dumb" floats
free and teaches you nothing at the end of the course. "I felt
dumb when the whole group read the bounce at $x = 2$ straight off the
factored form and I was still plotting points — so I asked Theo what
he checks first" is a feeling with an address and an exit. The
feeling is real data about you; the method is the handle you can
actually turn — the same pairing [[Mistakes Are Data]] argues for at
the boards.
