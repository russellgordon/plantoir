**The question:** who is dance for — the people watching, the people doing
it, or nobody in particular?

This is the last seminar of the year, and deliberately the one you could not
have argued at the start of the course. Since then you have performed for a
room, watched a room perform, improvised alone with your eyes shut, and
danced somewhere that was not this studio. All of that is evidence.

## Prepare

Bring **two** moments from your own year: one where the dancing plainly had an
audience, and one where it plainly did not. Thirty seconds on each. Then bring
one moment from outside this course — a wedding, a gym, a kitchen, a screen, a
stage — and be ready to say who you think it was for. Look in
[[Your Movement Journal]] rather than remembering cold. The first weeks are further
away than it feels.

## Four positions, and they do not agree

**For the audience.** A dance nobody sees has not finished happening.
Choreography is a message with a receiver, and nearly every choice you made
this year — facing, timing, the pause before the ending — was a choice about
what somebody else would see. Remove the watcher and most of those choices
stop meaning anything.

**For the people doing it.** Most of the dancing on earth has no audience,
because everyone present is dancing. Social dance, ceremony, a floor at a
party: the value is in being one of the bodies, not in being looked at. On
this account, performance dance is the unusual case, not the normal one.

**For the dancer alone.** You do not owe anybody a performance. Dancing in a
room by yourself is a complete activity — it changes your mood, your body, and
what you know about yourself, and no audience improves any of that. If this is
right, the audience is a bonus at best and a distortion at worst.

**For whoever is paying.** Schools, funders, festivals, competitions, and
platforms decide which dances get made and which get seen. Follow that, and
you get an honest answer that is not about art at all.

> [!question] The test that sorts you
> If you had to choose — dance forever with no audience, or watch forever
> without dancing — which do you take? Whatever you answer, the position you
> argued all seminar should match it. If it does not, one of the two was for
> show.

Nobody settles this today. [[What Dance Does in a Society]] is the wider
version of the question, and [[What You Take With You]] asks what dance leaves
in you either way.
