Two lists: what you carry every day, and what a field day adds. Copy both
into your phone the first week, because the field-day list is the one
that matters and it is the one people forget.

## Every class

- A bound notebook that will survive a term outdoors. Not loose sheets.
- Pencils, an eraser, a ruler with millimetres, and coloured pencils
  for maps.
- A calculator, or the one on your device.
- A device if you have one; there are class sets if you do not, and
  nothing on this site requires you to own hardware.

## Field days

- Closed shoes with a grip. Boots if the ground is wet, and it usually
  is at the shoreline.
- Long trousers, tucked into socks in the woodlot. This is a tick
  measure, and it is the effective one.
- Layers, including something windproof — the waterfront is colder than
  the schoolyard by more than you expect.
- Insect repellent, sunscreen, a hat, and water. Enough water.
- Your notebook and a pencil. Pencil works in rain and in cold; pens do
  not reliably do either.
- Any medication you might need, on you, not in a bag left somewhere.

> [!note] The list above is not a form
> Nothing on it is sent to anyone. Tick the boxes as you pack the night
> before, and your browser keeps the ticks on this computer.

## What you do not need to buy

Clinometers, augers, tapes, thermometers, hand lenses and GPS units are
supplied. If a field-day item is genuinely a problem to obtain, tell me
privately before the day and it will be sorted quietly — see
[[Getting Help]]. Nobody misses fieldwork over boots.

## Before the first field day

Read [[Fieldwork Safety]] properly, not quickly. Bring the signed form.
Tell me about any allergy, asthma, or condition I should know about,
including things you would rather not announce to the room — a note
handed over is fine.

What the days themselves involve is set out in [[The Shoreline Study]],
[[The Woodlot Study]] and [[The Farm Visit]].
