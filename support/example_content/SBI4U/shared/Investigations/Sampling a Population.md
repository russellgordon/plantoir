> [!abstract] At a glance
> **Time:** one class, partly outdoors. **Groups of three.** Two methods,
> compared honestly.

You cannot count every individual, so you estimate — and the estimate is
only as good as the assumptions behind it.

## Method A — Quadrats, for things that do not move

Random placement (using coordinates, not judgement), count within each,
scale up by area.

## Method B — Mark and recapture, for things that do

Capture a sample, mark them harmlessly, release, wait, recapture:

$$N = \frac{M \times C}{R}$$

where $M$ is marked initially, $C$ is the second sample size, and $R$ is
how many of those were marked. We use beans in a tub for the practice run,
then invertebrates outdoors.

## Analysis

1. Estimate the population by both methods where possible. How far apart
   are they?
2. List every assumption Method B makes — no births, no deaths, no
   migration, marks do not fall off, marked individuals mix evenly, marking
   does not change survival. Which is least safe for your organism?
3. If marked individuals became easier for predators to see, which
   direction would your estimate be wrong in?
