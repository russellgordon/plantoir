A case is not a story to have opinions about. It is evidence with a
decision hidden in it, and there is a method.

## Three passes

**First pass — the story.** Read it straight through without stopping.
What kind of organization, who are the people, what has happened. Do not
start solving anything yet.

**Second pass — the pen.** Read again and mark two things in different
ways: **facts** (what the case states) and **inferences** (what you have
concluded). Most bad case analysis is inference presented as fact, and
this is where you catch your own.

**Third pass — the gaps.** What would you need to know to be sure? Write
the three questions down. In a real organization you would go and ask;
in an examination, naming what you would need is itself worth marks.

## The four questions, in order

1. **What is the problem?** Not the symptom. "Turnover is up" is a
   symptom; the problem is whatever is causing it.
2. **Whose problem is it?** Which level of management, which function.
3. **What are the options?** At least three, including doing nothing —
   which is sometimes right and always worth pricing.
4. **What do you recommend, and what does it cost?**

## Two habits that separate strong analysis from weak

- **Quantify where the case lets you.** If the numbers are there, use
  them. "Turnover is high" is weaker than "nine of twenty-two left in a
  year".
- **Argue against yourself once.** Write the strongest objection to your
  own recommendation and answer it. Every marker in this course is
  looking for that paragraph.

> [!success]- What a weak analysis looks like (click to expand)
> It summarises the case back, blames a named person's personality,
> recommends "better communication", and does not say what it costs or
> how anyone would know it worked. If your draft has three of those four,
> start again from the four questions.

The cases are not marked. What you say while you work one is evidence
all the same — [[How Marks Work]] explains why the things you say count
— and the final [[The Case Examination]] is nothing but this method
under a clock.
