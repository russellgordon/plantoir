Most of a math course vanishes as it happens — boards get erased,
pages get filed. Your journal is the part that stays: a running
record where a semester of counting arguments, surprising
probabilities, and slow investigation work becomes something you can
look back through and actually *see* changing. In this course the
journal carries a second job as well. It is the working notebook of
[[The Culminating Investigation]] — the place where a question got
narrowed, a dataset got rejected, and a dead end got recorded before
you forgot why you abandoned it.

The hub is [[Math Journal]]. The pages below support it in order:

| Page | When you need it |
| --- | --- |
| [[Your First Entry]] | Week one, facing a blank page |
| [[What a Strong Entry Looks Like]] | Once the habit exists |
| [[Journal Checklist]] | Before each unit's collection |
| [[Judging Your Own Work]] | Whenever a task is nearly done and still fixable |
| [[Showing Growth]] | Mid-course, when old entries become evidence |
| [[Final Reflection]] | The last week, reading the whole record |

> [!tip] Written forwards, read backwards
> Entries take ten minutes and feel small. The value arrives months
> later, when [[Showing Growth]] calls week-one-you as a witness, and
> again when your investigation report needs to say *why* you chose
> this data — an answer that lives in an entry you wrote long before
> you knew it mattered.
