## The question

If a critical approach determines what you find, is a reading discovery
or invention?

## Start with a case

Ophelia's madness, read three ways: as psychological collapse, as the
only unedited speech the play permits her, and as a formal necessity —
somebody has to die for Act 5 to work. All three can be built from the
text.

## Positions to consider

| Position | The argument | The objection |
| --- | --- | --- |
| The lens discovers | The pattern was always there; the approach only made it visible | Then why do different approaches find different patterns in the same text? |
| The lens invents | You find what you look for; the text is a mirror | Then no reading could be wrong, and some plainly are |
| It is a negotiation | The text constrains; the approach selects | Comfortable, and it dodges the question of when a reading goes too far |
| It depends on falsifiability | A reading that no passage could refute is imposition | Strict, and it would disqualify several respected schools |

## The test worth proposing

What passage would refute your reading? If you cannot name one, is your
reading of the text or of the theory? Take that question into
[[The Lens Essay]] — it is the cost paragraph in disguise.

## Afterwards

One paragraph: the reading you hold most confidently, and the passage
that would break it.
