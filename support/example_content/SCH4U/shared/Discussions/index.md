Questions with no answer key.

Nothing in this folder is a separate mark. What happens here is
**evidence**, which is a different thing: what you say in a discussion
counts towards the same expectations your written work does, and some of
what you understand will only ever show up in a room like this. See
[[How Marks Work]] for where the three kinds of evidence sit.

What I am listening for is **how you argue** — the evidence you bring,
the weaknesses you name before anyone else names them, and whether you
took on the strongest version of the other position rather than the
easiest one.

Grade 11 taught you to defend a number: what was measured, with what
instrument, how well. That still holds, and it is now the floor rather
than the achievement. Grade 12 adds a second demand. Most of what you
will say this year rests on a **model** — collision theory, Le
Châtelier, the Brønsted-Lowry picture, a table of standard potentials —
and a model is not evidence. It is a claim about when it can be trusted.
So the sentence worth saying in this room sounds like this: *here is
what I measured, here is the model I read it through, and here is where
that model stops being true.* It is also the sentence that earns the
most on paper, which is not a coincidence.

> [!note] Changing your mind out loud is a win
> If you hear something better than what you walked in with and say so,
> that is the best thing that can happen in this room, and I will say so
> at the time. Nobody has ever come off worse here for being persuaded.
