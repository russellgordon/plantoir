## What you are making

A running record of your work in this course: one entry after every
workshop day. Entries are short — ten honest minutes beats thirty performed
ones — and they are **private**. I read them; other students never do. That
privacy is deliberate. The journal only works if you can admit in it what
you would not say aloud: that a rehearsal went badly, that you froze, that
a group decision worried you. Nothing you admit in an ordinary entry can
cost you anything, because ordinary entries are not marked. What carries a
mark is [[Milestone Journal Entries]] — four entries written in class, one
at the end of each unit — and those are built out of the honest ones.

## How to work

Each entry answers three questions, in whatever order helps:

- **What we did** — the facts of the day, briefly. Which task, which
  convention, what your group tried.
- **What I noticed** — the interesting part. A choice that worked, a
  moment the room went quiet, something a classmate did that you want to
  steal, friction in your group and where it came from.
- **What I would try next** — one concrete thing. Not "do better":
  something you could actually do next class.

Two other kinds of entry count:

- [[Writing in Role]] — an entry written as a character you are working on
  is often more revealing than an entry written as yourself.
- Responses to performances we watch, using the describe-first habit from
  [[Watching Like an Artist]].

## Success criteria

| Quality | What it looks like |
| --- | --- |
| Regularity | An entry exists for every workshop day, written close to the day |
| Honesty | Entries name real difficulties, not just successes |
| Noticing | Observations are specific — a moment, a choice, a person |
| Growth | "What I would try next" items reappear later as things actually tried |
| Range | Some entries are in role; some respond to work you watched |
