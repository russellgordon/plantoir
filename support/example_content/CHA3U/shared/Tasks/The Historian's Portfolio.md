**Kept from the first week to the last, marked as one thing at the end.**
Individual. Three parts: [[Your Inquiry Notebook]], and the two written
reflections [[Skills You Are Building]] and [[Where History Leads]].

The portfolio is the only assessed work in this course that is about your
own thinking rather than about the American past. It is where you keep the
record of what you asked, what you found, and where your position moved —
and it is the reason [[The Long Argument]] will not begin from a blank
page in January.

## Written in class, and why

Six periods this term carry a portfolio milestone on the agenda, and the
entries written in those periods are the ones that carry the mark. Anything
you add at home is yours, is worth doing, and is not marked.

That is not an arbitrary rule. Practice you do between classes is practice,
and marking it would turn every evening into an assessment. The milestones
fall where the course has just given you something to think with:

- the last class of Unit 1, after the retrieval — how your answer to what
  the Revolution was for has moved across the unit;
- the class of the Unit 2 seminar, while the disagreement is still fresh;
- the class near the end of Unit 2 that returns to who could vote in 1877;
- the middle of Unit 3, after mass production and what it did to work;
- late in Unit 3, when [[Skills You Are Building]] is written;
- the closing portfolio class in Unit 4, where [[Where History Leads]] is
  written and the notebook is handed in as a set.

Bring the notebook to all six. An entry written from memory is visibly
thinner than one written from the pages beside it.

## How it is marked

| Criterion | What earns the marks |
| --- | --- |
| Sourcing | Every entry can be traced back — author, title, date, repository, reference |
| Question | Each entry names which kind of question it is asking, and stays that kind |
| Movement | You can show a position that changed, what changed it, and when |
| Transfer | Your claims about your own skill each have one piece of your own work behind them |
| Application | The current-issue entry is analysed with the tools, not decorated with them |

The third row is the one students skip and the one worth most. A term's
notebook in which nothing ever moved usually means the sources were chosen
to agree.

> [!warning] "I have improved a lot" is not evidence
> Nor is "I can evaluate sources now". Both of those are claims about you.
> The mark follows claims about work: which source you dropped, in which
> task, once you knew who had paid for it. [[Judging Your Own Work]] is
> where those lines get written down in the first place, which is why the
> self-check rows are worth copying into the notebook as you go.

Your own judgement of your work is not part of your mark, here or anywhere
else in the course — see [[How Marks Work]]. What the portfolio marks is
whether the record is real, precise, and honest about what moved.

Triangulation — the evidence you will not have unless you go and get it.

The portfolio arrives as a stack at the end of Unit 4, and by then every
decision that made it good or thin was taken months earlier. Both prompts
below are about catching those decisions while they are still happening.

OBSERVE — Unit 1, Day 20, the first portfolio milestone written in class
  Watch for: whose notebook is OPEN while they write, and whose is shut in
  a bag. The milestone entry asks how a position moved across the unit,
  which cannot be answered from memory — but it can be answered
  plausibly from memory, and a plausible entry written in one pass looks
  the same on the page in January as one written from three dated entries
  in front of it. This is the single cheapest thing to see all term and it
  is invisible in the finished set.
  Going well: pages turned backwards before anything is written; a student
  quoting their own October wording and disagreeing with it.
  Stuck: a notebook opened at a blank page and filled forwards; a student
  who cannot find the entry they are describing.
  Record: three columns on your day plan — open, shut, no notebook. Two
  minutes, whole class, and the pattern in Unit 1 predicts the stack in
  Unit 4 almost exactly.

TALK — Unit 4, Day 19, the closing portfolio class, while they write "Where
History Leads" with the notebooks in front of them
  This is the one period all term when every notebook is open in the room
  and the careers list is the thing being worked on, which is why the two
  questions belong here rather than earlier.
  Ask: "Open the notebook at October. What were you asking then that you
  would now say was the wrong shape of question?"
  Then: "Which of the jobs on that list actually does the thing you have
  got best at — and which one does the thing you avoid?"
  A strong answer names a real early entry and diagnoses it — a factual
  question sent after something only a comparison could settle, say — and
  then names a career by the WORK rather than by the title. That is A1.1
  and A2.4 heard in the same two minutes, and neither is safe to judge
  from the written entry alone: the reflection is written after the fact
  and every student knows what a good one is supposed to say.
  Record: initials and one phrase, on the class list, as you move on.

The product evidence is the stack, handed in on that same closing class —
which is exactly why the conversation has to happen while the notebooks are
still open in front of them, and not after you have taken them away.
