**Shown in the studio near the end of Unit 1.** Individual. 45–60
seconds of your own movement.

A short solo built from a vocabulary that is yours — not steps you were
taught, movement you generated.

## How it is built

1. **Choose a stimulus.** An object, a photograph, a piece of music, a
   line of text, a place. It goes in your journal with the date.
2. **Improvise from it**, three times, on three different days — the
   first of them guided, in class on Unit 1, Day 7 — and write down what
   came out each time. See [[Improvisation Warm-Ups]].
3. **Keep four or five movements** that feel like yours and are worth
   repeating.
4. **Order them**, decide what repeats, and fix the beginning and the
   end. A signature with no ending is unfinished.
5. **Name what the body is doing** — in your journal, movement by
   movement: which joints, in which direction, and what is taking the
   weight. Shapes are not enough; the skeleton is doing the work.

## What is being marked

| Criterion | What it looks like |
| --- | --- |
| Vocabulary | The movement is generated, not borrowed |
| Elements | You made deliberate choices about body, space, time, and energy |
| Repeatability | You can perform it twice the same way |
| Performance | The solo is danced full-out, and it holds together from the first move to the last |
| Anatomy | You can name what the body is doing — joints, not just shapes |

> [!tip] Distinct beats impressive
> A signature made of three clear movements you own is worth more here
> than eight difficult ones you cannot repeat. The clearest solos in this
> unit are usually the simplest.

[[The Elements of Dance]] is the vocabulary you are choosing from, and
[[Making a Movement Vocabulary]] is how the generating works.

TALK — Unit 1, Day 10, at the checkpoint in the working period, after each

  Ask: "Which joint is doing the most work in your third movement, and
  what stops it going further?"
  Then: "Put your movements in order of how much work the spine is doing.
  Which one is at the top, and what is it doing that the others are not?"

  its own movements by what the skeleton is being asked for rather than by
  how they look. That is C1.2. The
  journal from step 5 already names the joints, so this is not the only
  evidence — it is the evidence that tells you whether the naming is
  understood or copied out, which a page of correct terms never will.
  Record: one line per student on the class list, right then.
