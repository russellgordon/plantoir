Every task page carries its criteria, published before you start. Most
people read them once, at launch, and never again — and then hand in a
report that misses two rows they could have fixed in fifteen minutes.
This page is the fifteen minutes.

We do this together the first time, on a piece of work that belongs to
nobody in the room, so the move is not new to you when it is your own
being judged.

## The routine

1. Open the task page and put its criteria beside your draft.
2. Go row by row. For each one write **yes**, **not yet**, or **partly**
   — and one sentence of evidence: the graph, the line, the number a
   reader would look at. "Yes" with no evidence is a wish.
3. Find your weakest row. Not the easiest to fix — the weakest.
4. Write one sentence saying what you will change, and when.
5. Fix it, in the rest of this period or the next one. This is why the
   self-check is never scheduled on the day something is due: a
   judgement you have no time to act on is just a worry.

Steps 2 and 3 are the whole exercise. Naming your weakest row honestly is
harder than any of the fixing, because it means saying out loud the thing
you were hoping nobody would look at closely.

> [!example]- One row, judged honestly
> From [[Investigation Reports]], the row called *"The conclusion is
> justified, not just stated"*.
>
> **Partly.** I wrote $g = 9.4 \pm 0.4\ \text{m/s}^2$ and then said the
> accepted value was "confirmed". My range does not reach 9.8, so it is
> not confirmed — my result disagrees, and I have not said why.
> Weakest row. **Change:** rewrite the conclusion to say the two
> disagree, and name the one thing most likely to have pushed my number
> low. Next period.
>
> That entry is worth more than "looks fine" on all seven rows, and it
> took less time to write.

## What this is not

It is not a mark, and it never becomes one — see [[How Marks Work]].
Neither your judgement nor a classmate's is part of your percentage, and
that is deliberate: it cuts both ways, so nothing you write here can cost
you anything. That is exactly what makes it worth writing honestly.

What it changes is the work. In a physics report the weakest row is
almost always one of three: a number with no uncertainty, a graph whose
axes were chosen after the data was in, or a conclusion that claims more
than the measurement supports. All three are twenty-minute fixes once
they have been named, and unfixable once the report is handed in.

> [!tip] Read your judgement out loud to the person beside you
> It catches the rows where you were being generous. You will usually
> hear it yourself before they say anything.
