The closing entry. Roughly 400 words, written **in class**, in the last
twenty minutes of [[The Science Showcase]] — which is why it is one of the
three journal entries that carry a mark. Read the prompts below beforehand
and think about them; the writing itself happens in the room.

## What to cover

1. **The sentence you wrote in [[Your First Entry]]** about how science
   works. Quote it. Would you still write it? If not, what replaced it,
   and what changed it — a specific investigation, a discussion, an
   argument you lost.
2. **The investigation you would run again.** Not the one that went
   best. The one where you now know what you should have measured.
3. **A mechanism you can explain now that you could not at the start.**
   Explain it here, briefly, to a reader who has not taken the course.
4. **What you still do not understand.** Named precisely. "Refraction"
   is not precise; "why the bending depends on the wavelength when the
   geometry does not" is.
5. **What you are taking with you** if you never take another science
   course.

> [!question]- Prompts, if you are staring at a blank page
> When did you first argue with a result instead of accepting it?
> Which page on this site did you actually go back to more than once?
> What did you believe in the first unit that the fourth unit
> contradicted? Whose reasoning in this room changed yours, and on what?
> What would you tell somebody starting this course in the first week —
> not the encouraging version, the useful one?

## Who to write it for

Not for me. I was in the room; I already know what happened. Write it
for a student taking this course next year who is reading your journal
to find out what it is like and what to do differently.

That reader changes what belongs in it. They do not need "the optics
unit was interesting". They need "I spent two classes measuring before
I understood what the focal length was actually telling me, and the
thing that finally worked was drawing the ray diagram before touching
the lens."

> [!important] This is the entry I read first
> If you are short of time at the end of the semester, put it here. A
> thin journal with a sharp final reflection tells me more than a thick
> one that stops at description — and [[Journal Checklist]] weights it
> accordingly.
