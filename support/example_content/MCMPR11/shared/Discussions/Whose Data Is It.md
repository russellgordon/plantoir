Every time a Guardian logs a wildlife sighting, a stream temperature, or a
boat that shouldn't be where it is, that observation becomes data. Who
owns it, who can see it, and who decides what happens to it next is not
a side question — it shapes the whole system a programmer would build to
hold it.

### The Coastal Guardian Watchmen

Along BC's central and north coast and Haida Gwaii, First Nations
including Gitga'at, Gitxaała, Haida, Heiltsuk, Kitasoo Xai'xais,
Metlakatla, Nuxalk, and Wuikinuxv run Guardian Watchmen programs,
coordinated through the Coastal Stewardship Network — a program of
Coastal First Nations – Great Bear Initiative. Guardians patrol their
territories on the water, logging wildlife sightings, illegal fishing
and hunting activity, spawning salmon counts, stream conditions, and
more, on tablets in the field. That data feeds a coast-wide Regional
Monitoring System, and Nations receive regular reports back from it —
their own observations, returned in a form they can use to enforce their
own land and marine use plans.

The software choices behind a system like that — who hosts the data, who
has admin access, what gets shared automatically to an outside agency and
what stays local — are political choices before they are technical ones.

### When the data disagrees

In March 2015, the Heiltsuk Nation disputed a Fisheries and Oceans Canada
(DFO) stock forecast for central-coast herring that it believed was
inflated — a retired DFO scientist himself later said the forecast was
likely about double what it should have been. DFO moved to open a
commercial fishery on that forecast anyway. Heiltsuk members occupied the
DFO office near Bella Bella for three days; the fishery was ultimately
closed. The dispute wasn't really about who *had* data — both DFO and
Heiltsuk were working from fish counts — it was about whose count got
trusted, and who had the authority to act on it.

Programs like the Pacific Salmon Foundation's First Nations FSC Catch
Database Project point at one working answer: First Nations fisheries
organizations running their own catch-monitoring software, then
exchanging standardized reports with DFO on their own terms, rather than
handing raw data to an outside database they don't control.

### Discussion Prompts

1. **Whose count wins?** In the Heiltsuk case, a Nation's own monitoring
   contradicted a federal agency's forecast, and the agency proceeded
   anyway. If you were designing the database both organizations used,
   could software design choices affect whose numbers get treated as
   authoritative? How?
2. **Ownership vs. access.** The Guardian Watchmen's Regional Monitoring
   System returns data to the Nations that collected it. Compare that to
   a system where a government agency collects data *about* a Nation's
   territory but keeps the database itself. What is actually different
   about the two systems, in terms of what a Nation can do with the
   information?
3. **Designing for it.** If you were hired to build or upgrade a
   monitoring database for a coastal Nation's Guardian program, what
   would you need to ask before writing a single line of code? Think
   about hosting, admin permissions, what gets shared automatically to
   outside agencies, and what a Guardian should be able to see that a
   government analyst should not.

### How we run this

Run this one as a fishbowl.

- Set five or six chairs in a small circle in the centre of the room;
  everyone else sits in a larger circle around them.
- The inner circle discusses the three prompts above for about twelve
  minutes. Outer-circle students say nothing, but each picks one
  inner-circle speaker to track and writes down one assumption that
  speaker made and never had to defend.
- Swap: a new inner circle forms, picking up from where the first group
  left off rather than starting over.
- After the second round, outer-circle students read out one tracked
  assumption each — this is where the discussion gets specific instead
  of general.
- Close as a whole class: does anyone want to revise their answer to
  prompt 3 after hearing the fishbowl?
