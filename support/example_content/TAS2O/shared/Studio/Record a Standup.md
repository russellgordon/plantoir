Thirty seconds, to camera, on one real story: script it tight, frame
it clean, deliver it twice, and pick the better take. The standup is
how a reporter appears in their own reporting, and it is the building
block of the online news program channel — every episode is standups
and sequences, stitched. Today you make your first one broadcastable.

> [!warning] Care and consent notes
> **Strap before the bag opens**, and on a tripod the camera never
> stands unattended — plate locked, legs spread, one person within
> arm's reach always. **Your background has people in it** — anyone
> recognisable behind you is in your shot without asking to be, so
> choose an angle where bystanders are distant, or get their
> [[Releases and Consent|consent]]. **Headphones at moderate
> volume**. **Both takes back up before the card clears**;
> [[Backing Up Your Media]] as always.

## What you need

- [ ] The class camera on a tripod, and a microphone if the kit has one
- [ ] A real story from your beat — thirty seconds needs true material
- [ ] Your script, written and cut twice before you reach the tripod
- [ ] A teammate to operate camera and call the countdown

## The work

1. **Script for the mouth, not the page.** Read it aloud and cut
   every stumble. Thirty seconds is about seventy-five spoken words,
   and the news goes first — [[The Inverted Pyramid]] as always.
2. **Frame yourself clean.** Reporter on a third, eyes a third from
   the top, nothing sprouting from your head —
   [[Composition and Framing]] does not pause for the person being you.
3. **Check the light and the sound before take one.** Face toward
   the light, never silhouetted, and confirm levels on your first
   sentence — a perfect delivery with unusable audio is a rehearsal.
4. **Memorise the map, not the words.** Know your first and last
   sentences cold; between them, know the three beats — a reporter
   who knows the story can survive a stumble.
5. **Deliver it twice, all the way through.** Push through mistakes
   to the end both times — recovering on camera is a skill, and take
   two is always looser than take one.
6. **Pick the better take out loud.** Watch both with your operator
   and name why the winner wins — energy, a cleaner stumble, better
   eyes. Judging your own takes is how every anchor got watchable.

## What can go wrong

- **Seventy-five words become one hundred and twenty.** The script
  never met a timer — cut the setup; the standup starts where the
  news starts.
- **Eyes keep dropping to the script.** The map was not memorised —
  put the paper down; three beats will carry you.
- **Great take, humming fridge.** Nobody wore headphones during the
  level check. The room is part of the recording, every time.

## Level up

Record a third take walking — two steps toward the camera on your key
sentence. Movement adds energy and difficulty in equal measure, which
is why reporters practise it before they need it.
