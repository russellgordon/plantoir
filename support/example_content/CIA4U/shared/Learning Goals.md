What you should be able to do by the end of CIA4U, in plain words first

## The two rules this course marks by

**An argument is judged by its evidence and by the assumptions it
states.** Not by how strongly it is held, and not by whose side it is
on. If you have not said what your argument assumes, you have not
finished making it.

**Every figure carries a year and a source.** A number without a date is
not evidence in economics, because the thing it measures has already
moved. Name the table or the series, not just the institution.

## By the end of the course you should be able to

- **Build a model and say what it holds still** — supply and demand,
  costs and the firm, aggregate supply and demand, comparative
  advantage — and choose between them for a given question.
- **Retrieve a Canadian economic figure yourself**, from Statistics
  Canada, the Bank of Canada, or a federal budget, and cite it so that
  someone else can find the same number.
- **Separate a positive claim from a normative one**, and say which kind
  of disagreement you are actually having.
- **Separate efficiency from fairness**, and admit which one your
  evidence speaks to.
- **Analyse a current Canadian economic issue** — housing, tariffs,
  productivity, inflation, labour, inequality — with a model, real data,
  and a recommendation whose cost you name.
- **Represent fairly a position you disagree with**, in its strongest
  form.

## The self-check that matters

Hand yourself an economic claim you have never seen. Can you say what
model is behind it, what data would test it, what year that data is
from, and what it assumes? That is the whole course, and it is exactly
what [[The Data Examination]] asks.
