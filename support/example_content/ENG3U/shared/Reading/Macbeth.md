> [!abstract] At a glance
> **William Shakespeare**, around 1606 · the shortest of the tragedies ·
> read across sixteen classes, aloud, and seminared

> [!warning] What this text deals with
> Murder, including of a child; the psychological collapse of two
> people; suicide. The violence is mostly offstage and the play is
> unrelenting about consequence.

## Before we start

Three things worth holding:

**It is short and it accelerates.** Scenes get briefer, the murders get
faster, and the play's form performs the narrowing of Macbeth's options.

**The supernatural is unresolved.** The witches predict; the play never
confirms that they cause. Banquo hears the same prophecy and does
nothing — which is the play controlling a variable, per
[[Character as Argument]].

**It was written for a king.** James VI and I claimed descent from
Banquo and had written about witchcraft. That does not settle what the
play means; it does explain some of what it is careful about.

## What to track from Act 1

| Thread | Watch for |
| --- | --- |
| Blood | Where it is washable and where it is not |
| Sleep | Who sleeps, who cannot, and what the play says that costs |
| Clothing | Borrowed robes, ill-fitting titles |
| Equivocation | Statements true in one sense and false in another — the play's obsession |
| The marriage | Who finishes whose lines, and when that stops |
| Light and dark | Who calls for darkness, and what they want it to hide |

## Questions we will argue about

1. Do the witches cause anything? What would count as evidence either
   way?
2. Whose tragedy is this — Macbeth's, Scotland's, or Lady Macbeth's?
3. The play gives us the murderer's inside and not the victims'. What
   does that do to an audience?
4. Is "tomorrow, and tomorrow, and tomorrow" recognition, or the absence
   of it?
