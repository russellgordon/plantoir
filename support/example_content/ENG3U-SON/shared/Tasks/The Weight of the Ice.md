Build an argument essay on *Indian Horse* around ONE idea that develops
across the whole novel — a symbol, a theme, a change in Saul himself. Track
it from where it starts to where it ends, and argue for what that
development means.

About 800–1000 words. Longer than Unit 1's essay because the evidence base
is bigger — a whole novel, not two short stories — but the same discipline
applies: every paragraph earns its place.

## Success criteria

| Quality | What it looks like |
|---|---|
| Tracks a development | Your idea isn't static — you show it changing, and explain what the change means |
| Evidence from across the novel | Not all from one part — at least one moment from early, middle, and late in the book |
| MLA in-text citation | See [[MLA In-Text Citation]] — every quotation cited correctly |
| Revised, not just proofread | The Day 38 conference and Day 35 checkpoint visibly changed something between your outline and your final draft |
| Literary present | See [[Writing About Literature]] |

## How to work

1. **Day 33 — launch.** Choose your idea and start a thesis.
2. **Day 34 — MLA In-Text Citation** workshop — you'll need this before you
   draft.
3. **Day 35 — thesis and outline.** I'll circulate and give real feedback
   on both before you write a word of the draft.
4. **Day 36 — revise your outline** based on Day 35's feedback, then start
   drafting your first body paragraph.
5. **Day 37 — draft day.** Finish the draft.
6. **Day 38 — peer conference.** Structured feedback from a partner; I'll
   also confer individually with a few students each period across the
   unit's essays.
7. **Day 39 — revise and self-assess.** Act on Day 38's feedback, then
   check your own draft against [[Judging Your Own Work]] — this is your
   SECOND time using it on your own, without the walkthrough from Unit 1.
8. **Day 40 — final polish and hand in.**

## The Reading Note

Attach a short Reading Note (5-8 sentences, written Day 40) to your essay:
which reading strategy actually helped you track your idea across a
300-page novel, and how did what you noticed as a reader shape the thesis
you ended up arguing? This is marked separately from the essay, on the
specificity of what you name — not on how much you enjoyed the book.
