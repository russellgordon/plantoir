In a working newsroom, ongoing assignments are called **beats** — and
that is what these tasks are. Not exercises to be marked and filed:
real coverage, with real readers at the other end of every byline.

Each task page publishes its success criteria before you start,
phrased as things a reader or an editor could see — and those criteria
describe the published work, never the person who made it.
[[How Marks Work]] explains where the five tasks and
[[Publication Day]] sit in your mark, every criteria table honours
[[Our Newsroom Standards]], and everything traces back to our
[[Learning Goals]].

Two of the six are made in the shop rather than the newsroom, with a
user at the other end instead of a reader. They assume one laser
cutter and one 3D printer shared by the whole class —
[[How the Newsroom Runs]] lists what the room needs.

## The six tasks

- [[Your First Byline]] — report, write, edit, and publish one short
  real news story from school life, with your name on it.
- [[The Athletics Package]] — cover real school athletics as a team
  with rotating roles: recap, captioned photos, and a social post.
- [[The Sign Shop]] — design a laser-cut sign for a real person who
  is not you, prototype it in cardboard, test it at distance, and
  cut the final one.
- [[The Rig]] — solve a problem your Unit 2 crew actually had with a
  3D-printed part: model it, print it, test it against numbers, and
  print it better.
- [[The Front Page]] — the term's real stories laid out for print and
  site, with the hierarchy argued out loud.
- [[Publication Day]] — the newsroom publishes its term for invited
  readers, across all four channels.
