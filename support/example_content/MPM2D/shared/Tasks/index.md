Every task runs over several classes, and using that working time well
is part of the evidence. Each page states its success criteria up
front, as things an observer could see in your work — reasoning,
growth, and communication, never speed, as [[How Marks Work]] explains.
Every criteria table traces back to our [[Learning Goals]].

## The five tasks

- [[Break-Even]] — pairs invent a small venture, model its costs and
  revenue as a linear system, and pitch what the crossing point means.
- [[The Quadrilateral Case File]] — groups receive four vertices and
  must prove, beyond reasonable doubt, exactly what shape they hold.
- [[The Perfect Arc]] — photograph a real arc, model it in vertex form,
  and interrogate what the model can and cannot claim.
- [[Inaccessible Heights]] — teams measure three unreachable heights,
  each by two independent methods, and defend an error bracket.
- [[The Math Symposium]] — the culminating task: one curated exhibit,
  one growth statement, one defended conversation.

The first four are the semester's evidence. The symposium and the
[[Final Examination]] together are the final evaluation at the end of
the course — [[How Marks Work]] explains how the two halves fit.
