A list of school-plausible events goes up, one at a time, rapid fire.
For each, the room calls it: news, or not? The verdict is worth
nothing — the WHY is everything. Every defence must name one of the
seven tests from [[News Values]], out loud, until it is reflex.

## How to run it

1. Reveal one event. Thumbs up or down on a count of three.
2. One voice per side, thirty seconds each. Name a test or sit down.
3. If the room splits near the middle, stop — that item is the lesson.
4. Six to eight items, then done. Speed is part of the training.

> [!example]- One round, called honestly (click to expand)
> - *The cafeteria adds a new vending machine.* Not news — novelty
>   only, and barely. Nobody's Tuesday changes.
> - *The caretaker retires after thirty years.* News — proximity,
>   prominence, human interest. Three tests, one story.
> - *Exam-week library hours double.* News — timeliness and impact:
>   hundreds of readers, and useful only if published NOW.
> - *A teacher's cousin wins a lottery elsewhere.* Not news here —
>   no proximity, no impact. Interesting is not the same as news.

## One variation

Students bring the items — two each, one clearly news, one clearly
not, and the room sorts them. A good decoy takes the same judgement.

> [!tip] Protect the unpopular verdict
> The student who votes "not news" against the whole room and names
> a real test is doing editing, not contrarianism. Every strong
> [[One-Minute Pitch]] starts with someone willing to call "not
> news" on their own idea first.
