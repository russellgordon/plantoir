> [!warning] This replaced the 2019 course, and it was revised in place
> The 2024 policy supersedes *The Ontario Curriculum, Grade 10: Career
> Studies, 2019*, and the code did not change — so a resource written for
> 2019 looks current and is not. The Ministry's own change note names the
> difference: **A1.2 was revised and A1.3 is new**, both concerning mental
> health. A cross-check against the 2019 document matched 25 of 27
> expectations, and the two that differ are exactly those.

## What this course is, and how long it runs

Career Studies is a **half credit — 55 hours**, normally taken in the
same semester as Civics and Citizenship rather than across a full one.
In this example course it runs across the first half of the semester, and the
Civics and Citizenship course (CHV2O) picks up the same period the school
day after it ends.

> [!note] One honest gap
> The credit value is **not stated in the curriculum policy itself**.
> The Ministry's Career Studies fact sheet calls it a compulsory half
> credit, and Ontario's secondary schools policy defines a half credit as
> 55 hours, but the curriculum document does not say so. This site treats
> it as a half credit on that basis, and says so here rather than
> implying the policy states it.

## A wording oddity, reproduced rather than corrected

The stem above `A2` reads **"Throughout *the* course, students will:"**
while strand A and `A1` read **"Throughout *this* course"**. That is what
the source prints, so it is what these pages print.

## Why the codes matter more than they look

A code is a shared address. When a student asks "why are we doing this?",
the answer is a link. When a department writes a course outline, the
codes are what make one person's planning legible to another.
