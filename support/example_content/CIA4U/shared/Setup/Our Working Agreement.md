Economic questions are political questions with numbers attached. Taxation,
unions, immigration, resource development, the size of government — the
households in this room hold genuinely different views on all of it, and
those views arrive with you. That is most of what makes the course worth
taking, and it only works under an agreement. This is ours.

## Argue with the claim, not the person

- Take on the claim and the evidence behind it. "That estimate depends on a
  comparison group I do not find credible" is an argument. "You only think
  that because of where you live" is not. Let people finish, particularly
  when you are certain they are wrong.
- **Say which kind of disagreement you are having.** A positive one is about
  what is true, and evidence can in principle settle it. A normative one is
  about what should be, and no dataset settles it. Most arguments that go
  nowhere are two people having different ones at each other.
- **Changing your mind out loud, because the evidence went against you, is
  the most respected thing anyone does in this room** — and it is what I am
  listening for in a seminar, far more than for who won it.

## What is on this syllabus is somebody's Tuesday

The abstractions here are not abstract for everyone. Somebody's parent works
in a tariffed industry, somebody's family is on a waiting list for housing,
in some households the grocery bill is the binding constraint on every other
decision, and in some the regulation being argued about is the family's.

- **Nobody is required to disclose their family's finances.** Not ever, not
  for any reason, not to make a point land harder or to prove one sincere.
- No argument here may be answered with a demand that someone reveal their
  circumstances. If you are about to ask, ask the claim instead.
- You never have to share an experience. "Pass" is a complete answer and it
  costs you nothing.

## My views are not the answer key

I hold positions on most of this and you will probably work out what they
are. Marks never depend on agreeing with them. What earns marks is an
argument that names its evidence, states its assumptions and deals fairly
with the other side's strongest version — and a well-built case against my
own view marks higher than a weak one that flatters it.

> [!warning] When a discussion lands close to home
> Some of this will sit near something real for you — a layoff, a rent
> increase, a family business, a status file still open. Tell me before or
> after, not necessarily why, and we will arrange it: a different role, notice
> before the topic arrives, or sitting one out. That is a professional
> arrangement, not a favour, and nobody else's business.
