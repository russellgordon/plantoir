The first entry in your [[Drama Journal]] is the hardest one, because the
page is blank and you do not yet trust what belongs on it. So for week one
only, the entry is guided: answer the three prompts below, a few sentences
each, and you are done. Ten minutes, written the same day as class.

## The three prompts

1. **What we did** — the facts, briefly. Which warm-up, which convention,
   what your group was asked to make.
2. **What surprised me** — one specific moment you did not expect. In you,
   in a classmate, in the room.
3. **What I am nervous or curious about** — name it plainly. This course
   asks people to do unusual things in front of each other; noticing how
   that sits with you *is* the work.

## What honest looks like

These fragments are the register to aim for — plain, specific, true:

> "When we froze for the first tableau I laughed and wrecked it. Not
> because it was funny. I think I laugh when everyone can see me."

> "Maya just decided to be the mother in our group without asking and it
> worked. I would never volunteer like that. I kind of want to be able to."

> "The room went actually silent during the last group's freeze and it
> felt different than silence usually feels. I do not have a word for it
> yet."

None of these is polished, and none is performing for a reader. Each one
names a real moment and stays with it — which already beats a page of
"drama was fun today, I learned a lot."

> [!important] Nobody reads this but me
> As [[Drama Journal]] promises: entries are private. I read them; other
> students never do, and nothing you admit here is ever quoted, projected,
> or used as an example without your explicit okay. The nervousness you
> name in week one becomes your best evidence of growth by the end of the course — but
> only you will decide if anyone else ever hears about it. This is
> [[Why Trust Comes First]], applied to the page instead of the stage.
