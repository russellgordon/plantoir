## What you are making

A Fermi question asks for a number nobody can look up — how many breaths
will you take in your lifetime? how much water does this school drink in
a year? — and rewards reasoning, not recall. Your pair will answer
three: one assigned to you, one chosen from a class menu, and one you
invent yourselves.

Each question gets a poster panel showing the whole chain of reasoning —
every assumption named, every step visible, in the spirit of
[[Showing Your Thinking]] — ending in an estimate written in
[[Scientific Notation|scientific notation]] with an honest error
bracket: "we believe the true value sits between $10^7$ and $10^9$."
The bracket is not hedging — it is the mathematics of knowing how sure
you are.

## How to work

1. Start with the assigned question. Break it into pieces small enough
   to estimate — a per-person amount, a per-day amount — and anchor the
   big numbers the way [[How Big Is a Million]] did.
2. Write every assumption down as you make it. "We assumed 16 breaths
   per minute" is a step in the chain, not a confession.
3. For the menu question, each partner estimates alone first, then
   compare — exactly as in [[Estimation Duels]]. Where your answers
   differ, the difference points at an assumption worth arguing about.
4. Invent your third question together. The best ones come from things
   you genuinely wonder about — no question is too odd if answering it
   needs a chain of reasoning.
5. Convert each final estimate to scientific notation, then argue the
   bracket: how far off could your shakiest assumption make you?
6. On festival day, half the room presents while half walks the gallery
   asking questions; then we swap.

All three panels are built in class. That is deliberate: work that
carries a mark is done where I can see it being made and talk to you
while you make it — [[How Marks Work]] explains why. Before the gallery
walk you will judge your own panels against the table below, using
[[Judging Your Own Work]], with the rest of that period left for fixing
your weakest row.

## What is marked as yours

The panels are your pair's; the mark is not shared. Two things are
judged as yours alone, and both happen on gallery day: the **half-page
you write in the Reflect section below**, and the answers you give to
visitors about the steps you built. Your partner cannot write the
half-page for you, and a visitor asking "why that number?" is asking
the person standing beside the panel.

## Success criteria

| Quality | What it looks like on your panel |
| --- | --- |
| Visible chain | A visitor can follow every step from question to estimate |
| Named assumptions | Each guess is written down and roughly defended |
| Rates, kept honest | Every step is a quantity per something, and the units survive the multiplying |
| Scientific notation | Final values are written as $a \times 10^n$, correctly |
| Honest brackets | The error range follows from the shakiest assumptions |
| Invented question | Your own question genuinely needs a reasoning chain |
| Gallery conversation | You can answer "why that number?" for any step |
| Your own account | Your half-page names an estimate you would defend, and what could sink it |

## Reflect

Before the panels come down, each partner writes half a page: which of
your three estimates would you defend most confidently, and which single
assumption could most easily make you wrong by a factor of ten? Honest
answers here are worth more than confident ones.

> [!success]- If your pair is stuck (click to expand)
> Stuck pairs almost always try to estimate the whole thing in one leap.
> Shrink the question instead: one person, one day. Estimate that, then
> multiply your way back up — and write every multiplication down as a
> step in the chain.
