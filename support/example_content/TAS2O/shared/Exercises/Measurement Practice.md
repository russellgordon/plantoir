These questions follow the millimetres-and-inches section of
[[Doing the Numbers]] and the morning you measured a phone, a tripod
screw, and a sheet of plywood with calipers. One number carries all of
them: 1 inch = 25.4 mm.

## Questions

1. The screw on top of a camera tripod is ¼″-20. How wide is it in
   millimetres, and what does the "20" mean?
2. A phone's maker lists it as 71.5 mm wide. How wide is that in
   inches, to two decimal places?
3. A sheet sold as "⅛ inch plywood" measures 2.90 mm, 3.00 mm, and
   2.95 mm in three places. What would you write down as its
   thickness — and how far is it from what the label promised?
4. A teacher asks for a door sign "about six inches by four". What
   size do you draw it in millimetres?
5. **The kerf.** You draw a square exactly 50.00 mm on a side and cut
   it from plywood. The calipers say the square came out 49.82 mm.
   How wide is the laser's kerf on this material?
6. **Challenge.** The class tolerance coupon shows that a peg slides
   into a hole only when the hole is drawn at least 0.2 mm wider than
   the peg. You want a printed slot that a sheet of the plywood from
   question 3 slides into snugly. How wide do you draw the slot, and
   why not simply draw it at 3.175 mm?

## Answers

> [!success]- Answer 1
> 0.25 × 25.4 = **6.35 mm** across. The "20" is the thread pitch: 20
> threads to every inch along the screw — which is why a metric bolt of
> almost the same width will not screw in.

> [!success]- Answer 2
> 71.5 ÷ 25.4 ≈ **2.81 inches**. Divide to go from millimetres to
> inches; multiply to go the other way.

> [!success]- Answer 3
> The average of the three: (2.90 + 3.00 + 2.95) ÷ 3 = **2.95 mm**.
> The label's ⅛ inch is 3.175 mm, so the real sheet is about 0.23 mm
> thinner — small, and exactly the size of the gaps that make a slot
> rattle.

> [!success]- Answer 4
> 6 × 25.4 = 152.4 mm and 4 × 25.4 = 101.6 mm. "About" invites a
> sensible round number, so **150 mm × 100 mm** is a good answer — say
> which you chose and why.

> [!success]- Answer 5
> The square lost 50.00 − 49.82 = 0.18 mm across. Each edge lost half
> the kerf, and a width has two edges, so the whole kerf came off once:
> the kerf is about **0.18 mm**. Measure it on your own test card — it
> changes with the material and the settings.

> [!success]- Answer 6
> Start from the sheet you measured, not the label: 2.95 mm + 0.2 mm
> clearance = **3.15 mm**. Drawn at 3.175 mm plus clearance, the slot
> would be close to 0.4 mm wider than the real sheet, and the sign would
> wobble in it. Measure the thing, then add the tolerance the coupon
> taught you.
