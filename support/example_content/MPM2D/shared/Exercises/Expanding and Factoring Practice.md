These questions follow [[Expanding and Factoring]] — the area model
behind every one was built at the boards in [[The Border Problem]].
Expanding and factoring are the same rectangle read in opposite
directions, and the model is not training wheels: it is the reason.

## Questions

1. Expand and simplify $3x(2x - 5)$.
2. Expand and simplify $(x + 4)(x + 7)$.
3. Expand and simplify $(2x - 3)^2$. Watch the middle term.
4. Factor $6x^2 + 15x$.
5. Factor $x^2 - 5x - 24$.
6. Factor $2x^2 + 11x + 5$.
7. Factor each difference of squares: (a) $9x^2 - 25$;
   (b) $4x^2 - 36$ — but look for a common factor first.
8. **Explain with the area model.** Draw a rectangle with sides
   $x + 3$ and $x + 5$, split into four regions. Explain where each
   term of the expansion lives — and why factoring runs it backwards.
9. **From factors to intercepts.** Factor the quadratic relation
   $y = x^2 - 2x - 8$ into the form $y = a(x - r)(x - s)$ to state its
   zeros ($x$-intercepts), determine the axis of symmetry, and sketch.

## Answers

> [!success]- Answer 1
> $6x^2 - 15x$. The $3x$ multiplies *both* terms — a one-row area
> model, if you want to see it.

> [!success]- Answer 2
> $x^2 + 7x + 4x + 28 = x^2 + 11x + 28$. Four products, then collect
> the two middle strips.

> [!success]- Answer 3
> $(2x-3)(2x-3) = 4x^2 - 6x - 6x + 9 = 4x^2 - 12x + 9$. The middle
> term appears *twice* — writing $4x^2 + 9$ is the classic square
> trap, and the check $x = 1$ exposes it: $(2-3)^2 = 1$, not $13$.

> [!success]- Answer 4
> $3x(2x + 5)$. Verify by expanding it straight back. ✓

> [!success]- Answer 5
> Two numbers multiplying to $-24$, adding to $-5$: $-8$ and $3$, so
> $(x - 8)(x + 3)$. Check $x = 1$: $(-7)(4) = -28 = 1 - 5 - 24$. ✓

> [!success]- Answer 6
> $(2x + 1)(x + 5)$. The outer and inner products, $10x$ and $x$,
> rebuild $11x$ ✓ — expanding to verify takes ten seconds.

> [!success]- Answer 7
> (a) $(3x - 5)(3x + 5)$ — the middle terms cancel by design.
> (b) Common factor first: $4(x^2 - 9) = 4(x - 3)(x + 3)$. Jumping
> straight to $(2x - 6)(2x + 6)$ works but hides the finished factor.

> [!success]- Answer 8
> The regions are $x^2$, $5x$, $3x$, and $15$: total $x^2 + 8x + 15$,
> each term a region — the square, two strips merging into $8x$, the
> corner. Factoring hands you the area and asks for the sides: the
> same rectangle, rebuilt — the move [[The Border Problem]] made visible.

> [!success]- Answer 9
> Factoring gives $y = (x - 4)(x + 2)$, so $r = 4$ and $s = -2$: the
> zeros ($x$-intercepts) sit at $x = 4$ and $x = -2$. The axis of
> symmetry runs halfway between them at $x = \frac{4 + (-2)}{2} = 1$,
> giving vertex $(1, -9)$ and $y$-intercept $-8$. Factored form
> $y = a(x - r)(x - s)$ displays the zeros directly — the connection
> [[Factors and Intercepts]] explores.
