> [!abstract] At a glance
> Whole class plus invited guests · one class period · four units of
> thinking, presented and defended live

## What you are making

The symposium is the finale: every pair hosts a station, and invited
guests — other teachers, administrators, family — work the room the
way visitors work a science fair. Your station's centrepiece is the
flight plan from [[The Flight Path]], surrounded by one artefact
from each earlier unit: the speed-camera verdict, the landing
profile, the package. Every guest asks the same two questions, at
every station: *where is the mathematics doing the work?* and *where
does the model stop?* You finish with a five-minute tour you can give
repeatedly, a one-page handout, and answers to whatever the room
throws at you.

## Milestones

- [ ] Station materials made: the flight plan, one artefact per unit,
      one-page handout
- [ ] The five-minute tour rehearsed until it survives interruptions
- [ ] The two symposium questions answered in writing, by you and not by
      your pair, in the period set aside for it the class before
- [ ] The two-minute tour given to me before the doors open
- [ ] [[Final Reflection]] drafted in the class straight after, while
      the day is still fresh

## How it is assessed

Per [[How Marks Work]], communication is the evidence today: precise
vocabulary, representations chosen for the audience in front of you,
and honesty about each model's limits. It is a shared room and a
shared station, but the mark is not shared — what is judged is **your**
telling of it, given by you, and **your** written answers to the two
questions, drafted before you ever say them out loud.

The tour I mark is the one you give **me**, two minutes, at your own
station in the quarter of an hour before the doors open. Once the guests
arrive I am hosting a room and could not honestly claim to have heard
any one of you properly — so the version that decides a mark is the one
where I am standing in front of you with nothing else to do.

Guests leave written notes at your station. Those are for you: no
visitor's opinion, and no classmate's, is part of anybody's mark.
[[Final Reflection]] closes your [[Math Journal]] in the next class —
read, written back to, and never scored.

## Success criteria

| Quality | What it looks like in your work |
| --- | --- |
| A clear story | Somebody who was not in those four units can follow them |
| Precision | Vocabulary and notation used correctly, aloud |
| Grace under fire | Questions answered, not deflected |
| Honest limits | Every model's edges volunteered, not extracted |
| Yours alone, on paper | Your own written answers to the two questions, drafted before you say them |
| Yours alone, out loud | The two-minute tour you give me before the doors open |

> [!success]- If nerves are the obstacle
> The tour is a conversation, not a recital. You have been answering
> these two questions since the first week; the guests are simply
> new people asking them. Rehearse once with someone patient, then
> trust the preparation.

Triangulation — the evidence you will not have unless you go and get it.

Symposium day itself is no use to you for this. You are hosting, guests
are moving, and you will not hear two consecutive sentences from any one
student. Everything below therefore happens on Unit 4, Day 20 — the
working period the day before, and the last moment any of it can still
change what a station shows.

OBSERVE — Unit 4, Day 20, while the tour gets timed
  What goes on the table is prescribed: the flight plan, one artefact per
  unit, a handout. What is NOT prescribed is what survives five minutes,
  and every tour runs long the first time. Watch what a pair cuts. Cutting
  a page of algebra and keeping the result is a judgement about what a
  result IS; cutting the interpretation to keep the working is the
  opposite one, and the handout that arrives tomorrow looks tidy either
  way.
  Going well: a pair times itself, hears eight minutes, and drops three
  lines of algebra rather than the sentence about what the model
  ignores.
  Stuck: nothing cut, the tour read faster, and a handout that is a
  contents page.
  Record: one word per pair on the class list — what they cut. That word
  is B2.5's "interpret and communicate the results" being decided in
  front of you, and it is the only place the decision is visible.

TALK — Unit 4, Day 20, during the twenty minutes they spend writing
  This is the one quiet stretch of the day, and it is the only reason
  these conversations fit at all: while the room is writing its answers,
  you are free. Take students out one at a time, two minutes each, and
  accept that twenty minutes buys you eight or nine of them — so go to
  the students you have the least conversation evidence for and let the
  rest wait for the examination review.
  Do not use the two questions every station must answer. They are
  printed on this page, they are being written in front of you, and by
  the time you ask them the answers are recitations.
  Ask: "A guest has two minutes and stops at one of your four pieces at
  random. Which one would you least like them to pick, and what would you
  say about it anyway?"
  Then: "Pick one of your four pieces and tell me what is changing in it,
  and with respect to what. Now tell me whether that is true of any of
  the others."
  A strong second answer names both the quantity and the variable without
  hesitating, and then notices that three of the four are rates of change
  wearing different clothes: how fast the car was travelling, how fast
  the aircraft was falling, and how the material used changes as the box
  gets taller. That is A1.1 heard — real applications of rates of change,
  described rather than computed, which is what A1.1 asks for. A strong
  first answer is simply an honest one: a student who can name the
  weakest corner of their own station and still has something true to say
  about it is showing you more than the tour will.
  Record: two ticks per student on the class list, and the piece each one
  named as their weakest.

C2.8 is product evidence here — the dot and cross product work is on the
flight plan, and it holds still. The rest of the product is the station,
the one-page handout, and the written answers, on Unit 4, Day 21.
