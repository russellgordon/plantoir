Record a two-minute interview with audio an editor can actually use —
which means the work starts before anyone asks a question. Room
choice, microphone distance, and a level check decide the recording's
fate before it starts; the questions lean on [[Interviewing]], and
the sound leans on everything in [[Clean Audio]].

> [!warning] Care and consent notes
> **Consent goes on the recording** — ask, on tape, "Do you agree to
> be recorded for the class news site?" and get the yes before
> question one; [[Releases and Consent]] explains why. **Microphones
> are precision instruments** — never blow into one or tap it; speak
> to it instead. **Cables coil loosely** and go back counted.
> **Headphone volume stays moderate** — your hearing is part of the
> kit. **Back up before the card clears**, same day, every time.

## What you need

- [ ] The class audio recorder or microphone kit, batteries checked
- [ ] Headphones — monitoring is not optional
- [ ] A teammate, and your [[Interview Question Practice]] questions
- [ ] A quiet room you chose on purpose

## The work

1. **Choose the room with your ears, not your eyes.** Stand silent
   for thirty seconds and listen: fans, hallway noise, hum. Soft
   surfaces — curtains, carpet, coats — beat hard echoing walls.
2. **Set microphone distance to a hand's width.** Close enough that
   the voice beats the room, far enough to survive laughter.
3. **Run a level check on a real answer.** Ask a throwaway question
   and watch the meter — peaks healthy but never touching the top.
   Too quiet drowns in hiss when boosted; too hot distorts forever.
4. **Record the silence before and after.** Ten seconds of the empty
   room at each end. That "room tone" is what editors patch pauses
   and cuts with — silence from a different room never matches.
5. **Ask, then actually listen.** One question, then quiet. Nod
   instead of "mm-hm" — agreement noises stacked on the good quote
   cannot be removed.
6. **Monitor the whole take.** Headphones on for all two minutes. If
   a chair scrapes, finish the thought, then ask again — a retake now
   costs thirty seconds; a ruined answer found later costs it all.

## What can go wrong

- **Fine in the room, terrible in the file.** You monitored with your
  ears, not the headphones — the mic hears the fan and the hallway
  you stopped hearing weeks ago.
- **Levels set on a shy answer.** People speak up once the real
  questions start, and the meter distorts — check levels on a story,
  not a yes.
- **No room tone.** The interview is usable but the edit is not —
  every cut pops. Ten seconds of silence is audio's cheapest
  insurance.

## Level up

Record the same interview in the worst room you can find and play
both openings for your desk — nothing teaches room choice faster
than hearing the same voice lose.
