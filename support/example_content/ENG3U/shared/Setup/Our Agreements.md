## What we agree to

1. **Attack the idea, never the person.**
2. **Bring the passage.** At this level a claim without evidence is not
   yet a claim.
3. **Steelman before you object.** State the strongest version of the
   position you are about to argue against.
4. **You may pass**, without explanation.
5. **Changing your mind out loud is the best thing that happens in this
   room.**
6. **What is said about somebody's own life stays here.**

## The texts, and what they deal with

This year includes a play about murder and the corrosion of a marriage, a
novel about grief, addiction, and the aftermath of residential schools,
and non-fiction about how this country was built. Every text page names
what it deals with.

You may step out of any class. You need not explain, and it costs you
nothing.

## Reading Indigenous writing

[[Monkey Beach]] is by Eden Robinson, who is Haisla and Heiltsuk, and is
set in Kitamaat. We name the nation, we use the words the writer uses,
and we do not convert what a family knows into a symbol for something
else because that is easier to write essays about. Where the novel
declines to explain something, that refusal is part of the text.

The non-fiction unit reads Thomas King's *The Inconvenient Indian*
alongside it, which argues about exactly this — how stories about
Indigenous people have been told in North America, and by whom. King was
read as a Cherokee writer for his whole career, and in November 2025 he
accepted genealogical findings that he has no Indigenous ancestry. We
keep the book and we stop calling him an Indigenous writer, and the
question that leaves open is one we work on rather than one we get past.
[[The Inconvenient Indian]] sets it out before you read a word of him.

## Using AI honestly

It can produce an essay about *Macbeth* in seconds, and every assessment
here measures the reading and thinking that essay skipped.

| Fine | Not fine |
| --- | --- |
| Definitions, historical context, a word you are reaching for | An interpretation, a thesis, a paragraph |
| Quizzing yourself before a seminar | Generating seminar questions |
| Checking a citation format | Producing the citation from a source you have not read |

Say where you used it and how, in a line at the end.
