The five tasks below run over several classes each, and using that
working time well is part of the evidence; the examination at the end is
its own kind of day. Each page states its success criteria up front, as
things an observer could see in your work — reasoning, growth, and
communication, never speed, as [[How Marks Work]] explains. Every
criteria table traces back to our [[Learning Goals]].

## The five tasks

- [[The Speed Camera]] — defend or overturn a speeding ticket from
  position–time data, by closing average rates onto the instant.
- [[Smooth Landing]] — design a descent profile whose height,
  velocity, and acceleration all behave at touchdown.
- [[The Packaging Brief]] — optimize a real package under material and
  shelf constraints, and say honestly what "best" cost.
- [[The Flight Path]] — fly a heading through wind with vectors, then
  land where the approach line meets the runway plane.
- [[The Math Symposium]] — the finale: threads from all four units,
  presented and defended before invited guests.

## And then the examination

- [[Final Examination]] — three hours on paper, the whole course, and
  the larger part of the final thirty per cent alongside the symposium.
