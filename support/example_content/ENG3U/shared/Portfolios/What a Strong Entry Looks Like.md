Two entries about the same class.

## Thin

> Read Act 2. Macbeth kills Duncan. Discussed whether he had a choice.

## Strong

> 2.1–2.2. What struck me: Macbeth cannot say "murder". He says "it",
> "the deed", "this business" — eleven substitutions before the killing
> and none after. Lady Macbeth says "blood" plainly in the next scene.
>
> In discussion I argued that the evasions show guilt. Devon said they
> show planning — you cannot say the word out loud in a house full of
> servants. I think both work, and the difference matters: one is a
> psychological reading and one is practical, and the play may be
> refusing to separate them.
>
> Question: does he ever use a direct word for it after Act 3?

Three moves: a **counted detail**, a **thought that changed** and who
changed it, and a **question left open**.

## The shape to copy

| Move | Prompt |
| --- | --- |
| Notice | One quoted phrase, with act/scene or page |
| Think | What it does |
| Argue | What somebody else said, and where you now stand |
| Ask | What you cannot yet answer |
