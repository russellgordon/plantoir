The first entry in your [[Tech Journal]] is the hardest, and it is
hardest for a reason that has nothing to do with writing: the page is
blank and you do not yet trust what belongs on it. So for week one
only, the entry is guided. Four prompts, a few sentences each, written
the same day as class. Ten minutes, done.

## The four prompts

1. **What we did** — the facts, briefly. The warm-up, the circuit your
   bench put together, where you got to before tools-away.
2. **A number I wrote down** — one measurement from the day, with its
   unit and what it was measured across. If the meter never came out,
   say what quantity you would have wanted to know and why.
3. **What surprised me** — one specific moment you did not expect. In
   the circuit, in the instrument, or in yourself.
4. **Where I stand with electronics right now** — honestly. Some of
   you spent last year in this room; some of you have never held a
   meter; some of you have been fixing things at home since you were
   nine. Naming your starting point is the first piece of data in the
   record, and it is the one you will want most at the end.

## What honest looks like

These fragments are the register to aim for — plain, specific, true,
and unembarrassed about not knowing things:

> "We measured across the resistor and got 2.9 V, then across the LED
> and got 2.1 V. Those add up to the 5 V we started with, which
> Sana pointed out before I noticed. I do not know yet whether that
> always happens or whether we got lucky."

> "I had the meter on the wrong setting for about a minute and got a
> reading of 1 that did not change no matter where I put the probes.
> I thought the circuit was dead. The circuit was fine."

> "Last year I could tell you what a resistor was. Today I found out
> I cannot tell you what value one is by looking at it, which is a
> different and more annoying kind of not knowing."

None of these is polished and none performs for a reader. Each names
a real moment and stays with it, which already beats a page of "the
lab was fun and I learned a lot". Notice too that two of them record
a mistake. The mistakes are the parts that will still be useful to you
in four months.

> [!important] Nobody reads this but me
> As [[Tech Journal]] promises: entries are private. I read them,
> other students never do, and nothing here is quoted without your
> explicit okay. The honesty you risk in week one becomes your best
> evidence of growth by the end of the semester — and only you decide
> who ever hears it.
