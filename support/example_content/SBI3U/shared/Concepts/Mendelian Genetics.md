Mendel's insight was that inheritance is particulate: traits are carried
by discrete factors that do not blend. Blending was the accepted view, and
it was wrong.

## The vocabulary, precisely

| Term | Means |
| --- | --- |
| Gene | A stretch of DNA coding for something |
| Allele | One version of a gene |
| Genotype | Which alleles an individual has |
| Phenotype | What you can observe |
| Homozygous | Two identical alleles |
| Heterozygous | Two different alleles |
| Dominant | Expressed when present, even singly |
| Recessive | Expressed only when homozygous |

## Two laws

**Segregation:** the two alleles of a gene separate into different
gametes. **Independent assortment:** genes on different chromosomes are
inherited independently — which meiosis explains, though Mendel had no
idea why it was true.

## Why peas

Because they self-pollinate reliably, grow fast, and have traits with two
clean forms. Choosing a good experimental organism is a research skill,
not a lucky accident, and it is worth naming as one.
