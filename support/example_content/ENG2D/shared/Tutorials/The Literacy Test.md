Everybody in Grade 10 in Ontario writes the provincial literacy test, and
passing it is a requirement for the diploma. This page is what it
actually asks and how this course prepares for it.

## What is on it

| Section | What it asks |
| --- | --- |
| Reading | Several passages — informational, narrative, graphic — with multiple-choice and short written answers |
| Writing: news report | Write a news report from a headline and a picture |
| Writing: opinion piece | Take a position on a given question and defend it in a series of paragraphs |
| Short writing tasks | A few sentences, several times, in response to what you read |

The reading is what most people find harder, and it is not because the
passages are difficult. It is because the questions ask for evidence and
students answer with impressions.

## What actually helps

1. **Answer the question that was asked.** Underline the question's verb:
   *explain*, *identify*, *support*. Each one asks for a different shape.
2. **Use evidence from the passage, every time.** For a short-answer
   question, a sentence with a quoted phrase in it will nearly always
   outperform a longer sentence without one.
3. **In the news report, answer the five W's in the first paragraph**,
   invent plausible detail, and keep it neutral. It is not a story about
   your feelings.
4. **In the opinion piece, take a position in the first sentence.**
   Three reasons, one paragraph each, with an example in each. A clear
   ordinary position beats a clever unfinished one.
5. **Write legibly and leave two minutes to reread.** Half the errors
   people make are caught by a single reread.

## How this course prepares

Not by drilling. The forms appear inside real work: news reports in the
media unit, opinion writing in [[The Media Deconstruction]] and in
[[Whose Reading Counts]], and evidence-based short answers in every close
reading period. Three periods in December are spent on the test's own
formats — the news report, then the opinion piece, then a full practice
under timed conditions.
