What you should be able to do by the end of the course, in plain words first
and in the Ministry's words underneath.

Civics is a half credit, and it takes over the period Career Studies has
been using — same room, same timetable slot, right through to the middle of
the end of the course. You will notice the handover. What carries across is
the habit of asking who decided something and on what evidence; what is new
is that the decisions are public ones, and other people's.

## By the end of the course you should be able to

- **Find who actually decided something** — the level of government, the
  body, the motion, and the date.
- **Read a civic document** — a bill, a budget line, a court decision —
  for what it commits somebody to.
- **Judge where information came from**, who paid for it, and what it
  leaves out.
- **State a position and defend it** against the strongest version of the
  opposing case.
- **Explain how a right is limited in Canada**, and why that is written
  into the Charter rather than added afterwards.
- **Say who is missing from a decision**, with figures rather than
  impressions.
- **Plan and carry out a civic action**, and report on it honestly —
  including if nothing changed.

## The self-check that matters

Take an issue you have never studied and a folder of sources you have
never seen. Can you work out who decides, what is really in dispute,
which sources to trust, and what you would do about it? That is the whole
course, and it is exactly what [[The Issue Examination]] asks.
