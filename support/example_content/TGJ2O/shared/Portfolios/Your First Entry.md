The first entry in your [[Newsroom Journal]] is the hardest — the
page is blank and you do not yet trust what belongs on it. So for
week one only, the entry is guided: three prompts, a few sentences
each, written the same day as class. Ten minutes, done.

## The three prompts

1. **What we covered** — the facts, briefly: the warm-up, the
   story or shoot you started, where your desk got to by the bell.
2. **What surprised me** — one specific moment you did not expect.
   In the reporting, in the camera, in yourself.
3. **Where I stand with news right now** — honestly. A feed you
   scroll, a team you follow, a headline that once fooled you, or
   barely any of it — naming your history is the record's first
   data point.

## What honest looks like

These fragments are the register to aim for — plain, specific, true:

> "My first interview lasted ninety seconds because I read all seven
> questions in a row and got seven one-word answers. Then I said
> 'wait — what was the save actually like?' and got the only quote
> worth keeping. I keep thinking about that."

> "I shot forty-one frames at practice and forty were from the
> same spot. Only the one where I crouched by the net got a second
> look. Moving is apparently most of photography."

> "I assumed news was just whatever happens. Then we argued ten
> minutes about the vending machine versus the caretaker and I
> changed my mind twice. Deciding what counts is the job. Okay."

None of these performs for a reader. Each names a real moment and
stays with it — which already beats "class was fun, I learned a lot."

> [!important] Nobody reads this but your editor
> As [[Newsroom Journal]] promises: entries are private, between
> reporter and editor. I read them; other students never do, and
> nothing you admit here is quoted without your explicit okay. The
> honesty you risk in week one becomes your best evidence of growth
> by the end — but only you decide who ever hears about it.
