History here is not a story you are told and repeat back. It is an
argument built from evidence, about a past its own participants
disagreed on.

## A normal period

1. **A source.** A document, a photograph, a poster, a set of figures, a
   recorded voice. You meet it before it is explained.
2. **An argument.** Usually in threes, about what the source can and
   cannot show. "How do you know?" is the standard question here, and it
   is not an accusation.
3. **A judgement.** You commit to a position, in writing, with the
   evidence attached — and you say what would change your mind.

Naming the concept comes last. That is why [[Historical Significance]]
and [[Cause and Consequence]] assume you have met the idea already.

## What the course asks of you

- **Read before class.** Most periods start from a source you were meant
  to have seen; arriving without it means catching up on what everyone
  else is already arguing about.
- **Separate what a source says from what you conclude.** That
  distinction is most of the discipline.
- **Name your source every time you state a fact.**
- **Represent fairly the positions you disagree with.** On several tasks
  it is a criterion in its own right — the counter-argument row on
  [[The War Question]] is where you meet it first — and everywhere else
  it is what separates an argument from an opinion.

## The shape of the year

Four units — 1914 to 1929, 1929 to 1945, 1945 to 1982, and 1982 to the
present — each asking the same three questions of a different period:
the conditions, the conflicts, and the effect on identity and
citizenship.

The year ends with [[The Commemoration Inquiry]]: one real decision
about what a community remembers — a monument, a name, a day, an
absence — investigated, with a recommendation to whoever could act on
it. Then a three-hour [[The Document Examination]] on unseen sources.

## If you fall behind

Say so early. [[Getting Help]] lists every way to reach me and
[[Help Sessions]] has the times I am in the room. A missed source is a
ten-minute fix in week two and a real problem in week ten.
