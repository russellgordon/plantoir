Green Industries is learned by doing green industry work. You will spend
most of this course on your feet — in the shop, on the school grounds, in
the woodlot, at the waterfront, and at a working vegetable farm five
minutes down the road. These pages are what you read before and after.

## A normal period

1. **Briefing, about ten minutes.** What today's job is, where it
   happens, what the hazards are, and who is doing what.
2. **Kit and check.** Boots, gear, tools, and the check in
   [[Before You Start Work]] — never skipped.
3. **The work, about fifty minutes.** Building, planting, propagating,
   measuring, maintaining, or surveying.
4. **Clean, store, and record.** Tools cleaned and back on the wall, site
   left safe, and your record written while it is fresh —
   [[Writing a Work Record]].

## The four places

- **The shop and the school grounds.** Tools, construction, propagation,
  and the beds we maintain all year.
- **The woodlot**, a walk away — tree and shrub identification, forest
  health, pests and disease.
- **The waterfront**, also a walk. Shoreline planting, erosion, and water
  quality; the site of [[Planting a Shoreline]].
- **The farm.** A working vegetable operation, visited as a visit. It is
  somebody's business and somebody's living.

> [!note] Roughly two periods in three are outdoors or in the shop
> Which makes weather part of the course, and makes the kit list in
> [[What to Wear and Bring]] the difference between a good day and a
> miserable one. We work in rain and in cold. We stop for lightning,
> extreme heat, and unsafe ground, and you will know by the morning.

## What the course asks of you

Turn up ready to work, boots on and phone away. Ask for the training
before you touch the tool — nobody expects you to already know. Stop when
something is wrong and say so, which is the most professional thing you
will do all semester. Keep [[The Evidence File]] current from week one. And
look after the person working next to you.

## The shape of the semester

Four units: how living things grow; tools, measurement, and making;
growing and producing; and the industry around us. It ends in
[[The Site Project]] — a real green-industry job on one of our sites,
carried from survey and plan to a maintenance schedule the next class can
follow. Marks are in [[How Marks Work]]; [[Using This Site]] shows you
around these pages.
