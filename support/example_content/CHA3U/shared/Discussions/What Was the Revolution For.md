**The question:** The Declaration of Independence asserts that all men are
created equal. The government the Revolution produced protected slavery,
left women without political standing, and continued the dispossession of
Native nations. What, then, was the Revolution for — and who decides?

Every word there is doing work. *For* assumes a purpose. *Produced* assumes
a settlement can be read back as an intention. *Who decides* is the part the
seminar usually spends the longest on.

## Prepare

Bring **one document and one person**. The document should be short and
primary: a passage of the Declaration, an article of the Constitution or the
Bill of Rights, a colonial resolve, a petition. The National Archives
publishes transcripts of all three founding documents, so nobody needs to
work from a summary.

For the person, take one of the individuals the curriculum names for this
period — Abigail Adams, Benjamin Banneker, Alexander McGillivray, Phyllis
Wheatley, Pontiac, Paul Revere — and find one thing they actually said or
wrote.[^person] Record both sources as [[Reading a Primary Source]] requires.

[^person]: The point of the pairing is that a document tells you what the
    Revolution said and a person tells you what it did. If your person's
    words and your document's words agree completely, you have probably
    chosen a person the document was written for.

## Three positions, and a fourth that refuses the question

**It was for a principle, imperfectly applied.** The language of the
Declaration outran its authors and was used against them almost immediately,
by people they had not meant to include. On this account the Revolution's
purpose is the standard it set, and the history that follows is the argument
about honouring it.

**It was for self-government by a colonial elite.** Read B1.4's documents in
sequence — Declaration, Articles, Constitution, Bill of Rights — and the
settlement looks less like a principle than a transfer of authority to men
who already held property and local office. The evidence is the settlement
itself.

**It was several revolutions at once**, with different participants pursuing
different ends, and it ended differently for each. This is the hardest
position to argue and the easiest to support, provided you keep the groups
distinct rather than merging them into "the colonists."

**Purpose is the wrong question.** Events do not have purposes; people do,
and there were hundreds of thousands of them. On this view the real question
is whose purposes prevailed, which turns a seminar about ideals into one
about power. That is a legitimate move, and it has to be argued for.

The five things marked in every seminar are on [[The Four Seminars]]; read
them once, in September, and they hold for all four.

Open with your position, name the evidence that would move you, and carry
the disagreement into [[Was the Revolution Inevitable|Was the Revolution Inevitable?]] and
[[The Revolution Question]].
