Your [[Newsroom Journal]] is collected at the end of each unit.
Before you hand it in, run this check — it is the same lens I read
with, so anything you catch here is something we never need to have
a conversation about. Ten minutes with this list is usually worth
more than one extra entry.

## The check

**Coverage**

- [ ] There is an entry for every class in the unit
- [ ] Entries were written close to the day, not reconstructed the
      night before collection — reconstructed entries always show
- [ ] All three prompts appear across the unit: what I covered, what
      I learned, what I would chase next
- [ ] At least one entry includes a real artifact — a clipping, a
      contact sheet, a cut list, or a marked-up draft from the day

**Quality**

- [ ] Every problem is located, not just felt — "my lead buried the
      save until paragraph four", not "the story felt off" (see
      [[What a Strong Entry Looks Like]])
- [ ] At least one entry records something spiked, reshot, or
      declined — not just the wins
- [ ] My own part is named, not hidden inside "we" — what *I* asked,
      shot, cut, suggested, or misread
- [ ] Help is credited where it happened — a classmate, an editor, a
      source — the way [[Our Newsroom Standards]] asks

**Growth**

- [ ] Each entry ends with one concrete "what I would chase next"
- [ ] At least one earlier "chase next" item shows up later as a
      thing I actually chased — with a sentence about how it went

> [!tip] The last two boxes are the whole game
> Anyone can write "I will ask better follow-ups." The journal
> becomes evidence the day a later entry says "I asked what the
> huddle talk was, and got the quote that led the story" — a loop
> from intention to attempt to result. [[Showing Growth]] will ask
> you to point at exactly that loop, so leave yourself some.
