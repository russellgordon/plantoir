Your group has exactly 24 m of fencing and a herd of impatient goats.
The pen must be rectangular, and the goats would like as much grass
as possible. Every group gets the same 24 m — so this is not about
having more fence. It is about what shape squeezes the most area out
of it.

## The task

Find the dimensions of the rectangular pen with the greatest area,
and — the harder half — convince a sceptic that *no* rectangle using
24 m of fence can beat yours. "We tried lots and this was the best"
will not survive cross-examination; you need a reason the top is the
top. Somewhere along the way, your board should show how the area
*changes* as the shape changes, not just one winning answer.

> [!question]- Getting started (click to expand)
> - Pick a width, any width. The fence budget then decides the
>   length for you — how?
> - A table of width against area is cheap. So is graphing it. What
>   shape is the graph making, and why should it be symmetric?
> - A width of 1 m and a width of 11 m give the same area. Coincidence?
> - Where between them must the best pen hide?

## What mathematics tends to surface

Area as a relation — one number in, one number out — comes out
quadratic, and its graph is a parabola opening downward: the arch
from [[Graph Talks]], now with your goats on it. The best pen lives
at the very top of the arch, and "where *exactly* is the top of this
curve?" is the question that [[The Vertex Form]] exists to answer.
The two widths where the area dies to zero pin down the symmetry,
foreshadowing [[Zeros and the Quadratic Formula]], and
[[Using Desmos]] will happily draw the arch — but *you* locate and
defend its peak.

## An extension

Same goats, same 24 m — but now one side of the pen runs along the
barn, which costs no fence. Find the new best pen. The winning shape
is no longer a square: what did the wall do to the symmetry, and can
your group say *why* the answer moved? The technique lands for good
in [[Quadratic Graphing Practice]], and the arch returns, airborne,
in [[The Perfect Arc]].

> [!note] The answer is not on this page
> No dimensions are printed here. The peak of the arch is your
> group's to find, and to defend, at the boards.
