The newsroom's injuries are the slow kind. The shop's are fast: a
flame that catches while nobody is watching, a hot nozzle, a blade that
slips. Every rule on this page exists because somebody, somewhere, found
out the hard way, and every one of them is in force from the first shop
day to the last. Nobody runs a machine in this room until they have
passed the sign-off at the bottom.

## Who is responsible for what

Ontario's *Occupational Health and Safety Act* is the law that governs
every workplace in the province, and it gives each person in a
workplace a job:

| Role | In a workplace | In this room |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Worker** | Works safely, uses the protective equipment required, reports hazards, never removes or disables a safety device | You |
| **Supervisor** | Tells workers about the hazards, makes sure protective equipment is used, takes every reasonable precaution | Me |
| **Employer** | Provides the training, the equipment, and the supervision, and keeps equipment maintained | The school board |

The Act also gives every worker three rights: to **know** about the
hazards, to **take part** in fixing them, and to **refuse** work they
believe is unsafe. Those hold here too. If something at a machine feels
wrong, stop and tell me — that is the law working, not you being
difficult.

Chemical products in a workplace come with **WHMIS** labels and a
**Safety Data Sheet** (SDS) from the supplier: what the product is, what
it can do to you, and what to do if it does. The SDS binder for our
acrylic, filament, and cleaners lives by the door, and we read the ones
for our materials together on the first shop day.

## The laser cutter

The laser is the one machine in this room that can start a fire on its
own, so its rules are absolute:

- **Never unattended, not for a second.** Whoever's file is cutting
  stands at the machine, watching, for the whole job — and I am within
  sight of it. If you have to walk away, you press pause first.
- **Extraction on, lid closed.** The fume extraction runs whenever the
  laser does, and the lid stays closed until the job ends. The lid
  switch is a safety device: it is what keeps the beam inside the box.
- **Watch for the flame that stays.** A tiny flicker where the beam
  touches is normal. A flame that keeps burning after the beam has
  moved on is not: press stop, call me, and step back. The extinguisher
  beside the laser is the kind its maker specifies, and it is mine to
  use.
- **Only materials on the approved list.** Corrugated cardboard,
  laser-grade plywood, and cast acrylic. **Never** PVC or vinyl — they
  give off a corrosive chlorine-based gas that harms people and eats
  the machine — and never anything you cannot name for certain. If it
  is not on the list, it does not go in.

## The 3D printer

- **Hot parts.** The nozzle runs at around 200 °C for PLA and the bed
  at around 60 °C. Hands stay out while it moves, and nothing is
  touched until the screen says it has cooled.
- **Air.** Printing gives off fine particles, so the printer lives
  where the room is ventilated, and nobody sits with their face beside
  it for a whole print.
- **Removing supports and trimming.** Safety glasses on, pliers or a
  deburring tool rather than fingers, and every cut away from your
  body. Craft-knife blades go in the blade container, never the bin.
- **Watched, not abandoned.** A print runs only while someone
  responsible is in the room. A print that starts to fail — spaghetti,
  a part knocked loose — gets stopped, not left to finish.

## Materials and waste

Offcuts are sorted the day they are made: clean cardboard to paper
recycling, plywood and acrylic scraps big enough to use again into the
reuse bin for test cards, failed prints into the filament box, dust
vacuumed rather than blown. [[The Footprint of Media]] explains why the
sorting matters beyond keeping the floor clear.

## What you wear

Closed-toe shoes, hair tied back, nothing dangling, sleeves that stay
put, and safety glasses whenever something is being trimmed, sanded, or
pulled apart — the full list is in [[What to Bring]].

## The sign-off

Before your first cut or print you complete the sign-off: a short quiz
on this page, then three things shown to me at the machine. It is
recorded, and nobody operates a machine without it.

- [ ] I can name the three roles and the three rights
- [ ] I can say which materials go in the laser, and why PVC never does
- [ ] I have shown where the stop button and the extinguisher are
- [ ] I have shown how I check the extraction is running
- [ ] I have shown how to load the printer's filament without touching
      the nozzle

The list is here to read down before the sign-off, and again any time
the shop has been quiet for a few weeks.

> [!important] The rule that overrides every deadline
> No sign and no print is worth a burn or a fire. If a machine is
> doing something you do not understand, you stop it and ask. That
> decision will always be supported here, without exception.
