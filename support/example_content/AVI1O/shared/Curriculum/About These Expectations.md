Two typographical errors in the source are reproduced rather than
corrected, because these pages are meant to be the document: `C1.1`
prints "Eva Hess's" where the artist is Eva Hesse, and `C2.2` prints a
list that mixes singular and plural — "metaphor, similes, symbols,
synectics".

## Why the codes matter more than they look

A code is a shared address. When a student asks "why are we doing this?",
the answer is a link. When a department writes a course outline, or a
teacher takes over a class partway through, the codes are what make one
person's planning legible to another.

## How this course reads them

Strand A is what you make; strand B is what you notice about what has
been made, by you and by others; strand C is what makes both possible —
terminology, techniques, and responsible practice. C is not a unit: safe
handling of materials, correct vocabulary, and credit for other people's
images run through every studio period.
