> [!abstract] At a glance
> **Time:** one class, written up in the next period and handed in at the
> bell — the third of the marked [[Investigation Write-Ups]]. **Groups of
> three at the table, write-up yours alone.** Video required — see
> [[Analysing Video of Motion]].

An air table, two pucks, one glancing collision, and vector arithmetic
that either balances or tells you something.

## Safety

Pucks leave the table. Keep the floor clear and do not lean over the
edge to watch.

## Method

1. Film from directly above, with a known length in frame.
2. Collide one moving puck with a stationary one, off-centre.
3. Track both pucks frame by frame, before and after.
4. Extract four velocity vectors, each as magnitude and direction.

## Analysis

1. Resolve everything into components. Does momentum balance in $x$? In
   $y$? Report both as percentages.
2. Compute total kinetic energy before and after. Elastic or not?
3. If the collision was close to elastic with equal masses, measure the
   angle between the two outgoing velocities. Theory says $90^\circ$ —
   how close were you, and why not exactly?
4. Which of your measurements dominates the uncertainty, and how would you
   reduce it with the same equipment?
