One student, sixty seconds, one story idea. The pitch must land
three things before the timer dies: WHAT the story is, WHY NOW
rather than next month, and WHY OUR READERS — not readers in
general — should care. Then the room votes: green light, or one
killer question the pitch cannot yet answer.

## How to run it

1. Two pitchers per round, timer visible, hard stop at sixty.
2. The room votes green or not — no half votes.
3. A "not yet" must name its killer question: what to find out first.
4. Green-lit pitches go on the board — deadline week loves a full board.

> [!example]- One pitch, sixty seconds (click to expand)
> "Half the printers in the building are down and exams start in
> two weeks. What: which printers work, why the rest don't, when
> they'll be fixed. Why now: assignments are due — demand is about
> to spike. Why our readers: every one of them will stand in that
> line. I need maintenance on record and a count I do myself."
> Green light — it names its sources and its own deadline.

## One variation

Pitch a story the room already knows and make it new — a fresh angle
on old news is the harder, more honest test of [[News Values]].

> [!tip] The killer question is a gift
> "Who will actually talk to you?" is not sabotage; it is the first
> line of the plan. Pitches that survive it become stories that
> survive [[Filing a Story]].
