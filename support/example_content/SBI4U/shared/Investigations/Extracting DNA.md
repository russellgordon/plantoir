> [!abstract] At a glance
> **Time:** one class. **Pairs.** Precipitating the molecule you have been diagramming.

Isolate genomic DNA from plant tissue (strawberries, split peas, or wheat germ)
using household reagents with biochemical mechanisms behind each step.

## Safety

- Ethanol is flammable: no open flames or hot plates in the room. Keep stock
  bottles capped and away from heat sources.
- Wear safety goggles and lab aprons.
- Dispose of plant homogenates in the organic waste container as directed.

## Method

1. **Mechanical disruption:** Mash 10 g of fresh strawberry or rehydrated
   split peas in a resealable plastic bag with 10 mL of lysis buffer (water,
   dishwashing detergent, and table salt $\\ce{NaCl}$).
2. **Filtration:** Filter the homogenate through cheesecloth or a coffee
   filter into a clean beaker to remove cell wall debris and bulk cellulose.
3. **Enzymatic protein digestion:** Add 1 mL of meat tenderizer or contact
   lens solution containing proteases (such as papain or bromelain). Swirl
   gently and incubate at 50 °C for 10 minutes to denature histones and
   cellular nucleases.
4. **Precipitation:** Tilt the test tube and slowly layer an equal volume of
   ice-cold 95% ethanol down the side, forming a distinct top layer over the
   aqueous extract.
5. **Spooling:** Observe the white, fibrous precipitate aggregating at the
   liquid interface. Gently twirl a clean glass rod or wooden applicator to
   spool the DNA fibres.

## Analysis

1. Explain the biochemical role of each reagent:
   - Why does dishwashing detergent break open the plasma membrane and nuclear
     envelope? (Relate this to the amphipathic structure of phospholipids from
     [[Carbohydrates and Lipids|Carbohydrates and Lipids]]).
   - What is the chemical function of $\\ce{Na+}$ ions from the salt? (Relate this
     to the negatively charged phosphate backbone of DNA from [[Nucleic Acids]]).
   - Why is protease required to isolate pure nucleic acids?
   - Why does DNA precipitate in cold ethanol but remain dissolved in aqueous buffer?
2. Compare your yield and purity with another group using a different plant tissue.
   What biological characteristics of the tissue (e.g., ploidy level, water content)
   explain differences in yield?
