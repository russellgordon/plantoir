## Purpose

Visit six stations, decide whether each change is physical or chemical, and
justify each decision with evidence.

> [!danger] Safety
> Goggles the entire time. Station 4 uses dilute acid — rinse spills with plenty
> of water and tell me. Never lean over an open flame.
>
> **Disposal, and it differs by station.** The precipitate from station 6 goes
> in the labelled waste jar, never the sink. Station 4's contents are rinsed
> away with plenty of running water. Wax goes in the bin once it has set solid.
> Read this before you start, not when you have finished.

## Stations

| # | What you do | Watch for |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 1 | Dissolve salt in water | Does anything new appear? |
| 2 | Melt candle wax, let it resolidify | Is it still wax? |
| 3 | Mix baking soda and vinegar | Gas? Temperature? |
| 4 | Drop magnesium into dilute $\ce{HCl}$ | Bubbles, warmth |
| 5 | Tear and crumple paper | Any new substance? |
| 6 | Mix lead-free solutions to form a precipitate | Solid from two clear liquids |

## Analysis

1. Which station was hardest to classify, and what would settle it?
2. Station 3 gets colder; station 4 gets warmer. Both are chemical. What does
   temperature change tell you, and what does it *not*?
3. In an open container, station 3 appears to lose mass. Explain using
   conservation of mass — see [[Physical and Chemical Changes]].
