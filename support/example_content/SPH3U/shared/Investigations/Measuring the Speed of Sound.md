Two methods, one of which is limited by your reaction time and one of
which is not. Comparing them is half the point.

## Safety

Tuning forks are struck on a rubber block, never on a bench, and never
near anyone's ear.

## Method A — Echo timing

Stand a measured distance $d$ from a large flat wall (the gym end wall
works). Clap rhythmically until the echo of each clap arrives exactly with
the next clap. Time 20 claps. The sound travelled $2d$ in each interval.

## Method B — Resonance in a tube

Hold a struck tuning fork of known frequency over a tube standing in
water. Raise the tube until the sound suddenly swells: that is the first
resonance, at $L_1 = \lambda/4$. Find the second at
$L_2 = 3\lambda/4$ if the tube is long enough.

Then $\lambda = 4L_1$ (or better, $\lambda = 2(L_2 - L_1)$, which cancels
the end correction), and $v = f\lambda$.

## What to record

Method A: $d$, time for 20 claps, three trials. Method B: frequency,
$L_1$, $L_2$, three trials. Room temperature for both.

## Analysis

1. Compute $v$ both ways, with uncertainties.
2. Compare with $v = 331 + 0.6T$ for the room temperature you recorded.
3. Method B does not involve your reflexes at all. Does its uncertainty
   come out smaller? By how much, and where does what remains come from?
