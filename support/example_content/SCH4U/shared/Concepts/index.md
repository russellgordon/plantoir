A concept page says one idea cleanly, once, in a form you can reread in
months from now and still follow.

It is deliberately the *second* time you meet the idea. In this course
it almost always arrives first as something you did — a sealed tube you
watched stop changing, a set of boiling points you were asked to explain
before anyone gave you a reason, a strip of zinc in a beaker with a
voltmeter across it. The doing comes first because an explanation you
have already half-built for yourself sticks. These pages open by naming
where the idea was met, and then say it properly.

## What changed since Grade 11

Last year the questions were mostly *how much*. How many moles, how many
grams, which reactant runs out first. Those questions had one answer and
the answer was a number.

This year the questions are *why that much and no further*. A reaction
that stops with reactants still in the flask is not a failed reaction —
it has arrived somewhere, and the somewhere is predictable. Energy stops
being a side effect and becomes the thing that sets the direction.
Structure stops being a catalogue and starts predicting boiling points
you have not measured yet.

> [!important] Three sentences that carry most of the year
> A reaction going downhill in energy tells you **nothing** about how
> fast it goes. A model is only useful once you can say where it stops
> being true. And a number without a temperature attached to it is not
> a measurement of an equilibrium.

## Where these pages sit

A concept page explains. An [[Exercises/index|exercise page]] drills. A
[[Reference/index|reference page]] is a lookup sheet you keep open while
you work and never read from top to bottom. You need all three, and
which one you reach for tells you something about where you are stuck.

Class pages link here, and these pages do not go stale — so start here
when you are studying, when you missed a class, or when a calculation
that made sense in the room falls apart at your kitchen table. If it is
still not moving after twenty minutes, [[Getting Unstuck]] is written
for exactly that moment.
