> [!abstract] At a glance
> **Time:** one class. **Groups of three.** A standard curve, and a
> conclusion that depends on it.

DNA is negatively charged, so it moves toward the positive electrode
through a gel that slows large fragments more than small ones.

## Safety

The power supply runs at 100 V or more: lid on before power on, power off
before the lid comes off, every time. Stains and gloves as instructed.

## Method

1. Cast the agarose gel and load the wells: a size standard plus your
   samples.
2. Run until the dye front is two thirds down.
3. Photograph the gel beside a ruler.
4. Measure the migration distance of every band.

## Analysis

1. Plot log(fragment size) against distance for the standard. It should be
   close to linear — say how close.
2. Use your standard curve to estimate the sizes of the unknown fragments.
3. In the forensic scenario, which sample matches? State how confident
   you are and what would make you more so.
4. Why does size affect speed here, but charge does not? (Every fragment
   has the same charge per unit length — which is exactly why this works.)
