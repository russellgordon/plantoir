Dance is learned by dancing. Almost every period in this course is spent
on your feet, and the pages on this site are what you read before and
after — not instead.

## A normal period

1. **Warm up.** Same shape most days, for a physiological reason — see
   [[The Standard Warm-Up]].
2. **The work.** Technique, improvisation, making, rehearsing, showing,
   or watching. The class page names which.
3. **Cool down.** Short, and not optional.

## What the course asks of you

- **Come in clothes you can move in.** Every day, not just showing days
  — [[What to Wear]].
- **Try it before you decide you cannot.** Everyone in this room is
  visible, including the people who have trained for years, and everyone
  is uncomfortable at some point.
- **Take correction as information.** A note from me is not a judgement
  about you; it is the fastest way to move better.
- **Keep the journal current.** It is the record of your own thinking,
  and it becomes the final evaluation — [[Your Movement Journal]].
- **Look after the person you are dancing with**, especially in partner
  work.

## Experience is not the entry price

Some of you have danced for ten years and some have never taken a class.
Both are normal here. The trained dancers will be asked for detail and
range; the beginners will be asked for clarity and for movement that is
genuinely theirs. Neither starts ahead in the marks — read
[[How Marks Work]] and you will see why.

## The shape of the year

Four units — the moving body, making a dance, dance in the world, and
bringing work to an audience — ending in [[The Showing]], performed for
an invited audience, and the portfolio you have been building since
the start of the course.

## If something hurts

Stop, and tell me. Nothing here is worth an injury, and stopping is
never a mark against you — [[The Safe Dancer]] makes that a piece of
assessed work rather than an afterthought.
