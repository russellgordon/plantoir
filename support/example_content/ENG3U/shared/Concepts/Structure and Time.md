Structure is the order a writer chose. Time is the raw material they
arranged. At this level, "the plot" is rarely the point; the arrangement
almost always is.

## Questions that open a text

- **Where does it start, and what has been left out?**
- **What is withheld, and until when?**
- **How fast does time move, and where does it stop?** Four pages on one
  afternoon means the afternoon is the argument.
- **What is repeated at intervals?** Structure includes rhythm.
- **What is the last thing we are told?** Endings are chosen last and
  matter most.

## Non-linear structures

| Structure | What it does |
| --- | --- |
| Frame narrative | Raises the question of who is telling and why |
| Braided timelines | Lets two moments comment on each other |
| Retrospect | Puts an older voice over a younger experience |
| Circular | Returns changed; the change is the meaning |

[[Monkey Beach]] braids a boat journey in the present with a childhood
told out of order. The braid is not decoration: the novel is about how
grief reorganises memory, and the structure performs that before any
character says it.

## In drama

Act divisions, scene changes, and the pace of alternation. *Macbeth* is
Shakespeare's shortest tragedy and its acceleration is deliberate: scenes
get shorter, the murders get faster, and Lady Macbeth disappears from a
play she began by driving.
