## Carbohydrates

Monomers are monosaccharides with multiple hydroxyl ($-OH$) groups and a
carbonyl ($C=O$) group — $\ce{C6H12O6}$ for glucose. Two join by a
condensation reaction, releasing a water molecule to form a covalent
glycosidic linkage; many form a polysaccharide, broken back down by
hydrolysis.

| Polysaccharide | Where | Job |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Starch | Plants | Storage, coiled and compact ($\alpha\text{-1,4}$ and $\alpha\text{-1,6}$) |
| Glycogen | Animals | Storage, highly branched for fast release |
| Cellulose | Plant cell walls | Structure — linear $\beta\text{-1,4}$ sheets, indigestible to us |

Starch and cellulose are both glucose polymers. The difference is the
orientation of one glycosidic bond, and it is the difference between food and
fibre.

## Lipids

Not true polymers. A triglyceride is formed when a glycerol molecule's three
hydroxyl groups react with the carboxyl ($-COOH$) groups of three fatty acid
chains through condensation, yielding three ester linkages. Saturated chains
have no double bonds, pack tightly, and are solid at room temperature;
unsaturated chains have kinks from cis-double bonds and remain liquid.

Phospholipids combine a polar, charged phosphate head with two non-polar
hydrocarbon tails, forming bilayers spontaneously in water — see
[[Membranes and Transport]].
