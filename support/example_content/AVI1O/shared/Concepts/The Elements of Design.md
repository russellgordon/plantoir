Look at anything in this room for five seconds and you have already used all
seven of these. Where the edges fall. What colour. Whether that surface would
be rough under your hand. The elements of design are not new things to notice
— they are names for what your eye is doing anyway. The names matter because
you cannot deliberately choose something you have no word for, and you cannot
ask anyone for help with it either.

## The seven

The curriculum lists them alphabetically, so we will too. Nothing in that
order says which one matters most.

| Element | What it is | The choice hiding in it |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Colour | Hue, and how pure or dulled | No colour behaves alone |
| Form | Volume — a shape with depth | What the light does to it |
| Line | A mark longer than it is wide | Weight, speed, confidence |
| Shape | A flat area with an edge | The gaps are shapes too |
| Space | Depth, distance, emptiness | How much you leave empty |
| Texture | How a surface feels, or seems to | Real, or only implied |
| Value | How light or dark something is | Squint — does it still read? |

## They are not separate dials

Change one and others move. Push a colour lighter and you have changed its
value at the same time. Add texture to a flat shape and it starts to look
like form. Crowd a page with shapes and the space is what you have altered,
even though you never touched it.

Value is the one beginners skip and professionals guard. A work can have
gorgeous colour and still collapse, because the lights and darks are all the
same strength and nothing separates from anything. Photograph your work in
black and white — if it turns to mud, that is a value problem, and
[[Photographing Your Work]] has the quickest way to check.

## Three jobs, all semester

You will use these words to **make** — "this needs a darker value behind the
figure" is an instruction you can act on — to **describe**, since every
critique in [[Describing What You See]] runs on them, and to **repair**,
which is making, just later. They arrive properly in [[The Elements Study]]
and they never leave.
