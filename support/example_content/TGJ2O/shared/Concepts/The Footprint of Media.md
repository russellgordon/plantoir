Publishing has physical consequences. Some are obvious — paper, ink,
delivery — and some are invisible, which is exactly why they are worth
naming in a course about communication.

## What this work costs the environment

| Where | What it uses or produces |
| --- | --- |
| **Paper and print** | Pulp, water, energy; ink and solvent; and every misprinted run twice over |
| **Devices** | Mined metals, manufacturing energy, and a short replacement cycle |
| **Streaming and hosting** | Data-centre electricity and cooling, continuously, for as long as the file exists |
| **Studio lighting and equipment** | Power, and heat that then has to be cooled |
| **Light pollution** | Outdoor shoots and signage, which affect sleep and wildlife |
| **Sound pollution** | Events, amplification, and testing at volume |
| **Batteries and e-waste** | Lithium cells, and gear that becomes waste rather than repair |

Video is the item most people underestimate: streaming is by far the
largest share of consumer internet traffic, and every view spends
energy in a data centre and in the network on the way to the viewer.
A file published once and watched ten thousand times has a footprint
ten thousand times over.

## Lifecycle analysis: from raw materials to e-waste

Every piece of communications gear — cameras, microphones, editing suites,
and server racks — carries an environmental footprint long before it enters a
newsroom and long after it leaves:

1. **Extraction and manufacturing.** Camera sensors and lithium-ion batteries
   depend on mined metals (lithium, cobalt, copper, and rare-earth elements)
   whose extraction disrupts local ecosystems and requires significant energy.
   Manufacturing silicon chips produces substantial carbon emissions and
   chemical waste.
2. **Operational energy and cloud infrastructure.** Cloud storage, online
   content delivery networks (CDNs), and generative processing draw continuous
   power from electrical grids. Cooling massive data centres consumes millions
   of litres of water and continuous megawatts of electricity.
3. **End-of-life and toxic e-waste.** Obsolete hardware often ends up in
   landfills where toxic substances — such as heavy metals, flame retardants,
   and battery electrolytes — risk leaching into soil and groundwater. In
   Ontario, electronics must be directed through certified recycling programs
   governed by the Resource Productivity and Recovery Authority (RPRA) to
   recover raw elements safely and keep toxins out of domestic waste streams.

## Sustainable production habits in our newsroom

Sustainable media practice is built into our daily routines, not treated as an
afterthought:

- **Power management on editing desks.** Workstations and displays enter sleep
  mode when idle for five minutes. Power bars are switched off at the end of
  the day rather than left in vampire-draw standby mode.
- **Lighting efficiency.** Studio setups utilize high-efficiency LED panels
  rather than power-hungry halogen or incandescent lamps, drastically lowering
  both electrical load and studio cooling demands.
- **Modular repair and maintenance.** Cables, tripod legs, and microphone
  clips are repaired in-house whenever possible rather than replaced. Worn
  equipment is refurbished for field training rather than discarded.
- **Paperless digital routing.** Story pitches, interview releases, and proof
  edits move through shared digital folders, eliminating unread print drafts.

## What can actually be done about it

Sustainable practice here is not abstract; every line below is
something this newsroom can do this term:

- **Print only what will be read.** Set the run from actual pickup
  numbers, and proof digitally before committing a run.
- **Use recycled stock and vegetable-based inks**, and recycle the
  offcuts and the unsold copies.
- **Publish web-first**, with print reserved for the issues that
  deserve it.
- **Export at sensible sizes.** A 1200-pixel image instead of a 6000-
  pixel one is the same story and a twentieth of the bytes, every time
  it is loaded.
- **Rechargeable batteries**, charged in a managed cycle rather than
  bought by the packet.
- **Repair and redeploy equipment**, and recycle what is genuinely
  finished through a certified processor — with the storage wiped
  first.
- **Switch the studio lights off between takes.** They are the biggest
  single load in this room.
- **Keep the archive tidy.** Duplicate files stored forever cost energy
  forever.
