> [!abstract] At a glance
> Individual · three hours in the examination period · closed book ·
> alongside the final stage of [[The Independent Study]], this is the
> final 30%

## What is on it

| Part | Roughly | What it asks |
| --- | --- | --- |
| A. Sight passage — prose | 25% | An unseen passage: comprehension, inference, and one analytical paragraph |
| B. Sight passage — poem | 20% | An unseen poem, read closely, in a paragraph |
| C. Short answers on the studied texts | 20% | Precise recall and interpretation across the play, the novel, and the non-fiction |
| D. Essay | 35% | One argument about a studied text, planned and written in the room |

## What it rewards

- **Passages known cold.** Three per text, chosen now. Not word-perfect —
  known: what happens, who is there, what it shows.
- **Reading the question's verb.** *Analyse*, *evaluate*, *compare* — three
  different shapes.
- **Five minutes of planning** before the essay. Every year the strongest
  essays have a visible plan in the margin.
- **Analysis longer than quotation**, under pressure. Practised often
  enough now that it should not cost you thinking time in the room.

## How it is marked

The same four categories as everything else, on writing produced under
time. What changes is not the standard but the conditions, and the
criteria say so.

| Category | What I am looking for |
| --- | --- |
| Knowledge | The studied texts are known accurately enough to be quoted from memory and used precisely |
| Thinking | An unseen passage is read for what it does, not summarised; the essay's claim is arguable and the evidence is chosen |
| Communication | Organised under time — a plan behind the essay, paragraphs that do one job each, a reader who never has to reread |
| Application | The method built on texts we studied together works on a text met for the first time this morning |

## How to prepare

1. Three passages per text, annotated, in your notes.
2. Redo your analytical paragraphs from the autumn — from the passage,
   not from the old paragraph.
3. Two timed sight passages before the examination: one in class, one at
   home.
4. Rebuild your essay outline template until it is automatic.
5. Bring your questions to the four review classes.

> [!tip] In the room
> Budget by the weightings and wear a watch. Do the essay while you are
> fresh if you have your argument; do the sight passages first if you do
> not. Answer everything — partial answers earn partial marks.

OBSERVE — Unit 4, Day 17, the sight passage written under time
  Watch for: the first four minutes. Reading twice with a pencil before

  four minutes that transfer to the examination room.
  Going well: marks in the margin before any sentence is written.
  Stuck: the opening sentence written during the first read.
  Record: two columns while they work — you have nothing else to do in

TALK — Unit 4, Day 16, while three passages per text are chosen in class
  The agenda says reasons are given, so listen past the first one.

  while there is still time to do something about it.
  Record: initials and one text name — the weak one — on a single sheet
  for the whole class.
