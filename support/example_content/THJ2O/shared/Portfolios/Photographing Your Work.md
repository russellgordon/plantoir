Most of what you make in this course cannot be handed in. A bed stays in
the ground, a bench stays on the site, a crop gets eaten. The photograph
is the only thing that comes with you, so it has to carry the evidence.

## Before, during, after

The *before* is the one people miss. Take it the moment you arrive, from
a spot you can stand in again — nobody believes a planting improved
anything without a picture of it beforehand.

- **Before.** Wide, showing the whole area and something fixed at the
  edge — a post, a corner, a tree — so you can line the later shots up.
- **During.** Your hands doing the skill — get someone else to take it.
- **After.** Same spot, framing, and height as the before, plus a close
  shot of the detail you are proud of.

## Getting a usable frame

Stand so the light comes from behind or beside you, and check your own
shadow is not lying across the subject. Overcast days are easiest; bright
midday sun blows out a gravel path and buries a bed edge in shadow. Fill
the frame — a build shot from across the yard is a photo of a yard.

Hold the phone level, especially for a before-and-after pair, and shoot
square-on to the face you want measured. If scale matters — a bed depth,
a stem thickness — put a tape in the shot, which is the one habit that
turns a picture into data. Flash off, lens wiped, three of everything.

## Naming and filing

Rename the same day, in the same shape, so it is findable at the end of the
course:

```
lastname-sitename-stage-yyyy-mm-dd
gordon-shoreline-before-2026-05-04
```

Then caption it while you remember: what it shows, when, and what your
part was — photographs without captions go anonymous in a month.

Photograph places, not people, unless the person has agreed. At the farm
and on any client's site ask first — it is somebody's business, and what
you see there is theirs. It all feeds [[The Evidence File]] and, in the
end, [[The Evidence Portfolio]].
