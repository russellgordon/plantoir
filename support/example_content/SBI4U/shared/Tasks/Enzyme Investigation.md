> [!abstract] At a glance
> **Format:** a designed investigation plus a full report. **Designed and
> run in pairs; the report is yours alone.** **Due:** handed in on the
> first class of Unit 2. **Assessed on:** Thinking and Investigation,
> Communication, Application.

You design this one. I supply the enzymes and the veto.

## The brief

Ask a question about enzyme behaviour that the equipment here can answer,
then answer it. Your procedure is approved before anything is opened.

Questions that have worked: does the optimum temperature of catalase
differ between liver and potato? Does an inhibitor's effect depend on
substrate concentration? Does pepsin's optimum pH match the stomach's?

## Requirements

- One manipulated variable, at least five levels, three trials each.
- Controlled variables listed and actually controlled.
- A graph whose shape you explain with protein structure.
- An uncertainty treatment — biological material varies, and saying by how
  much is part of the science.

The report structure is on [[Writing a Lab Report]], and the sentence-level
habits on [[Writing About Biology]].

## Where your enzyme earns a living

One paragraph, at the end of the report. Name a use of your enzyme, or of
a close relative of it, in the food industry or in medicine — a dairy, a
bakery, a brewing line, a diagnostic kit, a drug that inhibits one on
purpose — and say which of the properties you measured is the reason it
can be used there, and what would go wrong in that setting if the
property were different. A pH optimum that sits where a stomach sits, or
a rate that survives forty degrees, is a commercial fact as well as a
biochemical one. [[Proteins and Enzymes]] and
[[Where This Biology Leads]] are starting points.

## Designed together, written alone

The question, the procedure and the bench work are your pair's. The
report is yours: two people may hand in the same table of rates, and two
people who hand in the same explanation of the curve have handed in one
report between them.

## How this is assessed

Published on launch day, Unit 1, Day 10. Run them against your draft with
[[Judging Your Own Work]] at the end of Unit 1, Day 13.

| Quality | What it looks like in your report |
| --- | --- |
| A question the equipment can answer | One manipulated variable, at least five levels, and a hypothesis with a mechanism in it rather than a direction |
| Controls that were actually controlled | Every controlled variable listed, and the reading that shows it held — not a promise that it did |
| Three trials that mean three trials | The full set of raw rates, including any you set aside, with the reason beside it |
| The graph explains itself | Axes scaled to the data and labelled; the curve read for its shape on both sides of the peak, not only for where the peak is |
| The mechanism is named | Why the rate falls after an optimum and why it plateaus are different answers, and yours says which one your curve is showing and how the protein's shape produces it |
| The conclusion answers the question you asked | Stated against your own hypothesis, with the numbers that decide it named, and "this does not settle it" where the five levels cannot separate two explanations |
| Uncertainty treated as biology | How much your living material varied, which step could have misled you and in which direction, and one change that would reduce it |
| The enzyme put to work | A use in food production or in medicine named, tied to a property you measured rather than to one you read about, and what would go wrong there if that property were different |

Triangulation — the evidence you will not have unless you go and get it.

OBSERVE — Unit 1, Day 12, the bench period
  Watch for: whether the pair runs the five levels the same way each time.
  Where the timing starts. Whether the bath is checked once the period
  gets long, or set at the beginning and trusted. Whether the syringe is
  rinsed between concentrations. A report shows you the rates that came
  out; nothing in it distinguishes five careful runs from five hurried
  ones with the same means.
  Going well: one of the pair on the clock and the same start signal every
  trial, and somebody re-reading the thermometer without being asked.
  Stuck: the timing starting whenever both of them are ready, and the bath
  untouched since the first tube went in.
  Record: two ticks per pair on the day plan — same start signal, bath
  rechecked. Thirty seconds a bench.
  Note: the second criteria row asks for the reading that shows a control
  held, so the bath does get self-reported — as one number, taken at some
  point. Watching is how you learn whether that number was read three
  times or written down once and reused.

TALK — Unit 1, Day 11, at the procedure approvals already on that agenda
  This is before any data exists, which is the point: you are hearing the
  design thinking while it can still change something.
  Ask: "Which one of your controlled variables would wreck this first if
  it slipped, and what would that look like in your numbers?"
  Then: "Five levels — why those five? What sits between two of them that
  you would like to know and are not going to?"
  A strong answer names a specific variable, says which direction the
  rates would move, and can point at the gap in the spacing where the
  interesting behaviour probably is. That is A1.5 heard — controlling the
  relevant variables, and knowing which of them is relevant, is a decision
  taken before the bench and invisible afterwards. A procedure that lists
  eight controls reads identically whether or not anybody worked out which
  one mattered.
  Record: three columns on the approval sheet — named a control and its
  direction, named a control only, neither.

The product evidence is the report, handed in on the first class of
Unit 2.
