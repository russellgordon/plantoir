In May 1939 the **MS St. Louis** sailed from Hamburg carrying more than 900
Jewish refugees. Cuba refused them. The United States refused them. A
committee of Canadians petitioned the government to let the ship land here,
and Canada refused them too. The *St. Louis* returned to Europe. Britain,
France, Belgium and the Netherlands took the passengers in, and after the
German invasions 254 of the 620 who had landed on the continent were
murdered in the Holocaust — the figure the United States Holocaust Memorial
Museum's research established. Canada apologized in the House of Commons on
7 November 2018.

This inquiry asks whether the refusal was a mistake of the moment or the
policy working exactly as designed.

## The question

**Given what was known in Canada at the time, and given what Canada was
capable of, could more have been done — and by whom?**

Note "by whom." A country is not a single actor. Government, civil service,
press, churches and organized communities each decided separately.

## The evidence set

**Immigration policy in the 1930s**, including the phrase "none is too many"
— reportedly an official's answer to how many Jews Canada should admit, and
later the title of Irving Abella and Harold Troper's history of it.

**The arrival figures**, which are the unarguable part. The curriculum
states it plainly: from 1933 to 1945 Canada admitted **fewer than 5,000**
Jewish refugees. Set that against the annual immigration series and against
a population of over ten million. **Canada's conduct at the Evian Conference
in 1938**, where thirty-two countries met about refugees and almost none
opened a door.

**The record of pressure from inside Canada** — the Canadian Jewish Congress
and Saul Hayes, the member of Parliament A. A. Heaps pressing Mackenzie
King, and later Max Enkin and the Tailor Project, which brought skilled
workers out of the displaced persons camps while the door was still shut.
**The antisemitism the policy sat in**: university quotas, restrictive
covenants on property, "Gentiles Only" signs, the riot at Christie Pits.

> [!danger] Precision is not optional on this page
> This is a subject where a rounded number or a half-remembered date does
> real damage. Use the figures your sources actually state, name the source
> in the sentence, and where a body states its own uncertainty, carry that
> uncertainty into your writing. If you cannot verify it, leave it out — an
> argument is not improved by a detail you invented to strengthen it.
