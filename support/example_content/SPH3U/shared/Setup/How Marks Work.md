Physics marks worry people for one wrong reason: the belief that the
number is a verdict on how clever you are. It is not. It is a judgement
about work — what you built, what you measured, what you can explain —
and every piece of it is written down before you start.

## The seventy and the thirty

Every Grade 11 credit in Ontario is built the same way. **Seventy per
cent** of your mark comes from work spread across the whole semester, and
it leans towards your **most recent and most consistent** work rather than
averaging September against January. A unit you struggled through in
October and understand properly by December is judged on December.

**The seventy** is six things, in the order you meet them:
[[Investigation Reports]] — the five lab write-ups that carry a mark —
[[Motion Story]], [[Forces in a Real Situation]],
[[Model Roller Coaster]], [[The Energy Report]], and
[[Sound in a Space]]. They are not equally weighted: the write-ups run
all semester and add up, and the roller-coaster analysis and the energy
report are the two largest single pieces. The earlier ones are the
rehearsals that make the later ones possible. Ask me for the exact split
whenever you want it. It is not a secret; it is just not the useful thing
to memorise.

**The thirty per cent** comes from a final evaluation towards the end of
the course: the [[Motor and Generator Report]], which is the culminating
task and asks for everything at once, and the [[Final Examination]],
written on paper under the same conditions as everyone else. Two very
different rooms, deliberately, so that neither one afternoon nor one
device decides this alone.

## What is not in the seventy

Practice sets. Rough work. The first version of a write-up. Being wrong
in a discussion. Your [[Physics Journal]]. The work you do between
classes to get ready for the next one is practice, and practice is never
the thing I mark — if it were, you would stop taking risks with it, which
is the only reason it works.

The tasks themselves are built in the working periods, where I can watch
them being made, and that is deliberate. Two of them need something a
classroom cannot give you — a real motion filmed out in the world, a room
measured while it is being used — and those two task pages say so. The
rest happens where I am.

If it is not a page in [[Tasks/index|Tasks]], or named on one, it is not
being marked — which is how five of the twelve investigations come to
carry a mark and the other seven do not. If a task page does not say how
it will be judged, that is a bug: tell me and I will fix the page.

## The four categories

Ontario reports physics in four categories, and they measure genuinely
different things:

| Category | The question it asks |
| --- | --- |
| Knowledge and Understanding | Do you know the physics, and can you explain it? |
| Thinking and Investigation | Can you plan an investigation and reason from data? |
| Communication | Can another person follow your reasoning? |
| Application | Can you use the physics somewhere it has not been used for you? |

A perfect calculation with no diagram, no units, and no sentence is not a
perfect answer. It is a strong Knowledge answer and a weak Communication
one, and the report card will say so.

The fourth category is why [[Sound in a Space]] and
[[Forces in a Real Situation]] exist. Anyone can finish the problem set
that follows the lesson; the course is asking whether you can walk into a
corridor or a wet road nobody has analysed for you and get somewhere.

## Three kinds of evidence, and two of them are not paper

**What you make** is the obvious one: reports, graphs, a working motor, a
built track. **What I watch you do** is the second: whether you take a
doubtful reading again, whether you change one thing at a time, whether
the positive direction gets chosen before the graph is drawn or after the
answer is known. **What you tell me** is the third — the conferences in
the working periods are not a progress check, they are evidence in their
own right, and some things you understand will only ever show up there.

All three count. If your write-up is unfinished and you can talk me
through what your data means and what you would measure next, that
conversation is real evidence and it is used. That is not a consolation
prize. It is how the mark is meant to work.

## Feedback, and where it lands

Every task except the examination has a point partway through where you
find out what is wrong while you can still fix it, and time afterwards
whose job is fixing it. The shape is not the same for both kinds of work,
so here is exactly what the schedule gives you:

- **The six task pages** get a conference during a working period, and
  then the next working period to act on what it found.
- **The five write-ups** get the analysis period itself. Put a draft in
  front of me during it, I will name the weakest row, and the rest of
  that period is yours to deal with it.

That is why nothing substantial in this course is launched and then
simply collected. Once per unit you will also judge your own draft
against the criteria before I ever see it: [[Judging Your Own Work]] is
the routine, and we do it together on somebody else's work the first
time.

## What is not in your mark at all

How you work is reported separately, on the same report card, as **E, G,
S or N** — six habits: responsibility, organization, independent work,
collaboration, initiative, and self-regulation. They matter, we will talk
about them, and they do not move your percentage. Your mark is about the
work; that column is about the worker, and mixing the two tells you less
about both.

There is one narrow exception, and it is narrow on purpose. Expectation
A1.5 asks you to conduct inquiries "using appropriate materials and
equipment safely, accurately, and effectively", so safe and accurate
bench technique is written into the curriculum, and it is marked like
anything else in the curriculum — on [[Investigation Reports]], which
says how. That is the whole of it: what your hands do at the bench. It is
not punctuality, effort, tidiness, or how easy you are to work with.

Your own judgement of your work, and your classmates', are not part of
your mark either. You will assess your own work against the criteria
often, and you will read each other's drafts, because both are the
fastest ways to get better. But the mark is mine to determine, from
evidence, and nothing a classmate writes about your work becomes part of
it.

Where a task is built or measured in a group, the thing that is marked is
yours alone — your analysis, your report, your answer at the
presentation. Every group task page says which part that is.

## Late and missed work

Talk to me before the due date and it is a conversation; there is almost
always a version of the deadline that works. A report that arrives late
is still marked on the physics in it — lateness is a habit, and habits
live in the E/G/S/N column, not in your percentage.

The one way lateness reaches your mark is work that never arrives at all,
because then there is nothing to judge. That is worth avoiding by a wide
margin, and the way to avoid it is to talk to me early. See
[[Getting Help]].
