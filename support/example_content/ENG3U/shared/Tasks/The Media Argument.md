> [!abstract] At a glance
> Made in pairs, marked one person at a time · three class periods — one
> to analyse, one to make, one to act on the feedback and show it · an
> analysis of about 700 words and one short media text you produce, made
> in class · assessed on Thinking, Application, and Communication

## Part one: the analysis

Take one text from [[Media Texts We Study]] or one you propose, and
answer, with evidence from the text itself:

1. What is the claim, stated as its makers would state it?
2. Who made it, for whom, and with what interest?
3. What evidence carries the claim, and what does the form let it skip?
4. Which four techniques do the persuading, and how?
5. What is left out, and who would have said it?

## Part two: the text you make

A ninety-second audio segment, one designed piece — poster, page, feed —
or a storyboarded sequence of six frames, arguing something you actually
believe, to a named audience, using at least three techniques you
identified.

Short on purpose. You get the making period and the one after it, in
this room, and what you hand in is what those periods produced — not an
evening's work I never saw you do. Ninety seconds that know exactly what
they are beat three minutes with one good idea in them.

## Whose mark is whose

You work in a pair and there is no pair mark. Two things are yours alone:

1. **Your half of the analysis**, named at the top — two of the five
   questions each, and question five answered by both of you separately,
   in silence, in the last fifteen minutes of the first period. "What is
   left out" is where two readers most often differ, and it is no use to
   anybody as one agreed answer.
2. **Your own 200-word note**: your audience, the techniques you argued
   for, the concession you wanted, and the one choice you are least sure
   about, and one thing your reading or listening this term taught you
   that you used here. Written on your own, in the first ten minutes of
   the day it is due, without reading each other's first.

## How it is marked

| Category | What I am looking for |
| --- | --- |
| Thinking | The analysis reads the text rather than describing it; the "left out" answer names people, not topics |
| Application | The made text uses its techniques deliberately and takes a real position |
| Communication | Both parts are clear; the made text works on its intended audience |

> [!important] The line
> You may persuade. You may not fabricate — no invented statistics, no
> invented quotations, no real person made to appear to say something
> they did not. Learning the techniques is exactly why the line matters.

Triangulation — the evidence you will not have unless you go and get it.

OBSERVE — Unit 4, Day 3, the last fifteen minutes, while question five is
written apart and in silence
  Two answers to question five will reach you on paper. What the paper
  cannot tell you is whether they were arrived at separately, and that is
  the one thing this task turns on: question five is the piece that is
  each partner's own.
  Watch for: what each of them does when the talking stops. Going back
  into their own marked copy of the text is reading; writing down what
  the pair agreed twenty minutes ago is transcription, and the two pages
  look identical either way.
  Going well: two marked copies open, and two answers that name
  different people.
  Stuck: one copy between them, or a whisper across the desk before
  either of them starts.
  Record: two columns on the pairs list — went back to the text, wrote
  from the conversation.

TALK — Unit 4, Day 4, the two minutes with each pair already on that
agenda
  Ask each partner in turn, so you hear two answers rather than one.
  Ask: "Which technique are you using that you did not admire in the text
  you analysed?"
  Then: "What will your audience be able to say back to you that you have
  not answered?"
  A strong answer names a technique and the effect it is meant to have on
  a named audience, and can say what the concession costs them. That is
  D3.3, identifying techniques appropriate to the form and explaining how
  they will communicate a specific aspect of the intended meaning — a
  plan, and the finished text can only ever show you the result.
  Record: two initials and one word each on the pairs list.

The product evidence is the analysis, the made text and each partner's
own note, handed in on Day 5.
