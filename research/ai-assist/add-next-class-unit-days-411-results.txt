THE FINDING THE SCORES HIDE - and the reason this is not close
  The suites score add_next_class by its NAME, and every "add the next
  class" probe stayed "OK". Their arguments did not. One trial each with the
  full arguments printed (the suite truncates them):
    smaller: all 6 phrasings  -> {"unit": "next"}
    larger:  5 of 6 phrasings -> {"days": 1, "unit": "next"};  1 -> {"days": 1}
  The schema text (Windows', verbatim) says 'Pass "next" to start a new
  unit'. Both models read the word "next" in "add the NEXT class" as that
  instruction, so the shipped runner would START A NEW UNIT - Unit N+1,
  Day 1 - for a teacher who asked for the next day of the unit they are in.
  It reports success. That is the "wrong unit" damage this bundle was told
  to hunt, and it happens on 6 of 6 and 5 of 6 phrasings, not at the margin.

VERDICT

  larger:  The pre-registered rule, read literally, also FAILS ("read" went

WHAT WAS NOT TRIED, deliberately
  Rewording the `unit` description ("only when the teacher says a NEW
  unit"). The brief forbids steering the model through a tool description,
  and doc 10's precedent is why: one added sentence once cost 20 trials and
  broke three perfect probes. If Russell still wants the empty call refused,
  the cheap route is to change no schema. Count `add_next_class` as needing
  more than the window supplies, by name. The trade-off is that the rule then
  names a tool instead of reading its schema. That is for him to decide (#411).

WINDOWS, by inference only (not measured here)
  Windows' local add_next_class has always declared `unit` with this text.
  If its router reads the same sentence the same way, an ordinary "add the
  next class" on Windows may already start a new unit. That needs a Windows
  measurement with full arguments printed, not a tool-name score.
