## What belongs in Writing Workshops

Craft, not theory. A Writing Workshops page shows a student *how* to do
something — build a thesis, punctuate a quotation, cluster research notes
— through a concrete comparison or a worked example, never through
abstract advice like "use strong evidence" that gives no guidance on what
strong evidence actually looks like next to weak evidence. If the page you
want to write explains *what a concept is* rather than *how to do
something with it*, it probably belongs in Concepts instead.

## Reminders

- **Show, don't just tell.** Every workshop page needs a concrete
  weak/strong comparison, a worked example, or a step-by-step walkthrough
  — something a student could hold up against their own draft, not just
  advice to follow on faith.
- **Demonstrate one Obsidian feature, genuinely** — a table, a callout, a
  small diagram, a checklist, a footnote — whichever one actually serves
  the content. Don't force a feature the page doesn't need.
- **Link it from a class page**, directly or through one page a class page
  already links to — check the day this skill is actually taught.
- **If this page teaches or is evidenced by a specific curriculum
  expectation**, add the block near the end, after everything else:

```markdown
%%curriculum-start%%
## Curriculum connection
![[X1.1]]
%%curriculum-end%%
```

Only transclude a code this page genuinely addresses — not the whole
strand, and never a code that merely sounds related.
