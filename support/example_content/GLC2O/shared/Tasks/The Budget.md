**Unit 3. Individual. Handed in twenty-five minutes into Day 5, at the
end of the revision block.** A spreadsheet, plus two pages of writing.

A twelve-month budget for your first year after secondary school,
sourced, and a written argument about how it would be funded.

## The budget itself

Take [[The Cost of a Month]] out to a full year. A year is not twelve
identical months — tuition and program fees arrive at the start, tools
and equipment arrive once, income from summer work arrives in a lump,
and some months have nothing coming in at all. Show the months
separately and show the running total, because the running total is
where the trouble appears.

| Must appear | Why it is required |
| --- | --- |
| Every figure with its source and the date you looked | The number is checkable, and you can re-check it in Grade 12 |
| Net income, not gross | Deductions are real money and students routinely forget them |
| Income marked certain, likely, or hoped-for | A budget that balances on hopes does not balance |
| At least one month with a shortfall, if there is one | Hiding it is how real budgets fail |
| A running balance, month by month | Timing is the thing an annual total conceals |

## The written part

**One page on funding.** How the year would be paid for. If borrowing is
part of it, compare at least two of the forms set out in
[[Money In, Money Out]] — what each costs, what it
requires, what happens if you cannot repay. If it is not part of it, say
what makes that possible. Government student assistance, grants,
bursaries, awards, savings, employment income, and family or community
support all count, and you should say which of them you would actually
have access to.

**One page on saving and managing.** Where money you are not spending
would sit and why, what the account would cost you in fees, and two
specific habits you would use to keep the plan on track — how bills get
paid on time, and how you would know when the plan was slipping.

## What earns the marks

- **Sourced numbers.** Every figure traceable, with a date.
- **Realistic timing.** The months differ, and the cash-flow problems
  are visible rather than averaged away.
- **Borrowing understood.** Cost, requirement, and risk for each form
  compared, not just listed.
- **A funded plan.** The income side is plausible for your actual
  circumstances.
- **Named gaps.** What you could not find out, and who you would ask.

If your plan involves no tuition and no move, the budget is not easier —
it is a different budget, and the same rules apply to every line of it.
