By mid-course your [[Tech Journal]] stops being only a log and becomes
something rarer: **evidence**. The person who wrote your first entries
does not quite exist any more — and because that person wrote
honestly, and wrote down numbers, you can prove it. Put early-you and
current-you side by side and let the difference speak for itself.

## Growth is a claim plus evidence, like any measurement

"I got better at electronics" is a claim, and on its own it is worth
about as much as "the voltage was fine". A service log never says
"fixed it somehow" — it records the symptom, the tests, the values
measured, the part replaced, and the verification, so that the next
technician can trust it. Growth writing meets the same standard: every
claim backed by two dated entries of your own, quoted rather than
summarised.

> In my first-week entry I wrote: "I had the meter on the wrong
> setting for about a minute and thought the circuit was dead." Last
> week I caught the same mistake on the bench next to mine before
> they powered up — the leads were still in the current jack and the
> dial was on volts, which would have blown the fuse the moment they
> touched the supply. I did not know that was a thing that could
> happen. Now I check other people's jacks without thinking about it.

The claim is *instrument discipline stopped being a rule and became a
reflex*. The evidence is two moments, months apart, both on the
record. Nobody can argue with that, including the part of you that
suspects you have not changed. It is the same standard
[[How Marks Work]] applies everywhere in this course: what can you
point to?

## How to cite yourself

Treat your earlier entries the way a report treats sources.

- **Date it.** "In my entry from the second week of the course…" The
  distance is part of the evidence.
- **Quote a phrase, not a page.** Your words then carry more truth
  than your summary now. A single sentence from
  [[Your First Entry]] — "I cannot tell you what value a resistor is
  by looking at it" — says more than a paragraph of "I was new to
  components."
- **Then show the now.** A recent moment, just as specific, that the
  earlier version of you could not have managed. Include the numbers;
  they are what makes it undeniable.

## Growth is not always a bigger build

Some of it is a new noticing. Hearing a supply's current limit kick in
and knowing what that sound means. Looking at a schematic and seeing
the divider before you see the parts. Reading a datasheet's absolute
maximum column and feeling the small flinch that stops you designing
right up against it. Catching, in [[Spot the Hazard]], the hazard that
consists of something being *absent*.

None of that photographs well and none of it looks like an
achievement from the outside. It is growth all the same, and your
early entries are the proof that it was not always there — which is
the entire reason we bother writing them down.
