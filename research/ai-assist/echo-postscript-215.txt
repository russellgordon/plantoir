PART ONE — "hide <page>" chose no tool at all
----------------------------------------------
  sentence                        chose
  hide unit 4, day 21             (no tool) — replied with the sentence back
  Hide Unit 4, Day 21             (no tool) — echo
  hide unit 2, day 3              (no tool) — echo
  hide Unit 4, Day 2              (no tool) — echo
  Hide this page: Unit 4, Day 21  (no tool) — echo
  unpublish unit 4, day 21        unpublish_pages {ICS4U, 1, "Unit 4, Day 21"} OK
  Unpublish Unit 4, Day 21        unpublish_pages OK
  Unpublish Unit 2, Day 3         unpublish_pages OK

Five phrasings of "hide", five with no tool. The same request worded
"unpublish" was right every time.

IDENTICAL WITH AND WITHOUT THE REQUEST CAP (max_tokens 512, #166), so this is
not caused by anything that landed on 2026-09-19: the tool surface and the
system prompt were byte-identical across every merge that day, and the uncapped
body is the one v1.1.0 sent. The failure errs in the SAFE direction — nothing
is hidden and nothing else happens — and what a teacher sees is their own
sentence handed back, which reads as broken.

The larger assistant was NOT measured here; it is not downloaded on this Mac.
So nothing in this file says anything about the 4B, and a claim that it is
unaffected would be a guess.

This is not the first sign. conversational-residue-results.txt already had
"HIDE — the inversion case" at 3/3 declined outright on the small tier, and
#167 showed a single token — a weekday word, a course code — flipping its
answer. The word was on record as the weak spot before this.

PART TWO — one echo poisons the whole conversation
---------------------------------------------------
Found again in an ICD2O window and then replayed exactly, on ICD2O AND ICS4U,
pages "Unit 4, Day 20" and "Unit 4, Day 21". Identical in all four:

  conversation                                          result
  FRESH: "Unpublish Unit 4, Day 20"                     unpublish_pages OK
  turn 1: "hide unit 4, day 20"                         no tool — echoes the
                                                        sentence, dateline and all
  turn 2, after that echo:                              no tool — ECHOES AGAIN
    "Unpublish Unit 4, Day 20"

So it is not the course token and not the page: it is the HISTORY. The echoed
reply was kept in `messages`, and the model then copied the pattern it could
see — user says X, assistant says X — for every later request. One unrecognised
phrase made the assistant useless until the window was closed and reopened.
The FRESH row is the one that says what the fix had to be: a clean conversation
works.

WHAT WAS DONE, AND WHAT WAS REJECTED
-------------------------------------
Two things, both in code, neither of them a change to what the model is shown.

1. "hide" is answered in code. `AssistCardCommand.wholeUnit` became
   `wholeUnitOrClassPage`: one frame, one verb table, and the DAY arm gated on
   the verb. Contract rows: `assist-cases.json` → `hideIsUnpublish`.

2. An echoed reply is refused and the turn is wound back out of `messages`.
   Contract rows: `assist-cases.json` → `echoedRequest`.

REJECTED, and each for a reason worth keeping:

  * Steering the model with a sentence in a tool description. This repository's
    own rule, and the precedent is measured: one clarifying sentence added to
    `publish_pages`' description took the promise-card score from 110/110 to
    90/110 and broke three probes that had been perfect. A small model reads a
    description naming another tool as a recommendation, not a boundary.
  * Mirroring the frame on the PUBLISH side ("publish unit 4, day 3"). Publish
    is the direction that reaches students; unpublishing errs safe. And there
    is nothing to buy: "Publish Unit 2, Day 3" is 10/10 on this tier.
  * `show` / `unhide` as publish-side synonyms. "Show unit 4" is at least as
    likely to mean "display it to me", and resolving that guess by publishing
    is the wrong way to be wrong. Not without a measurement, and not in v1.2.0.
  * Folding the echo's trail line into "assistant answer was cut off". That
    event's NAME would be false — that answer never finished and this one did
    — and a line describing something other than what happened is worse than no
    line, because it will be believed. A new event instead:
    "assistant repeated the request back".
  * Making the echo rule fuzzy, so a preamble ("Sure: hide unit 4, day 21")
    would be caught. A rule that fires on a legitimate answer throws a real
    reply away and tells the teacher it did not follow, which is worse than the
    fault it replaces.

CORRECTED ON REVIEW, BEFORE MERGE — THE PUBLISH ARM HAD WIDENED
----------------------------------------------------------------
The first version of the frame read the VERB after stripping "please" and a
trailing question mark, which widened publish as a side effect. An adversarial
differential fuzz across two compiled copies of the matcher — dev's and the
candidate's, every verb/noun/number/tail/spacing combination — found 0 matches
lost, 0 arguments changed, and **141 new publish_pages matches**, none of them
asked for:

  'please publish unit 4'   dev: NONE   candidate: MATCH publish_pages Unit 4
  'publish unit 4 please'   dev: NONE   candidate: MATCH publish_pages Unit 4
  'publish unit 4?'         dev: NONE   candidate: MATCH publish_pages Unit 4
  'publish  unit 5'         dev: NONE   candidate: MATCH publish_pages Unit 5
  'publish unit, 4'         dev: NONE   candidate: MATCH publish_pages Unit 4

"publish unit 4?" is the case that decided it: a teacher typing a question mark
is plausibly ASKING, and that sentence would have published a whole unit with
no model in the loop — the same ambiguity this file uses to reject "show unit
4". Publish is now a function of its own, unchanged from dev, and the tolerance
belongs to hide/unpublish alone. Re-run on a wider sweep after the gate:

  36,864 inputs, dev vs shipped:  0 matches LOST
                                  0 arguments CHANGED
                                  0 new publish_pages
                                  2,850 new unpublish_pages

Five of the 141 are pinned as refused rows in hideIsUnpublish, and the mac test
now asserts the TOLERANCE as well as the reference — a test naming only
"publish unit 4, day 3" did not catch this, which is the lesson.

WHAT WAS CORRECTED IN THE SUITES
---------------------------------
  * trimmed-surface-suite.py — `intercepted()` now reads the widened family
    through `unit_or_class_page()`, and a new
    `assert_hide_is_unpublish_matches_contract()` checks that reading against
    the contract's 13 accepted and 20 refused rows BEFORE any probe is sent,
    the way the deploy-at-a-time guard already does. The run prints the row
    count it agreed with.
  * trimmed-surface-suite.py's mirror was ALSO tightened after a measurement:
    552 inputs through it and a compiled copy of the real matcher disagreed on
    three shapes, all one-way (Python accepted, Swift did not) — a TAB between
    words, an integer that overflows 64 bits, and non-ASCII digits. The mirror
    now splits on the literal space, and reads a number the way Int(_:) does.
    Re-checked over 2,410 inputs: 0 disagreements. (A later, wider check of
    599,228 inputs agreed on every row and probe and still found three
    Unicode-whitespace classes where the two differ; the mirror is a guard
    for the rows, not a proof of equivalence.) None was reachable from a
    contract row or a probe, so no number here was ever distorted — but a
    mirror whose comment claims a spelling "cannot go unnoticed" had better not
    have three of them.
  * shelf-phrasings-suite.py — its hand-copied "matched in code" column was
    corrected for TWO phrasings, both of which had gone stale without anyone
    noticing: "Deploy at 6:30 AM" (stale since #168) and "Unpublish Unit 2,
    Day 3". That column only labels output and scores nothing, which is why it
    was able to rot; the comment above it now says so, and points at the
    arrangement to copy.
