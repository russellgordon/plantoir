## What we agree to

1. **Attack the idea, never the person.**
2. **Bring the passage.** At this level a claim without evidence is not
   yet a claim.
3. **Steelman before you object.** State the strongest version of the
   position you are about to argue against.
4. **You may pass**, without explanation.
5. **Changing your mind out loud is the best thing that happens in this
   room.**
6. **What is said about somebody's own life stays here.**

## The texts, and what they deal with

This course includes a play about grief, surveillance, and a young man
contemplating suicide, and a novel about a state built on the control of
women's bodies, including sexual violence. Every text page names what it
deals with.

You may step out of any class. You need not explain, and it costs you
nothing.

## Reading through a lens

This course teaches critical approaches — formalist, feminist,
postcolonial, Marxist, psychoanalytic, reader-response. A lens is a tool
for making something visible, not a verdict to be applied. Two things
follow: every reading has to be argued from the text, and no lens is
allowed to become a way of not reading.

Some of these approaches touch politics directly. Argue the reading, not
the person holding it — and expect to be argued with in the same way.

## Using AI honestly

It can produce an essay about *Hamlet* in seconds, and every assessment
here measures the reading and thinking that essay skipped.

| Fine | Not fine |
| --- | --- |
| Definitions, historical context, a word you are reaching for | An interpretation, a thesis, a paragraph |
| Quizzing yourself before a seminar | Generating seminar questions |
| Checking a citation format | Producing the citation from a source you have not read |

Say where you used it and how, in a line at the end.
