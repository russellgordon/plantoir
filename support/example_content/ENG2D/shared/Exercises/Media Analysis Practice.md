The method is on [[Media Forms and Techniques]].

**1.** An advertisement shows a family at a kitchen table, shot slightly
from below, in warm light, no music, with the product out of focus in the
foreground. Name three techniques and one effect each.

> [!success]- Answer 1
> Low angle: the family is given stature — this is aspiration, not
> instruction. Warm grade: nostalgia and safety. Shallow focus with the
> product foregrounded: the product is *present* in the memory without
> competing with it. And the absence of music tells you the target
> audience is one that distrusts being told how to feel.

**2.** Two front pages cover the same protest. One leads with a
photograph of a broken window; the other with a wide daylight shot of the
crowd. Neither has published a false fact. What is the difference called,
and what does each argue?

> [!success]- Answer 2
> Selection and emphasis, not falsehood — see [[Bias and Perspective]].
> The first argues that the event is a threat to order; the second, that
> it is a public expression of a position. The choice of lens made the
> argument before any word was written.

**3.** A documentary about a factory closure interviews eleven former
workers and no executives. Is that bias?

> [!success]- Answer 3
> It is a perspective, and it may be a justified one — the film may be
> about what closure does to workers. It becomes bias if the film claims
> to explain *why* the factory closed while systematically excluding the
> people who decided. State what the film can and cannot support.

**4.** What is left out of a thirty-second advertisement for a food
product? List four categories.

> [!success]- Answer 4
> Price, labour, supply chain, and everybody who does not fit the
> intended audience. Also: the product's competitors, and any measure by
> which it is worse.

**5.** Take one advertisement you like and write ninety words of
analysis on it.

> [!success]- Answer 5
> No fixed answer. The check: does every claim point at something visible
> or audible in the text? Analysis that could have been written without
> watching it is description of a genre, not analysis of a text.
