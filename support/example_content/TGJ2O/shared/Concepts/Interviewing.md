Your first take in [[Record a Clean Interview]] probably holds the
moment this page is about: the scripted question earned a one-word
answer, and the accidental follow-up — "wait, really?" — earned the
quote. An interview is a conversation with a purpose, and the purpose
is served by the other person doing the talking.

## Questions that open people up

Closed questions can be answered in a word: "Did you expect to win?" —
"No." Open questions hand over the floor:

- "Walk me through the last two minutes of the game."
- "What was going through your head when the whistle went?"
- "Tell me about the moment you knew."

None of those can be answered with yes or no, and none of them suggest
the answer you are hoping for. Order matters too: begin with easy,
factual questions that let a nervous person settle, and save the hard
one for late, once trust exists. Building a set like this is exactly
what [[Interview Question Practice]] drills.

## Listening is the real skill

A question list is a safety net, not a script. The best follow-up
questions cannot be written in advance because they respond to what was
just said — which means the real work of an interview is listening, not
asking. Silence is a tool: leave a pause after an answer, and people
often fill it with the honest version.

- [ ] Research done — names, spellings, dates, and the obvious facts
- [ ] Eight to ten questions written, ordered easy to hard
- [ ] Recorder tested, charged, with space left on the card
- [ ] Quiet location chosen and booked
- [ ] Person told who you are, what this is for, and that you are
      recording

## Technical setup, audio hygiene, and location checks

Clean audio capture requires technical discipline before asking the first
question:

- **Microphone positioning.** Place a directional microphone (or lavalier) 15
  to 20 cm from the subject's mouth, aimed toward the chin. This isolates the
  speaker's direct vocal sound while minimizing room reverberation and off-axis
  noise.
- **Location acoustics and background noise.** Choose an interior space with soft
  furnishings that absorb reflections. Avoid hard parallel walls, running
  refrigerators, and air vents. Record thirty seconds of silent room tone at the
  start of every location shoot to enable clean noise profiling during editing.
- **Level monitoring and gain staging.** Monitor audio live with wired
  headphones throughout the entire interview. Set pre-amplifier gain so dialogue
  peaks consistently between -12 dB and -6 dB, leaving adequate headroom to
  prevent digital clipping and distortion when a subject speaks louder.

## The quote promise

Recording someone is making a promise: their words will reach readers
as they said them, in their intended meaning. Quote exactly, check the
recording rather than your memory, and never sharpen a quote to make it
better copy. If someone hesitates about being recorded or named, that
is not an obstacle to talk them out of — it is information. What you
may publish, and what permission looks like, lives in
[[Releases and Consent]].
