## What you are making

A running record of your thinking: one short entry after every class,
written the same day while the details are warm. Ten honest minutes
beats thirty performed ones — and entries are **private**. I read
them; other students never do. The journal only works if you can
admit in it what you would not say at the boards — that you still
cannot tell a permutation from a combination without rereading the
question twice, that the conditional-probability tree makes sense
while you draw it and evaporates an hour later, that you nodded along
to a conclusion you did not actually believe.

## How entries work

Each entry answers four prompts, in whatever order helps:

- **What we did** — the facts, briefly: the number talk, the thinking
  task, where your group got to.
- **What clicked** — the moment something connected, and what did the
  connecting: a tree diagram, a classmate's phrasing, a smaller case
  you could list by hand.
- **What is foggy** — named precisely. "Probability is confusing" is
  weather; "I default to multiplying probabilities and only remember
  to ask about independence when someone else does" is a location.
- **What I would try** — one concrete move for next class. Not "focus
  more": something you could actually do.

Once [[The Culminating Investigation]] launches, add one line to
every entry about it, even on days we do not touch it: a source you
found, a question you narrowed, a variable you decided not to
collect. Those lines are the audit trail your final report will need,
and reconstructing them in the last week is not possible — which is
precisely why the investigation starts in the second unit and not the
final one.

Conjectures are welcome any time. "I think a bigger sample cannot fix
a biased one, and I want to test that on the survey we looked at
today" is a perfect journal sentence — dated, testable, and yours.

## Success criteria and collection

The journal comes in at the end of each unit, read against these
criteria — [[Journal Checklist]] turns them into a self-check before
you hand it over.

| Quality | What it looks like |
| --- | --- |
| Regularity | An entry for every class, written close to the day |
| Honesty | Real fog named, not just victories |
| Precision | Foggy things located, not just felt |
| Growth | "What I would try" items reappear as things actually tried |
| Traceability | The investigation's decisions are dated and explained |

> [!important] Which entries carry a mark, and which do not
> The nightly entries are practice, and practice is not marked in
> this course — see [[How Marks Work]]. What I evaluate is the
> **milestone entry**: the last fifteen minutes of the final class of
> Units 1, 2 and 3, written here, in the room, looking back across the
> unit against the five qualities above. The nightly entries are what
> make that fifteen minutes possible, which is the honest reason to
> keep them. Unit 4 ends differently: [[Final Reflection]] is its
> milestone entry, written in class on the last day before the review
> classes begin.
