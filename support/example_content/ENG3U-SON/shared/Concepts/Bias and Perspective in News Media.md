The essays you have been reading in [[Funny, You Don't Look Like One]]
keep pointing at something mainstream Canadian media assumes about
Indigenous people without ever stating it outright — an assumption so
embedded that most readers never notice it as a choice at all. That is
what bias actually looks like most of the time: not a shouted opinion,
but an unstated default.

## Perspective

**Perspective** is the vantage point a text is told from — whose
experience, knowledge, and priorities shape what gets included, how it is
framed, and what gets left out entirely. Every text has one; there is no
view from nowhere. A news story about a protest looks different
depending on whether it centres the protesters, the police, the
bystanders, or the business owners whose windows got broken — and which
one a given outlet centres is itself a decision, even if nobody says so.

## Bias

**Bias** is a slant in how information is selected, framed, or
presented — consistently favouring one perspective, interpretation, or
group over others. Bias is not always dishonest and not always
intentional: a headline writer working under deadline, choosing which
single fact to put first, is making a biased choice whether or not they
mean to. What matters for you as a reader is learning to notice it, not
assuming a text has no bias simply because nobody flagged it as
opinion.[^1]

[^1]: "Objectivity" in journalism is best understood as a professional
    ideal to aim at, not a state any single piece of writing fully
    achieves — every choice about what to report, quote, and headline is
    still a choice. That is a reason to read critically, not a reason to
    distrust everything equally; some texts work harder at fairness than
    others, and measuring how hard is part of the job.

## Reading for it

Ask, of any news or media text: whose perspective is centred here, and
whose is missing? What is stated as fact, and what is quietly assumed?
Those two questions will do most of the work, and you will get direct
practice with both of them very soon.
