What you should be able to do by the end of CHC2D, in plain words first
and in the Ministry's words underneath.

## By the end of the course you should be able to

- **Read a primary source** for what it is evidence of, not just what it
  says.
- **Build an argument** about the past from evidence, and answer the
  strongest objection to it.
- **Use the four concepts** — significance, cause and consequence,
  continuity and change, and perspective — to reason rather than to
  label.
- **Be precise about uncertainty**: say what is established, what is
  contested, and who says so.
- **Explain how Canada changed** between 1914 and now, and who did the
  changing.
- **Represent fairly a position you disagree with.**
- **Cite properly**, so that anyone can check you.

## The self-check that matters

Hand yourself a document you have never seen. Can you say who made it,
for whom, and why — and then what it is evidence of, and what it is not?
That is the whole course, and it is exactly what
[[The Document Examination]] asks.
