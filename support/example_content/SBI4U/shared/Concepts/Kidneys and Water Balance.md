The kidney is a filter that throws almost everything away and then takes
back what it wants — which sounds wasteful and is the only design that
works.

## Three steps in the nephron

1. **Filtration** at the glomerulus: pressure forces water, ions, glucose,
   and urea out of the blood. Cells and proteins stay.
2. **Reabsorption** in the tubules: glucose, most water, and needed ions
   are taken back, largely by active transport.
3. **Secretion**: additional wastes and excess ions are added directly.

About 180 litres are filtered per day and about 1.5 litres leave as urine.

## Why filter-then-recover

Because a filter based on size can be non-specific and fast, while
selectivity is applied afterwards by transporters that each handle one
thing. Trying to be selective at the filter would require a transporter
for every unwanted molecule, including ones the body has never met.

## ADH

When you are dehydrated, the hypothalamus detects it, the pituitary
releases ADH, and the collecting ducts become more permeable to water.
More water returns, urine is concentrated. Alcohol suppresses ADH, which
is most of why a hangover involves thirst.
