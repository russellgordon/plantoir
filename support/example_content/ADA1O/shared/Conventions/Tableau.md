A **tableau** is a frozen stage picture: bodies arranged and held still, like a
photograph made of people. It is the first convention we learn because it
strips drama down to its bones — no lines to remember, no movement to plan,
just the question every scene eventually answers: *what should the audience
see, and what should it mean?*

## Making one worth looking at

A weak tableau is a group of people standing near each other. A strong one
makes five deliberate choices:

| Choice | The question it answers |
| --- | --- |
| Levels | Who is high, who is low — and what does that say about power? |
| Focus | Where do the bodies point the audience's eye? |
| Contrast | What differs — stillness against reach, calm against strain? |
| Space | Who is close to whom? Who is left alone? |
| Expression | What is each face and hand doing, precisely? |

> [!tip] The eye goes where the bodies point
> If every performer looks at the same spot, the audience will too — even if
> there is nothing there. That is [[Focus and Emphasis|focus]], and tableau is
> the cleanest place to practise it.

## Working in sequence

One frozen image is a photograph; three in a row are a story. Moving between
tableaux — freeze, flow, freeze — forces a group to decide what the *turning
points* of their story are, which is harder and more useful than it sounds.

> [!success]- Try this with any fairy tale (click to expand)
> Choose the three moments the story cannot be told without. If your group
> argues about which three, the exercise is already working — that argument
> is dramaturgy.

## Tableau on the professional stage

Tableau is not only a classroom exercise. In this interview from Canada's
Theatre Museum, the two founding directors of Opera Atelier, a Toronto
company that stages opera and ballet from the seventeenth and eighteenth
centuries, talk about tableau and the thinking behind their stage pictures.

![](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=EIOxEqlYgXo)

*Pynkoski and Lajeunesse on tableau and aesthetics (Part 7 of 9). Canada's
Theatre Museum, interview recorded in Toronto in 2011.*

Listen with the table above beside you:

- What do they say a stage picture is for? Is it the answer this page
  gives, or a different one?
- Which of the five choices do they talk about, in their own words?
- They work with trained singers and dancers. Name one thing they ask for
  that you could ask of your own group tomorrow.

The interview plays from YouTube, so it needs an internet connection.

Tableau combines naturally with [[Thought Tracking]] — tap a frozen shoulder
and the character speaks a single thought aloud — and it is the backbone of
our first performance task, [[Tableau Story Sequence]].
