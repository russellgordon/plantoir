Reporters research constantly — the skill is not avoiding the search
bar but using it like a reporter: primary sources first, provenance
always, credit for everything that helped.

## Primary sources first

The league's own schedule beats a repost of the schedule. The
policy document beats an article about the policy; the person who
was there beats the person who heard about it. Every hop away from
the origin adds someone else's summary, and summaries drift. When a
search result describes a document, your next move is finding the
document.

## Ask who published this, and when

Before believing any page, answer three questions: who wrote or
published it, when, and why it exists. A named author on an
organisation's own site, dated this year, is one kind of source; an
undated, unattributed page selling something is another. A claim
you cannot attribute or date is a rumour with formatting.

## Trace quotes to their origin

A quote is only as good as its chain of custody. "As she reportedly
said" means someone else claims she said it — find the interview,
the recording, the transcript, or treat the quote as unconfirmed. A
line that cannot be traced to where it was actually said does not go
in our stories, however good it sounds.

## Designs other people have shared

For a making project the search is for designs as well as facts.
Model-sharing sites hold thousands of free 3D models and cut files,
and finding one that nearly solves your problem is good research, not
cheating — if you do two things. Read its **licence** before you
download it, because it decides whether you may change it and share
the change ([[Copyright and Fair Dealing]] has the labels). And read it
as evidence rather than an answer: who made it, for which phone or
tripod, and did anyone who printed it report that it worked?

## Credit what you use

Whatever helped — a document, an article, a photograph, a
machine-generated summary you verified — gets named where you used
it, per [[Copyright and Fair Dealing]] and the credit standard in
[[Our Newsroom Standards]]. Searching well is professional skill;
crediting well is professional honesty.

These are the everyday half of [[Misinformation and Verification]] —
the checks you run on your own sources before a reader has to. The
reps live in [[Verification Practice]].
