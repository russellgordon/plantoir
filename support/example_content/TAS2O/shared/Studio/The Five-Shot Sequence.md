Five shots of one person doing one thing — hands, face, wide,
over-the-shoulder, and one creative angle — cut together in that
order into a story that needs no dialogue at all. This is the oldest
drill in video journalism because it works: every skill in
[[Shooting Video]] hides inside these five frames.

> [!warning] Care and consent notes
> **Strap first, always** — neck or wrist before the camera leaves
> the bag, and handoffs are two hands to two hands, never a toss.
> **Ask before you shoot** — your subject agrees before the lens
> comes up, and a recognisable face on anything published needs
> [[Releases and Consent|a release]]. **Back up before the card
> clears** — same day; [[Backing Up Your Media]] is the routine.

## What you need

- [ ] The class camera (a phone is fine), charged, with an empty card
- [ ] A teammate doing a real, repeatable task — sharpening a pencil,
      pumping a bike tire
- [ ] Your reporter's notebook with the five shots listed
- [ ] Ten minutes with your editing software afterward

## The work

1. **Pick a task with visible hands.** The sequence lives on action —
   a person thinking hard makes five identical shots; a person doing
   something gives you five different ones.
2. **Shot one — close-up on the hands.** Fill the frame with the
   action itself. Hold the shot a full ten seconds, steady; you can
   always trim, but you cannot un-wobble.
3. **Shot two — close-up on the face.** Concentration is the story
   here. Focus on the eyes, and let your subject forget the camera
   before you press record.
4. **Shot three — wide.** Step back until person, task, and place all
   fit in frame. This is the shot that tells viewers where they are.
5. **Shot four — over the shoulder.** Shoot past your subject's
   shoulder toward the work, so the viewer stands where they stand.
6. **Shot five — something creative.** Low angle, high angle, through
   a doorway, reflected in a window. One risk per sequence.
7. **Cut them together in order, no dialogue.** Trim each shot to its
   cleanest few seconds and watch the story appear — the cuts feel
   invisible because every shot shows something new.

## What can go wrong

- **Shots too short to cut.** Recording stopped the moment the action
  looked done. Hold every shot ten full seconds — editors call the
  extra "handles", and they are what makes trimming possible.
- **Your subject performs.** Grinning at the lens breaks all five
  shots at once. Ask them to repeat the task until it turns boring —
  boring for them is natural for the camera.
- **Five shots, one distance.** If the wide and the close-up look
  alike, the cuts will jump. Move your feet between every shot.

## Level up

Shoot the same task as a second sequence in a different order, or add
natural sound only — the scrape of the pencil, the click of the pump —
and notice how much story sound carries before a single word is said.
