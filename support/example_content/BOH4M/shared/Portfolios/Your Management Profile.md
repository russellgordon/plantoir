The first entry in your portfolio, written in the second week of the
leadership unit and revisited at the end of the course.

## What to do

1. Complete one personality or working-style instrument. Your teacher
   will offer a free one; if you have taken Myers-Briggs, True Colors, or
   an emotional-intelligence measure elsewhere, you may use that result
   instead.
2. Write the result down, exactly as the instrument reported it.
3. Then do the part the instrument cannot: **test it**.

## The writing (about 500 words)

- Where does the result match how you actually behave? Give two specific
  occasions — a group project, a shift at work, a team you led.
- Where does it not match? Everyone has a mismatch; the honest ones are
  the interesting part.
- What did the situation explain that the label did not? Think about
  where you were the newest person, the only person of your kind in the
  room, or under real time pressure.
- What would you want a manager to know about how you work, and what
  would you want them to ignore?

> [!warning] A label is a hypothesis, not a verdict
> Instruments like these are used in workplaces for self-awareness and
> team-building, and they are sometimes misused to sort people. Your
> critical assessment of the instrument is worth as much here as your
> result — see [[Personality and Behaviour]].

## At the end of the course

You will re-read this before writing
[[Your Leadership Statement]], and say what changed. Keep it somewhere
you will find it.

The analysis is written in class, and it is marked against the four rows
on [[The Management Portfolio]] — the same four rows as every other
entry.
