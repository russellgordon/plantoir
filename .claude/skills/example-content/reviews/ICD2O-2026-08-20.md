## CONFIRMED

### 1. `The Algorithm Field Guide` asks for an original design and never gives it a class period

This is the most serious finding in the file and the clearest instance of a
defect class fixed everywhere except its first occurrence.

The task requires two halves. `shared/Tasks/The Algorithm Field Guide.md`:

> Your field guide documents **three real algorithms from your own life** …
>
> Then you turn designer: choose a task you actually do that nobody has
> ever written down, and write **one new algorithm** for it — precise
> enough that a classmate can execute it [[Human Robot]]-style without
> asking you a single question.

The second half carries two of the seven criteria rows —

> | The moves are visible | Your original was broken into pieces before it was written as steps |
> | A literal-proof design | Your original survived the most literal reading |

— and is the page's own stated evidence for one of its three curriculum codes.
From the same file's hidden block:

> (A1.2, also on this page, is evidenced by the designed original and
> the illustrated guide — plan, design, share — not by either of these
> questions.)

`shared/Curriculum/A1.2.md` reads "use a variety of tools and processes to
plan, **design**, and share algorithms and computational artifacts", and
`A1.1.md` reads "apply computational thinking concepts and practices when
planning and **designing** computational artifacts".

**No agenda in Unit 1 ever mentions it.** All four working periods are about
the three DOCUMENTED algorithms:

- `Unit 1, Day 5` — "day 2 — working period: three algorithms found in this
  building, written down as steps"
- `Unit 1, Day 8` — "day 3 — working period with the time left: start with
  what our conference on day 2 said was a habit rather than an algorithm"
- `Unit 1, Day 9` — "day 4 — workday in pairs, driver and navigator: assemble
  and illustrate what you have documented so far"
- `Unit 1, Day 10` — "day 5 — working period: each entry tested on somebody
  who has never done that task"

I searched every Unit 1 class page, whitespace flattened, for `original`,
`design`, `invent`: the word "original" appears **zero** times in Unit 1, and
"design" appears exactly once — in a HOMEWORK checkbox on `Unit 1, Day 13`:

> - [ ] Bring your field guide ready to present tomorrow — a classmate
>       will run your designed algorithm exactly as written.

So the original is designed at home, unsupervised, and first meets a reader on
`Unit 1, Day 14` — "2. Present: field guides, with a classmate as the very
literal robot" — which is the presentation and the end of the task. The task's
own method says the opposite should happen:

> 4. Field test in class: a classmate executes your design as a
>    [[Human Robot]] … Every place they stall or improvise is a revision
>    waiting to happen — **revise**.
> 5. Assemble and illustrate the guide, and note what the field test
>    changed. **Most of that happens in the working periods, where I can
>    see the drafts as well as the finished thing** — [[How Marks Work]]
>    explains why what I watch counts.

A student who follows the schedule exactly has their design run literally for
the first time on the day it is presented, with no period afterwards in which
to revise — so the row "A literal-proof design | Your original survived the
most literal reading" is one they can only pass by having tested it at home
off their own bat. That is addendum #24's test failing (a row a student fails
by obeying the schedule) and addendum #21's (a half-written leg: the input
side of the task is scheduled and the output side is not).

It is also the Growing Success problem the payload elsewhere is careful about.
`Launch Day` bounds its one out-of-class element explicitly ("The only part
done outside class is the first one — finding your user and talking to them.
Everything that carries a mark is built here."). Every other task names each
of its components on a working period: `The Device Recommendation` has a
three-row "The working periods" table that maps to `Unit 1, Day 11/12/15`
item for item; `The Quiz Machine`'s six working periods each name a different
component; `The Remix Project` gives its individually-marked write-up two named
slots (`Unit 3, Day 10` last ten minutes, `Unit 3, Day 13` last twenty). The
Field Guide is the one task that does not, and it is the first one students
meet.

**Smallest correct fix**: write the leg. `Unit 1, Day 8`'s Field Guide item is
already only "with the time left"; the honest change is to name the original
design on `Unit 1, Day 9` or `Day 10` ("design your original, then break it
into pieces before writing it as steps") and to run the literal test of the
original in the `Unit 1, Day 13` warm-up, which is already `[[Human Robot]]`
and would need only "— today, on your own design" appended, leaving `Day 14`
as the public run. Do not fix it by dropping A1.1/A1.2: the task genuinely
asks for the thing, so the schedule is what is wrong.

### 10. Five of the six triangulation blocks ask a PREDICTION as their second question

Sentence-level checks are clean (see the header) — this is addendum #14,

tasks meets the same move six times. Two could be swapped for a disagreement

## The single most important finding

**Finding 1.** ICD2O's first task asks every student to design an original
algorithm, publishes two criteria rows about it, rests two curriculum codes on
it, and never once names it on an agenda. All four of its working periods are
spent on the other half of the deliverable. A teacher running this course
discovers at marking time that the piece carrying A1.1 and A1.2 was built at
home, by whoever had somewhere quiet to build it — which is the exact outcome
the working-period rule exists to prevent, on the very first task, where the
course teaches students what its habits are.
