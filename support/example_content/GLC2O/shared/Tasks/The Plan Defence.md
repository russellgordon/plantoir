**The final evaluation. Worth 30% of the course.** Two and a half hours
of scheduled time in the last week, most of it spent listening to other
people. Your own defence is about twelve minutes, and a class does not
fit into one block — so the defences run across the scheduled time and
the booked sessions in the same week until everyone has been heard. Your
slot is arranged in advance, I am at every one of them, and you know
which slot is yours before the week starts.

You present [[The Pathway Plan]] and walk through
[[Your Career Portfolio]], to an adult who is not your teacher.

## Who you present to

A guidance counsellor, an administrator, a teacher from another
department, a co-op employer, a community partner, or a family member
you have arranged in advance. Your teacher will confirm who is
available; if you want to bring someone, ask early rather than late.

The reason it is not your teacher is that your teacher already knows
your reasoning. Explaining a plan to someone with no context is a
harder and much more useful test, and it is the situation you will
actually be in when you apply for something.

Whoever it is, they are your audience and your questioner, not your
examiner. I am in the room throughout, and the mark is mine to
determine — from what you say and what you show, the same as every
other piece of work in this course.

## The twelve minutes

- **Four minutes — the plan.** Where you are going, why that, and what
  the next three steps are. Not read aloud.
- **Three minutes — the evidence.** Two or three things from your
  portfolio, chosen because they support what you just claimed. Say what
  each one demonstrates.
- **Two minutes — the money.** What the first year costs and how it gets
  paid for, including where it is tight.
- **Three minutes — questions.** Theirs, not scripted.

## What is being assessed

- **Communication.** Clear, audible, and pitched at someone who has
  never heard any of this. No jargon from this course unless you explain
  it.
- **Selection.** You chose the right evidence for the claim, rather than
  showing everything you have.
- **Reasoning.** The plan follows from what you know about yourself and
  from what you found out, and you can say which is which.
- **Reflection.** You can describe how your goals changed over the ten
  weeks, what changed them, and what you now know about how you set
  goals and make decisions.
- **Composure under a real question.** Not having an answer is fine.
  "I don't know, and here is how I would find out" is a good answer.

## Preparing for it

The last two periods are for this: the first of them is where the
portfolio gets closed and ordered, and the second is rehearsals in
threes, with the listener asking one hard question each time. Rehearse
the opening sentence and the answer to "why that and not something
else", because those two carry most of the twelve minutes.

> [!tip] Practise being asked something you did not plan for
> Every rehearsal, have your partner ask a question you have not
> prepared. The point is not to have an answer ready — it is to get used
> to thinking out loud in front of someone, which is what an interview
> is and what most of this is preparation for.

Triangulation — the evidence you will not have unless you go and get it.

This one is the opposite of the rest of the course. The conversation
evidence arrives on its own here — the last three minutes ARE a
conversation, and you are marking it. What the defence cannot give you
is anything about how the argument was assembled, because by the time it
starts it is assembled. So the observation has to be taken beforehand,
and the two classes before the defence are where it is.

OBSERVE — Unit 3, Day 11, while the portfolio is closed and ordered
  Watch for: what comes OUT. A portfolio ordered for a defence is an
  argument, and the decision is in what gets left on the desk beside it.
  In the defence itself you get the student's ACCOUNT of that choosing —
  three minutes of it, and you are in the room for all of it. What Day 11
  adds is whether the account matches what actually happened, which is
  the difference between marking Selection on evidence and marking it on
  a good story told well.
  Going well: pieces are set aside, and the student can say which claim
  each surviving piece is there to support.
  Stuck: everything goes in, in the order it was made. That is a
  chronology, not a case, and it is the version that runs out of time at
  minute three.
  Record: two columns beside the names — chose, or kept everything.

TALK — Unit 3, Day 12, during the rehearsals in threes, while the other
two are running the rehearsal
  Ask: "Who outside this room have you said this plan out loud to, and
  what did they say back?"
  Then: "Which of the ways of deciding from Unit 1 did you actually use
  on this plan, and where did it stop helping you?"
  C1.3 names the people this communication is FOR — mentors, program
  administrators, employers, community organisations — and the defence
  tests one arranged listener on one afternoon. The first question finds

  Record: one line each on the class list. You will want it when you
  mark the reflection.

The product evidence is the plan and the portfolio, in the room on the
