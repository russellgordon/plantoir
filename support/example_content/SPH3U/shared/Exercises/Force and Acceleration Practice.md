**1.** A $1200\ \text{kg}$ car experiences a net forward force of
$3600\ \text{N}$. Find its acceleration.

> [!success]- Answer 1
> $a = F_{net}/m = 3600/1200 = 3.0\ \text{m/s}^2$ forward.

**2.** A $5.0\ \text{kg}$ box is pulled with $30\ \text{N}$ across a
floor where $\mu_k = 0.35$. Find the acceleration.

> [!success]- Answer 2
> $F_N = mg = 49\ \text{N}$, so $F_f = 0.35 \times 49 = 17\ \text{N}$.
> $F_{net} = 30 - 17 = 13\ \text{N}$, so
> $a = 13/5.0 = 2.6\ \text{m/s}^2$.

**3.** A $2.0\ \text{kg}$ mass hangs from a string attached to a
$3.0\ \text{kg}$ cart on a frictionless table, over a pulley. Find the
acceleration of the system and the tension.

> [!success]- Answer 3
> System: $a = (2.0 \times 9.8)/(5.0) = 3.9\ \text{m/s}^2$.
> Then for the cart alone, $F_T = ma = 3.0 \times 3.9 = 12\ \text{N}$.
> Note the tension is NOT the weight of the hanging mass — see
> [[Systems and Pulleys]].

**4.** A crate sits on a ramp at $20^\circ$ without sliding. What is the
minimum coefficient of static friction?

> [!success]- Answer 4
> $\mu_s \ge \tan 20^\circ = 0.36$.

**5.** A high-speed electric transit train incorporates low-friction magnetic bearings and aerodynamic body shaping to reduce passive resistance. A $40\ 000\ \text{kg}$ train experiences a static friction force from carbon bearings of only $80\ \text{N}$ at standstill compared to $2400\ \text{N}$ in traditional steel journal bearings.
- (a) If the traction motors exert a forward force of $32\ 000\ \text{N}$, compute the initial acceleration with each bearing type.
- (b) Explain how Newton's first and second laws guide engineers in reducing friction in athletic footwear versus industrial rotational equipment.

> [!success]- Answer 5
> (a) Low-friction magnetic/carbon bearings: $F_{net} = 32\ 000 - 80 = 31\ 920\ \text{N}$, $a = 31\ 920 / 40\ 000 = 0.798\ \text{m/s}^2$.  
> Traditional steel bearings: $F_{net} = 32\ 000 - 2400 = 29\ 600\ \text{N}$, $a = 29\ 600 / 40\ 000 = 0.740\ \text{m/s}^2$. The low-friction system delivers higher acceleration per kilowatt of motor input.  
> (b) Athletic footwear maximizes $\mu_s$ so athletes can apply large horizontal action forces to the ground without slipping (Newton's third law yielding large reaction drive force, $\vec{F}_{net} = m\vec{a}$); industrial bearings minimize $\mu_k$ so minimal applied force is wasted overcoming friction (preserving inertia and eliminating thermal degradation).

**6.** A $1400\ \text{kg}$ passenger vehicle travelling at $18\ \text{m/s}$ ($65\ \text{km/h}$) collides with a rigid barrier.
- (a) A rigid older vehicle with no crumple zone stops in $0.060\ \text{s}$. Compute the average net force acting on the vehicle.
- (b) A modern composite vehicle with an engineered crumple zone deforms progressively over $0.30\ \text{s}$. Compute the new average net force.
- (c) Evaluate the societal and environmental trade-offs of lightweight plastic/composite vehicle components.

> [!success]- Answer 6
> (a) $\vec{a} = (0 - 18) / 0.060 = -300\ \text{m/s}^2\ (\approx 30.6\ g)$. $\vec{F}_{net} = m\vec{a} = 1400 \times (-300) = -4.2 \times 10^5\ \text{N}$ (fatal impact force).  
> (b) $\vec{a} = (0 - 18) / 0.30 = -60\ \text{m/s}^2\ (\approx 6.1\ g)$. $\vec{F}_{net} = 1400 \times (-60) = -8.4 \times 10^4\ \text{N}$ (a five-fold reduction in impact force, survivable with seatbelts and airbags).  
> (c) Lightweight polymer and composite structures reduce vehicle curb mass, significantly cutting lifetime fuel consumption and tailpipe carbon emissions. Societal trade-offs include higher repair and replacement costs, specialized recycling requirements, and the need for precision crumple engineering to ensure passenger survival.

**7.** A $65\ \text{kg}$ student stands on a bathroom scale calibrated in newtons inside an elevator. Draw a free-body diagram and calculate the scale reading (apparent weight $F_N$) when the elevator:
- (a) accelerates upward at $1.8\ \text{m/s}^2$;
- (b) moves downward at a constant velocity of $4.0\ \text{m/s}$;
- (c) accelerates downward at $2.4\ \text{m/s}^2$.

> [!success]- Answer 7
> FBD: Gravity $\vec{F}_g = mg = (65)(9.8) = 637\ \text{N}$ downward; Normal force $\vec{F}_N$ (scale reading) upward. Equation of motion: $F_{net} = F_N - F_g = ma \implies F_N = m(g + a)$.  
> (a) Upward acceleration ($a = +1.8\ \text{m/s}^2$): $F_N = 65(9.8 + 1.8) = 754\ \text{N}$ (feels heavier).  
> (b) Constant velocity ($a = 0$): $F_N = 65(9.8 + 0) = 637\ \text{N}$ (equals true weight).  
> (c) Downward acceleration ($a = -2.4\ \text{m/s}^2$): $F_N = 65(9.8 - 2.4) = 481\ \text{N}$ (feels lighter).
