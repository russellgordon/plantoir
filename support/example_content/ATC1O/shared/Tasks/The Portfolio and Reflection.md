**The final evaluation. Submitted complete, with a reflective piece
written in the examination period.** Individual.

A dance course cannot be evaluated by an essay alone. Your portfolio is
the record of a year of work, and the reflection is what you make of it.

## The portfolio

Everything, organized, from the first week to now — see
[[The Dance Portfolio]] for how it is kept:

- Journal entries, including the improvisation notes from Unit 1. The
  ones that carry a mark are the ones written in the studio at a
  milestone, where the class page asks for one
- Video of yourself at three points in the year
- Choreographic notes, including the versions you abandoned
- Written work: [[The Safe Dancer]], [[The Dance Review]], programme
  notes
- Feedback you received, and what you did about it

## The reflection (written in the examination period, about 900 words)

1. **What changed.** Compare your first video with your final one.
   Be specific: what does your body do now that it did not?
2. **What dance gave you that is not dance.** Skills, qualities, and
   habits you have taken into other settings — and the evidence. Name one
   kind of work where they would be worth having, and say why.
3. **What you learned about yourself** through the work. This is the
   expectation about personal growth, and it deserves an honest answer
   rather than a polite one.
4. **What you would work on next**, and how you would go about it.

## What is marked

| Criterion | What it looks like |
| --- | --- |
| Completeness | The record is genuinely there, and dated |
| Evidence | Claims about growth point at specific artefacts |
| Insight | You notice something that is not obvious |
| Range | The account covers what did not work as well as what did |
| Communication | Clear writing, dance terminology used correctly |

> [!tip] The first video is the whole trick
> Nobody enjoys watching their first one. It is also the only way to see
> a year of change in two minutes, and every strong reflection in this
> course leans on it — see [[Video of Yourself]].

Triangulation — the evidence you will not have unless you go and get it.

OBSERVE — Unit 4, Day 17, the final filming and the first side-by-side
comparison
  Watch for: what a student points at when the two clips run beside each
  other. A specific difference — where the weight arrives now, what the
  spine does — or a verdict about themselves. B3.3 asks them to USE the
  portfolio to analyse strengths and areas needing improvement; the
  reflection is written weeks later with time to compose, and this is the
  only moment you see the analysis happen at speed and unaided. It
  corroborates the Evidence row, which the written piece can rehearse.
  Going well: a finger on the screen and a sentence with a body part in
  it.
  Stuck: "I'm better now"; or the first clip left unopened.
  Record: two columns on the class list — "named a change", "named a
  feeling".

TALK — Unit 4, Day 22, at the portfolio conferences already on that
agenda, one dancer at a time
  Ask: "If I handed your portfolio to a year from now's Grade 9 class,
  which three files would you pull out, and what would you tell them each
  one is for?"
  Then: "Has anybody outside this room said something about a change in
  you this year — who, and what were they seeing?"
  A strong first answer includes something that did not work among the
  three, and says what it teaches. A strong second names an actual person
  and occasion rather than a general claim about confidence. That is B3.1
  and B3.3 heard, and it is worth having before the examination period,
  because by then they have had a week to make the written answer sound
  good.
  Record: three file names and a person's name, on the conference sheet.

The product evidence is the portfolio handed in on Day 26 and the
reflection written in the examination period.
