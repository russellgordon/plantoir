$F_{net} = ma$ claims two proportionalities at once: acceleration goes up
with force, and down with mass. You will test them one at a time, because
testing two things at once tests neither.

## Safety

The hanging mass falls at the end of every run. Keep feet clear and put
something soft on the floor. Do not exceed 500 g on the hanger.

## Method

A cart on a track, a string over a pulley, a hanger of slotted masses.

**Part 1 — Vary the force.** Keep the TOTAL mass of the system constant by
moving masses from the cart to the hanger, one at a time. Record the
acceleration for each. This is the step every group gets wrong: adding
mass to the hanger without taking it off the cart changes both variables.

**Part 2 — Vary the mass.** Keep the hanging mass fixed and add mass to
the cart.

## What to record

A table for each part: hanging mass, total system mass, and acceleration
(three trials each). Then two graphs — acceleration against force, and
acceleration against $1/m$.

## Analysis

1. Are your graphs straight? What does the slope of each one mean?
2. Your measured acceleration will be lower than $F/m$ predicts. Name the
   two most likely reasons, and design a check for one of them.
3. Predict the acceleration for a combination you did not test, then test
   it. Report the prediction and the result, in that order.
