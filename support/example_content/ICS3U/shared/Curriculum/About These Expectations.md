Three notes about this course's status and this reproduction:

- **This curriculum is current.** A newer Grade 10 computer studies
  course exists — *Digital Technology and Innovations in the Changing
  World* (ICD2O, 2023) — but it replaced only the Grade 10 material.
  The Ministry states that, apart from that course, the 2008 computer
  studies curriculum remains in effect and all Grades 11 and 12
  computer studies courses continue to be based on it.
- **The document prints no teacher prompts.** Some Ontario curricula
  print "Teacher prompt:" text beneath an expectation; this one does
  not. What you see on each page is the whole of what the Ministry
  prints.
- **Its typography is reproduced, not tidied.** The document sets its
  `(e.g., …)` examples in italics but its `(i.e., …)` clarifications in
  roman, and that distinction is kept here. So is the source's American
  spelling of "Modeling" in `B2.4`, and its bracketed expansions such
  as "UML [Unified Modeling Language]".

Each page keeps its code, and every concept, exercise, and task in this
course links back to the codes it addresses — including every stage of
a longer project, where [[B4. The Software Development Life Cycle|B4]]
is met. The codes are also tags, so every page tagged `#B4` can be
gathered in one place.

## Why the codes matter more than they look

A code is a shared address. When a student asks "why are we doing
this?", the answer is a link. When a department writes a course
outline, or a teacher takes over a class partway through, the codes
are what make one person's planning legible to another.
