Every task page carries the criteria it will be marked against,
published before you start. Most people read them once, at launch, and
then hand in a review that misses two rows they could have fixed in
twenty minutes. This page is the twenty minutes.

Managers call this a self-review, and every organization you study this
term runs one before anything goes to a client or a board. We do it
together the first time, on somebody else's work, so that judging is not
a new skill on the day it is your own memo being judged.

## The routine

1. Put the task's criteria table beside your work. Not in your head —
   beside it, on the screen or on paper.
2. Go row by row. For each one write **yes**, **not yet**, or
   **partly**, and one sentence of evidence: the page, the paragraph,
   the slide, the thing a reader would actually see. "Yes" with no
   evidence is a wish.
3. Find your **weakest row**. Not the easiest to fix — the weakest.
4. Write one sentence saying what you will change, and when.
5. Change it, in the period you are in or the next one. This is why the
   self-review is never scheduled on a hand-in day: a judgement you have
   no time to act on is just a worry.

Steps 2 and 3 are the whole exercise. Naming your weakest row honestly
is harder than any of the fixing, because it means writing down the
thing you were hoping nobody would open.

> [!example]- One row, judged honestly
> From [[The Change Plan]]: *"Control — measures that would actually
> reveal a problem in time."*
>
> **Partly.** I have three measures and a date for each. Two of them
> are numbers the office already collects. The third is "team morale",
> which nobody counts and I would not know how to read. Weakest row.
> **Change:** replace it with something countable that would move for
> the same reason — voluntary overtime taken, or how many of the
> Friday handovers happen on time — before Thursday.
>
> That entry is worth more than "looks fine" written against all four
> rows, and it took less time.

## What it is not

It is not a mark, and it is not a request for one. Your judgement of
your own work is not part of your mark and neither is a teammate's —
[[How Marks Work]] says why. That is deliberate, and it cuts both ways:
nothing you write here can cost you anything, which is exactly what
makes it worth writing honestly.

It is also not a list of everything that is wrong. Four rows judged and
one fixed beats twelve rows apologised for.

## Where this fits

You will run this on a deck in Unit 1, on a profile in Unit 2, on a
strategic review in Unit 3, and on the written half of
[[The Management Review]] in Unit 4. By the fourth time it should take
about ten minutes, and by then it is the thing you do before anything
leaves your hands — which is the actual point, and the reason
[[The Control Process]] and this page are the same idea at two
different scales.

> [!tip] Read your weakest row out loud
> Say it to the person beside you. It catches the rows where you were
> being generous to yourself, and you will usually hear it before they
> say anything.
