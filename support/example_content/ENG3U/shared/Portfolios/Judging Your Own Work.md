Every task page publishes its criteria before you start. Most people read
them once, on launch day, and then hand in an essay that misses two rows
they could have fixed in twenty minutes. This page is the twenty minutes.

We do it together the first time, on a paragraph that belongs to nobody
in this room, so that the move is not new to you when the work being
judged is your own.

## The routine

1. Put the task page's criteria beside your draft — on paper, not in your
   head.
2. Take the rows one at a time. For each write **yes**, **not yet**, or
   **partly**, and one sentence of evidence: the paragraph, the sentence,
   the thing a reader would point at. "Yes" with no evidence is a wish.
3. Find your weakest row. Not the easiest to fix — the weakest.
4. Write the one change you are going to make, and say when.
5. Change it, in what is left of this period or in the next one. That is
   why this is never scheduled on the day something is due: a judgement
   you have no time to act on is only a worry.

Steps 2 and 3 are the exercise. Naming your weakest row honestly is
harder than any of the fixing, because it means writing down the thing
you were hoping nobody would notice.

> [!example]- One row, judged honestly
> From [[The Close Reading Essay]]: *"Thinking — the claim is arguable,
> the evidence supports it, the objection is real."*
>
> **Partly.** The claim is arguable: somebody could hold the opposite
> and quote for it. The objection is not real — I wrote "some readers
> might disagree" and then answered a version nobody would defend.
> Weakest row. **Change:** use Devon's reading from Tuesday, which I
> could not answer at the time, and answer it in the fourth paragraph.
> Next period, before the cut to 700.
>
> That entry is worth more than "looks good" written against eight rows,
> and it took less time.

Keep the entry in your [[Reading Journal]], dated, next to the draft it
judges. At the end of the course they are what makes
[[Showing Growth]] possible: you will have the record of what you thought
was weak in October and what you can now do about it.

## Where this fits

What you decide here is not part of your mark, and neither is what your
partner decides in a workshop — see [[How Marks Work]]. That is on
purpose, and it works in your favour: an honest "not yet" written here
cannot cost you anything, which is the only condition under which anybody
writes one.

What it changes is the work. A self-check that leads to one real revision
is the cheapest improvement available in this course, and by the last
unit it is a habit you run without being asked, which is the actual
point.

One last move worth making: read your judgement aloud to the person next
to you. You will hear the rows where you were being generous before they
say anything.
