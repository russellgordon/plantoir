A bound notebook, used in ink, in order, without pages torn out. That
combination is what makes it a **record** rather than a collection of notes.

## Every entry starts with

```
Date · Investigation title · Group members
```

## And then a safety section, before anything else

Two lines, ruled off at the top of the entry, written **before** you touch
equipment:

```
Hazards: what could go wrong here
Response: what I would do about it, and where the thing I need is
```

Read the investigation page and the pictograms on the containers to fill it
in. Guessing shows.

This is the one part of the notebook I mark — it is the seventh row of
[[Lab Reports]], and it is marked because knowing a hazard before you meet it
is written into this course as something you have to be able to do.
## While working

- Write readings **as they happen**, not from memory afterwards
- Mistake? One line through it, initial, keep going. Never scribble out
- Record the unexpected — the smell, the odd bubble, the reading that made no
  sense
- Sketch the setup. A rough sketch beats a paragraph

> [!important] Why ink and why bound
> Because a lab notebook is evidence. In research and in industry, notebooks
> settle patent disputes and safety investigations. The habits are the same at
> every level: contemporaneous, unaltered, honest.

## What a good one looks like

Not neatness. **Completeness and honesty** — including the trial that failed and
what you did next.

Apart from the safety section, the notebook is not marked. What it does is
make the seven marked [[Lab Reports]] possible: everything you write while the
work is happening is what you have to write from afterwards, and the criterion
about naming your limits honestly is more or less impossible without it.
