Send a wave down a string fixed at both ends and it reflects. The
returning wave meets the outgoing one, and at certain frequencies the two
lock into a pattern that appears not to travel at all: a **standing wave**.

## Nodes and antinodes

**Nodes** never move. **Antinodes** move the most. They alternate, half a
wavelength apart, and the pattern only forms when the length of the string
or tube fits a whole number of half-wavelengths.

That fitting condition is why instruments have specific notes rather than
a continuous smear: only certain wavelengths fit, so only certain
frequencies sound.

## Resonance

Every object has natural frequencies at which it will vibrate readily.
Drive it at one of them — even gently — and the amplitude grows. Push a
swing at its own rhythm and it goes high; push at the wrong rhythm and it
fights you.

Resonance is why a wine glass can be shattered by a voice, why a bridge
needs damping, and why a tuning fork over a tube of the right length gets
suddenly, startlingly loud. That last one is a measurement instrument —
see [[Measuring the Speed of Sound]].
