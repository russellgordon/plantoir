[[The Independent Study]] runs from early November to early January. This
page is how to choose the text you will live with.

## The rules

- **Novel-length, or a full collection**, at Grade 11 level or above.
- **Not a text we are studying together**, and not one you have already
  written about.
- **Available to you** — the library, this room, or your own shelf.
- **Approved by me** at checkpoint one, which is a two-minute
  conversation, not an application.

## A starting list

| Text | Writer | Why it rewards ten weeks |
| --- | --- | --- |
| *Fifteen Dogs* | André Alexis | A philosophical premise executed exactly; won the Giller Prize in 2015 |
| *The Break* | Katherena Vermette (Métis) | Multiple narrators, one night, and a community's response |
| *Ru* | Kim Thúy, translated by Sheila Fischman | Fragmentary form; a refugee childhood rendered in pieces |
| *The Book of Negroes* | Lawrence Hill | Historical fiction with an argument about who records history |
| *Indian Horse* | Richard Wagamese (Ojibwe) | Residential school, hockey, and a narrator explaining himself |
| *Five Little Indians* | Michelle Good (Cree) | Five survivors, five afterwards |
| *The Handmaid's Tale* | Margaret Atwood | If you are taking ENG4U, choose something else — you will meet it there |
| *Never Let Me Go* | Kazuo Ishiguro | Withholding as a formal principle |
| *Do Not Say We Have Nothing* | Madeleine Thien | Music, history, and structure at their most ambitious |

## Pitching something else

Bring the book, tell me what you think it is doing, and say why it will
still interest you in January, when you are writing about it. Most
pitches are accepted. The ones that are not are usually too short to
sustain 2,000 words, or already written about by half the internet in a
form you would have to fight.

> [!tip] The choice that goes wrong
> Choosing the shortest book on the list. The essay is the same length
> either way, and a thin book gives you less to write about — which
> makes the work harder, not easier.
