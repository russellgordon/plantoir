Measuring the speed of sound is one of the few genuinely fundamental
constants a high school class can get within a few per cent of, using
nothing but a stopwatch and a large space.

## Three ways to do it

1. **Distance and delay.** Clap in front of a wall a known distance away
   and time the echo. Speed is $2d/\Delta t$ — remember the round trip.
2. **Two observers.** One with a starting pistol far away, one timing the
   gap between the flash and the bang.
3. **Resonance in a tube.** Cleverest and most precise: a tuning fork of
   known frequency over a tube of adjustable length, finding the length
   where the sound suddenly swells. See
   [[Resonance and Standing Waves]].

The third avoids reaction time entirely, which is why it wins — and why
[[Measuring the Speed of Sound]] uses it after starting with the first.

## The temperature dependence is real

At $0^\circ\text{C}$ sound travels at 331 m/s; on a hot day it passes
350 m/s. A wind instrument that was in tune in a cold room goes sharp as
the room warms, and orchestras retune for exactly this reason.
