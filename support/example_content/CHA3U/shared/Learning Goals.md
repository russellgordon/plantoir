What you should be able to do by the end of CHA3U, in plain words first
and in the Ministry's words underneath.

## By the end of the course you should be able to

- **Read a primary source** for what it is evidence of, and notice who
  made it and why.
- **Build an argument** from evidence and answer the strongest objection
  to it.
- **Use the four concepts of historical thinking** to reason, not to
  label.
- **Be precise about uncertainty** — what is established, what is
  contested, and who says so.
- **Explain how the United States changed** across four periods, and who
  did the changing.
- **Notice how a story has been told**, and why it was told that way.

## The self-check that matters

Given a document you have never seen: who made it, for whom, why — and
what is it evidence of? Then, given a claim about the American past:
what evidence would settle it, and does it exist? That is the course.
