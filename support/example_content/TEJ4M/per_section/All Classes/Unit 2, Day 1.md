## Agenda

1. Warm-up: [[Predict the Circuit]] — an indicator and a motor on the
   same output pin. One of those is a lie
2. Bench: [[Switch a Load with a Transistor]]. Work out the base
   current the pin would have to supply *before* you choose a resistor,
   then find out what your pin can actually deliver. Unmarked, and what
   the room gets wrong here decides how much of Day 2 goes on drive
   current
3. Compare benches. Everybody chose a different base resistor and most
   of them work. Name it: [[Transistors as Switches]] — saturation,
   forced gain, and why "it lights up" is not proof of anything
4. The same load again, switched by a logic-level MOSFET. Measure the
   drop across each switch and turn it into watts of waste heat
5. Log both numbers side by side in your [[Tech Journal]], and the
   bench record in at tools-away

## Things to do before our next class

- [ ] Recalculate your base resistor for a load twice the size, and
      say what fails first.
- [ ] Journal: the switch you would choose for a device left running
      all summer, with the reason in numbers.
