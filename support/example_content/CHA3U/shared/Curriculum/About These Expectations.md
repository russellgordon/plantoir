Each page here holds one expectation, word for word. The codes are the
Ministry's own, and they are also tags, so every page tagged `#C2` can be
gathered in one place.

## The shape is deliberate

Strands B, C, D, and E each carry the same three overall expectations —
social, economic, and political context; communities, conflict, and
cooperation; identity, citizenship, and heritage — applied to a
different stretch of the American past. You will get better at those
three questions precisely because the period keeps changing underneath
them.

Strand A is different again: its stem reads **"Throughout this course,
students will:"** while the others say *by the end of this course*.
Historical inquiry, source criticism, and the concepts of historical
thinking are not a unit — they are what every unit is made of.

## What "FOCUS ON" means

Every overall expectation in strands B to E ends with a bracketed label
— `(FOCUS ON: Continuity and Change; Historical Perspective)` and so on.
Those name the concepts of historical thinking the Ministry expects that
expectation to lean on: **historical significance**, **cause and
consequence**, **continuity and change**, and **historical perspective**.
The labels appear here because they are part of the printed expectation.
Their definitions are not — those live in the document's History
introduction, printed pages 294–295, with the historical inquiry process
on 296–297.

## What this folder leaves out

The document opens the course with an at-a-glance **Overview** spread —
big ideas and framing questions, strand by strand — and prints **Sample
questions** under many specific expectations. Neither is reproduced here;
this folder is the expectations themselves. Both repay an hour in the PDF
when you are planning.

## Why the codes matter more than they look

A code is a shared address. When a student asks "why are we studying
this?", the answer is a link. When a department writes a course outline,
or a teacher takes over a class partway through, the codes are what make one
person's planning legible to another.
