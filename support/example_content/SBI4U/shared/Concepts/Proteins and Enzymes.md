Proteins do nearly everything: catalysis, structure, transport,
signalling, defence. Their monomers are twenty amino acids, and their
function follows entirely from their shape.

## Four levels of structure

1. **Primary:** the sequence, held by peptide bonds.
2. **Secondary:** helices and sheets, held by hydrogen bonds along the
   backbone.
3. **Tertiary:** the whole three-dimensional fold, held by interactions
   between side chains — including the hydrophobic clustering from
   [[Water and Life]].
4. **Quaternary:** several folded chains together, as in haemoglobin.

Change one amino acid in the primary sequence and everything above it can
change. Sickle-cell disease is a single substitution.

## Enzymes

Catalysts that lower activation energy. The substrate binds at the active
site, which is shaped for it — and shaped by the fold, which is why
temperature and pH matter so much: they change the shape.

Denaturation is that shape being lost. It is usually irreversible, which
is why a cooked egg does not uncook.

## What affects rate

Temperature, pH, substrate concentration, enzyme concentration, and
inhibitors — competitive (blocking the site) or non-competitive (changing
the shape elsewhere). [[Enzyme Activity]] measures three of them.
