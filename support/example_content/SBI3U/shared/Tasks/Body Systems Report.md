> [!abstract] At a glance
> **Format:** four pages with original diagrams. **Individually.**
> **Due:** end of Unit 4. **Assessed on:** Knowledge, Application,
> Communication.

Take one disorder and explain it as a failure in a system you have
studied — structure, function, and consequence.

## Choose one

Asthma · atherosclerosis · coeliac disease · anaemia · emphysema ·
type 1 diabetes · cystic fibrosis (respiratory or digestive angle).

## What to produce

1. **Normal structure and function**, with a diagram you drew.
2. **What goes wrong**, at the level of tissue or cell.
3. **The consequences**, traced through at least TWO systems. Nothing in
   the body fails alone, and this is where the marks are.
4. **Treatment or management**, and what it targets: the cause, or the
   symptom?
5. **One thing that is still unknown** about it, with a source.
6. **Why this condition gets the attention it does.** Research follows
   money and need, and both are worth naming: how common is it, what
   does it cost people and the health system, which societal changes —
   diet, air quality, sedentary work, an ageing population — made it
   more or less pressing, and what technology exists now that did not a
   generation ago. One paragraph, sourced, and honest about who benefits
   from the answer.

7. **Your own measurements of a working system.** Use the readings you
   took in class on Unit 4, Day 6 — pulse and blood pressure at rest,
   after two minutes of stepping, and through recovery, taken with the
   classroom stethoscope, sphygmomanometer and stopwatch. That period is
   where this part of the report is done, so bring your data book to it.
   Report the readings in a table, including any you retook and why, use
   the proper terminology (systolic, diastolic, recovery rate), and
   explain how the condition you studied would change what you measured.
   **Consent was asked before any reading was taken, and anyone could
   decline without a reason** — say in your report that it was, because
   a measurement of a person is not the same kind of number as a
   measurement of a leaf.
8. **Who works on this.** One career that deals with this condition — in
   the clinic, the laboratory, or public health — what the work actually
   involves, and how somebody gets there.
   [[Working in the Life Sciences]] is the starting point.

Write it to the standard on [[Writing About Biology]], and choose your
sources by the method in [[Finding and Judging Sources]].

## Success criteria

Published here on the day you choose your condition, and used again with
[[Judging Your Own Work]] on the working period before you hand it in.

| Quality | What it looks like in your report |
| --- | --- |
| Normal before broken | The healthy structure and function is explained first, in your own diagram, so the failure has something to be a failure of |
| The fault has a level | You say where it goes wrong — tissue or cell — and not merely that it does |
| Traced through two systems | The consequence in the second system follows from the first by a stated mechanism, not by being listed beside it |
| Treatment aimed somewhere | You say whether it targets the cause or the symptom, and how you know |
| Terminology exact | Systolic, diastolic, tidal volume, recovery rate — used correctly and not swapped |
| Your own readings, honestly reported | The table shows what you measured, including the retakes, with consent recorded |
| Sourced and attributed | Every number has a citation; the career and the societal-needs paragraph name where they came from |
| Drawn, not lifted | Every diagram is yours, labelled to the standard on [[Biological Drawing]] |

## Not permitted

An article copied and rearranged. Every diagram is yours, and every number
has a citation.

Triangulation — the evidence you will not have unless you go and get it.

OBSERVE — Unit 4, Day 6, the pulse and blood pressure practical
  Step 7 of the report is done in this period, which makes it the one
  half-hour where you can check a claim the report makes about itself.
  The criteria ask for the retakes to appear in the table and for the
  consent to be stated; here you find out whether the table is a record
  or a reconstruction.
  Watch for: a reading that looked wrong being taken again and written
  down TWICE, rather than crossed out and replaced by a tidier number.
  That is the half of this that is E2.3 — the equipment used to monitor a
  functional response, with the readings it actually produced. Watch also
  for how consent is asked: as a question, with the answer waited for.
  Consent is not in any expectation — it is in Working with Living
  Things, and it matters more than the mark does.
  Going well: two numbers written down when one of them was doubted;
  "is this still all right?" partway through.
  Stuck: a single tidy figure per stage; the cuff on before the sentence
  is finished.
  Record: two ticks per student on the class list — retake, consent. Then
  read the retake column against the tables that come in on Day 13.

TALK — Unit 4, Day 9, at the conference the agenda already schedules
  They will arrive with the two systems ready, because the agenda told
  them that is what the conference is for. Take it as given and go past
  it.
  Ask: "Take the reading you got on Day 6. What would your condition have
  done to that number, and through which step?"
  Then: "Which of your two systems shows the failure first, and how would
  somebody actually notice?"
  A strong answer travels in one direction through a mechanism instead of
  listing two systems side by side — that is E3.4, a disorder described
  rather than named, and the report can reach the same page by citation.
  Where the terms come out exactly right in saying it you are also
  hearing E2.1, but E2.1 is a terminology expectation and the written
  report evidences it better than a conversation can; do not lean on this
  for it.
  Record: one line each, plus a mark for anyone who could not connect
  their own measurement to their own condition.

The product evidence is the report, handed in on Day 13.
