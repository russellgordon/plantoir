Every task on this site says what earns the marks before you start. That
list is not there so you can check it once at the end. It is there so you
can be your own first reader, while there is still time to do something
about what you find.

This page is the method. We work it together the first time, on somebody
else's draft, before you are ever asked to do it alone.

## Three passes, in this order

**The criteria pass.** Open the task page beside your draft. Take one
criterion at a time and find the sentence in your own work that meets it.
Not the paragraph — the sentence. If you cannot put a finger on it, that
criterion is not met yet, however much the paragraph is about the right
subject.

**The evidence pass.** Go through every number, every figure and every
map layer and ask where it came from. Anything without an agency, a year
and a retrieval date is not evidence yet, it is a recollection.

**The reader pass.** Read it as the person it is written for — the
council, the coordinator, the landowner, the examiner. Stop at the first
place they would ask a question, and write the question in the margin.
That margin note is worth more than a tidy conclusion.

## What a criterion is really asking

| The criterion says | So look for |
| --- | --- |
| Explained, not named | A sentence saying what supplies the energy and what sets the rate |
| Observation and inference kept apart | A left column that would still be true if your inference were wrong |
| Sources are the responsible agencies | A publisher you could name out loud without checking |
| Uncertainty carried through | A range, or a stated limit, inside the conclusion itself |
| Format and register match the named audience | Something you would have written differently for a different reader |

## Then write yourself two lines

One thing to keep, and why it works. One thing to fix, with the time it
will take — "twenty minutes finding the year for the erosion figure" is
a plan, and "improve my sources" is not. Bring both lines to the
checkpoint, because the conference is much faster when you already know
where the weak part is.

> [!success]- What this looks like on a real draft (click to expand)
> A student's process study said the bank was retreating quickly. The
> criteria pass found no sentence giving a rate. The evidence pass found
> the roots-exposed measurement in the notebook, with a date, and the
> first-week photograph beside it. The two lines came out as: *keep the
> paired photograph, it is the only dated comparison in here; fix the
> word "quickly" — twenty minutes plotting the two root heights and
> saying how many centimetres over how many months, with the error.*
>
> The rewritten sentence was worth more than the whole page it sat in,
> and it took less time than rereading the page had.

## This never becomes a mark

Your judgement of your own work, and your classmates' judgement of it,
are not part of anybody's grade — Ontario is explicit that evaluation is
the teacher's job alone. That is exactly what makes this useful. You can
be hard on your own draft here without it costing you anything, which is
not true of any other reading it will get.

The place to record what you find is your own work, and the notes go
where you will see them again: [[Your Field Notebook]] for fieldwork,
the reflection beside each sheet in [[A Map of Your Own]], and the margin
of the draft itself for everything else.
