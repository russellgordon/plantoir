Two thousand words is not a long essay with more paragraphs. It is a
different structural problem: a reader has to be able to hold your
argument in their head for eight pages, which means the shape has to be
visible from the inside.

## What changes at length

| At 900 words | At 2,000 |
| --- | --- |
| One claim, three moves | One claim, five or six moves, in a defensible order |
| Evidence from a few chapters | Evidence from across the whole text |
| One acknowledgement of the counter-reading | A counter-reading section that genuinely risks something |
| Sources optional | Sources used to extend and complicate, per [[Research Writing]] |

## Signposting without announcing

Do not write "in this essay I will". Instead, end each section with a
sentence that creates the need for the next:

> If the delay is epistemological rather than temperamental, then the
> Mousetrap is not a postponement but a method — and the play's
> vocabulary of proof should intensify around it. It does.

That paragraph ending has told the reader what is coming and why it
follows.

## Ordering the moves

Three defensible orders: by strength, by the text's chronology, or by
technique. Write the order you chose at the top of your outline with one
sentence defending it. An essay whose order is accidental reads as
accidental, and at this length a reader notices by page three.

## The counter-reading section

Not a sentence. A full move: state the strongest opposing reading in its
own terms, show the evidence for it, and then say precisely what it
cannot account for. If your counter-reading is easy to defeat, you have
chosen the wrong one.

## Before you submit

- [ ] Read the whole thing aloud, in one sitting.
- [ ] Cover the body and reread the thesis: does the essay prove it?
- [ ] Check every quotation against the text, and every citation against
      [[Citing Sources]].
- [ ] Read only your topic sentences, in order. Do they tell the
      argument?
