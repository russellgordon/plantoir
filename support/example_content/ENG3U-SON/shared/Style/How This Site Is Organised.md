The sidebar on the left is organized around what kind of thing a page is,
not around which unit it belongs to — a course runs by date, but the ideas
in it get reused across units, so filing them by date would bury them the
second time you needed one.

## The folders

- **Concepts** — the literary and rhetorical ideas this course teaches:
  what a motif is, what iambic pentameter sounds like, how a rhetorical
  appeal works. Written to be understood on their own, after you've already
  met the idea in class.
- **Texts** — one landing page per story, novel, or play we study: why
  we're reading it, what to watch for, where it comes from.
- **Discussions** — the seminars and circles that anchor a class period,
  with the real question behind each one.
- **Writing Workshops** — the craft mini-lessons: how to build a thesis, how
  to cite a quotation, how to outline.
- **Media Studies** — the media-literacy activities: deconstructing an ad,
  comparing headlines, building a storyboard.
- **Portfolios** — where reflection and self-assessment live, including
  [[Judging Your Own Work]].
- **Tasks** — the five pieces of work that carry a mark. Each one states its
  success criteria before you start.

## How to actually find something

**All Classes**, in the sidebar under your section, is the spine of the
whole course — every day, in order, newest at the top. If you know roughly
when something happened, start there; every class page links out to
whatever that day actually used.

If you don't know when, [[Key Links]] holds the handful of pages worth
knowing the location of by heart, and the search bar (top of the sidebar)
searches everything at once.

## What you won't find browsing the sidebar

Some folders — Setup, Style, Tutorials, and this course's curriculum
expectations — are reached through [[Key Links]] rather than sitting in the
visible sidebar. They are not hidden because they are unimportant; they are
hidden because you reach them a different way, and a sidebar that showed
everything would show too much.
