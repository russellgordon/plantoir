This course can answer the oldest question in math class without
dodging. The world reports itself in logarithms — earthquake
magnitudes, decibels, pH — and reading those scales wrong means
hearing "magnitude 7" as *a bit worse* than magnitude 5 when it
means *a hundred times* the shaking, which is exactly the literacy
[[The Logarithm]] hands you. Anything with a dose, a decay, or a
dilution runs on the curves of [[Safe Concentration]]. Anything that
vibrates — sound, signals, the alternating current in the wall — is
trigonometry, and [[The Sound of Two Waves]] lets you hear an
identity with your own ears. And the question "how fast is it
changing *right now*?" is asked daily by every science, every
market, and every epidemiologist alive; this course walks you to its
edge, and calculus answers it.

The second answer is about transfer: you may never prove a compound
angle formula after this course, but you will spend a lifetime modelling
messy situations, checking claims before trusting them, and arguing
from constraints. That training happens here whether the algebra
resurfaces or not.

Questions worth arguing about:

1. "When will I use this?" is sometimes an honest question and
   sometimes code for something else — frustration, being lost, a
   fair complaint. How could a classmate tell which they just heard?
2. A headline calls one earthquake "two points bigger" than another.
   Who benefits when the public cannot read the logarithm inside
   that sentence — and who pays?
3. List three quantities from your own week that are reported on a
   log scale without ever saying so. Whose job is it to notice?
4. Nobody asks a basketball player when they will use wind sprints.
   What is different about mathematics — or is anything?
5. For much of this course the honest answer is "you need it for
   calculus". Is *next year* a real answer or a deferral? Defend
   your position — and decide what it would take for a deferral to
   be legitimate.

The test of today's talk is what you do at [[The Signature Function]]
— where the mathematics is not dressed up as useful, but pointed at
something you already care about.
