## What it is for

Every other piece of evidence in this course was gathered somewhere
comfortable: at a bench with a partner beside you, over four periods, with
the site open. This one is three hours, alone, cold, across all five
strands at once — the closest thing here to a first-year university
assessment, met while somebody who knows you is still the person marking
it.

## What is on it

| Part | Roughly | What it asks you to do |
| --- | --- | --- |
| A. Biochemistry | 20% | Macromolecules and their structure–function links; enzymes, inhibition, and kinetics; membranes and transport |
| B. Metabolism | 20% | Respiration and photosynthesis stage by stage, each stage placed in the right organelle and against the right membrane; ATP yield; chemiosmosis; what changes when a step is blocked |
| C. Molecular genetics | 25% | Replication and repair; transcription and translation, including a sequence handed to you to transcribe, translate and then mutate; mutation consequences; gene regulation; the biotechnology toolkit |
| D. Homeostasis | 20% | Loops located precisely — receptor, control centre, effector; nervous and endocrine control; the kidney |
| E. Populations | 15% | Growth models and their assumptions; sampling; interactions between species; sustainability |

Woven through: **the skills strand**. One question hands you data you
have not seen — a rate curve, a gel, a growth series — and asks you to
process it, state a conclusion, and say what its uncertainty allows.

## What to expect, precisely

- **Mechanism, not vocabulary.** "The enzyme denatures" earns little;
  naming what heat does to the bonds holding the active site's shape
  earns full marks.
- **Diagrams are marked** on the conventions in [[Biological Drawing]],
  and pathway diagrams must be yours.
- **Calculations show their route** and carry units and significant
  figures, per [[Uncertainty and Error]].
- **One question is set in an unfamiliar context** — an organism or a
  disorder you have not studied. It is testing whether the principles
  transfer, which is the whole point of a Grade 12 course.

## How to prepare

1. **Redo problems, do not reread them.** Five from each set, cold:
   [[Biochemistry Practice]], [[Metabolism Practice]],
   [[Molecular Genetics Practice]], [[Homeostasis Practice]],
   [[Population Practice]].
2. **Redraw the pathways from memory** — respiration, photosynthesis,
   transcription and translation, one feedback loop. If you cannot draw
   it, you cannot yet explain it.
3. **Re-read your own investigations.** Your [[Biology Journal]] entries
   about what went wrong at the bench are the most efficient revision
   there is.
4. **Practise transfer.** Take a principle and apply it to an organism
   the course never mentioned. That is what the hardest question does.
5. **Bring questions to the review classes.** This page is what is on
   the examination.

> [!tip] In the three hours
> Molecular genetics is the largest part; do it while you are fresh.
> If an explanation stalls, write the mechanism you DO know — partial
> mechanisms earn partial marks and a blank space earns none.

## How this is assessed

Against the same expectations as everything else. Per [[How Marks Work]],
this examination is part of the final 30% of the course mark alongside
[[Population Study]], so that neither one afternoon nor one investigation
decides your grade alone.

There is no rubric handed out on the day, so here is what earns marks,
published now and gone through again in the format walk-through on
Unit 5, Day 17:

| Quality | What it looks like on the paper |
| --- | --- |
| Mechanism before name | The steps that produce the effect, in order, in the right words — "it denatures" names the outcome and stops there |
| Located, not just named | A stage put in its organelle, a gradient put across the right membrane, a break put at a component |
| Diagrams that carry information | Drawn to the conventions in [[Biological Drawing]], labelled, and yours — a pathway diagram earns nothing for being neat and everything for being right |
| Calculations with a visible route | The working shown once in full, units carried, and the answer to a number of figures the data supports, per [[Uncertainty and Error]] |
| Data read rather than described | The unfamiliar rate curve, gel, sequence or growth series processed, a conclusion stated, and the uncertainty allowed to limit the claim |
| Transfer to something new | The unfamiliar organism or disorder answered from principle, with the principle named, rather than from a memory of a similar case |
| Partial credit taken | The part of the mechanism you do know, written down — the marks for it are there whether or not the rest arrives |

Triangulation — the evidence you will not have unless you go and get it.

An examination is the one piece of work in the course that arrives with no
observation and no conversation attached, and there is no honest way to
add either to the three hours themselves. The four review periods before
it are where both are available, and they are the last chance in the
course to find out something a paper will never tell you. Anything you
gather here is also worth having for the report card comment, which is
written after the paper is marked.

OBSERVE — Unit 5, Days 15, 16 and 17, while the review questions are worked
  Watch for: the first thirty seconds of a question a student cannot
  start. A diagram or a list of what is given appearing before any prose;
  the question read twice; or notes opened immediately. On the paper a
  blank space looks identical whether it was abandoned in ten seconds or
  fought over for ten minutes, and a wrong answer that began with a
  correctly drawn system is a different piece of evidence from one that
  did not.
  Going well: a representation on the page before a sentence is — the
  axes, the loop, the pathway skeleton.
  Stuck: rereading the notes for the shape of an answer rather than
  reaching for the system.
  Record: one column per review period beside your class list, three
  marks — drew first, wrote first, opened notes. Three passes across the
  week and you have a pattern rather than a moment. That is A1.12 watched:
  choosing a representation is a decision, and the finished paper shows
  you the diagram without showing you whether it came first.

TALK — Unit 5, Day 18, at the individual conferences already on that agenda
  Do not spend these on reassurance. You will not reach everybody in one
  period, so start with the students whose paper you would find hardest to
  predict — the quiet ones, and the ones whose write-ups and whose talk
  have been telling you different things all semester.
  Ask: "Somebody has been on a drip for two days and their blood sodium
  has fallen. Talk me through what is happening to the water in their
  cells, and then what their kidneys are doing about it."
  Then: "What measurement would tell you whether you were right?"
  A strong answer moves outward from the cell to the nephron and to ADH
  in one continuous account, rather than reciting the excretory system in
  the order the notes have it. That is E3.1 heard in a case nobody
  prepared for: a written answer to a question set for the whole room
  cannot show you whether the route was found or recalled.
  Record: one line each on the conference list, and take it straight to
  the report card comment.

The product evidence is the paper itself. That one arrives on its own.
