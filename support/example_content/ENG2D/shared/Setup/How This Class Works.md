Grade 10 academic English is a reading and writing course with a
performance in the middle of it. Most periods you will be reading closely,
drafting, or arguing about a text with other people.

## The shape of a period

| Part | Roughly | What happens |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Opening | 10 min | A passage, a question, or a piece of writing on the board |
| The work | 50 min | Close reading, drafting, seminar, workshop — the class page says which |
| Landing | 10 min | What we established, written down before it evaporates |

The class page for each day says what the period is. Read it before you
arrive and you will never be lost.

## Four habits this course assumes

1. **Annotate as you read.** The method is on [[Close Reading]], and
   "I read it" without marks on the page is not a defence at this level.
2. **Come with a claim.** Seminars and discussions start where you
   already are; arriving empty means listening to other people learn.
3. **Draft early, revise late.** Every major piece is drafted in class,
   read by somebody, and rewritten. That sequence is not optional.
4. **Read the criteria before you start.** Every task page publishes them
   in advance because they are meant to guide the work, not to explain
   the mark afterwards.

## The literacy test

Everybody in Grade 10 writes the provincial literacy test this year. This
course prepares for it deliberately rather than pretending it does not
exist — see [[The Literacy Test]] — and the preparation is genuinely the
same skill as everything else: read carefully, write clearly, prove your
point.

## Phones

In the tray at the door unless the class page says otherwise. Some
periods the phone is the tool — researching what a text left out,
screenshotting a feed to take apart — and the page for that day says so.
