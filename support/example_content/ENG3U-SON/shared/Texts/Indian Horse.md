Richard Wagamese was Ojibway, born in 1955 into the Wabaseemoong
Independent Nations community in northwestern Ontario. Taken from his
family as a child, he grew up mostly in foster care and discovered
literature as a teenager in a public library. He went on to become the
first Indigenous writer to win a National Newspaper Award for column
writing (1991) and the author of fourteen books before his death in 2017.
*Indian Horse* (2012), his best-known novel, won the Burt Award for First
Nations, Métis and Inuit Literature and was adapted into a feature film in
2017.

## Why we're reading it

*Indian Horse* is the whole of Unit 2 — the first time this course spends
a sustained stretch of weeks with a single book rather than a single
class. It follows Saul Indian Horse from early childhood through a
residential school and into a hockey career, narrated by Saul himself,
looking back. Before you start it, read [[Before We Begin - Residential Schools and Indian Horse|Before We Begin: Residential Schools and Indian Horse]] —
the class builds its own agreement for how to read and discuss this book
honestly, and that happens the day before this novel opens.

## What to watch for on a first read

- [ ] Who Saul is closest to in the early chapters, and what happens to
      each of those relationships as the novel goes on
- [ ] Where hockey shows up before it becomes the novel's central subject
      — it starts earlier than you'd expect
- [ ] What Saul tells you directly, as narrator, and what he leaves for
      you to notice yourself
- [ ] Moments where the novel moves between present and memory without
      announcing the jump

These aren't questions to answer right away — they're worth copying into
your reading journal and returning to as the novel unfolds. The concept
[[Symbolism and Motif]] returns partway through the novel, once ice and
hockey have had room to develop as more than setting.
