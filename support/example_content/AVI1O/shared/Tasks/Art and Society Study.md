**Presented in Unit 3, on Day 13 or Day 14.** Pairs. A short
presentation with images.

Art does jobs. This task asks what job a particular kind of art was
doing, and for whom.

## What you do

Choose **two works** made in different societies or different centuries
that share a function — both religious, both commemorative, both
protest, both advertising, both portraits of the powerful.

Then answer:

1. **What function** does each serve, and for whom?
2. **What does each tell you** about the society that produced it — what
   it valued, feared, or wanted remembered?
3. **What do the two share**, and where do they differ, in what they
   were for rather than what they look like?
4. **Who paid**, if you can find out, and what difference that made.

## What is yours, and what is the pair's

You choose the two works together and you present together. Two things
are yours alone, and your mark comes from those:

- **One of the two works is yours** to research and to present — its
  function, its society, and who paid for it. Say at the start which of
  you took which.
- **Your own half-page on question 3**, written in the research and
  drafting period rather than at home, saying what the pair found and
  where the two of you read the comparison differently. This one is
  written here so that I can see whose thinking it is — which matters
  more on a pair task than anywhere else in the course.

Nothing your partner produces moves your mark, in either direction.

## What is marked

Every row below is judged on your half: the work you researched, the
part of the presentation you delivered, and your own half-page.

| Criterion | What it looks like |
| --- | --- |
| Function | Named precisely, with evidence from the work |
| Context | Accurate about the society, without sweeping claims |
| Comparison | A real comparison, not two descriptions side by side |
| Sources | Where you got it, cited — see [[Writing About Art]] |
| Care | Cultural material described respectfully and accurately |

> [!warning] Do not explain a culture you have not read about
> If a work comes from a community you are not part of, use sources from
> that community where you can, and say plainly what you could not find
> out. "This is what I could establish, and this is what I could not" is
> a strong answer here.

[[What Art Is For]] and [[Art and Its Society]] are the ideas behind
this.
