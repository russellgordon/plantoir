These questions follow [[Design Principles]] and the choices you fought
over in [[Build the Front Page]]. Layout argues in sizes and spaces.

## Questions

1. Three stories are ready: (a) the volleyball team's first regional
   title in nine years, (b) council cancels the winter semi-formal,
   (c) a new mural in the front hall. Which leads the page, and why?
2. Pick the pull quote from the caretaker retirement story: (a) "I
   started here in 1994." (b) "Kids think I fix boilers. Mostly I fix
   bad days." (c) "The party is Friday." Defend it — and place it.
3. One big photo, generous margins, headline floating in clear space.
   A classmate calls the margins "wasted room" — what are they doing?
4. **Explain why** a page where all three headlines are the same
   size is lying to the reader — even if every word on it is true.
5. **Fix this cramped page.** Every story runs edge to edge, three
   typefaces are in play, the caption touches the photo, and nothing
   has room to breathe. Your first three fixes, in order, with reasons.
6. **Challenge.** The lead photo shows a runner mid-stride, facing
   left, placed at the far left edge of the page. What is the
   problem, and what are the two honest fixes?

## Answers

> [!success]- Answer 1
> A judgement call — but (a) has the strongest case: a nine-year first
> carries novelty and human interest, and it comes with a photo. (b) is
> defensible if it touches more readers; (c) waits for a quiet week.

> [!success]- Answer 2
> (b) — it has a voice, it surprises, and it makes you want the story
> around it; (a) is a fact and (c) a notice. Set it mid-story, deep
> enough to pull a scanning reader in rather than repeat the lede.

> [!success]- Answer 3
> Directing attention. White space isolates the photo and headline so
> the eye lands there first — the page's way of saying *this matters*.
> Fill every margin and everything whispers at the same volume.

> [!success]- Answer 4
> Size is the page's honesty about importance. Identical headlines
> claim equal stories — a claim the newsroom itself does not believe,
> or it would not have picked a lead. Refusing to rank misinforms.

> [!success]- Answer 5
> One defensible order: margins first, since without breathing room no
> other fix is visible; one typeface family next, because three fonts
> are three voices interrupting each other; then real gaps between
> stories and around the caption, so the reader can tell things apart.

> [!success]- Answer 6
> She is looking off the page, and the reader's eye follows her gaze
> right out of it. Move the photo to the right edge so she faces into
> the page, or pick a frame facing the other way — but never flip the
> image: a mirrored photo is a false photo.
