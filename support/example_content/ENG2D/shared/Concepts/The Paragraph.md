One idea, developed and proved. If you cannot say the idea in a phrase,
you have two paragraphs or none.

## The analytical shape

1. **Topic sentence** — the claim, arguable, in your own words.
2. **Context** — one sentence placing the evidence.
3. **Evidence** — quoted, short, cited.
4. **Analysis** — two or three sentences on what the evidence shows.
   This is the part that carries the mark.
5. **Link** — back to the thesis, forward to the next paragraph.

## What weak paragraphs do

| Symptom | What it means | Fix |
| --- | --- | --- |
| The quotation is the longest sentence | Quotation dumping | Quote four words, not four lines |
| The last sentence repeats the first | No development | End with a consequence instead |
| Plot appears in three sentences | Summary | Cut it; the reader has read the text |
| Two claims in one paragraph | Two paragraphs | Split it |

## Cohesion

Sentences hold together through repeated key terms, pronouns with clear
owners, transitions, and — best of all — ending a sentence with the idea
the next one begins from. That last move is what makes professional prose
feel effortless.

## Testing your own

Cover everything but the topic sentences and read them in order. If they
tell the argument on their own, the essay is organised. If they read like
a list of topics, it is not — yet.
