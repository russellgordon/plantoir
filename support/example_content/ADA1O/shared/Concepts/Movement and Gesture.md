Your body is telling the audience things right now — before you speak, and
sometimes despite what you say. Movement work in this course is about making
those signals *chosen* instead of accidental.

## Neutral first

Everything starts from **neutral**: feet under hips, weight even, arms
released at the sides, spine long, face at rest. Neutral is hard — most
people discover they habitually stand with a hip cocked, arms crossed, or
weight on one leg, and each of those habits is already a character. Neutral
matters because it is a blank page. From neutral, *any* choice reads; from a
habit, only choices bigger than the habit read. We begin most physical warm-
ups by finding neutral, and it is where every [[Tableau]] should begin before
the shape is built.

## Economy of gesture

One gesture, committed to fully, says more than ten half-gestures. Beginners
illustrate every word with their hands; the effect is static that blurs the
one movement that mattered. The discipline is subtraction: cut every gesture
you can, and the survivors gain power — the same contrast rule that governs
[[Focus and Emphasis]], because in a still body, one moving hand takes the
whole room's eye. When you feel your hands wandering, return them to neutral
and wait. Stillness is not the absence of acting; it is acting the audience
can read.

## The walk is the character

Before a character says a word, their walk has introduced them. Lead from the
chest and you are confident; lead from the nose, curious; from the knees,
exhausted. Speed, stride length, and how the arms swing all declare
[[Status]] instantly. When building a character for
[[Scene Study from a Story]], find the walk early — decide what part of the
body leads, then let the voice and the posture follow it. It is the fastest
route from *me in a costume* to someone else.

## The silence prompt

The sharpest question we ask all semester: *what changes if we do the scene
without words?* Remove speech and everything the body was mumbling must speak
clearly — the want, the obstacle, the relationship, all carried by movement
alone. This is the whole discipline of [[Mime]], and even one silent run of a
scene will show you exactly which of your physical choices were actually
choices.

> [!example]- Try it: the silent argument (click to expand)
> In pairs, improvise a thirty-second argument about a borrowed, broken
> phone — speaking. Then run it again in silence: same want, same obstacle,
> no words, no mouthing. Ask your watchers which version made the
> relationship clearer. Be ready for the answer, because it is usually the
> silent one.
