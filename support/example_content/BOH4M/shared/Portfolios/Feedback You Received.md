An entry about being on the receiving end, written while the appraisal
unit is fresh.

## What to collect

Three pieces of feedback you have received this year — from a teacher, a
coach, a supervisor, a colleague, a customer. At least one that was
difficult to hear.

For each one, record:

- What was actually said, as close to word for word as you can manage.
- What you did with it: nothing, argued, changed something, changed
  something and then stopped.
- Whether it was specific enough to act on.

## The analysis (about 400 words)

1. Using [[Performance Appraisal]], assess the feedback itself. Was there
   evidence? Was it timely? Did it end with something to do?
2. The harder question: what made the difficult one difficult — the
   content, the delivery, the timing, or the person? Be precise.
3. What would have made you act on the one you ignored?
4. What does this tell you about how you will give feedback when you are
   the manager?

> [!warning] This is about the feedback, not about the person who gave it
> Write about what was said and what it caused. Keep names out of it, the
> same rule we use in [[Bosses We Have Known]].

Most people find they acted on the feedback that came with a specific
example and ignored the feedback that came as a judgement. That finding
is the whole reason appraisal instruments look the way they do — see
[[Appraisal Methods]].

The analysis is written in class, and it is marked against the four rows
on [[The Management Portfolio]] — the same four rows as every other
entry.
