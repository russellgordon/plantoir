The expectations in this folder are reproduced from **The Ontario
Curriculum, Grades 11 and 12: Mathematics** (revised 2007), the Grade 12
university preparation course **MDM4U — Mathematics of Data
Management**, published by the Ontario Ministry of Education.

> [!info] The official sources
> [Mathematics of Data Management, Grade 12, University Preparation (MDM4U) — Ontario Curriculum and Resources](https://www.dcp.edu.gov.on.ca/en/curriculum/secondary-mathematics)

> Reproduced for educational use.

> [!warning] About the codes
> The 2007 document prints the strand letters — its headings read
> "A. Counting and Probability" and so on — and numbers each strand's
> overall expectations `1., 2., …` and its specific expectations
> `1.1, 1.2, …`. What it does **not** print is the combined form:
> codes like `A1` and `D3.2`, used throughout this site, join the
> printed letter to the printed number in the style teachers and
> course outlines commonly use. They are consistent within this site,
> but you will not find the combined codes printed in the Ministry's
> pages.

A few honesty notes about the reproduction:

- The asterisk in strand E's first overall expectation is the
  document's own, pointing at the chapter's single printed footnote,
  reproduced here once: *"This culminating investigation allows
  students to demonstrate their knowledge and skills from this course
  by addressing a single problem on probability and statistics or by
  addressing two smaller problems, one on probability and the other on
  statistics."*
- The sample problem in `D3.2` is printed beside a bar graph — profits
  in billions of dollars against year, with the vertical axis
  deliberately not starting at zero, which is the point of the
  question. A bracketed note marks its place; the Ministry prints no
  numeric values for it, so none are invented here.
- The mathematics is typeset (binomial coefficients, exponents,
  fractions) the way the document prints it, using LaTeX — wording
  untouched. Where the document writes a relationship in words rather
  than symbols — conditional probability is printed as
  "P(A given B)", not with a vertical bar — those words are kept.

Each page keeps its code, and every lesson, exploration, and task in
this course, and every stage of
[[E1. Designing and Carrying Out a Culminating Investigation|the culminating investigation]]
that strand E asks for, links back to the codes it addresses. The codes
are also tags, so every page tagged `#D3` can be gathered in one place.

## Why the codes matter more than they look

A code is a shared address. When a student asks "why are we doing
this?", the answer is a link. When a department writes a course
outline, or a teacher takes over a class partway through, the codes
are what make one person's planning legible to another.
