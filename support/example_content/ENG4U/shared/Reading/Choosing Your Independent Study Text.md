[[The Independent Study]] runs from September to January and ends in an oral
defence. Choose accordingly.

## The rules

- **Novel-length, or a full collection or play**, at a level that will
  sustain 2,000 words and a defence.
- **Not a text we study together**, and not one you wrote about in
  Grade 11.
- **Approved at checkpoint one**, which is a conversation, not an
  application.

## A starting list

| Text | Writer | Why it rewards a semester of reading |
| --- | --- | --- |
| *Fifteen Dogs* | André Alexis | A philosophical premise executed exactly; Giller Prize, 2015 |
| *The Break* | Katherena Vermette (Métis) | Multiple narrators, one night, and a community's response |
| *Beloved* | Toni Morrison | Testimony, haunting, and a structure that withholds |
| *Do Not Say We Have Nothing* | Madeleine Thien | Music, history, and formal ambition |
| *Never Let Me Go* | Kazuo Ishiguro | Withholding as a principle; pairs with the dystopia unit |
| *Wuthering Heights* | Emily Brontë | Frames inside frames, and narrators with interests |
| *Things Fall Apart* | Chinua Achebe | The postcolonial lens, argued by the novel itself |
| *The Sympathizer* | Viet Thanh Nguyen | A confession under duress — read it beside Offred's tapes |
| *Five Little Indians* | Michelle Good (Cree) | Five survivors, five afterwards |
| *Fugitive Pieces* | Anne Michaels | Language, memory, and a prose that argues by rhythm |

## Pitching something else

Bring the book, say what you think it is doing, and say what your
argument might be. Most pitches are accepted. The ones that are not are
usually too thin to sustain the essay, or so heavily written about that
you would spend the term arguing with the internet rather than the text.

> [!warning] The choice that goes wrong
> A book chosen because it is short. The essay is 2,000 words either
> way, and a thin text gives you less to say — which makes the work
> harder, not easier.
