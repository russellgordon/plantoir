**Due: late Unit 3.** Individual. About 700 words.

One dance work, watched properly and written about using the critical
analysis process.

## The work

A live performance if one is available, or a recorded work your teacher
approves. It must be a whole work, not a clip, and you must be able to
watch it twice. Choose a work that is **arguing about something** — a
social or environmental issue, a claim about people. [[Dance That Argues]]
has several, and so does Unit 3.

## The four moves

Follow [[The Critical Analysis Process]] in order, and label the
sections:

1. **Initial reaction** — what you noticed first, before analysing.
2. **Description** — what is actually there: bodies, space, time,
   energy, sound, design. No judgement yet. Name the form or forms the
   work draws on, and say how its movement compares with the forms you
   have practised in this course.
3. **Analysis and interpretation** — how the parts work together, and
   what the work seems to be about. Separate the *content* from the
   *expressive and interpretive* choices, and say what each contributes.
4. **Judgement** — is it effective, and by what standard?

Then one more paragraph: say how the choreography itself carries the
work's argument — not the programme notes, the movement.

## What is marked

- **Description before judgement.** The most common failure is an
  opinion in paragraph one with no evidence under it.
- **Specific evidence** — moments, not impressions.
- **The form placed** — you name the work's vocabulary and set it beside
  the forms you have practised.
- **The distinction** between content and interpretation, used well.
- **The argument** — you say what the work takes on, and how the
  choreography rather than the programme note carries it.
- **Vocabulary** — the terminology of dance, used accurately.

> [!tip] Watch it twice, differently
> First time, watch the whole thing without writing. Second time, watch
> one dancer only, or the space only, or the sound only. Almost every
> strong review in this course came from a second viewing with a narrow
> focus.

[[Content and Interpretation]] is the distinction that carries this, and
[[Dance That Argues]] is where the last paragraph comes from.

Triangulation — the evidence you will not have unless you go and get it.

OBSERVE — Unit 3, Day 13, the seminar, while positions are being tested
against real works
  Watch for: what a student reaches for the moment somebody disagrees with
  them — a piece of choreography, or the subject matter and the programme
  note. The review is written across three periods, with the work watched
  twice at home in between, and revised on Day 13; the seminar is the only
  place you see a student find the moment while somebody is arguing with
  them. C2.2 asks
  how choreographers USE dance to address an issue, which is a question
  about the dancing.
  Going well: "at the point where the three of them stop moving" — a
  moment, offered as evidence, about a work nobody prepared them to
  defend.
  Stuck: the argument stays on what the dance was about and never arrives
  at what the dance did.
  Record: the seating order down a strip of paper, a tick for a moment
  named and a dash for a topic named.

TALK — Unit 3, Day 12, at the checkpoint, when they bring you the
description section
  Ask: "Read me the sentence in your description you are least sure is
  actually true. What would you have to watch again to check it?"
  Then: "What would have to be different in the work for you to change
  your verdict?"
  A strong answer to the first picks a real sentence — usually one that
  has quietly become interpretation while claiming to be description — and
  knows the exact moment to re-watch to test it. That is the critical
  analysis process actually running, B1.1 rather than recited. The second
  is the assessing half of B1.2, the half most reviews leave out: a
  judgement you can state the conditions for is a judgement; one you
  cannot is a preference. A draft shows you the sentences and never which
  of them the writer would defend.
  Record: one line on the copy of the description they hand you.

The product evidence is the review handed in on Day 14.
