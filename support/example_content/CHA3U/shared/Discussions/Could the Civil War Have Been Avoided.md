**The question:** Between 1820 and 1860 the United States found repeated
political settlements for the same dispute, and then did not. Could the war
have been avoided — and, before you answer, avoided at what price, and paid
by whom?

The second half of that question is not a rhetorical addition. Every
proposal for avoiding the war in 1861 involved a guarantee about slavery,
and a seminar that discusses avoidance without naming that guarantee is
discussing something else.

## Prepare

Bring **one settlement and one voice**. For the settlement, take a
compromise or a statute the curriculum names — the Missouri Compromise, the
Compromise of 1850, the events in Kansas, the Compromise of 1877 — and be
able to say precisely what each side gave up.

For the voice, bring someone who lived under the arrangement rather than
negotiating it: a narrative, a petition, a resolution from a Black
convention, a fugitive advertisement read against itself. Record both as
[[Reading a Primary Source]] sets out.

> [!note] What the Emancipation Proclamation did and did not do
> Come knowing the document rather than its reputation. The National
> Archives records that Lincoln issued it on 1 January 1863, that it
> "applied only to states that had seceded from the Union, leaving slavery
> untouched in the loyal border states," that it "expressly exempted parts
> of the Confederacy that had already come under Union control," and that
> "the freedom it promised depended upon Union military victory." Any
> argument about the war's purpose has to survive contact with those four
> facts.

## Three positions

**It was avoidable, and politics failed.** Compromise had worked before;
different leadership, a different election, or a different sequence in
1860–61 could have deferred the crisis again. The evidence is the record of
proposals actually on the table that winter.

**It was not avoidable, because the dispute was not negotiable.** A
compromise over slavery is a settlement about whether human beings may be
owned, and every previous one had held only by postponing that question
while the enslaved population grew. On this account the war was the form the
conflict finally took, not an alternative to it.

**Avoidance was possible and would have been purchased from the enslaved.**
This position accepts the first argument's mechanics and rejects its
accounting: the price of every deferral was paid by people with no part in
the negotiation, and a full answer has to say so out loud.

Open with your position, name the evidence that would move you, and take
the argument into [[What Held the Union Together|What Held the Union Together?]] and
