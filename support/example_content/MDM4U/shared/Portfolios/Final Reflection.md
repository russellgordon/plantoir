The final reflection is the last piece of writing in this course: one
sustained look **across** your whole [[Math Journal]], written in
class on Unit 4, Day 18, and handed in with the journal at the end of
that period. Every other entry looked at a single day. This one reads
the entire record and answers the only question a course can really
be measured by: *what do you leave with that you did not arrive
with?*

## Before you write

Reread your journal front to back in one sitting — expect the
earliest entries to feel written by someone else. As you read, flag:

- one entry that embarrasses you a little — that is usually where the
  growth is buried
- one "what I would try" that you actually tried, and what happened
- one conjecture you made, and what became of it
- the entry where [[The Culminating Investigation]] changed
  direction — every real investigation has one

## The shape

Three sections, roughly a page, built the way [[Showing Growth]]
taught — date it, quote your own words, then show the now:

1. **How my mathematics changed.** The biggest difference between
   what week-one-you and now-you can *do*, proven with two cited
   entries — a counting problem you can now set up without guessing
   whether order matters, a conditional probability you can compute
   and, harder, state in a sentence that means what you intend, a
   dataset you can look at and immediately ask who is missing from.
2. **How my relationship with uncertainty changed.** The other axis,
   and in this course the more interesting one. What do you do now
   when a number arrives without a source? How do you feel about
   saying "I cannot tell from this data" out loud — and is that the
   same as it was at the start of the course? Your first entry is the baseline;
   quote it.
3. **Where the thinking goes next.** This course does not end at an
   edge so much as open onto everywhere. Inference is the front door
   of every empirical field you might study — psychology, medicine,
   economics, ecology, quality control, sport. Probability runs
   insurance, logistics, cryptography, and the recommendation
   systems that shaped your week. And the plain civic version — being
   the person in the room who asks who was sampled — will find you
   whether or not you ever study any of them. Name where each one
   will find you.

> [!tip] Do not write it as a thank-you card
> The temptation is to perform gratitude and round everything up.
> Resist it. The reflections worth reading include the unit that
> never clicked, the conjecture that died, the investigation question
> you would choose differently if you started again. You have a
> semester of evidence that honesty is what makes this writing good.
> Trust it one last time.
