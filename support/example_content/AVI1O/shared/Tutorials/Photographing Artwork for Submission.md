Most work in this course is handed in as a photograph, and a bad
photograph loses marks the work did not lose. A drawing shot at an angle
in your own shadow reads as grey, crooked, and unfinished, whatever it
looks like on the table. Two minutes of setup is the whole difference.

This page is the narrow version: one usable image, for handing in and for
the site. Documenting a whole body of work — lighting, backgrounds,
records kept all semester — is [[Photographing Your Work]], and that is the
page for building [[The Portfolio]].

## The phone

- **Turn the flash off**, and filters with it. Flash puts a white blaze in
  the middle of anything glossy, and a filtered photograph is not your work.
- **Match the frame to the work**, landscape or portrait, and fill it. Do
  not shoot a small drawing from across the room and crop later.
- **Tap the work to set focus and exposure**, then check: if the paper
  looks grey, slide the exposure up until the white is white.

## Light, shadow, and glare

Work near a window with the light coming from the side, and stand so your
own shadow falls outside the frame — leaning over a page is the commonest
fault by far. Overhead fluorescents put a glare band across wet paint and
graphite. For anything under glass or freshly varnished, tilt the piece
rather than the camera, until the reflection slides off.

## Square on

Put flat work on a table and stand directly above the centre of it, phone
parallel to the surface: if the top edge is shorter than the bottom edge
in your photo, you are leaning. For a sculpture, shoot from standing
height about a metre away, against the plainest wall you can find.

## Cropping and naming

Crop to the edge of the work, or to a thin even margin — no table, no
hands, no sketchbook underneath. Then rename the file the same day, in the
same shape every time, naming the medium in the vocabulary you will be
graded on in [[The Media Trials]]:

`lastname-unit2-linocut-relief-print-nov14`

Copy this list into [[The Sketchbook]] — the boxes below are printed on
the page and nothing you do to them is saved anywhere.

- [ ] Flash and filters off
- [ ] No shadow of yours in the frame
- [ ] Square on — edges parallel, corners square
- [ ] White paper actually looks white
- [ ] Cropped to the work, named, and the same day

Take the photograph before you hand the work in, never after: once a piece
has gone into [[The Exhibition]] or home in a bag, the good image is much
harder to get.
