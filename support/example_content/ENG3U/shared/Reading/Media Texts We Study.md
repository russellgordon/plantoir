Four media texts, read with the same care as the print ones.

| Text | Form | What it teaches |
| --- | --- | --- |
| A documentary segment on a Canadian land or water dispute | Documentary | Interview selection, sequence, and the unopposed voice-over |
| An episode of a Canadian narrative podcast | Audio | How sound builds a case, and what an ear cannot check |
| Two long-form articles on the same event, from different outlets | Journalism | Selection and emphasis, without anybody lying |
| A public information campaign | Persuasion | A claim with a budget behind it |

## How we read them

The five questions on [[Media Texts and Their Arguments]], then a
technique inventory, then the question that matters most: what is left
out, and who would have said it?

## Audio is harder than it looks

A podcast gives you no citations, no way to reread, and a voice that has
been edited for warmth. Listen twice: once for the argument, once with a
pen, marking every claim you are asked to accept without evidence. Most
students find the second pass genuinely uncomfortable, which is the
lesson.

## What it feeds

[[The Media Argument]], which asks you to take one apart and then make
one.
