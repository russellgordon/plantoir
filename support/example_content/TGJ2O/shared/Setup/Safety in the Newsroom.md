A newsroom does not look dangerous, which is exactly why the injuries
here are the slow kind: eyes, wrists, backs, hearing. This page is the
agreement we make at the start of the course and refer back to before
every shoot.

## The hazards, and what prevents them

| Hazard | Where it shows up | Prevention |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Ergonomic** — wrists, neck, back | Long editing sessions | Screen at eye level, feet flat, elbows at about 90°; stand and move every 30 minutes |
| **Eye strain** | Editing, colour work, screens in a dark room | The 20-20-20 habit below; room light matched to the screen, never a bright screen in a dark room |
| **Hearing damage** | Headphones during audio work | Moderate levels, and a break every hour — hearing loss does not come back |
| **Electrical** | Studio lights, extension leads, chargers | No daisy-chained power bars; damaged leads go to me, not back in the drawer |
| **Temperature** | Studio lights, outdoor shoots | Lights off between takes; on a shoot, water, sunscreen, and layers |
| **Mechanical** | Tripods, light stands, booms, cases | Sandbag every stand; carry cases with your legs; nobody walks backwards while filming |
| **Trips and falls** | Cables across floors, dark studio | Tape or mat every run; the studio floor is clear before the lights go down |
| **Lifting** | Gear cases and equipment carts | Two people, or a cart. Nothing heavy is carried up stairs alone |
| **Off-site** | Games, events, interviews in the community | Never alone, location and return time filed with me, phone charged |

## Three habits worth having for life

1. **20-20-20.** Every 20 minutes, look at something 20 feet away for
   20 seconds. It costs nothing and it is the single best defence
   against the eye strain that ends editing careers.
2. **Set the level, then leave it.** Monitor audio at a level where you
   can still hear someone speak in the room. Turning it up to hear
   detail is how hearing goes.
3. **Tidy as you go.** A clear bench and a taped cable are not neatness
   for its own sake; they are how nobody trips carrying $2,000 of
   camera.

## A safety audit, which you will run

Twice this term the newsroom audits itself: walk the room with a
clipboard, list every hazard from the table above that you can actually
see, rank them by how likely and how serious, and fix what can be fixed
that period. Write the rest up. The finding you cannot fix yourself is
still worth recording — that is exactly what a workplace health and
safety committee does, and it is where "somebody should do something"
becomes a document with a date on it.

## WHMIS, equipment care, and incident reporting

Safety in media production includes the chemical and electrical standards
mandated across Ontario workplaces:

- **WHMIS and chemical cleaners.** Cleaning optical lenses, camera sensors, and
  display glass requires specific solvents (such as high-purity isopropyl
  alcohol and optical lens solutions). Always verify Workplace Hazardous
  Materials Information System (WHMIS) supplier labels and ensure proper
  ventilation before applying sprays or compressed gas dusters. Never mix
  cleaning chemicals.
- **Electrical and battery inspection.** Check camera battery packs for signs of
  swelling, damage, or overheating before charging. Inspect power cables and
  studio extension cords for fraying or exposed copper prior to every shoot;
  damaged cables must be quarantined immediately rather than patched with tape.
- **Incident reporting and Passport to Safety.** Any near-miss, equipment drop,
  or physical strain must be reported to the teacher-editor immediately.
  Documenting safety incidents ensures repairs happen promptly, mirrors
  standard Ontario health and safety committee reporting, and reinforces the
  work habits tracked in the Ontario Skills Passport.

> [!important] The rule that overrides every deadline
> No story is worth an injury, and no shot is worth going somewhere you
> should not be. If a location, a crowd, or a situation feels wrong,
> you leave and we discuss it afterwards. That decision will always be
> supported here, without exception.
