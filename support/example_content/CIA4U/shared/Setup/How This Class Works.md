CIA4U runs for 86 periods of 75 minutes, plus a three-hour final
evaluation. The curriculum document says only that each course has a
credit value of 1; the 110 hours behind that credit come from Ontario's
secondary schools policy, *Ontario Schools, Kindergarten to Grade 12:
Policy and Program Requirements*. The prerequisite, as printed, is any
university or university/college preparation course in Canadian and world
studies, English, or social sciences and humanities.

## The shape of the term

| Unit | What it is about | What it builds to |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 1 — Scarcity, Choice, and Models | Why choice is unavoidable, and what a model is actually for | [[The Scarcity Analysis]], then [[The Market Model]] |
| 2 — Firms, Markets, and Government | How firms behave under different market structures, and why governments intervene | [[The Firm Study]], then [[The Intervention Argument]] |
| 3 — The Whole Economy | Measuring output, employment and prices, and the two levers that respond | [[The Indicators Report]], then [[The Policy Brief]] |
| 4 — Canada in a Global Economy | Trade, exchange, and the terms Canada actually trades on | [[The Trade Question]], then [[The Economic Issue Report]] |

[[The Economic Issue Report]] is the culminating task and carries the
weight to match. The final evaluation is [[The Data Examination]], three
hours, with tables in front of you rather than in your memory.
[[The Notebook and Portfolio]] runs underneath all four units: the
notebook you keep every week, and the two portfolio pieces written at
either end of the term.

## Economics dates, and this course is built around that

Most of your courses teach material that will still be true in five years.
This one does not. The Bank of Canada's target for the overnight rate was
2.25% when it was held on 15 July 2026, and the next fixed announcement
date is 2 September 2026. The unemployment rate was 6.4% in July 2026,
released on 7 August 2026 in Table 14-10-0287-01, and another one is
already on the calendar. Both of those sentences will be wrong soon, and
both of them will still be checkable, which is the difference.

So what this course teaches is retrieval, not recall. You are not learning
a rate; you are learning which table the rate comes out of, where that
table lives, and when the next release lands.
[[Where Canadian Economic Data Lives]] is where that starts, and you will
be back at it all term.

A paper that quotes a stale rate as though it were current loses marks for
it. A paper that names its table and its release date does not — even when
the figure has moved by the time the paper is read, because a reader can
see exactly what you had and when you had it. That habit is the single
biggest difference between the work that does well here and the work that
does not.
