[[Indian Horse]] keeps naming real things inside a fictional story: real
teams, a real history of Indigenous players being kept out of the
professional game no matter how good they were, a real institutional
history behind St. Jerome's. Wagamese is not asking you to treat these as
background colour. He is asking you to bring what you know about the
real history into the novel, and to notice what the novel does with it.

## Allusion

An **allusion** is a reference to a real person, event, place, or another
text, made without full explanation because the writer expects you to
recognise it and bring its meaning along with you. An allusion works only
if the reader can supply what is being pointed at — which means an
allusion is also a test of what a reader is assumed to already know, and
a gap in that knowledge is a gap in the reading.

## Historical context

**Historical context** is the real circumstances surrounding a text — the
events, institutions, and social conditions of its setting — that shape
what happens in it and what it means. A novel does not have to be
strictly factual to depend on real history: *Indian Horse* is fiction,
but Saul's exclusion from professional hockey only makes sense, and only
lands with its full weight, once you know that real Indigenous players
faced exactly that exclusion, systematically, for decades.

> [!question]- Self-check: why does this matter for reading, not just for history class? (click to expand)
> Because the novel is arguing something through its history, not just
> reporting it. If you read Saul's story as one boy's bad luck, you miss
> the point Wagamese is making — that what happened to Saul was not an
> exception, it was the system working as designed. Historical context is
> what turns a personal story into an argument about a pattern.

## Reading with the history, not around it

When a novel alludes to something real, the responsible move is to find
out what it is actually referring to before deciding what the allusion
means — not to guess, and not to skip past it because it slows the
reading down. That habit will serve you well beyond this text.

Once you have finished the novel, it is worth revisiting your notes from
[[Before We Begin - Residential Schools and Indian Horse|Before We Begin: Residential Schools and Indian Horse]]
with this idea in mind.
