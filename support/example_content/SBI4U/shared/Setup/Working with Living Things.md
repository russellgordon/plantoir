This course keeps living organisms and studies preserved ones. Both carry
obligations, and we state them out loud rather than assuming them.

## The principle

An organism in this room is not equipment. It is here because it teaches
something that a diagram cannot, and it is owed the minimum of harm
consistent with that.

## What that means in practice

- **Plants and cultures** are kept properly: watered, lit, at temperature,
  and not abandoned over a long weekend.
- **Invertebrates** used in behaviour investigations are returned to
  suitable conditions afterwards.
- **Preserved specimens** are handled respectfully. There is no joking
  with a dissection specimen, and no photographs of one.
- **Nothing is collected from the wild** for this course without checking
  what is protected and what is not.

## Opting out

If a dissection is a genuine problem for you — ethical, religious, or
personal — tell me privately before the class and an alternative will be
ready. You do not have to justify it, and it does not affect your mark.

> [!note] Why we still do it
> A heart in your hands is not the same as a heart on a screen: the
> thickness of the left ventricle wall is something you understand in
> about two seconds and never forget. That is the trade this page is
> weighing, and it is worth being honest that it is a trade.
