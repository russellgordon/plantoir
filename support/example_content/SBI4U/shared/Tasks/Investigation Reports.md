> [!abstract] At a glance
> **Format:** five write-ups, one per unit, each finished and handed in
> inside the period given to it. **Yours alone**, worked up from data
> your group collected together. **Assessed on:** Thinking and
> Investigation, Communication, and Knowledge wherever a row asks for a
> mechanism.

Eight of this course's investigations are written up in class. Five of those
write-ups are marked, and they are deliberately spread one to a unit, so
that by the end of the course there is a line of evidence running through
the whole semester rather than four large points with a gap between each.

## Why the writing happens at the bench

A write-up composed four days later is a write-up about what you can
still remember. What is worth marking — the thinking you did while the
numbers were still unfamiliar and still slightly wrong — has evaporated
by then, and what replaces it is a tidy account of what the result turned
out to be.

So each of the five is written in the period given to it, with the data
in front of you, the apparatus still on the bench and me in the room to
ask. Every one of those periods teaches the interpretation you need
before you start writing: Unit 1, Day 9 does the intercept and the line
of best fit first; Unit 5, Day 6 does the direction each broken
assumption pushes your estimate first. Then you write.

Four of the five follow their investigation immediately.
[[Osmosis in Plant Tissue]] is the exception: it is set up two classes
before it is massed, because the cylinders need time in sucrose before
anything has changed.

## Shared data, separate reports

Bench work is done in pairs or threes and the raw numbers belong to all
of you. **Everything after the raw numbers is yours.** Two people may
submit the same column of masses. Two people who submit the same reading
of what those masses mean have submitted one report between them.

## The six rows

These do not change from write-up to write-up. That is the point of
having them: five reports judged against one unchanging list is the only
arrangement in which you can watch yourself getting better at something
specific. Use them with [[Judging Your Own Work]] in the last ten minutes
of a writing period, while there is still time to act on what you find.

| Quality | What it looks like in your write-up |
| --- | --- |
| A claim the data will carry | One paragraph answering the purpose, resting on your own numbers and on the biology that explains them, with the uncertainty inside the sentence — including "this does not settle it" where that is the honest answer |
| The shape is read, not just the peak | Where the maximum, the intercept or the crossing point falls, and why the curve behaves as it does on each side of it, explained in biology rather than in adjectives |
| What could have moved your number | How much the living material varied, which step could have misled you and in which direction, and one change that would actually reduce it |
| A record somebody else could reuse | Quantity and unit at the head of every column, the raw counts kept beside anything calculated from them, and results carried only as far in figures as the instrument supports |
| Nothing quietly missing | Every reading you took still present, including the ones you set aside, each with a line saying why you set it aside |
| Graphs and drawings that do work | Axes scaled to the data and labelled, a fit drawn rather than imagined, drawings to the conventions rather than to taste |

The third row is the one that moves a report from Grade 11 to Grade 12.
"There may have been experimental error" is a form of words. "The two
soft cylinders would have gained less mass than the others, which pushes
the 0.4 mol/L point up" is a measurement of your own method, and it is
what a marker is looking for.

## The three that are not marked

[[Enzyme Activity]] in Unit 1, [[Fermentation Rate]] in Unit 2 and
[[Reaction Time and the Nervous System]] in Unit 4 are written up in
class in exactly the same way and carry no mark at all. They are where
the six rows are learned, and the first of them is where we run
[[Judging Your Own Work]] together for the first time. Nothing you write
on one of those three can cost you anything, which is precisely what they
are for.

Triangulation — the evidence you will not have unless you go and get it.

These five write-ups are the steadiest product evidence in the course, and
they are also the cheapest place to gather the other two kinds, because
each has a bench day and a writing day beside it. Unit 4 puts the pair
back to back, which is why both prompts below live there: watch on Day 8,
ask on Day 9.

OBSERVE — Unit 4, Day 8, while the step tests are being run
  The method calls for the same exercise, for the same length of time, for
  every subject, and the whole comparison depends on that being true. It
  is also the first thing to go once a group is behind.
  Watch for: whether the step test is timed or estimated, whether the
  cadence holds or drifts, whether the 30-second readings come off a clock
  or arrive when somebody remembers. Recovery times reach the table
  looking equally trustworthy either way.
  Going well: one person doing nothing but timing, counting out loud, and
  the same count called for every subject in the group.
  Stuck: a shorter go for the fourth subject because the period is ending.
  Record: one tick per group — exercise standardised, or not. One circuit
  of the room while they work.
  Note: the third criteria row does invite them to write down that the
  exercise varied, and the better groups will. What you saw is how you tell
  a group that noticed from a group that had nothing to notice.

TALK — Unit 4, Day 9, while the write-ups are being written
  Ask: "Two of you counted the same recovery and disagreed by four beats.
  Which count went into the table, and on what grounds?"
  Then: "Your resting rate is one number. How sure are you it was
  resting?"
  A strong answer treats the baseline as a measurement with a spread of its
  own rather than as the fact the experiment started from, and can say
  which of the three counts it came from and why. That is A1.8 heard: a
  report can carry a competent paragraph about error with none of that
  thinking behind it, and the paragraph is what you would otherwise be
  marking.
  Record: three columns down the class list — baseline questioned, baseline
  assumed, not noticed.

The product arrives by itself: five reports, collected in the period each
one is written.
