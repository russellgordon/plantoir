**The question:** A country founded in a revolt against an empire acquired
territory across a continent and then overseas, projected force widely, and
maintained bases, alliances and interventions far from its borders. Is
"empire" the right word for that, or a polemical one?

This seminar is about definition before it is about judgement. Decide what
would make the term apply, then see whether the record meets your own test.

## Prepare

Bring **one criterion and one case**. Choose your criterion from the table,
or write a better one; choose your case from what the curriculum names for
these strands — the Monroe Doctrine, the Spanish-American War, the building
of the Panama Canal, participation in the two world wars, the Cold War,
Korea, Vietnam, covert operations, or membership in international
organisations. Be able to state what your case shows against your criterion.

| Criterion | The evidence it demands | The obvious counter-case |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Formal rule over territory whose people lack full political rights | Statutes, court decisions, colonial administrations | Territories were often organised into states |
| Control of another state's policy without governing it | Treaties, loans, troop deployments, covert files | Alliances that the other party sought |
| Military presence maintained abroad over decades | Base agreements, force levels, budgets | Presence requested by host governments |
| Economic dominance shaping other states' choices | Trade shares, investment, conditions attached to aid | Trade is not by itself coercion |
| The country's own self-description | Speeches, doctrines, party platforms | Denial is not disproof |

## Three positions

**Yes, and the word is descriptive.** Continental expansion at the expense
of Native nations was itself an imperial acquisition, and the overseas
record continues rather than begins the pattern. On this view the objection
to the word is about self-image, not about evidence.

**No, and the word obscures more than it reveals.** An empire has colonies,
subjects and a governing metropole; a state with alliances, bases and
economic weight is something else, and calling it empire flattens the
difference between rule and influence.

**It depends on the decade and the place, and that is the finding.** The
same country behaved very differently in different regions and periods, and
a defensible answer may be "empire here, in these years, on this criterion"
— which is a conclusion, not a fence to sit on, provided you name the years.

The criteria are the same five as last time — [[The Four Seminars]].

that Canada's own position in this argument is a source too — but bring
