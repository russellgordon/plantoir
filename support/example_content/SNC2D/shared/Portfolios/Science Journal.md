A test mark tells you where you stood on one morning. Your journal
shows what changed across a semester, and you are the only person who
can build it.

## What it is for

This year you design investigations rather than follow them, which
means most of the interesting work happens **before** you touch any
equipment — choosing what to measure, deciding what to hold constant,
committing to a prediction. None of that survives in a data table. It
survives here or nowhere.

## What goes in

| Goes in | Does not go in |
| --- | --- |
| Predictions, written before you measured | Marks and grades |
| Reasoning you later found out was wrong | Everything you handed in |
| A result that surprised you, and why it did | Copies of the concept pages |
| Questions you still cannot answer | A summary of what we covered |
| Connections you noticed between units | Anything you wrote to impress me |

The left column is what a reader learns something from. The right
column already exists somewhere else.

## The habit that makes it work

Write the entry the day of the work. The details you will want at the
end of the course — what you tried first, what you assumed without
noticing, the moment your group changed the plan — are gone within a
week and no amount of effort in the final month brings them back. Ten
minutes on the day beats an hour in the last week of the semester.

## Rhythm

- After every investigation, one entry.
- After every discussion, two or three sentences on what moved.
- Once mid-semester, a [[Showing Growth]] comparison.
- At the end, [[Final Reflection]].

Three of these are written in class, in a period set aside for them, and
those three are the only ones that carry a mark: the Unit 1 milestone
entry, the growth comparison at the end of Unit 2, and the final
reflection. The rest is yours. See [[Journal Checklist]].

> [!important] Predictions get written down before the measurement
> Not after. A prediction you record afterwards is not a prediction, and
> your memory of what you "basically expected" is generous to you in a
> way you will not notice. Writing it first is the only way to find out
> whether your reasoning about mechanism is any good — which is the
> whole point of Grade 10.
