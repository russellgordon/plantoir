Every task page carries a table of success criteria, published before you
start. Most groups read it once, on launch day, and never again — and then
perform a piece that misses two rows they could have fixed in a single
rehearsal. This page is that rehearsal.

We do it together the first time, out loud, on somebody else's images, so
that the move is not new to you the day it is your own work being judged.

## The routine

1. Open the task page and put the criteria table where the whole group can
   see it. On paper is better than on a phone.
2. Go row by row. For each one say **yes**, **not yet**, or **partly** —
   and give one piece of evidence: the moment, the image, the thing an
   audience would actually see. "Yes" with no evidence is a wish.
3. Find your weakest row. Not the easiest to fix — the weakest.
4. Say in one sentence what you will change, and in which rehearsal.
5. Change it, in the time that is left today or in the next working period.
   This is why the check is never scheduled on a performance day: a
   judgement you have no time to act on is just a worry in costume.

Steps 2 and 3 are the whole exercise. Naming your weakest row out loud is
harder than any of the fixing, because it usually means saying the thing
everybody in the group has been hoping nobody would mention.

> [!example]- One row, judged honestly
> From [[Tableau Story Sequence]]: *"Stillness — freezes are truly still:
> eyes, hands, breath under control."*
>
> **Partly.** Images one and four hold. In image three Dev's hand drifts
> every time, because he is holding the lantern above his head and his arm
> gives out at about eight seconds. Weakest row. **Change:** lower the
> lantern to chest height and let Sara be the high point instead — try it
> in the next working period, and if the image loses its meaning, change
> the moment rather than the arm.
>
> That entry is worth more than "looks good" against all six rows, and it
> took less time to say.

## Judging somebody else's work is the practice run

The first time we do this, it is not your piece on the floor. That is
deliberate: it is far easier to see a drifting hand in somebody else's
image than in the one you are standing in. Use [[Watching Like an Artist]]
to describe before you judge, and [[Giving and Receiving Notes]] for how to
say it — the two together are the whole method, turned inward afterwards.

## Where this fits

Your judgement of your own work is not part of your mark, and neither is a
classmate's — see [[How Marks Work]]. That is deliberate, and it cuts both
ways: nothing you say here can cost you anything, which is exactly what
makes it worth saying honestly.

What it changes is the work. A self-check that leads to a fix is the
cheapest improvement available in this course, and by January it is
something your group does without being asked — which is the actual point.
