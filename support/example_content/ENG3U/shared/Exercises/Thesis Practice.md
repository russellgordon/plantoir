Turn each observation into a claim with a mechanism, per
[[Thesis and Argument]].

**1.** Observation: Banquo hears the prophecy and does nothing.

> [!success]- Answer 1
> *Banquo functions as the play's control: by giving a second man the
> same prophecy and a different response, Shakespeare makes it impossible
> to explain Macbeth's actions by the prophecy alone, and forces the
> question of responsibility back onto the marriage.*

**2.** Observation: Lady Macbeth's last scene is in prose.

> [!success]- Answer 2
> *Lady Macbeth loses verse before she loses her life: after four acts of
> control expressed in metre, the prose of the sleepwalking scene marks
> the collapse of the register she used to command her husband, and the
> play stages her breakdown as a change of form rather than of content.*

**3.** Observation: in *Monkey Beach*, the present-tense journey keeps
being interrupted by memory.

> [!success]- Answer 3
> *The novel's braided structure enacts what it argues: Lisamarie cannot
> narrate her brother's disappearance in a straight line, and Robinson
> makes the reader's experience of the search formally identical to
> hers.*

**4.** Observation: King's book is funny.

> [!success]- Answer 4
> *The humour in* The Inconvenient Indian *is a rhetorical strategy
> rather than a tone: by making a reader laugh at the mythology first,
> King secures agreement before he presents the material that would
> otherwise trigger defensiveness.*

**5.** Take three observations from your own annotations and write a
claim for each. Then mark the one you could disprove.

> [!success]- Answer 5
> The one you could disprove is the one worth writing about. A claim that
> cannot fail cannot be argued, and an essay proving something
> unfalsifiable is a summary with quotations in it.
