> This 2022 course replaced the Civics course in *The Ontario

> the 2018 document will cite expectations that mean something else here.
> Check any inherited planning against this folder before reusing it.

## What this course is, and how long it runs

Civics and Citizenship is a **half credit — 55 hours**. In this example
course it runs across the second half of the semester, picking up the period
that Career Studies used from the first week.

The 0.5 value is stated in the 2018 parent document, which the 2022
policy expressly leaves in force for that purpose.

## Two things this course prints that a reader should know

> [!note] Renditions disagree in two places
> The Ministry publishes these expectations in more than one form, and
> two do not match. The **overview table's wording of `C1`** differs from
> the expectation page's, and the **title of `C2`** differs between them.
> These pages use the expectation page's form, because that is the
> authority for an expectation, and record the disagreement here rather
> than choosing silently.

**This course prints no elaborations at all** — no examples, no sample
questions, no teacher prompts. That is unusual and it is not an
extraction failure; it was verified three ways. The expectations stand on
their own wording, which puts more weight on how a course chooses to
teach them.

## Why the codes matter more than they look

A code is a shared address. When a student asks "why are we doing this?",
the answer is a link. When a department writes a course outline, the
codes are what make one person's planning legible to another.
