**1.** A template strand reads 3′-TACGGCTTAACT-5′. Give the mRNA and the
peptide.

> [!success]- Answer 1
> mRNA: 5′-AUGCCGAAUUGA-3′. Codons AUG CCG AAU UGA →
> methionine–proline–asparagine–stop.

**2.** Why must the lagging strand be built in fragments?

> [!success]- Answer 2
> DNA polymerase III only synthesises in the 5′ to 3′ direction, and the two
> template strands are antiparallel. As helicase unwinds the fork, the lagging
> template forces discontinuous synthesis of Okazaki fragments, primed by RNA
> primase, proofread by DNA polymerase I, and sealed by DNA ligase.

**3.** Distinguish a silent, a missense, and a nonsense mutation by
effect.

> [!success]- Answer 3
> Silent: no change in amino acid due to degeneracy of the genetic code.
> Missense: a single amino acid is replaced. Nonsense: creates a premature
> stop codon, truncating the polypeptide. A frameshift (insertion/deletion)
> shifts the entire downstream reading frame.

**4.** In the lac operon, what happens when lactose is present?

> [!success]- Answer 4
> Allolactose binds the lac repressor protein, causing an allosteric
> conformational change that releases it from the operator. RNA polymerase
> can then transcribe the polycistronic structural genes ($lacZ, lacY, lacA$)
> for lactose metabolism.

**5.** Two identical twins differ in a trait. Give a molecular explanation
that does not involve a difference in DNA sequence.

> [!success]- Answer 5
> Epigenetic modifications — such as DNA methylation (which typically silences
> transcription) and histone acetylation (which unpacks chromatin for active
> expression) — altering which genes are expressed in response to environmental
> signals. See [[Regulating Gene Expression]].

**6.** Compare the chemical structure and cellular roles of DNA and RNA.
Why is DNA better suited for long-term genetic storage while RNA serves as
a transient messenger and catalyst?

> [!success]- Answer 6
> DNA uses 2′-deoxyribose, which lacks a 2′-OH group, making its
> phosphodiester backbone chemically resistant to spontaneous alkaline
> hydrolysis. Double-stranded pairing protects genetic sequences. RNA contains
> ribose with a reactive 2′-OH group, enabling rapid cellular turnover and
> dynamic tertiary folding into catalytic ribozymes.

**7.** During a laboratory extraction of DNA from strawberry or cheek tissue,
explain the chemical role of:
a. Dish detergent (lysis buffer).
b. Protease / meat tenderizer.
c. Ice-cold 95% ethanol.

> [!success]- Answer 7
> a. Detergent amphipathic molecules disrupt and emulsify the phospholipid
>    bilayer of plasma and nuclear membranes, releasing cellular contents.
> b. Proteases hydrolyse histone proteins that package and condense DNA.
> c. DNA is polar and soluble in aqueous solution; adding cold ethanol decreases
>    dielectric constant, precipitating insoluble DNA fibers at the interface.

**8.** How did the Hershey–Chase experiment (1952) conclusively prove that
DNA, and not protein, is the genetic material of T2 bacteriophages?

> [!success]- Answer 8
> They labelled phage proteins with radioactive sulfur-35 ($\ce{^{35}S}$) and
> phage DNA with phosphorus-32 ($\ce{^{32}P}$). After infection and agitation
> in a blender, $\ce{^{32}P}$ was recovered inside bacterial cells and
> inherited by progeny phages, while $\ce{^{35}S}$ remained in the supernatant
> ghost coats.

**9.** Describe the three temperature steps of a Polymerase Chain Reaction
(PCR) cycle and state one major agricultural application of genetic
modification.

> [!success]- Answer 9
> 1. Denaturation (94–96 °C): separates double-stranded DNA templates.
> 2. Annealing (50–65 °C): allows sequence-specific DNA primers to bind.
> 3. Extension (72 °C): heat-stable Taq polymerase synthesises new strands.
> Agricultural application: insertion of the *Bacillus thuringiensis* (*Bt*)
> cry gene into crops (e.g., Bt corn) to produce an endotoxin conferring
> natural resistance to European corn borer pests.
