Conventions on [[Writing About Literature]].

**1.** Cut this quotation to the phrase that proves the claim. Claim:
*Hamlet cannot name the murder.*
Quotation: *"If it were done when 'tis done, then 'twere well it were
done quickly. If th' assassination could trammel up the consequence, and
catch with his surcease success…"*

> [!success]- Answer 1
> *"if th' assassination could trammel up the consequence"* — or even
> *"trammel up the consequence"*. The claim is about avoidance and
> abstraction, and those four words carry it.

**2.** Write two sentences of analysis for: *"a little water clears us of
this deed"*.

> [!success]- Answer 2
> *Lady Macbeth measures the murder in the volume of water needed to
> remove its evidence, which converts a moral problem into a housekeeping
> one. The line's confidence is the play's set-up: five acts later the
> same hands are being washed in her sleep, and the water has stopped
> working.*

**3.** This paragraph has three quotations and one sentence of analysis.
What is the fix?

> [!success]- Answer 3
> Cut two quotations. A paragraph proves one move; three pieces of
> evidence for one move means two of them are decoration. Then expand the
> analysis to three or four sentences, which is where the marks are.

**4.** Integrate this quotation into your own sentence: *"nothing in his
life became him like the leaving it"*.

> [!success]- Answer 4
> *Malcolm's report that Cawdor died better than he lived — "nothing in
> his life became him like the leaving it" (1.4.7–8) — arrives eight
> lines before Duncan trusts Macbeth, and the play never comments on the
> juxtaposition.*

**5.** Take a paragraph from your own draft. Count the words of quotation
and the words of analysis.

> [!success]- Answer 5
> If quotation exceeds analysis, the paragraph is not finished. Two to
> one the other way is a reasonable target at this level.

**6.** Revise this timid sentence to establish an authoritative critical
voice: *"I feel that Shakespeare might be trying to show that Hamlet is
perhaps somewhat indecisive."*

> [!success]- Answer 6
> *Shakespeare constructs Hamlet's delay as an epistemological crisis
> rather than a character defect, repeatedly framing action around demands
> for empirical proof.*
> Cut the qualifiers (*I feel that*, *might be*, *perhaps*, *somewhat*).
> Name the writer and state the mechanism directly.

**7.** Correct the spelling and homonym errors in this draft excerpt:
*"The novel's central cannon creates an illusive sense of order, but the
protagonist's soliloquey elicits an illicit desire for rebellion."*

> [!success]- Answer 7
> *"The novel's central canon creates an illusory sense of order, but the
> protagonist's soliloquy elicits an illicit desire for rebellion."*
> Key corrections: *canon* (accepted body of works/rules, not artillery
> *cannon*); *illusory* (deceptive, from *illusion*, not *illusive* or
> *allusion*); *soliloquy* (spelled with *-quy*); and the distinction
> between *elicit* (to draw out) and *illicit* (unlawful).

**8.** Correct the grammar and parallelism defect in this comparative
sentence: *"Atwood's novel exposes state surveillance through restricted
narration and by showing how language is controlled."*

> [!success]- Answer 8
> *Atwood's novel exposes state surveillance through restricted narration
> and controlled language.*
> (Or: *...by restricting narration and by controlling language.*)
> Maintain parallel grammatical structures across coordinating
> conjunctions (*through X and Y* or *by doing X and by doing Y*).
