Canada since 1982 has argued with itself about regions, and argued with
the world about its place in it. The two arguments are connected more
often than they look.

## Regionalism, and what drives it

Regional grievance in Canada is usually about three things at once:
**resources**, **representation**, and **respect** — who owns what is in
the ground, whose votes decide, and whose way of life is treated as
normal.

| Region | The recurring argument |
| --- | --- |
| The West | Resource revenue, federal policy, and Senate representation |
| Quebec | Language, jurisdiction, and the terms of belonging |
| The Atlantic | Fisheries, out-migration, and equalisation |
| The North | Land, self-government, and decisions made elsewhere |
| Urban and rural, everywhere | Services, infrastructure, and who is heard |

The useful move is to separate the grievance from the remedy. Two
regions can share a complaint about federal decision-making and want
opposite things done about it.

## Cooperation, too

Regionalism is not only conflict. Provinces negotiate trade between
themselves, coordinate on health and emergency response, and combine to
press Ottawa. Interprovincial agreement is less newsworthy than
interprovincial fighting, which is exactly why it is under-studied.

## Canada and the world

Since 1982 Canada's international role has been argued over rather than
settled: peacekeeping and its limits, trade agreements and their
domestic effects, humanitarian and military deployments, climate
commitments, and a relationship with the United States that is
simultaneously the country's largest economic fact and its most
persistent political question.

> [!warning] "Canada's role in the world" is a claim, not a description
> Every government describes its foreign policy in flattering terms, and
> so do its critics. Ask what was actually done — deployed, signed,
> funded, refused — and on what dates. See [[Canada and the World]].

[[Canada Since 1982]] carries the social and economic side of this
period, and [[Patriation, the Charter, and After]] the constitutional
one.
