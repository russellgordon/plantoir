## What you are making

A reporter's log of your course: one short entry after every
class, written the same day in the minutes
[[How the Newsroom Runs]] reserves for it. Ten honest minutes beats
thirty performed ones — and entries are **private**, between
reporter and editor: I read them; other students never do. The
journal only works if you can admit what you would not say at the
edit screen — the interview scared you; the praised photo was luck.

## How entries work

Each entry answers three prompts, in whatever order helps:

- **What I covered** — the facts, briefly: the story, the shoot,
  the cut, where your desk got to before the bell.
- **What I learned** — what clicked and what did the clicking: the
  question that opened a source up, the frame that worked and *why*.
- **What I would chase next** — one concrete move for next class.
  Not "get better at photos": something you could actually go do.

Clippings, contact sheets, and cut lists are welcome any time — a
marked-up draft with a sentence about the fix beats a paragraph of
summary. Even a one-line hunch is a perfect journal sentence: "I
think [[News Values|my lead buried the human interest]] because I
wrote the day in order" — dated, testable, and yours.

## Success criteria and collection

The journal comes in each unit, read against these criteria —
[[Journal Checklist]] turns them into a self-check before handing in.
It is **read and written back to, not marked**: nothing in here moves
your percentage, and [[How Marks Work]] explains why that is the
arrangement rather than an oversight.

| Quality | What it looks like |
| --- | --- |
| Regularity | An entry for every class, written close to the day |
| Honesty | Spiked stories and failed shoots recorded, not just wins |
| Precision | Problems located and quoted, not just felt |
| Growth | "Chase next" items reappear as things actually chased |
