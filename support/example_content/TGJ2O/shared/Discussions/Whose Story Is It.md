Spread out a term of our coverage and count. Who appears? Who gets
quoted — speaking in their own words — and who is only spoken about,
described by someone else? A newsroom's pages are a map of who it
thinks matters, and nobody decides to draw it; it accumulates, one
assignment at a time. The championship team appears eleven times;
the club that meets every Thursday in room 114 appears never. The
student profiled as "inspiring" was never asked whether inspiration
was the story they wanted told. There is a difference between
covering a person and using one, and it usually shows in who held
the pen — and the microphone.

Questions worth arguing about:

1. Run the count on our own pages honestly. Where coverage clusters,
   is that [[News Values|news judgement]] working — or a habit
   wearing news judgement's costume?
2. A story about a student with a disability doing an ordinary
   thing, framed as extraordinary. Who is it for? Coverage, or use?
3. A feature on the school's newcomer program in which teachers are
   quoted and no newcomer speaks. What happened between the pitch
   and the page — and who let it happen?
4. "Nobody on staff knows that community, so we'd get it wrong."
   Real constraint or comfortable exit? What does a newsroom that
   takes the constraint seriously actually DO next?
5. Someone asks not to be in a story that genuinely concerns them.
   When does the room's judgement override the person's — and who
   should have to make that case out loud?
6. Does a masthead where everyone is alike produce pages where
   everyone covered is alike? How can mentoring programs, industry
   associations, and [[Careers in Communications|non-traditional pathway supports]]
   broaden who tells stories in media?

Carry it back to practice: every assignment sheet gets one added
line — *who speaks in this story?* If the answer is "nobody it is
about," the pitch goes back. [[Interviewing]] is how missing voices
arrive; [[Releases and Consent]] is how they arrive protected; and
[[Our Newsroom Standards]] is where we wrote down that both matter.
