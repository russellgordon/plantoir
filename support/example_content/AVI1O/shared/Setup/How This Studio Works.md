Art is learned by making things. Almost every period in this course is
spent with materials in front of you, and the pages on this site are what
you read before and after — not instead.

## A normal period

1. **Look.** Ten minutes on something: a work, a demonstration, or each
   other's progress. The class page names which.
2. **Work.** The long middle of the period, at your own table, on the
   thing that is currently unfinished — [[How a Studio Period Works]].
3. **Clear up.** The last ten minutes, every time, and not optional —
   [[Clearing Up]].

## What the course asks of you

- **Bring the sketchbook every day**, not only when something is due.
  It is where the thinking is kept, and it is assessed — [[The Sketchbook]].
- **Start before you feel ready.** Waiting for a good idea is the most
  reliable way to lose a period.
- **Keep the failures.** The trial that went wrong is evidence of process,
  and evidence of process is worth marks here.
- **Name what you are doing** with the right word for it — the medium,
  the tool, the technique.
- **Look after shared tools and other people's unfinished work.** Half of
  what is on the drying rack is fragile and none of it is yours.

## Experience is not the entry price

Some of you have drawn since you could hold a pencil and some have not
made anything since Grade 6. Both are normal here. The experienced will be
asked for range and risk; everybody is asked for exactly what the criteria
table on each task names, and none of those rows is about how much you
knew in September. Neither of you starts ahead in the marks —
[[How Marks Work]] shows you why.

## The shape of the year

Four units — seeing and making, media and technique, art and meaning, and
bringing work to an audience. The year ends with [[The Exhibition]], a
public show you select, install, and stand beside, and with
[[The Portfolio and Reflection]] you have been building since September.

> [!warning] If something is not working
> A piece that has gone dead, a medium your hands hate, a material you
> react to, or a reason you cannot work the way the rest of the room is
> working — say so early. Most of it is fixable in one conversation, and
> none of it is a mark against you. [[Getting Help]] lists the ways to
> reach me, and [[Materials and Safety]] covers anything you are breathing
> or touching.
