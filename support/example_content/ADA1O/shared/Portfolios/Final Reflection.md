The final reflection is the last piece of writing in this course: one
sustained look **across** your whole [[Drama Journal]], written in the
final week. Every other entry looked at a single day. This one reads the
entire record and answers the only question a course can really be
measured by: *what do you leave with that you did not arrive with?*

## Before you write

Reread your journal front to back in one sitting — expect your earliest
entries to feel written by someone else. As you read, flag three things:

- one entry that embarrasses you a little — that is usually where the
  growth is buried
- one "what I would try next" that you actually tried, and what happened
- one moment involving other people that you still think about

## The shape

Three sections, roughly a page — claims and evidence in the spirit of
[[Writing About Drama]], built the way [[Showing Growth]] taught: date
it, quote your own words, then show the now.

1. **Then and now.** The single biggest difference between your earliest
self and your most recent one, proven with two cited entries.
2. **What I made and what it taught me.** One task —
   [[Tableau Story Sequence]], [[Scene Study from a Story]], the
   [[Culminating Performance]] — examined honestly: what the group needed
   from you, what you gave, what you would do differently.
3. **What leaves the room with me.** The skills you will still use when
   drama class is a memory: reading a room, holding a silence, giving a
   note without wounding, getting a group unstuck. This is the bet named
   in [[B3. Connections Beyond the Classroom|strand B3]] — that what you
   built here works everywhere. Say where you will use it, and for what.

> [!tip] Do not write it as a thank-you card
> The temptation is to perform gratitude and round everything up. Resist
> it. The reflections worth reading include the day the group fell apart,
> the risk that failed, the skill still unfinished. You have a term of
> evidence that honesty is what makes this writing good. Trust it one
> last time.
