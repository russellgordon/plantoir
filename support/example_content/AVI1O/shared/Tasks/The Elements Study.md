**Due: the end of Unit 1.** Individual. A set of five small works and a
page of writing.

Five studies, each one built around a single element or principle,
pushed as far as it will go.

## What you produce

Five small works — no larger than a hand-span — each one dominated by
one choice:

1. **Line**, doing all the work.
2. **Value**, from lightest to darkest, no colour.
3. **Texture**, real or implied.
4. **Balance**, symmetrical or deliberately not.
5. **Emphasis** — one thing the eye goes to, and everything else
   serving it.

Then a page of writing, in two parts:

- **Your five.** What each study was trying to do, whether it worked,
  and the correct terminology for what you used.
- **Somebody else's one.** A short paragraph on one study from the
  gallery walk that is not yours, using the same vocabulary: which
  element is doing the work, and what it does to a viewer. Describe it —
  this is not a rating, and nothing you write about a classmate's study
  goes anywhere near their mark.

## What is marked

| Criterion | What it looks like |
| --- | --- |
| The element | Clearly dominant, and pushed rather than mentioned |
| Craft | Made carefully, at the level Grade 9 can reach in a period |
| Expression | The choices carry a feeling or an idea, not just a demonstration |
| Terminology | The right words, used accurately about your own five studies and about the classmate's study you write on |
| Range | Five genuinely different results, not one idea five times |

> [!tip] Push it further than feels sensible
> A value study with four greys is an exercise; one with twelve is a
> discovery. The studies that go too far are the ones you learn from,
> and nothing here is big enough to waste much material.

[[The Elements of Design]] and [[The Principles of Design]] are what
this is drawn from, and every study belongs in [[The Sketchbook]] first.

Triangulation — the evidence you will not have unless you go and get it.

OBSERVE — Unit 1, Day 12, the revision period after the silent
look-around
  Watch for: what a student does with the study that is not working now
  that somebody has told them. Some reach for a fresh sheet and start a
  sixth study; some put a softer pencil back into the same sheet. Five
  finished studies arrive on Day 15 looking equally deliberate either
  way, and the writing will describe the survivor.
  Going well: the same sheet gets darker, rougher or emptier — a change
  to the element, not to the edges.
  Stuck: the period goes on borders, erasing and sharpening. Craft is a
  row here, so it is not wasted time, but it is the one row that was
  already safe.
  Record: three columns down the margin of your day plan — pushed,
  tidied, started over — initials only, on one circuit of the room.

TALK — Unit 1, Day 14, the conferences already on that agenda
  Ask: "Which study did you change after the look-around, and what did
  you decide NOT to change even though somebody mentioned it?"
  Then: "Did the feeling in the emphasis study come out of the element,
  or did you decide on the feeling first and then reach for an element
  to carry it?"
  A strong answer separates the element from the subject: not "it is
  about being lonely" but "the emphasis is one small dark shape in a
  large pale field, and that is what makes it lonely". That is A2.1
  heard out loud — feeling expressed THROUGH a chosen element — and five
  finished studies cannot tell you whether the choice came before the
  feeling or was found in it. The refusal in the first question is worth
  as much as the change: a student who can defend not acting is reading
  their own work, not obeying the room.
  Record: one line per student on the class list, in the period.

The product evidence is the five studies on Day 15 and the writing on
Day 17. That part arrives on its own.
