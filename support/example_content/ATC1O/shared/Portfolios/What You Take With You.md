Most of you will not become dancers. That is not a disappointing sentence — it
is the reason this page exists. A year in a studio builds a set of things that
are not dance, and they are more portable than the dancing is.

## Naming them honestly

The trick is to avoid the vague version. "Teamwork" and "confidence" are on
everybody's résumé and mean nothing on their own. What you have is more
specific, and each item has a moment in your portfolio attached to it — which
is what turns a claim into evidence.

| What dance built | Where the evidence is |
| --- | --- |
| Taking a correction without defending yourself | Journal entries after [[A Technique Class\|technique class]] |
| Working with people you did not choose | Your notes from [[Devising in a Group\|group devising]] |
| Reading a room — knowing when to speak, when to watch | What you wrote at [[Showing and Feedback\|showings]] |
| Discipline nobody is watching | Practice logs, and the drafts you did anyway |
| Saying something useful about somebody's work | Your responses in [[The Dance Review\|the review]] |
| Sticking with something that was bad for six weeks | Draft two against draft five |

For each row you keep, name the specific artefact — the date, the file, the
entry. "I got better at taking feedback" is an assertion. "Early in the course I argued
with a correction and later in the course I wrote it down and tried it" is a finding.

## Where dance actually lives near you

The other half of this page is outward. Dance is in your community whether or
not you noticed: studios and competitive teams, community centres and seniors'
programmes, cultural organisations and language schools, places of worship,
social dance nights, wedding halls, school and college programmes, gyms,
festivals, and a very large amount of dancing that happens on screens and in
living rooms with no organisation behind it at all.

Find **three** that are within reach of where you live, and for each one
answer: who is it for, what does it cost, and what would you have to do to
walk in the door. Then ask the harder question — who in your community is
served by none of them. Cost, transport, age, gender, disability, and language
all decide who dances, and the map of what exists is also a map of what does
not. [[Dance and Community Task]] is where you go and look.

You do not have to keep dancing. But knowing where it is means the option
stays open, which is a different thing from it quietly closing.
[[Who Is Dance For|Who Is Dance For?]] is the seminar that asks whether any of this was ever
about performance, and [[The Portfolio and Reflection]] is where you write it
down for marks.
