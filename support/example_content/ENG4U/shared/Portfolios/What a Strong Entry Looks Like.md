Two entries about the same class.

## Thin

> Read Act 3. Hamlet doesn't kill Claudius while he's praying. Discussed
> whether his reason is real.

## Strong

> 3.3. Hamlet gives a reason — killing a man at prayer sends him to
> heaven — and it is the only time in the play he explains a delay with
> theology rather than with evidence. Everywhere else the vocabulary is
> legal: *prove*, *ground*, *catch the conscience*.
>
> In seminar I said the reason is a rationalisation. Nadia said that
> assumes we know his real reason, which the play never gives us, and
> that assuming one is exactly what four centuries of criticism has done.
> I think she is right that my reading imports a psychology; I still
> think the register shift is evidence of something.
>
> Question: does he ever again explain himself in religious terms?

Three moves: a **pattern noticed** (register), a **thought that changed**
and who changed it, and a **question that could be tested**.

## The shape to copy

| Move | Prompt |
| --- | --- |
| Notice | One quoted phrase, with act/scene or page |
| Think | What it does |
| Argue | What somebody else said, and where you now stand |
| Ask | What you cannot yet answer |
