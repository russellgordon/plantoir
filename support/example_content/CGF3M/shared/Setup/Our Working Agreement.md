This course spends real time outdoors, on ground that is not ours, in
groups, and argues about questions where people who are not in the room
have a great deal at stake. That combination needs a few things settled
in advance.

## In the field

The field is a workplace, and it belongs to somebody — a municipality, a
landowner, a farmer, a conservation authority. We are guests every time.
That means gates left as we found them, nothing collected without being
asked, nothing disturbed that we do not need to disturb, and instructions
from the person who owns the place followed at once and without argument.

Safety instructions are not open to negotiation in the moment. If you
disagree with one, say so afterwards and you will get a real answer.
Everything specific is in [[Fieldwork Safety]], and it is read before
each field day, not once at the start of the course.

## In seminars

The discussions in this course are about who bears risk, who pays, and
who decides. Real communities are behind every one of them, and some
people in this room will have lived through a flood, an evacuation, or a
fire.

So: argue with the position, not the person. Represent the other side's
strongest version, not its weakest. Say "I have changed my mind" out loud
when it happens, because that is the point of a seminar. And when a
discussion touches a community you are not from — including First Nations
whose territory and rights are directly involved in many of these
questions — go to what that community has said for itself rather than
speaking for it.

## In your work

Every figure carries a source and a date. Every borrowed sentence is
quoted and attributed. Generative artificial intelligence may help you
find a source, and it is never a source; if you cannot trace a claim to
something published with an author and a year, it does not go in — see
[[Judging a Source]].

Group work means the work is divided and the understanding is not. Each
member should be able to explain the whole thing.

## What you can expect from me

Feedback in time to use it. Clear criteria before you start rather than
after you finish. An honest answer when I do not know something. Help
sought quietly, kept quietly. And a reason, whenever you ask for one, why
we are doing what we are doing today.
