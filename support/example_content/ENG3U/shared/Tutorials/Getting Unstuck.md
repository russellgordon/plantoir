Being stuck is not a sign that you are bad at this. It is a normal stage
of writing that experienced writers plan for. What separates them is that
they have moves to make, and they do not sit there.

## Stuck at the start

- **Write the middle first.** Nobody has to know. Openings are easier
  when you know what they are opening.
- **Say it out loud to a person**, then write down what you said. Your
  spoken sentence is usually better than the one you were trying to type.
- **Write the bad version on purpose.** "This poem is about losing your
  language and it makes me sad" is a bad first sentence and a perfectly
  good tenth one, once you know why it is sad.
- **Set a timer for six minutes** and do not stop moving the pen. What
  comes out is raw material, not a draft.

## Stuck in the middle

- **Read what you have out loud.** You will hear the place it stopped
  working, usually two sentences before you thought.
- **Ask "so what?" of your last sentence** and write the answer. That is
  often the paragraph you were missing.
- **Go back to the text.** Being stuck in an essay almost always means
  you are short of evidence, not short of words.

## Stuck because the reading is too hard

1. Read the paragraph aloud. Slowly. Out loud is not optional here.
2. Circle every word you cannot define and look up three of them.
3. Say the paragraph in one sentence to yourself, even if you are not
   sure.
4. If it still will not open, mark it `?` and read on — a later page
   often explains an earlier one.
5. Bring the `?` to class. That is what the first ten minutes are for.

## Stuck because you do not care about the topic

This is a real problem and it has a real solution: change the topic, or
find the part of it you do care about. Most task pages in this course let
you choose. Come and argue for your version — the pitch takes two minutes
and it is almost always accepted.

> [!tip] The rule that saves the most time
> Twenty minutes stuck is information. Forty is a waste. At twenty
> minutes, do something different: ask, move on, or write badly on
> purpose. Sitting in front of a blank page is not effort — it just feels
> like it.
