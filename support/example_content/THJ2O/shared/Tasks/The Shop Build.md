**Due: the end of Unit 2.** Pairs, with an individual mark. One built
object, from a drawing.

A cold frame, a raised bed, a potting bench, a compost bin, a nursery
tray rack, or a tool board — something the course will actually use.

One object, two marks. **Your take-off calculations are your own, your
build log is your own, and the cut list records who cut each piece** —
so divide the cutting and the fastening rather than letting one person
do it all. What you can show you did is what you are marked on.

## The work

1. **Read the drawing.** Plan, elevation, dimensions, materials list —
   see [[Reading a Working Drawing]].
2. **Take off the quantities.** How much material, at what sizes, with
   what waste allowance. Both metric and imperial, because suppliers use
   both.
3. **Cut and build**, square and to dimension, with the fastenings the
   job calls for.
4. **Finish it** for outdoor use, and say why you chose that finish.
5. **Record it** — photographs before, during, and after, with the
   measurements you actually achieved against the ones drawn.

## What is marked

| Criterion | The standard |
| --- | --- |
| Measurement | Accurate, in both systems, with calculations shown |
| Squareness and fit | It is square, and the joints close |
| Tool use | The right tool for the cut, used safely and within what it is made for |
| Durability | It will survive a winter outside |
| Record | Your build log matches what actually happened |

Four of those five rows describe one object that two of you made, so
each is judged on the pieces the cut list says you cut, the fastenings
your log says you drove, and the numbers on your own take-off. That is
why the cut list gets initialled as you go.

> [!tip] Measure twice is not a joke
> Every cut you get wrong costs material the department paid for, and
> the most common error in this build is reading the wrong side of the
> tape — see [[Measuring and Marking]].

The object stays in service, so build it as though the next class
depends on it, because they do.

Triangulation — the evidence you will not have unless you go and get it.

Pairs, so both prompts also have to produce evidence about ONE student.
Ask partners separately and note names, never a pair.

OBSERVE — Unit 2, Day 10, cutting to the list
  Watch which side of the mark the blade takes. Cutting on the waste side
  every time and cutting down the middle of the line every time both
  produce a frame that closes; across a dozen pieces the second one is
  three millimetres short and the build log will never say so. Second
  thing: whether the tape is hooked from the same end each time or
  flipped halfway down the cut list.
  Third, and this is the one the mark depends on: whose hands are on the
  saw. A pair in which one student makes every cut leaves the other with
  no B2.2 evidence at all — selecting, measuring, cutting, joining — and
  the finished object cannot tell you which of them did it.
  Going well: mark, line, cut beside it, offcut kept until the piece is
  checked against the drawing.
  Stuck: the tape reversed and nobody noticing; one student fetching
  material for four periods.
  Record: on the cut list you issued, initial each piece with whoever
  cut it. That is the individual evidence, and it costs a pencil stroke.

TALK — Unit 2, Day 12, at the conferences on fit already on that agenda
  The agenda tells them the conference is about fit, so open past it.
  Ask: "Show me on the drawing where this dimension came from."
  Then: "What did you have to convert, and what did you round?"
  A strong answer puts a finger on a stated dimension or a scale bar and
  says which units it was in. That is B2.3 with A4.3 attached — reading
  the drawing, and working in both systems — and neither is provable
  from a square box, because a pair can measure everything off the first
  piece they cut and never look at the plan again.
  Record: initial, on your copy of the drawing, the dimension each
  student pointed at.

The product evidence is the object and the build log, Day 14.
