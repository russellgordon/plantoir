A story is not a sequence of events. It is a sequence of events in which
somebody wants something and something is in the way — which is why "I
got up, ate breakfast, and came to school" is not a story, and "I got up
late" almost is.

## The parts, and what each is for

| Part | What it does | The question it answers |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Exposition** | Sets up the world and who matters in it | Who, where, when? |
| **Inciting incident** | Breaks the ordinary | What went wrong? |
| **Rising action** | Complicates the want | What is now harder? |
| **Climax** | The moment the outcome is decided | What is at stake right now? |
| **Falling action** | The consequences land | What changed? |
| **Resolution** | The new ordinary | Who is this person now? |

That shape is a description, not a rule. Plenty of good stories skip
parts, start in the middle, or refuse the resolution entirely — and when
one does, that refusal is usually the meaning.

## Conflict, in four flavours

- **Person against person** — the obvious one.
- **Person against self** — the most common in serious fiction, and the
  hardest to see, because it happens in silence.
- **Person against society** — the rules, the school, the law, the
  country.
- **Person against nature or circumstance** — the storm, the illness, the
  timetable.

Most good stories run two at once. In *The Marrow Thieves* the hunt is
person-against-society; the question of what Frenchie owes the group is
person-against-self, and it is the one that keeps you reading.

## Where the story actually starts

Writers begin as late as they possibly can. "A Mountain Legend" does not
begin with a boy's whole childhood; it begins with him at the foot of the
mountain. Ask of any opening: *why here?* The answer is nearly always
"because everything before this was not necessary."

> [!question] Worth arguing about
> Is a story still a story if nothing changes? Take any text where the
> character ends where they began and defend both answers. The good
> discussions in this room start from questions with two defensible
> sides.
