One notebook, all semester, for annotation notes, drafting, and the record of
what you can now do.

## What goes in

- **Responses to the reading** — not summaries. What you noticed, what
  you argued with, what you could not work out.
- **Passage notes with page numbers**, ready for seminars.
- **Drafting.** Every major piece starts here.
- **After discussions**: the position you came in with and where you left
  it, per [[Our Agreements]].
- **Your own reading**, a few lines every couple of weeks.
- **Vocabulary**, at the back: words met in reading, with the sentence
  they were found in.

## How it is used

In seminars, open on the desk. In workshops, as the source of your
draft. In the essay-writing periods, where a page of notes beats a blank
document every time — and in the examination, where the only thing you
bring is what you can remember having thought.

## The habit

Write the entry the same day. An entry written at the end of the course about a
the first class is a memory of a memory.

> [!tip] If journals have never worked for you
> Three sentences: what happened in the text, what you thought, one
> question for the writer. Three of those a week is a real journal, and
> it is enough to carry a seminar.
