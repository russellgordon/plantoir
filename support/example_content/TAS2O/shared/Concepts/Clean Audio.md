In [[Record a Clean Interview]] there was probably a take that looked
perfect and sounded like it was recorded in a swimming pool. That take
taught the central law of production: viewers forgive soft focus and
shaky framing, but muffled, echoing, or windy sound makes them leave.
Audio quality, more than anything visual, decides whether a piece feels
professional.

## The microphone is honest

A microphone has no attention. Your brain filters a noisy room — it
tunes out the ventilation fan, the hallway, the gym two doors down —
but the microphone records the room exactly as it is, and the recording
is the first time most people ever hear it. Trust headphones, not your
ears: monitor while you record, because the take you *hear* going wrong
can be fixed on the spot, and the one you discover tomorrow cannot.

## Distance, rooms, wind

**Distance** is the cheapest upgrade you will ever get. Sound fades
fast as a source moves away, so halving the distance between microphone
and mouth makes the voice dominate everything else. This is why phones
held at arm's length sound amateur and a microphone near the collar
sounds like broadcast.

**Rooms** are instruments. Hard surfaces — gyms, hallways, kitchens —
bounce sound back as echo; soft ones — curtains, carpet, a parked car —
absorb it. You cannot change the room, but you can usually change rooms
before an interview, or step a scrum away from the bleachers.

**Wind** does not record as a breeze; it records as destruction. A foam
cover helps, your own body as a windbreak helps more, and a doorway
helps most of all.

> [!warning] There is no "fix it in the edit"
> Editing can trim clean audio, but it cannot remove echo, wind, or a
> voice recorded from too far away — the damage is baked into the file.
> Sixty seconds of moving closer and changing rooms is worth more than
> six hours of editing.

Every rule here applies double when the camera is rolling: sound is
half of every frame you gather for [[Shooting Video]], and a
[[Record a Standup|standup]] lives or dies on the voice.
