> [!abstract] At a glance
> **Time:** one class. **Whole class, in groups of four.** Data pooled
> across the room.

Coloured paper dots scattered on patterned fabric, and students as
predators with ten seconds to feed.

## Method

1. Scatter 100 dots — 25 each of four colours — on the fabric.
2. Each "predator" collects as many as possible in ten seconds.
3. Survivors reproduce: every remaining dot is doubled, back to 100 total
   in the same proportions.
4. Repeat for five generations, recording the population composition each
   time.

## What to record

A table of colour proportions by generation, and a line graph of all four
colours over time.

## Analysis

1. Which colour increased? Why, specifically?
2. Did any colour disappear entirely? What does the model say about how
   easily variation is lost?
3. Now change the fabric between generations three and four. What happens,
   and what does that model in the real world?
4. **Where the model lies:** dots do not mutate, do not compete for food,
   and reproduce by doubling. Name one conclusion this simulation could
   mislead you into, and how you would test it properly.
