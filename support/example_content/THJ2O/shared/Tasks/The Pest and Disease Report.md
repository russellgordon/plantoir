**Due: late Unit 3.** Pairs, with an individual mark. About 700 words
plus photographs.

One pest or disease that is actually present, or actually a threat, in
our woodlot, our beds, or the farm — identified, monitored, and reported
with a management recommendation.

Monitor together; write separately. **Each of you writes and signs your
own sections 4 and 5 — the management recommendation and the judged
sources** — and your own monitoring sheet from the woodlot transect goes
in with your name on it. Sections 1 to 3 you may write jointly, and I
will ask each of you about them on your own.

## What you produce

1. **Identification**, with your own photographs and the features you
   used. Say how confident you are and what would confirm it.
2. **The damage**: what it does, at what stage, and how to tell it from
   something else.
3. **Monitoring**: how you would check for it, how often, and what
   threshold would mean action.
4. **Management**, using integrated pest management — cultural,
   physical, biological, and chemical controls in that order, and what
   Ontario's rules allow. **Recommend a course of action, not a product.**
5. **Sources**, with dates — government, extension, or industry, judged
   for reliability.

## What is marked

| Criterion | The standard |
| --- | --- |
| Identification | Correct, with evidence, and honest about uncertainty |
| Monitoring | A method someone could actually follow weekly |
| IPM reasoning | Prevention and non-chemical options considered first and seriously |
| Sources | Current, authoritative, and cited |
| Ethics | You address the trade-offs — non-target species, water, workers, and cost |

Identification and monitoring come from sections you may have written
together, so those two rows are judged on your own transect sheet and
on what you tell me about the find when I ask you on your own. The
other three rows are your writing already.

> [!warning] This course does not teach pesticide application
> Applying pesticides is regulated work with licensing requirements, and
> Ontario restricts cosmetic use. Your report identifies, monitors, and
> recommends an approach; it names no product and no rate. That is the
> professional boundary for a Grade 10 course, and it is assessed.

[[Pests and Diseases in Our Woodlot]] is the fieldwork behind this and
[[Where to Find Out]] is how you judge a source.

Triangulation — the evidence you will not have unless you go and get it.

OBSERVE — Unit 3, Day 10, the monitoring transect in the woodlot
  Day 10 is the day before the report is launched, and that is
  deliberate: nobody is performing for a criteria sheet they have not
  read yet. What you see is how this class monitors when monitoring is
  just the job. The transect sheet goes into the report on Day 18, so
  the product corroborates it.
  Watch for: leaves turned over. Damage at eye height is the damage
  everybody finds, and a report's photographs look thorough whether or
  not anyone looked at an underside, a stem base, or a bud. A2.3 is
  identifying the pest and describing its effect, and half the effects
  are on surfaces nobody photographs.
  Second thing: whether anything is recorded where nothing was found. A
  zero is data and it is the first entry to go missing, which is what
  makes a Day 12 claim about "spreading" unfalsifiable.
  Going well: crouching, a leaf turned, an empty row written down.
  Stuck: one photograph of the worst-hit tree and a blank sheet.
  Record: two ticks against each name on the transect sheet — looked
  underneath, wrote down a zero.

TALK — Unit 3, Day 12, during the research period, each partner on their
own
  Ask: "Who paid for the page you are reading, and what would they like
  you to conclude?"
  Then: "Which part of your recommendation would the person who has to
  carry it out complain about?"
  A strong first answer names what KIND of source it is — a government
  fact sheet, a manufacturer's catalogue, an industry newsletter — and
  then whose interest sits behind it. Naming the kind is A4.2, which
  asks only that a student can identify where the information comes
  from; naming the interest is C2.2, and so is a strong second answer,
  which puts a cost on somebody who is not the student: the person
  spraying, a neighbour's well, the bees. A written report reaches the
  same recommendation whether the sources were weighed or the first
  agreeable one was kept.
  Record: on the back of the transect sheet, one line per STUDENT —
  source, whose interest, who pays.

The product evidence is the report handed in on Day 18.
