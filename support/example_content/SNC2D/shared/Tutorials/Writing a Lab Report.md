Every formal report in this course uses the same seven sections. Learn
the shape once and it carries you through every science course after
this one — and through anything you ever have to write where somebody
needs to check your reasoning.

## The sections

| Section | What belongs there | Common mistake |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Question | The thing you set out to find out, in one sentence | Restating the title |
| Prediction | What you expect **and the mechanism you expect it from** | "I think it will work" |
| Variables | Independent, dependent, and what you held constant | Listing two and forgetting the third |
| Materials and procedure | Numbered steps someone could repeat exactly | Written as a story afterwards |
| Observations | Data, tables, graphs, qualitative notes — no interpretation | Explaining results here |
| Analysis | Calculations, graphs, what the numbers say | Repeating the table in sentences |
| Conclusion and limitations | The answer, the evidence for it, and what it cannot settle | Claiming more than the data supports |

Grade 10 adds the second and third rows. Last year a procedure was
handed to you and the variables came with it. This year you design the
investigation, which means **you** decide what to change, what to
measure, and what to hold still — and if you cannot name all three, you
do not yet have an experiment.

## Predictions need a mechanism

A prediction without a "because" is a guess with a confident tone.

> ❌ The dark container will heat up more.
>
> ✅ The dark container will heat up more **because** a dark surface
> absorbs a larger fraction of the incoming radiation, converting it to
> thermal energy rather than reflecting it away.

The second version can be wrong in a *useful* way. If the temperatures
come out equal, the first version tells you nothing; the second tells
you which link in the chain to go and check.

> [!important] Write the prediction before you measure
> Not after, and not "roughly what I was expecting". Record it, with
> its reasoning, in your [[Science Journal]] before the first reading.
> That record is the only way to find out whether your reasoning about
> mechanism is improving, which is the actual skill this course is
> teaching.

## Procedure: written so it can be repeated

Past tense, numbered, with quantities, concentrations, and instrument
ranges. "The solution was heated to 40 °C", not "Heat the solution".
The test is not whether it reads well — it is whether another group
could follow it and get your result without asking you a question.

## Conclusions: answer, evidence, limits

Three moves, in order.

> The dark container rose 8.4 °C over ten minutes and the light one
> rose 3.1 °C, under the same lamp at the same distance, which supports
> the prediction that surface colour affects the rate of heating.
>
> However, the two containers were not sealed identically and the dark
> one sat closer to the window, so some of the difference may come from
> the surroundings rather than the surface. The effect is larger than
> our thermometer's 0.5 °C resolution, so the difference itself is real
> even if its cause is not fully isolated.

That second paragraph is where the marks are. It states what the
evidence supports, and separately states what it cannot yet decide.

> [!warning] "Sources of error" does not mean "mistakes I made"
> A limitation is something about the method that would still be there
> if you ran it perfectly — resolution of the instrument, heat leaking
> to the room, a variable you could not control. "We measured the wrong
> tube" is a blunder; you repeat the trial. See [[Mistakes Are Data]]
> for how to tell them apart, and say **which way** each limitation
> pushed your result, not merely that it existed.

## Before you submit

- [ ] Prediction states a mechanism, and was recorded first
- [ ] All three kinds of variable named
- [ ] Every measurement carries a unit
- [ ] Graphs follow [[Drawing Scientific Diagrams]] and
      [[Reading a Graph Honestly]]
- [ ] Conclusion answers the question using your own numbers
- [ ] At least one limitation, with the direction it pushed the result
- [ ] One specific change you would make, not "be more careful"
