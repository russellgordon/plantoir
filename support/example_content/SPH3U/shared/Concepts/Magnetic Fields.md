A magnetic field is a region where a magnetic force would act. We draw it
with field lines: out of the north pole, into the south, never crossing,
closer together where the field is stronger.

## Current makes magnetism

A wire carrying current has a circular magnetic field around it. Curl the
fingers of your right hand around the wire with your thumb pointing along
the conventional current, and your fingers point the way the field goes.

Coil the wire into a solenoid and the fields inside add up into something
that looks exactly like a bar magnet's — with poles you can reverse by
reversing the current. That controllability is the whole reason
electromagnets run doorbells, scrapyards, and maglev trains.

## Earth's field

Earth has one, generated in its liquid iron core, and it is why a compass
works. Its magnetic north pole is near — but not at — geographic north,
and it wanders measurably from decade to decade. Navigation charts print
the local correction.

## Fields are how forces act at a distance

Nothing touches the compass needle. The magnet changes the space around
it, and the needle responds to the space where it sits. That idea —
field first, force second — is one of the most productive in physics, and
it returns in Grade 12 for gravity and electricity too.
