Every task page in this course publishes what it is looking for before
you start. Most people read that list once, on launch day, and then
hand in a paper that misses two things they could have repaired in half
an hour. This page is the half hour.

The first time, we do it together on a draft that belongs to nobody in
the room, so that the move is familiar before it is your own work being
judged.

## The routine

1. Put the task page beside your draft and work down its criteria, one
   at a time.
2. Against each one write **holds**, **partly**, or **not yet** — and
   one line of evidence: the paragraph, the figure, the sentence a
   reader would point at. A verdict with no evidence is a hope.
3. Name your weakest line. Not the cheapest to repair — the weakest.
4. Write one sentence saying what you will change, and in which period.
5. Change it, in the time that follows. This is why the self-check is
   never set for the day something is due: a judgement you have no time
   to act on is only a worry.

Steps 2 and 3 are the whole exercise. Naming the weakest line honestly
is harder than any of the repairing, because it means writing down the
thing you were hoping would go unread.

> [!example]- One line, judged honestly
> From [[The Indicators Report]]: *every figure carries three things —
> the value, the reference period, and the table it came from.*
>
> **Partly.** Output and employment carry all three. Both inflation
> figures carry a value and a month and no table, because I took them
> from a news article instead of the release. Weakest line, and it is
> the one this task says is worth the most.
> **Change:** retrieve both from 18-10-0004-01 in tomorrow's work
> period, and put the release date beside each.
>
> That paragraph is worth more than "looks fine" written against six
> criteria, and it took two minutes.

## Two habits it builds that outlast the course

**Reading a standard as a reader would.** An economic argument is
judged by somebody who did not write it, and the gap between what you
meant and what is actually on the page is invisible from the inside
until you go looking for it on purpose.

**Saying where your own case is weak before anyone else does.** Every
strong paper in this course names the evidence that cuts against it.
This is the same move, turned on your own work instead of on the
argument.

## Where this sits in the mark

It does not. Your judgement of your own work is not part of your mark,
and neither is a classmate's — [[How Marks Work]] says why. Nothing you
write on a self-check can cost you anything, which is exactly the
property that makes it worth writing honestly.

What it changes is the work. A self-check that produces one repair is the
cheapest improvement available in this course, and by the end of the course
most people run it without being told to.

One last thing worth doing: read your verdicts out loud to the person
beside you before you act on them. You will hear the rows where you
were being generous to yourself, usually before they say a word.
