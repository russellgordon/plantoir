The flagpole out front has a height, and nobody is allowed to climb
it. Your group's equipment: a metre stick, a tape measure, and a
sunny day. Your deliverable is not just a number — it is a number you
would bet on, with a margin you would defend.

## The task

Produce the height of the flagpole, stated as a value *plus or minus*
a margin — "9.4 m, give or take 0.3" — along with the method that
earned it. The metre stick cannot reach the top, so something else
must connect the reachable world to the unreachable one. Measure
carefully, record everything, and be ready to answer the auditor's
question: of all the measurements you took, which one is most likely
to have lied to you, and by how much could it move your answer?

> [!question]- Getting started (click to expand)
> - At this moment, your own shadow and your own height know
>   something about each other. What, exactly?
> - Whatever that relationship is — does the flagpole's shadow have
>   any reason to behave differently?
> - Measure both shadows *at the same time*. Why does ten minutes
>   apart quietly wreck the whole method?
> - Cloudy day? A mirror flat on the ground, and a careful line of
>   sight, builds the same pair of triangles without the sun.

## What mathematics tends to surface

The sun draws two triangles — yours and the flagpole's — with equal
angles, and equal angles force proportional sides: the central fact
of [[Similar Triangles]], caught doing outdoor work. The ratio of
height to shadow is the same for every object under the same sun, and
using it is proportional reasoning with real dirt on it. Later, a
clinometer and [[The Primary Trigonometric Ratios]] give a second,
sun-free route to the same pole — and if two independent methods
disagree by two metres, at least one of them is being flattered.

## An extension

Run the clinometer method and compare. Precision versus honesty:
which method *sounds* more precise, and which deserves more trust?
Rank your error sources — slouching metre stick, fuzzy shadow edge,
angle read a degree high — by how much each moves the final answer.
That audit is [[Checking Your Own Work]] at full scale, and the
skills point straight at [[Inaccessible Heights]] and
[[Trig Ratios and Laws Practice]].

> [!note] The answer is not on this page
> No height is printed here — the flagpole is not even the same at
> every school. The number, and its margin, belong to your group.
